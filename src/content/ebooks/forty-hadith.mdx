## About this book

Imam al-Nawawi collected forty hadith, and Ibn Rajab added two more, so this book holds 42. They are short enough to memorize and deep enough to study for years. This book helps with both: you learn the words, and you see why each one is written the way it is.

## How each hadith is laid out

Each hadith starts with the whole text in one piece: the Arabic with every vowel mark, the sound of it in Latin letters, and an English translation. Read it through once before anything else.

The word-by-word part comes next. For every word you get the Arabic, its sound, its meaning, and a short note on why it is written that way: its ending, its tense, or the rule behind it. Long hadith are split into parts, and each part has its own Arabic, translation and word list.

## Words you will meet in the notes

- **Nominative** (marfūʿ): the ending <span class="ar-in">ـُ</span> or <span class="ar-in">ـٌ</span>. It is used for the one who does an action and for what a sentence is about.
- **Accusative** (manṣūb): the ending <span class="ar-in">ـَ</span> or <span class="ar-in">ـً</span>. It is used for what an action is done to, and after words like <span class="ar-in">إِنَّ</span>.
- **Genitive** (majrūr): the ending <span class="ar-in">ـِ</span> or <span class="ar-in">ـٍ</span>. It is used after a preposition, and for the second word of a pair such as "the Messenger of Allah".
- **Tanwīn**: the doubled ending (<span class="ar-in">ـٌ ـً ـٍ</span>). It means "a" or "some".
- **Silent ending**: some verbs end in a silent letter after words like <span class="ar-in">لَمْ</span> and <span class="ar-in">لَا</span>, and a command ends the same way.
- **Passive**: a verb that does not name who did the action. The one acted on takes the doer's place.
- **Attached pronoun**: a small ending that joins a word, such as <span class="ar-in">ـهُ</span> ("his"), <span class="ar-in">ـكَ</span> ("you") and <span class="ar-in">ـنَا</span> ("us").

## Where the text comes from

The Arabic follows al-Nawawi's collection. The wording of every hadith was checked against the Arabic text on Wikisource, and the vowel marks were compared with the vowelled text published by Abu Amina Elias at abuaminaelias.com.

The English translations were written for this book from the Arabic. The grammar notes were written for this book too, and a qualified teacher has not yet reviewed them. Treat them as a learning aid, not a ruling.
