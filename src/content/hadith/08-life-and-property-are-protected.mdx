### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | أُمِرْتُ | umirtu | I was commanded | Past tense in the passive: ḍamma on the first letter and kasra on the one before the last. The one who commanded is not named. The ـتُ ending means "I", the one commanded. |
| 2 | أَنْ أُقَاتِلَ | an uqātila | that I fight | أَنْ makes the present verb after it end in fatḥa. أُقَاتِلَ means "I fight"; the long ā in the middle comes from the pattern that means fighting against someone. |
| 3 | النَّاسَ | an-nāsa | the people | Accusative (ـَ): they are the ones fought. |
| 4 | حَتَّى يَشْهَدُوا | ḥattā yashhadū | until they testify | حَتَّى means "until". It makes the verb after it drop its final ن, so ـُونَ becomes ـُوا. The ـُوا ending means "they". |
| 5 | أَنْ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ | an lā ilāha illā llāhu | that there is no god but Allah | لَا denies the whole kind, so إِلَهَ stays on fatḥa with no tanwīn. اللَّهُ is nominative (ـُ) after إِلَّا. |
| 6 | وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ | wa-anna Muḥammadan rasūlu llāhi | and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah | أَنَّ makes the first word accusative (مُحَمَّدًا, ـًا). رَسُولُ stays nominative (ـُ), and اللَّهِ is genitive (ـِ). |
| 7 | وَيُقِيمُوا | wa-yuqīmū | and they establish | Joined to يَشْهَدُوا by وَ, so it follows the same rule: the ن drops. |
| 8 | الصَّلَاةَ | aṣ-ṣalāta | the prayer | Accusative (ـَ): it is what they establish. |
| 9 | وَيُؤْتُوا الزَّكَاةَ | wa-yuʾtū z-zakāta | and they give the zakat | The ن of the verb drops, as in the rows above. الزَّكَاةَ is accusative (ـَ): it is what they give. |
| 10 | فَإِذَا | fa-idhā | so when | فَ means "so". إِذَا means "when" and starts a condition. |
| 11 | فَعَلُوا | faʿalū | they did | Past tense, they. The ـُوا ending marks "they". |
| 12 | ذَلِكَ | dhālika | that | A pointing word for something further off. It is what they did. It never shows a case vowel. |
| 13 | عَصَمُوا | ʿaṣamū | they protected | Past tense, they. "To protect, to keep safe". |
| 14 | مِنِّي | minnī | from me | مِنْ plus ـي ("me"). A protecting ن is added before the ـي, which is why the ن is doubled. |
| 15 | دِمَاءَهُمْ | dimāʾahum | their blood | Accusative (ـَ): it is what they protected. The attached ـهُمْ means "their". |
| 16 | وَأَمْوَالَهُمْ | wa-amwālahum | and their wealth | Accusative (ـَ): joined to دِمَاءَهُمْ, so it takes the same case. |
| 17 | إِلَّا بِحَقِّ الْإِسْلَامِ | illā bi-ḥaqqi l-islāmi | except for the right of Islam | إِلَّا means "except". بِ makes حَقِّ genitive (ـِ), and الْإِسْلَامِ is genitive because it completes حَقِّ. |
| 18 | وَحِسَابُهُمْ | wa-ḥisābuhum | and their reckoning | Nominative (ـُ): it is what the statement is about. The attached ـهُمْ means "their". |
| 19 | عَلَى اللَّهِ | ʿalā llāhi | is with Allah | عَلَى makes اللَّهِ genitive (ـِ). The phrase completes the statement: "their reckoning rests on Allah". |
| 20 | تَعَالَى | taʿālā | the Most High | A past-tense verb used as a phrase of praise. It ends in a long ā, so no vowel shows. |
</GrammarTable>
