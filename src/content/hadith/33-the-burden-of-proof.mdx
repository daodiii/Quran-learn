## The Hadith

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | لَوْ يُعْطَى | law yuʿṭā | if were given | لَوْ means "if". يُعْطَى is present tense in the passive: ḍamma on the first letter and fatḥa on the one before the last. The one who gives is not named. It ends in a long ā. |
| 2 | النَّاسُ | an-nāsu | the people | Nominative (ـُ): with a passive verb, they take the place of the one who does the action. |
| 3 | بِدَعْوَاهُمْ | bi-daʿwāhum | according to their claims | بِ makes دَعْوَى genitive, but it ends in a long ā, so no vowel shows. The attached ـهُمْ means "their". |
| 4 | لَادَّعَى | la-d-daʿā | they would claim | لَ opens the answer to "if". The verb is past tense, he. The doubled د comes from the pattern meaning "to claim something". |
| 5 | رِجَالٌ | rijālun | men | Nominative (ـٌ): they are who would claim. |
| 6 | أَمْوَالَ قَوْمٍ | amwāla qawmin | the wealth of some people | أَمْوَالَ is accusative (ـَ): it is what they would claim. قَوْمٍ is genitive (ـٍ) because it completes it. |
| 7 | وَدِمَاءَهُمْ | wa-dimāʾahum | and their blood | Accusative (ـَ): joined to أَمْوَالَ. The attached ـهُمْ means "their". |
| 8 | لَكِنَّ | lākinna | but | Like إِنَّ, it makes the noun after it accusative. |
| 9 | الْبَيِّنَةَ | al-bayyinata | the proof | Accusative (ـَ) after لَكِنَّ. |
| 10 | عَلَى الْمُدَّعِي | ʿalā l-muddaʿī | on the claimant | عَلَى makes الْمُدَّعِي genitive, but it ends in a long ī, so no vowel shows. |
| 11 | وَالْيَمِينَ | wa-l-yamīna | and the oath | Accusative (ـَ): joined to الْبَيِّنَةَ. |
| 12 | عَلَى مَنْ أَنْكَرَ | ʿalā man ankara | on the one who denies | مَنْ means "the one who". أَنْكَرَ is past tense, he. |
</GrammarTable>
