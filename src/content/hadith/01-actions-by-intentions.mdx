## The Hadith

## Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | إِنَّمَا | innamā | only | Made of إِنَّ ("indeed") plus مَا. The مَا stops إِنَّ from changing the word after it, so the next word stays in its normal nominative form. Together they mean "only". |
| 2 | الْأَعْمَالُ | al-aʿmālu | the actions | Nominative (marfūʿ): it is what the sentence is about, so it ends in ـُ (ḍamma). |
| 3 | بِالنِّيَّاتِ | bi-n-niyyāti | by the intentions | The preposition بِ makes the noun after it genitive (majrūr), shown by ـِ. The plural of a feminine word ending in ـات takes ـِ for the genitive. The phrase completes the meaning: "actions are [judged] by intentions". |
| 4 | وَإِنَّمَا | wa-innamā | and only | وَ adds a second statement; إِنَّمَا works the same as in word 1. |
| 5 | لِكُلِّ | li-kulli | for every | لِ means "for" or "belongs to" and makes كُلّ genitive (ـِ). |
| 6 | امْرِئٍ | imri'in | person | Genitive (ـٍ) because it completes كُلّ: "every person". The tanwīn shows it is indefinite, so "any person". The word امْرُؤ changes the vowel on its ر as well as on its end, and here both are kasra. |
| 7 | مَا | mā | what | Means "that which". It never changes its ending. Here it is the thing each person "has": "for every person [there is] what he intended". |
| 8 | نَوَى | nawā | he intended | Past tense, he (masculine singular). Past verbs end in a fixed fatḥa; the final long ā is part of the verb. It describes مَا: "what he intended". |
| 9 | فَمَنْ | fa-man | so whoever | فَ means "so" and starts the example. مَنْ means "whoever" and begins a condition: "if someone was…". |
| 10 | كَانَتْ | kānat | was | Past of كَانَ. The ـتْ ending marks a feminine subject, because هِجْرَة is feminine. |
| 11 | هِجْرَتُهُ | hijratuhu | his emigration | Nominative (ـُ) because it is the subject of كَانَتْ. The attached ـهُ means "his". |
| 12 | إِلَى اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ | ilā llāhi wa-rasūlihi | to Allah and His Messenger | إِلَى makes اللَّهِ genitive (ـِ). رَسُولِهِ is also genitive because وَ joins it to اللَّهِ, and a joined word follows the case of the word before it. |
| 13 | فَهِجْرَتُهُ | fa-hijratuhu | then his emigration | فَ opens the result of the condition ("then…"). هِجْرَتُهُ is nominative again, as the subject of the result. |
| 14 | إِلَى اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ | ilā llāhi wa-rasūlihi | to Allah and His Messenger | Same wording and same endings as word 12. The result repeats the condition word for word to show that the intention and the outcome are the same. |
| 15 | وَمَنْ كَانَتْ هِجْرَتُهُ | wa-man kānat hijratuhu | and whoever's emigration was | Same pattern as words 9–11, with وَ ("and") adding the second example. |
| 16 | لِدُنْيَا | li-dunyā | for some worldly gain | لِ ("for") makes the noun genitive. The ending is a long ـى, so no case vowel can be written, and the genitive is understood. There is no tanwīn, so it is not "a" specific world but any worldly thing. |
| 17 | يُصِيبُهَا | yuṣībuhā | he gains it | Present tense, he (masculine singular), nominative ـُ. The attached ـهَا is "it" and is feminine because دُنْيَا is. The clause describes the worldly gain: "one he hopes to gain". |
| 18 | أَوِ | awi | or | أَوْ gets a ـِ because the next word starts with a joining hamza, and two silent sounds cannot meet. |
| 19 | امْرَأَةٍ | imra'atin | a woman | Genitive (ـٍ), joined to دُنْيَا by أَوْ, so it copies دُنْيَا's case. |
| 20 | يَنْكِحُهَا | yankiḥuhā | he marries her | Present tense, he, nominative ـُ. The attached ـهَا is "her". The clause describes the woman. |
| 21 | فَهِجْرَتُهُ | fa-hijratuhu | then his emigration | Same as word 13: the result of the second condition. |
| 22 | إِلَى | ilā | to | Preposition. It makes the next word genitive. |
| 23 | مَا | mā | what | Means "that which". It never changes its ending. It stands as the object of إِلَى. |
| 24 | هَاجَرَ | hājara | he emigrated | Past tense, he, with a fixed fatḥa ending. It is the verb "to emigrate" in its Form III pattern, which is where the long ā after the first letter comes from. |
| 25 | إِلَيْهِ | ilayhi | to it | إِلَى changes to إِلَيْ before an attached pronoun, and ـهِ takes kasra because of the ي before it. It points back to مَا: "to that which he emigrated to". |
</GrammarTable>
