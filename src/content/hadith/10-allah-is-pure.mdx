## 1. Allah Accepts Only What Is Pure

<ArabicExample
  arabic="إِنَّ اللَّهَ تَعَالَى طَيِّبٌ لَا يَقْبَلُ إِلَّا طَيِّبًا، وَإِنَّ اللَّهَ أَمَرَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ بِمَا أَمَرَ بِهِ الْمُرْسَلِينَ فَقَالَ تَعَالَى: يَا أَيُّهَا الرُّسُلُ كُلُوا مِنَ الطَّيِّبَاتِ وَاعْمَلُوا صَالِحًا"
  transliteration="inna llāha taʿālā ṭayyibun lā yaqbalu illā ṭayyiban, wa-inna llāha amara l-muʾminīna bi-mā amara bihi l-mursalīna fa-qāla taʿālā: yā ayyuhā r-rusulu kulū mina ṭ-ṭayyibāti wa-ʿmalū ṣāliḥan"

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | إِنَّ اللَّهَ تَعَالَى | inna llāha taʿālā | indeed Allah, the Most High | إِنَّ puts اللَّهَ in the accusative (ـَ). تَعَالَى is a phrase of praise that ends in a long ā, so no vowel shows. |
| 2 | طَيِّبٌ | ṭayyibun | pure | Nominative (ـٌ): it is the second part of the statement. |
| 3 | لَا يَقْبَلُ | lā yaqbalu | does not accept | لَا denies. يَقْبَلُ is present tense, he, nominative (ـُ). It adds a description of Allah. |
| 4 | إِلَّا طَيِّبًا | illā ṭayyiban | except what is pure | إِلَّا means "except". طَيِّبًا is accusative (ـًا) because it is what He accepts. |
| 5 | وَإِنَّ اللَّهَ أَمَرَ | wa-inna llāha amara | and Allah commanded | The same إِنَّ pattern. أَمَرَ is past tense, he. |
| 6 | الْمُؤْمِنِينَ | al-muʾminīna | the believers | Accusative: they are the ones commanded. A masculine plural ends in ـِينَ for the accusative. |
| 7 | بِمَا | bi-mā | with what | بِ plus مَا ("that which"): the thing He commanded them with. |
| 8 | أَمَرَ بِهِ | amara bihi | He commanded with it | Past tense, he. بِهِ points back to مَا. |
| 9 | الْمُرْسَلِينَ | al-mursalīna | the messengers | Accusative (ـِينَ): they are the ones He commanded. It means "those who were sent". |
| 10 | فَقَالَ تَعَالَى | fa-qāla taʿālā | and He said | فَ means "so". قَالَ is past tense, he. |
| 11 | يَا أَيُّهَا الرُّسُلُ | yā ayyuhā r-rusulu | O messengers | يَا أَيُّهَا is the way to call a group whose name starts with ال. The group's name (الرُّسُلُ) is nominative (ـُ). |
| 12 | كُلُوا | kulū | eat | A command to several people, from the verb "to eat". The first letter of the verb has dropped, and ـُوا marks "you" (plural). |
| 13 | مِنَ الطَّيِّبَاتِ | mina ṭ-ṭayyibāti | from the pure things | مِنْ takes a fatḥa before ال. It makes الطَّيِّبَاتِ genitive. A feminine plural ends in ـَاتِ for the genitive. |
| 14 | وَاعْمَلُوا | wa-ʿmalū | and do | A command to several people. The ن has dropped, leaving ـُوا. |
| 15 | صَالِحًا | ṣāliḥan | righteous (deeds) | Accusative (ـًا): it is what they are told to do. It means "deeds that are righteous". |
</GrammarTable>

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا | yā ayyuhā lladhīna āmanū | O you who believe | الَّذِينَ means "those who", and it never changes its ending. آمَنُوا is past tense, they, with the ـُوا ending. |
| 2 | كُلُوا | kulū | eat | A command to several people, as in part 1. |
| 3 | مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ | min ṭayyibāti mā razaqnākum | from the good things We provided you | مِنْ makes طَيِّبَاتِ genitive (ـِ). مَا means "that which". رَزَقْنَاكُمْ is past tense: the ـنَا ending means "We", and the attached ـكُمْ means "you" (plural). |
| 4 | ثُمَّ ذَكَرَ | thumma dhakara | then he mentioned | ثُمَّ means "then". ذَكَرَ is past tense, he, meaning the Prophet. |
| 5 | الرَّجُلَ | ar-rajula | the man | Accusative (ـَ): he is the one mentioned. |
| 6 | يُطِيلُ | yuṭīlu | prolongs | Present tense, he, nominative (ـُ). It describes the man. |
| 7 | السَّفَرَ | as-safara | the travel | Accusative (ـَ): it is what he prolongs. |
| 8 | أَشْعَثَ أَغْبَرَ | ashʿatha aghbara | disheveled, dusty | Accusative (ـَ), with no tanwīn. They describe the state the man is in. Words of this pattern, meaning "most…", take no tanwīn. |
| 9 | يَمُدُّ | yamuddu | stretches | Present tense, he, nominative (ـُ). It describes his state too. |
| 10 | يَدَيْهِ | yadayhi | his hands | Accusative: they are what he stretches. A pair ends in ـَيْنِ, and the ن drops because ـهِ ("his") is attached. |
| 11 | إِلَى السَّمَاءِ | ilā s-samāʾi | to the sky | إِلَى makes السَّمَاءِ genitive (ـِ). |
| 12 | يَا رَبِّ | yā rabbi | O my Lord | A called name. The ي of "my" has dropped, leaving a kasra on the last letter. |
| 13 | وَمَطْعَمُهُ حَرَامٌ | wa-maṭʿamuhu ḥarāmun | and his food is unlawful | مَطْعَمُ is nominative (ـُ): it is what the statement is about. The attached ـهُ means "his". حَرَامٌ is nominative (ـٌ): the second part. |
| 14 | وَمَشْرَبُهُ حَرَامٌ | wa-mashrabuhu ḥarāmun | and his drink is unlawful | The same pattern. |
| 15 | وَمَلْبَسُهُ حَرَامٌ | wa-malbasuhu ḥarāmun | and his clothing is unlawful | The same pattern. |
| 16 | وَغُذِيَ | wa-ghudhiya | and he was nourished | Past tense in the passive: ḍamma on the first letter and kasra on the one before the last. The one who fed him is not named. |
| 17 | بِالْحَرَامِ | bi-l-ḥarāmi | with the unlawful | بِ makes الْحَرَامِ genitive (ـِ). |
| 18 | فَأَنَّى | fa-annā | so how | فَ means "so". أَنَّى means "how" or "from where". It ends in a long ā and never changes. |
| 19 | يُسْتَجَابُ | yustajābu | will be answered | Present tense in the passive: ḍamma on the first letter and fatḥa on the one before the last. It is nominative (ـُ). |
| 20 | لَهُ | lahu | for him | لِ takes a fatḥa before an attached pronoun. "For him" says whose prayer is answered. |
</GrammarTable>
