### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | إِنَّ اللَّهَ | inna llāha | indeed Allah | إِنَّ makes اللَّهَ accusative (ـَ). |
| 2 | تَجَاوَزَ | tajāwaza | has overlooked | Past tense, he, with a fixed fatḥa. The long ā marks the pattern meaning "to pass over something". |
| 3 | لِي | lī | for my sake | لِ plus ـي ("me"). |
| 4 | عَنْ أُمَّتِي | ʿan ummatī | about my community | عَنْ makes أُمَّةِ genitive. The attached ـي means "my", so a kasra shows. |
| 5 | الْخَطَأَ | al-khaṭaʾa | mistakes | Accusative (ـَ): it is what He overlooked. |
| 6 | وَالنِّسْيَانَ | wa-n-nisyāna | and forgetfulness | Accusative (ـَ): joined to الْخَطَأَ. |
| 7 | وَمَا اسْتُكْرِهُوا عَلَيْهِ | wa-mā stukrihū ʿalayhi | and what they were forced to do | مَا means "that which". اسْتُكْرِهُوا is past tense in the passive: ḍamma on the first letter and kasra on the one before the last. The ـُوا ending marks "they", the ones forced. عَلَيْهِ points back to مَا. |
</GrammarTable>
