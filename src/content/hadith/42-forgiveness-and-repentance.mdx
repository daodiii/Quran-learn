Below, the hadith is explained part by part.

## 1. Calling on Allah and Hoping in Him

<ArabicExample
  arabic="قَالَ اللَّهُ تَعَالَى: يَا ابْنَ آدَمَ إِنَّكَ مَا دَعَوْتَنِي وَرَجَوْتَنِي غَفَرْتُ لَكَ عَلَى مَا كَانَ مِنْكَ وَلَا أُبَالِي"
  transliteration="qāla llāhu taʿālā: yā bna ādama innaka mā daʿawtanī wa-rajawtanī ghafartu laka ʿalā mā kāna minka wa-lā ubālī"
  translation="Allah, the Most High, said: “Son of Adam, as long as you call on Me and hope in Me, I will forgive you for what you have done, and I do not mind.”"
  reference="Hadith 42, part 1 · Narrated by Anas ibn Malik"
/>

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | قَالَ اللَّهُ تَعَالَى | qāla llāhu taʿālā | Allah, the Most High, said | قَالَ is past tense, he. اللَّهُ is nominative (ـُ). تَعَالَى is a phrase of praise. |
| 2 | يَا ابْنَ آدَمَ | yā bna ādama | O son of Adam | ابْنَ is accusative (ـَ): a called name that is the first word of a pair ends in fatḥa. آدَمَ is genitive, but it is a name, so it takes no tanwīn and shows fatḥa. The alif of ابْنَ drops after يَا. |
| 3 | إِنَّكَ | innaka | indeed you | إِنَّ plus ـكَ ("you"). |
| 4 | مَا دَعَوْتَنِي | mā daʿawtanī | as long as you call on Me | Here مَا means "as long as". دَعَوْتَنِي is past tense with ـتَ ("you") and ـنِي ("me"). |
| 5 | وَرَجَوْتَنِي | wa-rajawtanī | and you hope in Me | The same pattern, joined by وَ. |
| 6 | غَفَرْتُ لَكَ | ghafartu laka | I forgive you | Past tense with ـتُ ("I"), used here as a promise. لِ takes a fatḥa before ـكَ. |
| 7 | عَلَى مَا كَانَ مِنْكَ | ʿalā mā kāna minka | for what has come from you | عَلَى plus مَا ("that which"). كَانَ is past tense, he. مِنْ plus ـكَ ("you"). |
| 8 | وَلَا أُبَالِي | wa-lā ubālī | and I do not mind | لَا denies the verb. أُبَالِي is present tense, I. The ـِي ending is part of the verb. |
</GrammarTable>

## 2. Sins as Great as the Clouds

<ArabicExample
  arabic="يَا ابْنَ آدَمَ لَوْ بَلَغَتْ ذُنُوبُكَ عَنَانَ السَّمَاءِ ثُمَّ اسْتَغْفَرْتَنِي غَفَرْتُ لَكَ"
  transliteration="yā bna ādama law balaghat dhunūbuka ʿanāna s-samāʾi thumma staghfartanī ghafartu laka"
  translation="“Son of Adam, if your sins reached the clouds of the sky and you then asked My forgiveness, I would forgive you.”"
  reference="Hadith 42, part 2"
/>

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | لَوْ بَلَغَتْ | law balaghat | if reached | لَوْ means "if". بَلَغَتْ is past tense, she: the ـتْ ending is feminine, because it is said of sins, a plural of things. |
| 2 | ذُنُوبُكَ | dhunūbuka | your sins | Nominative (ـُ): they are what reached. The attached ـكَ means "your". |
| 3 | عَنَانَ السَّمَاءِ | ʿanāna s-samāʾi | the clouds of the sky | عَنَانَ is accusative (ـَ): it is what they reached. السَّمَاءِ is genitive (ـِ) because it completes it. |
| 4 | ثُمَّ اسْتَغْفَرْتَنِي | thumma staghfartanī | and then you asked My forgiveness | ثُمَّ means "then". Past tense with ـتَ ("you") and ـنِي ("me"). |
| 5 | غَفَرْتُ لَكَ | ghafartu laka | I would forgive you | The answer to "if". Past tense with ـتُ ("I"). |
</GrammarTable>

## 3. Sins as Great as the Earth

<ArabicExample
  arabic="يَا ابْنَ آدَمَ إِنَّكَ لَوْ أَتَيْتَنِي بِقُرَابِ الْأَرْضِ خَطَايَا ثُمَّ لَقِيتَنِي لَا تُشْرِكُ بِي شَيْئًا لَأَتَيْتُكَ بِقُرَابِهَا مَغْفِرَةً"
  transliteration="yā bna ādama innaka law ataytanī bi-qurābi l-arḍi khaṭāyā thumma laqītanī lā tushriku bī shayʾan la-ataytuka bi-qurābihā maghfiratan"
  translation="“Son of Adam, if you came to Me with sins nearly as great as the earth, and then met Me without associating anything with Me, I would come to you with forgiveness nearly as great.”"
  reference="Hadith 42, part 3"
/>

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | إِنَّكَ لَوْ أَتَيْتَنِي | innaka law ataytanī | indeed, if you came to Me | إِنَّ plus ـكَ ("you"). أَتَيْتَنِي is past tense with ـتَ ("you") and ـنِي ("me"). |
| 2 | بِقُرَابِ الْأَرْضِ | bi-qurābi l-arḍi | with nearly the earth's fill | بِ makes قُرَابِ genitive (ـِ). الْأَرْضِ is genitive because it completes it. |
| 3 | خَطَايَا | khaṭāyā | sins | Accusative: it says what you bring. It takes no tanwīn, and it ends in a long ā, so no vowel shows. |
| 4 | ثُمَّ لَقِيتَنِي | thumma laqītanī | and then you met Me | Past tense with ـتَ ("you") and ـنِي ("me"). |
| 5 | لَا تُشْرِكُ بِي شَيْئًا | lā tushriku bī shayʾan | without associating anything with Me | لَا denies the verb, which is present tense, you, nominative (ـُ). It says the state you are in. شَيْئًا is accusative (ـًا). |
| 6 | لَأَتَيْتُكَ | la-ataytuka | I would come to you | لَ opens the answer to "if". Past tense with ـتُ ("I") and ـكَ ("you"). |
| 7 | بِقُرَابِهَا | bi-qurābihā | with nearly its fill | بِ makes قُرَابِ genitive. The attached ـهَا means "its", meaning the earth's. |
| 8 | مَغْفِرَةً | maghfiratan | forgiveness | Accusative (ـًا): it says what I would come with. |
</GrammarTable>
