### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | إِنَّ اللَّهَ | inna llāha | indeed Allah | إِنَّ makes اللَّهَ accusative (ـَ). |
| 2 | كَتَبَ | kataba | has prescribed | Past tense, he, with a fixed fatḥa. It means "wrote", and here "made obligatory". |
| 3 | الْإِحْسَانَ | al-iḥsāna | excellence | Accusative (ـَ): it is what He prescribed. |
| 4 | عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ | ʿalā kulli shayʾin | on everything | عَلَى makes كُلِّ genitive (ـِ). شَيْءٍ is genitive (ـٍ) because it completes كُلِّ. |
| 5 | فَإِذَا قَتَلْتُمْ | fa-idhā qataltum | so when you kill | فَ means "so". إِذَا means "when". قَتَلْتُمْ is past tense, and ـتُمْ means "you" (plural). |
| 6 | فَأَحْسِنُوا | fa-aḥsinū | then do well | فَ opens the result. A command to several people drops the final ن, leaving ـُوا. The verb means "to do well". |
| 7 | الْقِتْلَةَ | al-qitlata | the manner of killing | Accusative (ـَ): it is what they should do well. The pattern with a kasra on the first letter and ـة at the end means "the manner of doing it". |
| 8 | وَإِذَا ذَبَحْتُمْ | wa-idhā dhabaḥtum | and when you slaughter | Built the same way as row 5. |
| 9 | فَأَحْسِنُوا الذِّبْحَةَ | fa-aḥsinū dh-dhibḥata | then slaughter well | Built the same way as rows 6 and 7. الذِّبْحَةَ means "the manner of slaughtering". |
| 10 | وَلْيُحِدَّ | wa-l-yuḥidda | and let him sharpen | لِ in front of a present verb means "let him", and after وَ it loses its vowel. The verb ends in fatḥa on its doubled letter, which is allowed for verbs like this. |
| 11 | أَحَدُكُمْ | aḥadukum | one of you | Nominative (ـُ): it is the one who sharpens. |
| 12 | شَفْرَتَهُ | shafratahu | his blade | Accusative (ـَ): it is what he sharpens. The attached ـهُ means "his". |
| 13 | وَلْيُرِحْ | wa-l-yuriḥ | and let him ease | The same "let him" pattern. The verb ends in a silent letter, and its middle ي drops. |
| 14 | ذَبِيحَتَهُ | dhabīḥatahu | his animal | Accusative (ـَ): it is what he eases. The attached ـهُ means "his". |
</GrammarTable>
