### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | لَا | lā | not | Denies the verb that follows. |
| 2 | يُؤْمِنُ | yuʾminu | believes | Present tense, he, nominative (ـُ). The pattern with the أُ at the start means "to believe". |
| 3 | أَحَدُكُمْ | aḥadukum | one of you | Nominative (ـُ): it is the one who believes. The attached ـكُمْ means "you" (plural). |
| 4 | حَتَّى | ḥattā | until | Marks the end point. It makes the present verb after it end in fatḥa. |
| 5 | يُحِبَّ | yuḥibba | loves | Present tense, he, with the fatḥa that حَتَّى requires. The verb's root has two ب, so they are written together as one doubled letter. |
| 6 | لِأَخِيهِ | li-akhīhi | for his brother | لِ makes the next word genitive. أَخ is one of a few family words that show their case with a long vowel when they take a pronoun: the genitive is ـِي, so أَخِيهِ. The attached ـهِ means "his". |
| 7 | مَا | mā | what | Means "that which". Its ending never changes. |
| 8 | يُحِبُّ | yuḥibbu | he loves | Present tense, he, nominative (ـُ), with the two ب written as one doubled letter. |
| 9 | لِنَفْسِهِ | li-nafsihi | for himself | لِ makes نَفْسِ genitive (ـِ). The attached ـهِ means "his", so "for his own self". |
</GrammarTable>
