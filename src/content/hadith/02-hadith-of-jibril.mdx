## 1. The Man Arrives

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | بَيْنَمَا | baynamā | while | Made of بَيْنَ ("between") plus مَا. The مَا stops بَيْنَ from governing, so a full sentence can follow. Together they mean "while". |
| 2 | نَحْنُ | naḥnu | we | A separate pronoun. It never changes its ending. Here it is the subject. |
| 3 | جُلُوسٌ | julūsun | sitting | Nominative (ـٌ): it completes the statement about نَحْنُ. It is the plural of "sitting". |
| 4 | عِنْدَ | ʿinda | with, beside | A word for place, shown as an adverb with fatḥa (accusative). It needs a word after it: "beside…". |
| 5 | رَسُولِ اللَّهِ | rasūli llāhi | the Messenger of Allah | رَسُولِ is genitive (ـِ) because it completes عِنْدَ. اللَّهِ is genitive (ـِ) because it completes رَسُولِ. |
| 6 | صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ | ṣallā llāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam | peace and blessings be upon him | A prayer written as a past verb, with the meaning of a wish. اللَّهُ is nominative (ـُ) as the one who "blesses". عَلَى becomes عَلَيْ before ـهِ. |
| 7 | ذَاتَ يَوْمٍ | dhāta yawmin | one day | ذَاتَ is accusative (ـَ) as a word of time. يَوْمٍ is genitive (ـٍ) because it completes it. |
| 8 | إِذْ | idh | when, suddenly | Marks a sudden event inside the time that has been set. |
| 9 | طَلَعَ | ṭalaʿa | appeared | Past tense, he. It ends in a fixed fatḥa. |
| 10 | عَلَيْنَا | ʿalaynā | before us | عَلَى plus نَا ("us"). نَا never changes. |
| 11 | رَجُلٌ | rajulun | a man | Nominative (ـٌ): he is the one who appeared. The tanwīn means "a man", not one already known. |
| 12 | شَدِيدُ بَيَاضِ الثِّيَابِ | shadīdu bayāḍi th-thiyābi | very white in clothes | شَدِيدُ is nominative (ـُ) because it describes رَجُلٌ. بَيَاضِ and الثِّيَابِ are both genitive (ـِ), each completing the one before. Literally "strong of whiteness of the clothes". |
| 13 | شَدِيدُ سَوَادِ الشَّعَرِ | shadīdu sawādi sh-shaʿari | very black in hair | The same pattern as row 12, a second description of the man. |
| 14 | لَا يُرَى | lā yurā | cannot be seen | لَا denies. يُرَى is a passive present: the one who sees is not named. The ending is a long ā, so no case vowel is visible. |
| 15 | عَلَيْهِ | ʿalayhi | on him | عَلَى becomes عَلَيْ before ـهِ. |
| 16 | أَثَرُ السَّفَرِ | atharu s-safari | sign of travel | أَثَرُ is nominative (ـُ): with a passive verb, it takes the place of the doer. السَّفَرِ is genitive (ـِ) because it completes it. |
| 17 | وَلَا يَعْرِفُهُ | wa-lā yaʿrifuhu | and does not know him | وَ adds a statement. لَا denies. يَعْرِفُ is present, he, nominative (ـُ). ـهُ is "him", the one known. |
| 18 | مِنَّا | minnā | of us | مِنْ plus نَا: "from among us". |
| 19 | أَحَدٌ | aḥadun | anyone | Nominative (ـٌ): the one who would know. After a negative, أَحَد means "anyone". |
| 20 | حَتَّى جَلَسَ | ḥattā jalasa | until he sat | حَتَّى marks the end point of the action. جَلَسَ is past, he, with a fixed fatḥa. |
| 21 | إِلَى النَّبِيِّ | ilā n-nabiyyi | beside the Prophet | إِلَى makes النَّبِيِّ genitive (ـِ). The kasra sits on the ي. |
| 22 | فَأَسْنَدَ | fa-asnada | and he rested | فَ means "and then", showing one action right after another. أَسْنَدَ is past, he. |
| 23 | رُكْبَتَيْهِ | rukbatayhi | his knees | Accusative: the thing he rested. A pair ends in ـَيْنِ here, and the ن drops because ـهِ ("his") is attached. |
| 24 | إِلَى رُكْبَتَيْهِ | ilā rukbatayhi | against his knees | Genitive after إِلَى. The same ending as row 23, but this ـهِ means the Prophet's knees. |
| 25 | وَوَضَعَ | wa-waḍaʿa | and he placed | وَ adds an action. وَضَعَ is past, he, fixed fatḥa. |
| 26 | كَفَّيْهِ | kaffayhi | his palms | Accusative, a pair: the thing he placed. The same form as row 23. |
| 27 | عَلَى فَخِذَيْهِ | ʿalā fakhidhayhi | on his thighs | Genitive after عَلَى, a pair. Scholars differ on whether ـهِ means the man's own thighs or the Prophet's, and the grammar allows both. |
</GrammarTable>

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | وَقَالَ | wa-qāla | and he said | Past tense, he, with a fixed fatḥa. |
| 2 | يَا مُحَمَّدُ | yā Muḥammadu | O Muhammad | يَا calls someone. A single name being called ends in ḍamma and takes no tanwīn, and this ending is fixed. |
| 3 | أَخْبِرْنِي | akhbirnī | tell me | A command to one person, built on a silent last letter. The ـنِ is a protecting ن added before ـي ("me"). |
| 4 | عَنِ الْإِسْلَامِ | ʿani l-islāmi | about Islam | عَنْ takes a kasra before ال, so two silent sounds do not meet. الْإِسْلَامِ is genitive (ـِ) after عَنْ. |
| 5 | فَقَالَ | fa-qāla | so he said | فَ means "so" or "then". قَالَ is past, he. |
| 6 | رَسُولُ اللَّهِ | rasūlu llāhi | the Messenger of Allah | رَسُولُ is nominative (ـُ), the one who spoke. اللَّهِ is genitive (ـِ) because it completes رَسُولُ. |
| 7 | الْإِسْلَامُ | al-islāmu | Islam | Nominative (ـُ): it is the thing being defined. |
| 8 | أَنْ تَشْهَدَ | an tashhada | that you testify | أَنْ makes the present verb after it end in fatḥa, so تَشْهَدَ ends in ـَ. The whole phrase works like a noun: the answer to "what is Islam?". |
| 9 | أَنْ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ | an lā ilāha illā llāhu | that there is no god but Allah | لَا denies the whole kind, so إِلَهَ stays on fatḥa with no tanwīn. اللَّهُ is nominative (ـُ) after إِلَّا: "no god exists except Allah". |
| 10 | وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ | wa-anna Muḥammadan rasūlu llāhi | and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah | أَنَّ makes the first word after it accusative (مُحَمَّدًا, ـًا). The second part stays nominative (رَسُولُ, ـُ). اللَّهِ is genitive (ـِ). |
| 11 | وَتُقِيمَ | wa-tuqīma | and that you establish | The earlier أَنْ still applies after وَ, so this present verb also ends in fatḥa. |
| 12 | الصَّلَاةَ | aṣ-ṣalāta | the prayer | Accusative (ـَ): it is what is established. |
| 13 | وَتُؤْتِيَ الزَّكَاةَ | wa-tuʾtiya z-zakāta | and that you give the zakat | The same أَنْ makes تُؤْتِيَ end in fatḥa. الزَّكَاةَ is accusative (ـَ): it is what is given. |
| 14 | وَتَصُومَ رَمَضَانَ | wa-taṣūma Ramaḍāna | and that you fast Ramadan | تَصُومَ ends in fatḥa for the same reason. رَمَضَانَ is accusative (ـَ) and has no tanwīn, because it is a name ending in ـان. |
| 15 | وَتَحُجَّ الْبَيْتَ | wa-taḥujja l-bayta | and that you make pilgrimage to the House | تَحُجَّ ends in fatḥa on the doubled letter. الْبَيْتَ is accusative (ـَ): it is what the pilgrimage is made to. |
| 16 | إِنِ اسْتَطَعْتَ | ini staṭaʿta | if you are able | إِنْ means "if" and gets a kasra because the next word starts with a joining hamza. اسْتَطَعْتَ is past, you (masculine, singular). |
| 17 | إِلَيْهِ | ilayhi | to it | إِلَى becomes إِلَيْ before ـهِ. "It" means the House. |
| 18 | سَبِيلًا | sabīlan | a way | Accusative (ـًا): it is what the person is able to find. |
| 19 | قَالَ صَدَقْتَ | qāla ṣadaqta | he said: you spoke the truth | صَدَقْتَ is past, you (masculine, singular), said to the Prophet. The ـتَ marks the person spoken to. |
| 20 | فَعَجِبْنَا | fa-ʿajibnā | so we were amazed | Past tense. The ـنَا ending means "we". |
| 21 | لَهُ | lahu | at him | لِ with ـهُ. The لِ takes a fatḥa before an attached pronoun. |
| 22 | يَسْأَلُهُ | yasʾaluhu | he asks him | Present tense, he, nominative (ـُ). The ـهُ is "him". |
| 23 | وَيُصَدِّقُهُ | wa-yuṣaddiquhu | and says he is right | Present tense, he, nominative (ـُ). The doubled د shows the verb means "to say someone is truthful". |
</GrammarTable>

## 3. Faith

<ArabicExample
  arabic="قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنِ الْإِيمَانِ. قَالَ: أَنْ تُؤْمِنَ بِاللَّهِ وَمَلَائِكَتِهِ وَكُتُبِهِ وَرُسُلِهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ، وَتُؤْمِنَ بِالْقَدَرِ خَيْرِهِ وَشَرِّهِ. قَالَ: صَدَقْتَ"

/>

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | فَأَخْبِرْنِي | fa-akhbirnī | then tell me | فَ means "then". The command is built as in part 2: a silent last letter plus a protecting ن before ـي. |
| 2 | عَنِ الْإِيمَانِ | ʿani l-īmāni | about faith | عَنْ takes a kasra before ال. الْإِيمَانِ is genitive (ـِ) after it. |
| 3 | أَنْ تُؤْمِنَ | an tuʾmina | that you believe | أَنْ makes the present verb end in fatḥa. |
| 4 | بِاللَّهِ | bi-llāhi | in Allah | بِ makes the word after it genitive (ـِ). |
| 5 | وَمَلَائِكَتِهِ | wa-malāʾikatihi | and His angels | Genitive (ـِ) because وَ joins it to الله, which is genitive. This word normally takes no tanwīn, but with ـهِ ("His") attached it shows an ordinary kasra. |
| 6 | وَكُتُبِهِ | wa-kutubihi | and His books | Genitive (ـِ), joined in the same way. ـهِ means "His". |

| 9 | وَتُؤْمِنَ بِالْقَدَرِ | wa-tuʾmina bi-l-qadari | and that you believe in the decree | The earlier أَنْ still applies, so تُؤْمِنَ ends in fatḥa. الْقَدَرِ is genitive (ـِ) after بِ. |
| 10 | خَيْرِهِ وَشَرِّهِ | khayrihi wa-sharrihi | its good and its bad | Both genitive (ـِ). They spell out الْقَدَرِ, so they copy its case. The ـهِ means "its". |
</GrammarTable>

## 4. Excellence (Ihsan)

<ArabicExample
  arabic="قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنِ الْإِحْسَانِ. قَالَ: أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ كَأَنَّكَ تَرَاهُ، فَإِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ تَرَاهُ فَإِنَّهُ يَرَاكَ"
  transliteration="qāla: fa-akhbirnī ʿani l-iḥsān. qāla: an taʿbuda llāha ka-annaka tarāh, fa-in lam takun tarāhu fa-innahu yarāk"
  translation="He said: “Then tell me about excellence.” He said: “That you worship Allah as though you see Him, and if you do not see Him, then truly He sees you.”"
  reference="Hadith 2, part 4"
/>

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنِ الْإِحْسَانِ | fa-akhbirnī ʿani l-iḥsāni | then tell me about excellence | Built as in parts 2 and 3. الْإِحْسَانِ is genitive (ـِ) after عَنْ. |
| 2 | أَنْ تَعْبُدَ | an taʿbuda | that you worship | أَنْ makes the present verb end in fatḥa. |
| 3 | اللَّهَ | Allāha | Allah | Accusative (ـَ): the one worshipped. |
| 4 | كَأَنَّكَ | ka-annaka | as though you | Three parts: كَ ("like"), أَنَّ ("that") and ـكَ ("you"). أَنَّ is the kind of word that puts what follows it in the accusative, so "you" takes this form. |
| 5 | تَرَاهُ | tarāhu | you see Him | Present tense, you. The ending is a long ā, so no case vowel is visible. ـهُ is "Him", the one seen. |
| 6 | فَإِنْ | fa-in | and if | فَ means "and" and starts the condition. إِنْ means "if". |
| 7 | لَمْ تَكُنْ | lam takun | you do not | لَمْ turns the present into a past denial and makes the verb end in a silent letter. The middle letter of the verb drops out, so it reads takun, not takūn. |
| 8 | تَرَاهُ | tarāhu | see Him | The same word as row 5. It follows تَكُنْ: "you are not seeing Him". |
| 9 | فَإِنَّهُ | fa-innahu | then truly He | فَ opens the answer to the condition. إِنَّ means "truly". ـهُ is "He". |
| 10 | يَرَاكَ | yarāka | sees you | Present tense, he. ـكَ is "you", the one seen. |
</GrammarTable>

## 5. The Hour

<ArabicExample
  arabic="قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنِ السَّاعَةِ. قَالَ: مَا الْمَسْؤُولُ عَنْهَا بِأَعْلَمَ مِنَ السَّائِلِ. قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنْ أَمَارَاتِهَا. قَالَ: أَنْ تَلِدَ الْأَمَةُ رَبَّتَهَا، وَأَنْ تَرَى الْحُفَاةَ الْعُرَاةَ الْعَالَةَ رِعَاءَ الشَّاءِ يَتَطَاوَلُونَ فِي الْبُنْيَانِ"
  transliteration="qāla: fa-akhbirnī ʿani s-sāʿah. qāla: mā l-masʾūlu ʿanhā bi-aʿlama mina s-sāʾil. qāla: fa-akhbirnī ʿan amārātihā. qāla: an talida l-amatu rabbatahā, wa-an tarā l-ḥufāta l-ʿurāta l-ʿālata riʿāʾa sh-shāʾi yataṭāwalūna fī l-bunyān"
  translation="He said: “Then tell me about the Hour.” He said: “The one being asked knows no more about it than the one asking.” He said: “Then tell me about its signs.” He said: “That a slave woman gives birth to her mistress, and that you see barefoot, unclothed, poor shepherds competing to build tall buildings.”"
  reference="Hadith 2, part 5"
/>

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنِ السَّاعَةِ | fa-akhbirnī ʿani s-sāʿati | then tell me about the Hour | Built as in the earlier parts. السَّاعَةِ is genitive (ـِ) after عَنْ. "The Hour" means the end of the world. |
| 2 | مَا | mā | not | Here مَا means "not". It starts a denial. |
| 3 | الْمَسْؤُولُ | al-masʾūlu | the one asked | Nominative (ـُ): it is what the denial is about. It is the "one who is asked", a passive describing word. |
| 4 | عَنْهَا | ʿanhā | about it | عَنْ plus ـهَا ("it"). It means the Hour. |
| 5 | بِأَعْلَمَ | bi-aʿlama | more knowing | A word of the pattern "more…": it ends in fatḥa with no tanwīn even though it is genitive. The بِ is added to the word after "not" to make the denial stronger. |
| 6 | مِنَ السَّائِلِ | mina s-sāʾili | than the one asking | مِنْ takes a fatḥa before ال. السَّائِلِ is genitive (ـِ) because it follows مِنْ. It is the person asking. |
| 7 | فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنْ أَمَارَاتِهَا | fa-akhbirnī ʿan amārātihā | then tell me about its signs | Built as before. أَمَارَاتِهَا is genitive (ـِ) after عَنْ, with ـهَا ("its") attached. |
| 8 | أَنْ تَلِدَ | an talida | that gives birth | أَنْ makes the present verb end in fatḥa. The تَ means "she", because the one giving birth is female. |
| 9 | الْأَمَةُ | al-amatu | the slave woman | Nominative (ـُ): she is the one who gives birth. |
| 10 | رَبَّتَهَا | rabbatahā | her mistress | Accusative (ـَ): the one she gives birth to. ـهَا means "her". |
| 11 | وَأَنْ تَرَى | wa-an tarā | and that you see | أَنْ is repeated. The verb ends in a long ā, so the fatḥa that أَنْ requires is not visible. |
| 12 | الْحُفَاةَ الْعُرَاةَ الْعَالَةَ | al-ḥufāta l-ʿurāta l-ʿālata | the barefoot, the unclothed, the poor | الْحُفَاةَ is accusative (ـَ): they are what you see. The next two words describe it, so they copy its case. |
| 13 | رِعَاءَ الشَّاءِ | riʿāʾa sh-shāʾi | shepherds of sheep | رِعَاءَ is accusative (ـَ), spelling out who the group is. It has no tanwīn because of its ـاءَ ending. الشَّاءِ is genitive (ـِ) because it completes it. |
| 14 | يَتَطَاوَلُونَ | yataṭāwalūna | competing to build tall | Present tense, they (masculine, plural): the ـُونَ ending shows nominative. The pattern means each one does it against the others. It says what they are doing while you see them. |
| 15 | فِي الْبُنْيَانِ | fī l-bunyāni | in buildings | فِي makes الْبُنْيَانِ genitive (ـِ). |
</GrammarTable>

## 6. Who Was He?

<ArabicExample
  arabic="ثُمَّ انْطَلَقَ، فَلَبِثْتُ مَلِيًّا، ثُمَّ قَالَ: يَا عُمَرُ، أَتَدْرِي مَنِ السَّائِلُ؟ قُلْتُ: اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ. قَالَ: فَإِنَّهُ جِبْرِيلُ أَتَاكُمْ يُعَلِّمُكُمْ دِينَكُمْ"
  transliteration="thumma inṭalaqa, fa-labithtu maliyyan, thumma qāla: yā ʿUmaru, a-tadrī mani s-sāʾilu? qultu: Allāhu wa-rasūluhu aʿlam. qāla: fa-innahu Jibrīlu atākum yuʿallimukum dīnakum"
  translation="Then he left, and I stayed for a while. Then he said: “Umar, do you know who the questioner was?” I said: “Allah and His Messenger know best.” He said: “It was Jibril. He came to you to teach you your religion.”"
  reference="Hadith 2, part 6"
/>

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | ثُمَّ | thumma | then | Joins one event to the next, with a gap of time between them. |
| 2 | انْطَلَقَ | inṭalaqa | he left | Past tense, he. The verb starts with ان, which is how verbs of this pattern begin. |
| 3 | فَلَبِثْتُ | fa-labithtu | and I stayed | فَ means "and". The ـتُ ending means "I". |
| 4 | مَلِيًّا | maliyyan | for a while | Accusative (ـًا): it says how long. |
| 5 | ثُمَّ قَالَ | thumma qāla | then he said | قَالَ is past, he. Now the Prophet is speaking. |
| 6 | يَا عُمَرُ | yā ʿUmaru | O Umar | A single name being called ends in ḍamma with no tanwīn. |
| 7 | أَتَدْرِي | a-tadrī | do you know | أَ begins a yes-or-no question. تَدْرِي is present, you. The ـِي is part of the verb. |
| 8 | مَنِ السَّائِلُ | mani s-sāʾilu | who the questioner is | مَنْ ("who") takes a kasra before ال. السَّائِلُ is nominative (ـُ): it is the one being asked about. |
| 9 | قُلْتُ | qultu | I said | Past tense with ـتُ ("I"). The middle letter of قَالَ drops out before the silent ت, so it reads qul-. |
| 10 | اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ | Allāhu wa-rasūluhu aʿlamu | Allah and His Messenger know best | اللَّهُ and رَسُولُهُ are both nominative (ـُ): together they are the subject. أَعْلَمُ ("knows more") is nominative (ـُ) and has no tanwīn. |
| 11 | فَإِنَّهُ جِبْرِيلُ | fa-innahu Jibrīlu | it was Jibril | فَ means "so". إِنَّ with ـهُ means "truly he". جِبْرِيلُ is nominative (ـُ), the second part of the statement, with no tanwīn because it is a foreign name. |
| 12 | أَتَاكُمْ | atākum | he came to you | Past tense, he. The ـكُمْ is "you" (plural), the ones he came to. |
| 13 | يُعَلِّمُكُمْ | yuʿallimukum | teaching you | Present tense, he, nominative (ـُ). The doubled ل means "to teach". It says why he came. |
| 14 | دِينَكُمْ | dīnakum | your religion | Accusative (ـَ): it is what he teaches. The ـكُمْ is "your". |
</GrammarTable>
