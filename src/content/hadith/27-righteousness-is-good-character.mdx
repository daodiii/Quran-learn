## 1. An-Nawwas ibn Sam'an's Narration

<ArabicExample
  arabic="الْبِرُّ حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ، وَالْإِثْمُ مَا حَاكَ فِي نَفْسِكَ وَكَرِهْتَ أَنْ يَطَّلِعَ عَلَيْهِ النَّاسُ"
  transliteration="al-birru ḥusnu l-khuluqi, wa-l-ithmu mā ḥāka fī nafsika wa-karihta an yaṭṭaliʿa ʿalayhi n-nāsu"
  translation="Righteousness is good character. Sin is what stirs in your heart and what you dislike for people to find out about."
  reference="Hadith 27, part 1 · Narrated by an-Nawwas ibn Sam'an"
/>

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | الْبِرُّ | al-birru | righteousness | Nominative (ـُ): it is what the statement is about. The ر is doubled. |
| 2 | حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ | ḥusnu l-khuluqi | good character | حُسْنُ is nominative (ـُ): the second part, and the first word of a pair, so it takes no tanwīn. الْخُلُقِ is genitive (ـِ) because it completes it. |
| 3 | وَالْإِثْمُ | wa-l-ithmu | and sin | Nominative (ـُ): a new statement. |
| 4 | مَا حَاكَ | mā ḥāka | what stirred | مَا means "that which". حَاكَ is past tense, he, and means "it rankled, it stirred". |
| 5 | فِي نَفْسِكَ | fī nafsika | in yourself | فِي makes نَفْسِ genitive (ـِ). The attached ـكَ means "you". |
| 6 | وَكَرِهْتَ | wa-karihta | and you disliked | Past tense with ـتَ ("you", masculine, singular). |
| 7 | أَنْ يَطَّلِعَ | an yaṭṭaliʿa | that they find out | أَنْ makes the present verb end in fatḥa. The ط is doubled because of the verb's pattern. |
| 8 | عَلَيْهِ | ʿalayhi | about it | عَلَى becomes عَلَيْ before ـهِ. |
| 9 | النَّاسُ | an-nāsu | the people | Nominative (ـُ): they are who find out. |
</GrammarTable>

### Word by Word

<GrammarTable>
| # | Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning | Why it is written this way |
|---|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| 1 | أَتَيْتُ | ataytu | I came | Past tense with ـتُ ("I"). |
| 2 | رَسُولَ اللَّهِ | rasūla llāhi | the Messenger of Allah | رَسُولَ is accusative (ـَ): he is who was come to. اللَّهِ is genitive (ـِ) because it completes رَسُولَ. |
| 3 | فَقَالَ | fa-qāla | and he said | فَ means "and then". Past tense, he. |
| 4 | جِئْتَ | jiʾta | you came | Past tense with ـتَ ("you"). The long vowel of the verb shortens before the silent ت. |
| 5 | تَسْأَلُ | tasʾalu | to ask | Present tense, you, nominative (ـُ). It says what you came to do. |
| 6 | عَنِ الْبِرِّ | ʿani l-birri | about righteousness | عَنْ takes a kasra before ال. It makes الْبِرِّ genitive (ـِ). |
| 7 | قُلْتُ نَعَمْ | qultu naʿam | I said: yes | Past tense with ـتُ ("I"). نَعَمْ means "yes" and never changes. |
| 8 | اسْتَفْتِ | istafti | ask for a ruling | A command to one person, from a verb meaning "to ask for a ruling". Its last letter has dropped, so it ends in kasra. |
| 9 | قَلْبَكَ | qalbaka | your heart | Accusative (ـَ): it is who you ask. The attached ـكَ means "your". |
| 10 | الْبِرُّ مَا اطْمَأَنَّتْ | al-birru mā ṭmaʾannat | righteousness is what felt at ease | الْبِرُّ is nominative (ـُ). اطْمَأَنَّتْ is past tense, she, because the soul is feminine. The ن is doubled. |
| 11 | إِلَيْهِ | ilayhi | with it | إِلَى becomes إِلَيْ before ـهِ. |
| 12 | النَّفْسُ | an-nafsu | the soul | Nominative (ـُ): it is what felt at ease. |
| 13 | وَاطْمَأَنَّ إِلَيْهِ الْقَلْبُ | wa-ṭmaʾanna ilayhi l-qalbu | and the heart felt at ease with it | The same verb, but he, because the heart is masculine. الْقَلْبُ is nominative (ـُ). |
| 14 | وَالْإِثْمُ مَا حَاكَ فِي النَّفْسِ | wa-l-ithmu mā ḥāka fī n-nafsi | and sin is what stirred in the soul | The same pattern as part 1. فِي makes النَّفْسِ genitive (ـِ). |
| 15 | وَتَرَدَّدَ | wa-taraddada | and wavered | Past tense, he. The doubled د marks the pattern meaning "to go back and forth". |
| 16 | فِي الصَّدْرِ | fī ṣ-ṣadri | in the chest | فِي makes الصَّدْرِ genitive (ـِ). |
| 17 | وَإِنْ أَفْتَاكَ النَّاسُ | wa-in aftāka n-nāsu | even if people give you a ruling | وَ here means "even". إِنْ means "if". أَفْتَى is past tense, he, and ends in a long ā. The attached ـكَ means "you". النَّاسُ is nominative (ـُ): they are who give the ruling. |
| 18 | وَأَفْتَوْكَ | wa-aftawka | and they give you rulings | Past tense with the plural ending ـَوْا ("they") and ـكَ ("you"). |
</GrammarTable>
