# Al-Inshiqaq (The Splitting Open)

## Overview

- **Revelation:** Meccan
- **Verses:** 25
- **Theme:** The splitting of the sky on the Day of Judgment, followed by accountability and human response to revelation
- **Grammar Focus:** Idha clauses with an unspoken answer, the call ya ayyuha, amma conditional with fa-, the oath (la uqsimu), the lam of the oath with the strong nun, the question fa-ma lahum

<Callout type="tip" title="Unique Grammatical Architecture of Al-Inshiqaq">
Al-Inshiqaq showcases several advanced grammatical structures that make it exceptional for Arabic study. First, the two إِذَا clauses (vv. 1-5) build dramatic tension across five verses, and the answer to them is left unspoken: al-Jadwal supplies something like “each soul will know what it did”, and also notes the view that the answer is فَمُلَاقِيهِ in verse 6. Second, the أَمَّا...فَ parallel structure (vv. 7-9 vs. 10-12) creates a perfect grammatical mirror contrasting the righteous (book in right hand) with the wicked (book behind the back). Third, in the oath formula فَلَا أُقْسِمُ (v. 16) al-Jadwal calls the particle لَا an added particle (za'idah): it is not a negation but adds emphasis. Fourth, the verb تَرْكَبُنَّ (v. 19) takes the strong nun of emphasis (nun thaqilah) after the lam of the oath, which is the usual pattern for the answer to an oath. Finally, the ironic use of بَشِّرْ ("give good news") in verse 24 for announcing punishment is a masterpiece of Quranic balagha, subverting the word's positive meaning into devastating sarcasm.
</Callout>

<Callout type="rule" title="Form VII Reflexive: The Sky Splits Itself">
Form VII (infa'ala) indicates reflexive action -- the sky splits itself open by divine command. The pattern انشَقَّتْ (inshaqqat) from root ش-ق-ق shows the Form VII prefix اِنـ added to a doubled root, resulting in the geminated (mushaddad) قّ. Surah 81 (At-Takwir) also opens with a series of إِذَا clauses.
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** إِذَا is an adverb of time (zarf). In al-Jadwal it is tied to an answer that is left unspoken, something like “each soul will know what it did”; al-Jadwal also notes the view that the answer is فَمُلَاقِيهِ in verse 6. The clause after إِذَا is its mudaf ilayhi. السَّمَاءُ is the subject (fa'il) of a verb that is left out, and the verb that follows, انشَقَّتْ, explains it (tafsiriyyah, no place in the sentence). That is why السَّمَاءُ is nominative: the sentence is verbal, not a mubtada'-khabar sentence.

**Sarf (Morphology):** انشَقَّتْ is Form VII (infa'ala) from root ش-ق-ق. The pattern is اِنْفَعَلَ, but since the second and third radicals are identical (ق-ق), they merge into a geminate: اِنْشَقَّ. The silent feminine tāʾ (tāʾ al-taʾnīth) at the end marks agreement with السَّمَاءُ.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** Opening with إِذَا rather than إِنْ signals certainty -- this event WILL happen, not merely might happen. The dramatic opening establishes the apocalyptic register of the surah.

<Callout type="rule" title="Passive Voice for Divine Decree: حُقَّتْ">
The verb حُقَّتْ (huqqat) is a passive form meaning "it was made right/due" -- the sky was MADE to obey, and this obedience was its rightful obligation. The passive leaves the one who made it right unnamed, so the focus is on the rightness of the action. Combined with أَذِنَتْ (listened/obeyed), this creates a vivid personification: al-Jadwal calls it a hidden comparison (isti'arah makniyyah) with a listener who obeys a command.
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** Two verbal clauses joined by وَ to the idha clause of verse 1 (al-Jadwal: each is joined to it and stands in the same place as that clause). The phrase لِرَبِّهَا belongs to أَذِنَتْ (لِ marks the one listened to). The subject of أَذِنَتْ is a hidden pronoun for السَّمَاءُ. حُقَّتْ is passive, and its deputy subject (na'ib fa'il) is a hidden pronoun that returns to السَّمَاءُ. Al-Jadwal reads it as “its listening and obeying were made right”, with a word left out, and also allows that the sky was simply made fit for listening, so nothing is left out.

**Sarf (Morphology):** أَذِنَتْ is Form I from root أ-ذ-ن (to listen, to permit), conjugated with the feminine ta' suffix. حُقَّتْ is Form I passive from doubled root ح-ق-ق, where the damma on the first radical and fatha on the second mark the passive pattern (fu'ilat). The doubling (tashdid) on the ق reflects the merged identical radicals.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The personification of the sky "listening" to its Lord (أَذِنَتْ لِرَبِّهَا) is a hidden comparison (isti'arah makniyyah): al-Jadwal compares the sky, yielding to the power of God, with a listener who obeys a command. The addition of وَحُقَّتْ ("and it was fitting") adds that this obedience was right and due.

<Callout type="rule" title="Passive Voice: Divine Agency Without Naming the Agent">
مُدَّتْ (muddat) is passive voice from root م-د-د -- the earth "is stretched/flattened" by divine power. The one who stretches it is not named (a passive, majhul), so the sentence puts the earth and what happens to it in front.
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** This is the second إِذَا clause. The conjunction وَ joins this whole sentence to the first one. الْأَرْضُ is the subject (fa'il) of a verb that is left out, which al-Jadwal gives as انبسطت (“spread out”), and مُدَّتْ explains it (tafsiriyyah, no place in the sentence). The clause after إِذَا is its mudaf ilayhi, and the answer is the same unspoken one as in verse 1.

**Sarf (Morphology):** مُدَّتْ is Form I passive from doubled root م-د-د. The passive pattern fu'ilat applied to a doubled root yields مُدَّتْ (with the identical second and third radicals merged under tashdid). The root م-د-د means "to extend, stretch, spread."

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** After the sky splits (vertical drama), the earth stretches (horizontal expansion) -- the two dimensions of the cosmos are transformed. The shift from the reflexive انشَقَّتْ (Form VII) in verse 1 to the passive مُدَّتْ in verse 3 varies the sentence while keeping the theme of creation yielding to its Lord.

<Callout type="tip" title="Form IV and Form V: Two Types of Action">
This verse pairs two derived forms with distinct meanings. أَلْقَتْ (Form IV, af'ala) takes an object -- the earth throws out what is within it. تَخَلَّتْ (Form V, tafa''ala) is reflexive -- the earth empties ITSELF. The two verbs show the process in order: first the earth throws out its contents, then it is empty.
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** Two verbs joined to the unspoken verb of verse 3 (al-Jadwal: انبسطت), so both clauses stand in the place of the idha clause. مَا is the object of أَلْقَتْ, and فِيهَا completes it (silat al-mawsul); the phrase belongs to a word that is left out. تَخَلَّتْ needs no object -- the earth empties itself.

**Sarf (Morphology):** أَلْقَتْ is Form IV (af'ala) from root ل-ق-ي, with the characteristic hamza prefix alif. Al-Jadwal notes that the last radical of the root drops before the feminine tāʾ (pattern أَفْعَتْ). تَخَلَّتْ is Form V (tafa''ala) from root خ-ل-و (a defective root with و as third radical); it drops its last radical in the same way (pattern تَفَعَّتْ), and means “made itself empty”.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** Al-Jadwal calls وَأَلْقَتْ مَا فِيهَا وَتَخَلَّتْ a hidden comparison (isti'arah makniyyah): the earth is like a pregnant woman who throws out what is in her belly at a moment of terror. The earth is not only acted upon (v. 3) but throws out its contents herself.

<Callout type="rule" title="Rhetorical Repetition (Takrar) for Emphasis">
This verse repeats verse 2 word for word, now about the earth instead of the sky. The repetition (takrar) gives the two parts the same shape (sky: vv. 1-2, earth: vv. 3-5) and says the same of both: each listened to its Lord and each was made fit to obey.

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** The same structure as verse 2. The hidden pronoun now returns to الْأَرْضُ (the earth) from verse 3, whereas in verse 2 it returned to السَّمَاءُ (the sky). Here the two verbs are joined to the verbs of the second idha clause (al-Jadwal: أَذِنَتْ is joined to تَخَلَّتْ, and حُقَّتْ to أَذِنَتْ).

**Sarf (Morphology):** Same morphological forms as verse 2: أَذِنَتْ (Form I active, feminine) and حُقَّتْ (Form I passive, doubled root, feminine). No morphological variation from the earlier occurrence.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The repetition gives the sky part (vv. 1-2) and the earth part (vv. 3-5) the same closing refrain, so heaven and earth are described the same way. Verse 6 then turns to mankind.

<Callout type="rule" title="The Call to Mankind After Five Verses of إِذَا">
Verse 6 begins a new sentence: a call (nida'), يَا أَيُّهَا الْإِنسَانُ, and the sentence إِنَّكَ كَادِحٌ is the answer to the call (jawab al-nida'). Al-Jadwal does not make verse 6 the answer to إِذَا: it says the answer to the two إِذَا clauses is left unspoken (“each soul will know what it did”, or something like it). It adds the view that the answer is فَمُلَاقِيهِ, meaning فَأَنتَ مُلَاقِيهِ. Either way the shift from cosmic events to a direct address to mankind is sharp.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Maf'ul Mutlaq: Emphasis Through Cognate Accusative">
The construction كَادِحٌ...كَدْحًا uses the active participle followed by its own masdar (verbal noun) as a maf'ul mutlaq (absolute/cognate accusative). This adds emphasis: not just "laboring" but "laboring hard." The masdar كَدْحًا is accusative (mansub) and shares the root of the participle كَادِحٌ.
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** يَا أَيُّهَا الْإِنسَانُ is a call: أَيُّهَا is the called noun (a specific, unnamed person, built on damma, accusative in place), and الْإِنسَانُ is a badal of it that follows it in the nominative (al-Jadwal also allows 'atf bayan). The call opens a new sentence, and إِنَّكَ كَادِحٌ is the answer to the call. The pronoun كَ is ism inna (accusative) and كَادِحٌ is khabar inna (nominative). The phrase إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ belongs to كَادِحٌ, and al-Jadwal reads a word left out: “toward the meeting of your Lord”. كَدْحًا is maf'ul mutlaq (accusative). The فَ in فَمُلَاقِيهِ is a conjunction: مُلَاقِيهِ is joined to كَادِحٌ and is nominative. Al-Jadwal also allows it as the khabar of an unspoken أَنتَ, in a new sentence joined to the answer to the call. The pronoun هِ returns to رَبِّكَ.

**Sarf (Morphology):** كَادِحٌ is an active participle (ism fa'il) on pattern فَاعِل from root ك-د-ح. كَدْحًا is the masdar on pattern فَعْل. مُلَاقِيهِ is an active participle of Form III (mufa'il) from root ل-ق-ي, with the attached pronoun هِ (genitive). The Form III pattern (مُفَاعِل) indicates reciprocal or directed action -- "one who meets/encounters."

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The shift from cosmic imagery (vv. 1-5) to a direct address to mankind is sharp. The active participle كَادِحٌ describes a labour that is going on now. The فَ in فَمُلَاقِيهِ is a plain 'atf (joining) particle; the thought that this labour ends in meeting Him comes from the meaning of the words, not from a result fa'.

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** فَ joins this sentence to the call in verse 6 and opens the detail (al-Jadwal: a tafri'iyyah 'atf). أَمَّا is a conditional and detailing particle. مَنْ is a conditional noun (ism shart) that is the mubtada', and the clause أُوتِيَ كِتَابَهُ is its khabar. Al-Jadwal also allows مَنْ as a relative noun, with أُوتِيَ كِتَابَهُ as its sila and a fa added to the khabar, and allows the khabar to be the condition and the answer together. أُوتِيَ is passive and takes two objects: its deputy subject (na'ib fa'il) is a hidden pronoun for مَنْ, and كِتَابَهُ is the object that is left in the accusative (mansub). بِيَمِينِهِ belongs to أُوتِيَ and says how he is given it.

**Sarf (Morphology):** أُوتِيَ is the passive of Form IV (آتَى, “to give”) from root أ-ت-ي. The passive pattern for Form IV is أُفْعِلَ, giving أُوتِيَ. كِتَابَهُ follows pattern فِعَال from root ك-ت-ب, with the attached possessive pronoun هُ. يَمِينِهِ from root ي-م-ن originally means "right side" and by extension "oath, blessing."

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** Receiving the book in the right hand (بِيَمِينِهِ) symbolizes honor and salvation in Arabic culture, where the right hand represents blessing and the left/behind represents disgrace. The passive أُوتِيَ (was given) leaves the giver unnamed. The أَمَّا structure sets out the cases one by one, so the listener expects a second case.

Some scholars explain that the wicked person's right hand is chained to his neck and his left is twisted behind his back, forcing the book to be given from behind. The spatial imagery (right hand = honor; behind the back = shame) is immediately understood across cultures.

The expression يَدْعُو ثُبُورًا literally means "he calls for destruction" -- invoking his own ruin.

**Sarf (Morphology):** يَدْعُو from root د-ع-و is a defective verb (the third radical و appears in the present form). ثُبُورًا from root ث-ب-ر on pattern فُعُول is a masdar meaning "destruction, ruin, perdition."

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The contrast between مَسْرُورًا (delighted, v. 9) and ثُبُورًا (destruction, v. 11) is devastating. Where the righteous person returns joyfully, the wicked person calls out for his own annihilation. The verb يَدْعُو (he calls) shows him calling out for destruction.

The brevity of verse 12 (three words) mirrors the finality and inescapability of the punishment.

The choice of فِي أَهْلِهِ (among his people) parallels إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهِ (to his people) in verse 9 -- both mention family, but in opposite trajectories.

**Nahw (Syntax):** إِنَّهُ ظَنَّ follows the same pattern as verse 13: the clause ظَنَّ... is the khabar of this second إِنَّ, and al-Jadwal calls the sentence another reason (ta'lil akhar). ظَنَّ is a verb of the heart (fi'l qalbi) that takes two objects. Here its two objects are filled by the masdar mu'awwal أَنْ لَن يَحُورَ. أَنْ is the light form of أَنَّ with its noun left out, and the clause لَن يَحُورَ is its khabar. The particle لَن puts يَحُورَ in the subjunctive (mansub), marked by the fatha ending.

**Sarf (Morphology):** ظَنَّ is Form I from doubled root ظ-ن-ن (the two identical radicals merge under tashdid). يَحُورَ is Form I from hollow root ح-و-ر, following the pattern يَفْعُلُ. The root ح-و-ر means "to return, to come back" and is relatively rare in the Quran, making its use here distinctive.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The root ح-و-ر (to return) echoes the broader theme of returning to Allah established in verse 6 (مُلَاقِيهِ -- meeting Him). The wicked person's fundamental error was not a specific sin but a metaphysical denial: he thought he would never return to face his Creator. The emphatic لَن (never) reveals the absoluteness of his denial, and the repeated إِنَّ... أَنْ (light form of أَنَّ) shows how sure he was.

اتَّسَقَ (ittasaqa) is Form VIII (ifta'ala) from the same root و-س-ق that appeared in verse 17 (وَسَقَ). Form VIII often adds a reflexive sense: while وَسَقَ means "it gathered (others)," اتَّسَقَ means "it came together/became complete" -- the moon reaching fullness. #### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** الْقَمَرِ is the last thing sworn by, joined to الشَّفَقِ and so genitive. إِذَا is an adverb of time stripped of conditional force (al-Jadwal), tied to أُقْسِمُ: “I swear by the moon when it becomes full”. The clause اتَّسَقَ is its mudaf ilayhi. The jawab (answer to the oath) comes in verse 19.

**Sarf (Morphology):** اتَّسَقَ is Form VIII from root و-س-ق. Al-Jadwal explains that the و, the first letter of the root, is replaced by تَ because it stands before the Form VIII تَ: اِوْتَسَقَ becomes اِتَّسَقَ (with tashdid). The weight is اِفْتَعَلَ.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The moon becoming full (اتَّسَقَ) names a point of completion at the end of this list. The things sworn by (twilight, night, what it gathers, the full moon) all belong to the evening sky, and lead to the sworn statement in verse 19.

**Nahw (Syntax):** This verse is the jawab al-qasam (answer to the oath) for the oaths in verses 16-18, and the sentence has no place of its own. لَـ is the lam of the oath (lam al-qasam). تَرْكَبُنَّ is a present verb with the strong nun. So: تَرْكَبُونَ → تَرْكَبُونَنَّ → تَرْكَبُنَّ.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The expression طَبَقًا عَن طَبَقٍ ("state after state") is open, and commentators read it as many kinds of change: from this world to the next, from ease to hardship, from life to death to resurrection. The strong nun and the lam of the oath add certainty.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** After the oaths and the sworn statement in verse 19, the question turns to confront those who do not believe. The question implies that disbelief makes no sense after all this. The shift from second person (تَرْكَبُنَّ, v. 19) to third person (لَهُمْ) means the disbelievers are now spoken ABOUT rather than TO.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The verse builds on the question of verse 20 by adding a specific charge: not only do they not believe, but when the Quran itself is recited to them, they do not prostrate. The sajda mark (۩) marks a place of prostration for the reader. The passive leaves the reciter unnamed, so the sentence is about the Quran and their response to it.

**Sarf (Morphology):** بَشِّرْ is Form II imperative from root ب-ش-ر. Form II (فَعَّلَ) makes the verb act on someone else: بَشَّرَ means "to give someone news that makes them glad". **Balagha (Rhetoric):** The irony of بَشِّرْ is one of the most memorable rhetorical devices in this surah. It is a form of tahakkum (sarcasm). The command is addressed to the Prophet Muhammad. The indefiniteness of عَذَابٍ أَلِيمٍ (without الـ) suggests an unspecified, perhaps unimaginable, type of painful punishment -- leaving its nature open to dread.

<Callout type="rule" title="Inverted Nominal Sentence: لَهُمْ أَجْرٌ">
The normal Arabic word order would be أَجْرٌ لَهُمْ ("a reward is for them"). The verse inverts this to لَهُمْ أَجْرٌ (khabar muqaddam + mubtada' mu'akhkhar) -- "FOR THEM is a reward." This taqdim (fronting) of the predicate puts the focus on the RECIPIENTS (them, the believers). Putting the khabar first is common in Quranic Arabic.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ: A Reward Without Strings">
غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ has two complementary meanings. First, from the root م-ن-ن meaning "to cut off," it means "uninterrupted" -- a reward that never ceases. Second, from the same root meaning "to remind of a favor," it means "without reproach" -- a reward given freely without being reminded of it as a debt. Both meanings apply simultaneously: the believers' reward is eternal AND given graciously, without any diminishment or guilt.
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** إِلَّا introduces an exception (istithna') after verse 24, and al-Jadwal calls it a disconnected exception (munqati'): the believers were not among those who were to be given the tidings of punishment. الَّذِينَ is the mustathna (excepted party) in the place of nasb. Its sila is آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ. Al-Jadwal adds that if إِلَّا is read with the meaning of لَكِنْ (“but”), الَّذِينَ is the mubtada' and لَهُمْ أَجْرٌ its khabar. In the first reading, لَهُمْ أَجْرٌ is a new explaining sentence (isti'naf bayani) with no place of its own. In it, لَهُمْ is a prepositional phrase belonging to an unspoken khabar placed first (khabar muqaddam), and أَجْرٌ is the delayed mubtada' (nominative). غَيْرُ is an adjective (na't) of أَجْرٌ, nominative, and it owns مَمْنُونٍ (mudaf ilayhi, genitive).

**Sarf (Morphology):** آمَنُوا is Form IV past from root أ-م-ن. عَمِلُوا is Form I past from root ع-م-ل. الصَّالِحَاتِ is the feminine sound plural of صَالِحَة (active participle from ص-ل-ح), in the accusative (marked by kasra for sound feminine plurals). مَمْنُونٍ is a passive participle (maf'ul) from root م-ن-ن on pattern مَفْعُول.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The surah ends with mercy after severity -- a common Quranic pattern. After describing punishment for deniers (v. 24), the exception (إِلَّا) turns to the believers. The Quranic formula آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ pairs faith with action. The final word مَمْنُونٍ ("interrupted/diminished") is negated by غَيْرُ, and the surah ends with a reward that is not cut off, set against the عَذَابٍ أَلِيمٍ of the previous verse.

## Practice Exercises

<ExerciseBox question="Verses 1-5 hold two إِذَا clauses, and their answer is not spoken. Identify the two إِذَا clauses and the verbs in each. Then say what al-Jadwal does with verse 6 and why the address to mankind follows the description of the sky and the earth.">

**First إِذَا clause (vv. 1-2):**
1. إِذَا السَّمَاءُ انشَقَّتْ -- "When the sky splits open" (v. 1): السَّمَاءُ is the subject of a verb that is left out, and انشَقَّتْ explains it.
2. وَأَذِنَتْ لِرَبِّهَا وَحُقَّتْ -- "and listens to its Lord and is made fit to" (v. 2): two verbs joined to the clause above, standing in the same place as it.

**Second إِذَا clause (vv. 3-5):**
3. وَإِذَا الْأَرْضُ مُدَّتْ -- "And when the earth is stretched out" (v. 3): الْأَرْضُ is the subject of a verb that is left out (al-Jadwal: انبسطت), and مُدَّتْ explains it.
4. وَأَلْقَتْ مَا فِيهَا وَتَخَلَّتْ -- "and throws out what is in it and empties itself" (v. 4), joined to that verb.
5. وَأَذِنَتْ لِرَبِّهَا وَحُقَّتْ -- "and listens to its Lord and is made fit to" (v. 5), joined on again.

**What al-Jadwal does with verse 6:**
Al-Jadwal says the answer to the two إِذَا clauses is left out and gives it as something like “each soul will know what it did”. Verse 6 begins a new sentence: a call (يَا أَيُّهَا الْإِنسَانُ) with its answer (إِنَّكَ كَادِحٌ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ كَدْحًا). Al-Jadwal adds the view that the answer is فَمُلَاقِيهِ, meaning “then you are meeting Him”.

**Thematic Connection:**
The two clauses describe the sky and the earth, each of which listens to its Lord and obeys. Then the verse turns to mankind (يَا أَيُّهَا الْإِنسَانُ): you too are travelling toward your Lord, and you will meet Him (فَمُلَاقِيهِ). The sky and the earth obey; the listener is asked where he stands.
</ExerciseBox>

<ExerciseBox question="Compare the two أَمَّا...فَ structures in verses 7-9 and 10-12. For each structure, identify: (a) the أَمَّا clause, (b) the فَ apodosis, (c) the key vocabulary differences, and (d) the grammatical parallels that highlight the moral contrast.">

**First أَمَّا Structure (The Righteous, vv. 7-9):**
- **(a) أَمَّا clause (v. 7):** فَأَمَّا مَنْ أُوتِيَ كِتَابَهُ بِيَمِينِهِ -- "As for whoever is given his record in his right hand" (مَنْ is a conditional noun and the mubtada'; أُوتِيَ كِتَابَهُ is its khabar, with كِتَابَهُ accusative)
- **(b) فَ apodosis (vv. 8-9):** فَسَوْفَ يُحَاسَبُ حِسَابًا يَسِيرًا / وَيَنقَلِبُ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهِ مَسْرُورًا -- "He will be judged with an easy accounting / And will return to his people in happiness"

**Second أَمَّا Structure (The Wicked, vv. 10-12):**
- **(a) أَمَّا clause (v. 10):** وَأَمَّا مَنْ أُوتِيَ كِتَابَهُ وَرَاءَ ظَهْرِهِ -- "But as for whoever is given his record behind his back"
- **(b) فَ apodosis (vv. 11-12):** فَسَوْفَ يَدْعُو ثُبُورًا / وَيَصْلَىٰ سَعِيرًا -- "He will cry out for destruction / And will burn in a Blaze"

**Key Vocabulary Differences:**
| Element | Righteous (vv. 7-9) | Wicked (vv. 10-12) |
|---------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Location of book | بِيَمِينِهِ (right hand) | وَرَاءَ ظَهْرِهِ (behind his back) |
| Outcome verb | يُحَاسَبُ (passive: judged) | يَدْعُو (active: cries out) |
| Outcome object | حِسَابًا يَسِيرًا (easy accounting) | ثُبُورًا (destruction) |
| Final state | مَسْرُورًا (delighted) | سَعِيرًا (a blaze) |

**Grammatical Parallels:**
Both structures use the same syntax: أَمَّا + مَنْ + أُوتِيَ كِتَابَهُ + [location] / فَسَوْفَ + [verb] + [accusative noun] / وَ + [verb] + [accusative noun]. (In the first, the accusatives are حِسَابًا, a maf'ul mutlaq, and مَسْرُورًا, a hal; in the second, ثُبُورًا and سَعِيرًا are both objects.) This perfect parallelism forces the listener to compare each element directly. The فَسَوْفَ opening in both apodoses creates rhythmic symmetry, while the contrasting content (ease vs. destruction, joy vs. fire) creates maximum moral contrast through identical grammatical frames.
</ExerciseBox>

<ExerciseBox question="Analyze the passive voice constructions in this surah. List every passive verb, identify its form, and explain the theological significance of using passive voice in each case (i.e., why is the agent omitted?).">

**Passive Verbs in Al-Inshiqaq:**

1. **حُقَّتْ** (huqqat, vv. 2, 5) -- Form I passive from ح-ق-ق
   - Meaning: "it was made fitting/due"
   - The one who made it fitting is not named. The passive puts the rightness of the action in front.

2. **مُدَّتْ** (muddat, v. 3) -- Form I passive from م-د-د
   - Meaning: "it was stretched out"
   - The one who stretches it is not named. The passive puts the earth and what happens to it in front.

3. **أُوتِيَ** (utiya, vv. 7, 10) -- Form IV passive from أ-ت-ي
   - Meaning: "he was given"
   - The giver is not named. The passive puts the person who RECEIVES the book in front. Its deputy subject is a hidden pronoun, and كِتَابَهُ stays accusative as the second object.

4. **يُحَاسَبُ** (yuhasabu, v. 8) -- Form III passive from ح-س-ب
   - Meaning: "he will be held to account"
   - The one who holds to account is not named. The passive puts the person who is held to account in front.

5. **قُرِئَ** (quri'a, v. 21) -- Form I passive from ق-ر-أ
   - Meaning: "it was recited"
   - The reciter is not named. The passive puts the QURAN ITSELF in front, as the deputy subject.

**Theological Significance:**
In each of these passive verbs the doer is left out and the thing that happens to someone is put in front: the sky, the earth, the person who is given a book or held to account, the Quran that is recited. Note that انشَقَّتْ (Form VII) is not passive: it is a reflexive verb whose form looks like a passive in meaning, but it has its own subject.
</ExerciseBox>

## Grammar Summary

<Callout type="note" title="Key Grammar Concepts in This Surah">
- **Idha clauses**: two idha clauses (verses 1-5) with an unspoken answer (al-Jadwal also notes the view that the answer is fa-mulaqihi, v.6); in each, the noun after idha is the subject of an unspoken verb that the next verb explains
- **Oath formulas**: fa-la uqsimu with an added la (v.16); the wa in vv.17-18 joins more things to ash-shafaq, and ma wasaqa is a ma + verb verbal noun; the answer is la-tarkabunna (v.19)
- **Amma conditionals**: fa-amma man ...fa- structure (vv.7-9, 10-12); man is a conditional noun, kitabahu is accusative
- **Passive voice**: huqqat, muddat, utiya, yuhasabu and quri'a leave the doer unnamed (inshaqqat is Form VII, not a passive)
- **Vocative structures**: ya ayyuha al-insan (v.6) - ayyuha is the called noun and al-insan is a badal of it
- **Emphasis particles**: inna...kana constructions (vv.13,15), la-tarkabunna energetic mood (v.19)
- **Circumstantial (hal) constructions**: masruran (v.9) and la yu'minuna (v.20, with the idha sentence of v.21 as a second hal); masruran in v.13 is the khabar of kana
- **Masdar mu'awwal**: an lan yahura (v.14) - an is the light form of anna, and the whole fills the place of the two objects of zanna; also ma wasaqa (v.17) and bi-ma yu'una (v.23)
- **Rhetorical questions**: fa-ma lahum la yu'minun (v.20) - the fa links the question to an unspoken condition
- **Exception structures**: illa alladhina (v.25) - a disconnected exception (munqati')
- **Ironic language**: bashshirhum bi-'adhabin (v.24) - "good news" of punishment
- **Inverted nominal sentences**: lahum ajrun (v.25) - predicate before subject for emphasis
</Callout>
