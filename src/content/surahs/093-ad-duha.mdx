# Ad-Duha (The Morning Hours)

## Overview

- **Revelation:** Meccan
- **Verses:** 11
- **Theme:** Divine reassurance to the Prophet during a period of revelation pause, reminding him of Allah's continuous care and promise of future reward. Emphasizes gratitude and kindness to the vulnerable.
- **Grammar Focus:** Oath constructions (wa-), negative particles (ma), the lam of the oath (la-), rhetorical interrogation (alam), triple parallel wajadaka structure, conditional particle (amma) with fa- answer, imperative and prohibitive moods

<Callout type="tip" title="The Surah of Divine Consolation and Mirrored Commands">
Ad-Duha is built in three parts: oaths and reassurance (vv.1-5), three past blessings (vv.6-8), and three commands (vv.9-11). The middle part uses one frame three times: وَجَدَكَ + second object (accusative) + فَ + past verb. The three blessings can be lined up with the three commands: orphanhood (6) → do not oppress the orphan (9), being unguided (7) → do not repel the one who asks (10), poverty (8) → talk about the favor (11). The surah moves from oaths by morning and night, to the Prophet's own past, to rules for how to treat others, all in just 11 verses. Grammatically, it shows مَا negation, the lam of the oath (لَ), أَلَمْ as a question that makes the listener agree, the second object of وَجَدَ, and the أَمَّا construction.
</Callout>

<Callout type="rule" title="وَ القَسَم: The Oath Particle وَ">
When وَ begins a sentence before a noun (not a verb), it can be an oath particle (حَرْف قَسَم) rather than a simple "and". The noun after it is genitive (مَجْرُور), because this وَ takes the place of the oath preposition بِ (by). al-Jadwal ties the phrase to an unspoken verb, أُقْسِمُ ("I swear"): "I swear by the forenoon". This pattern is used for oaths across the Quran: وَالشَّمْسِ (by the sun, 91:1), وَالتِّينِ (by the fig, 95:1), وَالعَصْرِ (by time, 103:1).
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="ٱلضُّحَى: The Forenoon Light">
ٱلضُّحَى refers specifically to the mid-morning period when the sun has fully risen and its light fills the world — not sunrise (شُرُوق) and not noon (ظُهْر), but the bright, warm hours between them. From root ض-ح-و, it conveys exposure to light. Swearing by this particular time evokes warmth, clarity, and the return of light after darkness — a perfect metaphor for the return of revelation after its pause. The surah pairs it with night (verse 2), establishing a light/dark contrast that mirrors the Prophet's emotional state: revelation (light) and its temporary absence (darkness).
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** This is an oath. al-Jadwal ties وَٱلضُّحَىٰ to an unspoken verb, أُقْسِمُ (I swear), so the full sentence is "I swear by the forenoon". It opens the speech and has no place in the grammar. وَ is the oath particle (حَرْف قَسَم, not a conjunction) and stands in for بِ. ٱلضُّحَى is the thing sworn by (مُقْسَم بِهِ), in the genitive after it. The answer to the oath (جَوَاب القَسَم) comes in verse 3. The whole oath spans three verses: two oaths (vv.1-2) followed by the answer (v.3).

**Sarf (Morphology):** ٱلضُّحَى is from root ض-ح-و, on the فُعَل pattern. Here it is an ordinary noun. The alif maqsurah (ى) at the end makes it an اسم مَقْصُور: its case ending is hidden (مُقَدَّر), so it never shows.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** Opening with an oath lifts the speech from the everyday to the solemn. The Prophet was going through a pause in revelation, which his enemies took as a sign that he had been abandoned. By swearing by the moment when darkness gives way to bright light, Allah says: just as night always gives way to morning, the pause in revelation will give way to its return.

<Callout type="rule" title="إِذَا in Oath Qualifications: Specifying the State">
When إِذَا follows the thing sworn by, it does not start a condition. al-Jadwal reads it as a time word (ظَرْف) with no condition in it, tied to the unspoken verb أُقْسِمُ: "I swear by the night at the time it settles". The clause سَجَىٰ is the word that إِذَا owns (مُضَاف إِلَيْهِ), so it sits in the genitive position. This narrows the oath to one moment of the night: when it is still.
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** Second oath in the series, parallel to verse 1. وَ joins it to the first oath, and ٱلَّيْلِ is the muqsam bihi in the genitive. إِذَا is a time word (ظَرْف) in the accusative position, with no condition in it, tied to the unspoken verb أُقْسِمُ. The clause سَجَىٰ is its مُضَاف إِلَيْهِ, in the genitive position. The subject of سَجَىٰ is the hidden pronoun هُوَ (it, the night).

**Sarf (Morphology):** سَجَىٰ is a Form I past tense verb from root س-ج-و, a defective (nāqis) verb whose last root letter is و. al-Jadwal gives it as سَجَا يَسْجُو (bāb naṣara), "to be still": the و turned into alif after the fatha. The mushaf writes it with ى, not ا, to suit the imāla reading. The fatha is مُقَدَّرَة (hidden) because it sits on an alif. The verb means "to be still, to settle, to become calm".

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** al-Jadwal names a figure of speech here, الإِسْنَاد المَجَازِي (figurative attribution): سَجَىٰ is said of the night, but the stillness really belongs to the people in it, who go quiet. Swearing by the night in this calm state fits the mood of the surah: a quiet pause, not a threat. The two oaths together, bright morning and still night, make a pair.

<Callout type="rule" title="مَا النَّافِيَة: Negation of Past Tense">
مَا here is a negation particle (مَا النَّافِيَة), and al-Jadwal reads it as a negation in both clauses. Unlike لَمْ (which also negates the past but puts a present-tense verb in the jussive), مَا stands in front of the past tense verb and leaves it as it is. The verb وَدَّعَكَ keeps its usual past form (مَبْنِي عَلَى الفَتْح). So the verse says plainly: "He did not abandon you."
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="جَوَاب القَسَم: The Oath's Answer">
Verse 3 is the jawab al-qasam (answer to the oath): the statement that the oaths of verses 1-2 were sworn to affirm. al-Jadwal says this clause has no place in the grammar. Two oaths (the morning brightness and the still night) are sworn to affirm a personal reassurance: "Your Lord has not abandoned you." The subject is رَبُّكَ (your Lord): the close word رَبّ (Sustainer) with the possessive ـكَ (your), not the name اللَّه. In the second clause (وَمَاقَلَىٰ) the object is left out: "nor has He been displeased [with you]".
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** This is the jawab al-qasam: a verbal sentence negated by مَا, with no place in the grammar. The verb وَدَّعَكَ has an attached pronoun كَ as its direct object (مَفْعُول بِهِ), in the accusative. The subject (فَاعِل) رَبُّكَ comes after the verb (VSO word order). The second clause وَمَاقَلَىٰ is joined to the first by وَ. al-Jadwal says it too has no place in the grammar and is joined to the answer of the oath. Its subject is a hidden "He" pointing back to رَبُّكَ, and its object (you) is understood.

**Sarf (Morphology):** وَدَّعَكَ is Form II (فَعَّلَ) from root و-د-ع. Form I وَدَعَ means "to leave"; Form II وَدَّعَ means "to say goodbye to, to leave behind". The shadda on the دال marks Form II. قَلَىٰ is Form I. al-Jadwal gives its root as ق-ل-و (يَقْلُو, bāb naṣara) and notes that قَلَى يَقْلِي is also said, so the alif can come from wāw or yā'. The wāw turned into alif after the fatha, and the verb ends in alif maqsurah. The verb means "to dislike, to be displeased with".

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The two negations form a pair: مَا وَدَّعَكَ (He did not abandon you: no act of leaving) and وَمَاقَلَىٰ (nor was He displeased: no feeling of displeasure). The first negation speaks of what happened outside (the pause in revelation), the second of Allah's attitude. Together they are a full reassurance: neither the outward sign nor the inner reality supports the claim that Allah has turned away.

<Callout type="rule" title="اسْم التَّفْضِيل: The Comparative/Superlative خَيْر">
خَيْرٌ is an ism tafdīl (comparative/superlative noun) on the أَفْعَل pattern, though it has lost its initial hamza (the original form would be أَخْيَر). It means "better" when followed by مِنْ (than). The structure اسم التَّفْضِيل + مِنْ creates a standard comparison: خَيْرٌ لَكَ مِنَ الأُولَى (better for you than the first). The لَكَ (for you) specifies who benefits from this comparison — it is better *for you specifically*, making the statement personally directed at the Prophet.
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** This is a nominal sentence (جُمْلَة اسْمِيَّة) joined by وَ to the answer of the oath in verse 3. al-Jadwal reads لَ as the lam of the oath, and explains that it is used here because a positive statement follows the negative answer of verse 3. So the sentence has no place in the grammar and is part of the answer to the oath. ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ is the subject (مُبْتَدَأ) in the nominative, and خَيْرٌۭ is the predicate (خَبَر), also in the nominative. لَّكَ and مِنَ ٱلْأُولَىٰ are both tied to خَيْرٌۭ: the first says who benefits, the second gives the second term of the comparison, with ٱلْأُولَىٰ in the genitive after مِنَ.

**Sarf (Morphology):** ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ is from root ء-خ-ر, the active participle form آخِرَة (feminine of آخِر) used as a noun meaning "the Last [Life], the Hereafter." ٱلْأُولَىٰ is from root ء-و-ل, the feminine form أُولَى (first), a defective noun ending in alif maqsurah. خَيْرٌۭ is from root خ-ي-ر, an ism tafdīl whose regular form would be أَخْيَر, but خَيْر is the usual form.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** After the negative reassurance of verse 3 (He has not abandoned you), verse 4 turns to a positive promise. The لَ of the oath makes it a solemn promise, sworn by the same oaths as verse 3. لَّكَ (for you) makes this personal: it is not a general statement about the Hereafter but a promise to the Prophet that his future will be better than his present difficulties.

<Callout type="rule" title="لَسَوْفَ: A Sworn Future Promise">
لَسَوْفَ combines لَ (in al-Jadwal, the lam of the oath, used again for the same reason as in verse 4) with سَوْفَ (the future particle). While سَوْفَ alone points to the future, adding لَ makes it a sworn promise: "surely, certainly will." Compare: سَوْفَ يُعْطِيكَ ("He will give you": future) with لَسَوْفَ يُعْطِيكَ ("He will certainly give you": a sworn promise). The لَ makes this a Divine guarantee, not only a prediction.
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** This is a verbal sentence with a sworn future. It is joined by وَ to the answer of the oath, so it has no place in the grammar. لَ is the lam of the oath (al-Jadwal), سَوْفَ is the future particle, and يُعْطِيكَ is the present tense verb (مُضَارِع مَرْفُوع). The verb is Form IV (أَعْطَى/يُعْطِي) and takes two objects: the attached pronoun كَ (first object, the one who receives) and a second object that is left out (what is given). رَبُّكَ is the subject (فَاعِل). In فَتَرْضَىٰ, فَ is a joining فَ (عَاطِفَة) and the clause تَرْضَىٰ is joined to يُعْطِيكَ, with no place in the grammar. تَرْضَىٰ is a present tense verb with a hidden subject أَنْتَ.

**Sarf (Morphology):** يُعْطِيكَ is Form IV present tense from root ع-ط-و. Form IV (أَفْعَلَ) adds a causative meaning: Form I عَطَا means "to reach for and take," and Form IV أَعْطَى means "to give", that is, to make someone take. The prefix ي shows third person masculine singular. تَرْضَىٰ is Form I from root ر-ض-و (to be pleased, satisfied), another defective verb ending in alif maqsurah.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The verse does not say what Allah will give: يُعْطِيكَ (He will give you) leaves the gift unnamed. That leaves the giving open and large. فَتَرْضَىٰ (so you will be satisfied) names the giving by its effect, not by its content: it will go on until the Prophet is satisfied. His satisfaction becomes the measure of the giving.

<Callout type="rule" title="أَلَمْ: Rhetorical Negation Becomes Emphatic Affirmation">
أَلَمْ combines the interrogative hamza (أَ) with the negation particle لَمْ. Together they produce a rhetorical question expecting "yes": "Did He not find you an orphan?" means "He certainly found you an orphan." لَمْ governs the following verb in the jussive mood (مَجْزُوم) — hence يَجِدْكَ with sukun (not يَجِدُكَ with damma). This أَلَمْ construction appears in Ash-Sharh 94:1 (أَلَمْ نَشْرَحْ) and Al-Balad 90:8 (أَلَمْ نَجْعَل). Its rhetorical power lies in making the listener *participate* in affirming the truth — they must mentally answer "yes, indeed" — which is stronger than merely hearing a statement.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="The Triple وَجَدَكَ Pattern: Verses 6-8">
Verse 6 begins a triple parallel structure. Each of the three verses (6-8) follows the same frame: found-you + state + so-He-acted. Verse 6 uses أَلَمْ يَجِدْكَ (question form), while verses 7-8 switch to the declarative وَوَجَدَكَ. The accusative nouns (يَتِيمًۭا, ضَآلًّۭا, عَآئِلًۭا) are the second object of وَجَدَ: "He found you [as] an orphan". al-Jadwal says this for يَتِيمًۭا, and the other two have the same frame. Each فَ is a joining فَ (عَاطِفَة) that adds the next act. The frame gives a steady rhythm: orphan → sheltered, lost → guided, poor → made rich.
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** This is a verbal sentence in the form of a question. أَلَمْ is أَ (a question that makes the listener agree) + لَمْ (jussive negation). لَمْ puts يَجِدْكَ in the jussive (مَجْزُوم), marked by sukun on the last letter. al-Jadwal reads the sentence as a new beginning with no place in the grammar. The verb يَجِدْ takes two objects: كَ (you, first object) and يَتِيمًۭا (second object, accusative: "found you an orphan"). فَ is a joining فَ (عَاطِفَة): the clause آوَىٰ is joined to the opening statement, and has no place in the grammar. آوَىٰ is Form IV past tense; its object (you) is left out.

**Sarf (Morphology):** يَجِدْ is Form I present tense from root و-ج-د. It is an assimilated verb (مِثَال) — the initial و drops in the present tense (وَجَدَ → يَجِدُ, not يَوْجِدُ). يَتِيم is from root ي-ت-م on the فَعِيل pattern, meaning "one who has lost his father." آوَىٰ is Form IV (أَفْعَلَ) from root ء-و-ي, meaning "to give shelter, grant refuge." Form IV adds causative meaning: Form I أَوَى means "to take shelter," Form IV آوَى means "to provide shelter to someone."

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The shift from general promises (vv.3-5) to the Prophet's own past is moving. Allah reminds him of his most vulnerable moments, not to hurt but to show a thread of care that never broke. The word يَتِيمًۭا (orphan) carries great weight: tradition says the Prophet lost his father early and his mother when he was about six. By asking أَلَمْ (Did He not...?), Allah asks the Prophet to look back and see the protection that was always there. فَآوَى (so He gave refuge), through his grandfather Abdul-Muttalib and then his uncle Abu Talib, was never by chance.

**Nahw (Syntax):** Parallel to verse 6 but declarative (not a question). وَجَدَكَ is the verb and its object, with وَ as a conjunction. ضَآلًّۭا is the second object of وَجَدَ, in the accusative: "He found you lost". al-Jadwal joins the clause وَجَدَكَ to the opening statement of verse 6, and هَدَىٰ to وَجَدَكَ. فَ is a joining فَ (عَاطِفَة). هَدَىٰ is a past tense verb: its subject is a hidden "He" and its object (you) is left out for brevity (حَذْف المَفْعُول).

The tanwin fatha (ضَالًّا) marks it as indefinite accusative. هَدَىٰ is Form I from root ه-د-ي, a defective verb (نَاقِص) ending in alif maqsurah. The fatha is مُقَدَّرَة (estimated) on the alif.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** al-Jadwal names a direct metaphor (اسْتِعَارَة تَصْرِيحِيَّة) in وَوَجَدَكَ ضَالًّا فَهَدَىٰ: the law of Islam is compared to guidance, and not yet having it is called being lost. Only the "lost" side of the comparison is said aloud. This is the second panel of the triptych: verse 6 spoke of the Prophet's weakness as an orphan, verse 7 speaks of the search for guidance. The parallel with verse 6 (wajadaka... fa-) gives the blessings a steady rhythm.

The three فَ (فَـَٔاوَىٰ, فَهَدَىٰ, فَأَغْنَىٰ) add one act after another: so He sheltered, so He guided, so He enriched.

**Nahw (Syntax):** al-Jadwal reads this as a conditional sentence whose condition is unspoken: the first فَ links أَمَّا and its answer to that condition ("if your state was orphan, lost and poor, then..."). أَمَّا is the particle of condition and detail. ٱلْيَتِيمَ is the object of تَقْهَرْ, brought forward, accusative. The second فَ links the answer to أَمَّا (فَاء الجَزَاء). لَا is the prohibitive particle (لا النَّاهِيَة), which puts the verb in the jussive. تَقْهَرْ is jussive (مَجْزُوم), marked by sukun on the last letter. Its subject is a hidden أَنْتَ (you). The clause has no place in the grammar: it is the answer of a condition that does not put the verb in the jussive.

**Sarf (Morphology):** تَقْهَرْ is Form I from root ق-ه-ر (to overpower, oppress, subjugate). The sukun on its last letter is the sign of the jussive. قَهَرَ means to dominate by force; one of Allah's names is القَاهِر (the Subduer). Said about an orphan, it means to use their weakness against them or to treat them harshly.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The shift from verses 6-8 (past blessings) to verses 9-11 (commands) is marked by فَ. In al-Jadwal this فَ links the commands to an unspoken condition: if your state was orphan, lost and poor, then act accordingly. The first command mirrors the first blessing: you were an orphan (v.6), so do not oppress the orphan (v.9). The verb قَهَرَ (to overpower) is strong: it forbids active harshness, not only neglect. The negation لَا تَقْهَرْ sets a minimum: at the very least, do not oppress them. Kindness is understood to go beyond this.

So the verse does not command giving to every asker, but it forbids treating them with contempt.

<Callout type="rule" title="Form II Imperative حَدِّثْ: Intensive Proclamation">
حَدِّثْ is the imperative of Form II حَدَّثَ from root ح-د-ث. Form I حَدَثَ means "to happen, to occur." Form II حَدَّثَ means "to tell, narrate, report": the doubled middle letter turns an event (happening) into speech about it (telling). The imperative is built from the present tense by dropping the prefix (تُحَدِّثُ → حَدِّثْ). The sukun on the last letter marks the imperative. Said about a favor, it means to tell others about it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="The Grand Triad: Blessings → Commands">
Verse 11 completes the surah's structural masterpiece. The three أَمَّا clauses (vv.9-11) can be lined up with the three blessings (vv.6-8):

**Blessing → Command:**
- يَتِيمًا فَآوَى (orphan → sheltered) → فَأَمَّا اليَتِيمَ فَلَا تَقْهَرْ (do not oppress orphans)
- ضَالًّا فَهَدَى (lost → guided) → وَأَمَّا السَّائِلَ فَلَا تَنْهَرْ (do not repel seekers)
- عَائِلًا فَأَغْنَى (poor → enriched) → وَأَمَّا بِنِعْمَةِ رَبِّكَ فَحَدِّثْ (proclaim the blessings)

The first two commands are negative (لَا تَقْهَرْ, لَا تَنْهَرْ), while the third is positive (حَدِّثْ). The movement from prohibition to command mirrors the movement from problems to gratitude: first stop the harm, then actively do good.
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** The third أَمَّا construction, with one difference: after أَمَّا there is a prepositional phrase بِنِعْمَةِ رَبِّكَ instead of a direct object. بِ is a preposition governing نِعْمَةِ in the genitive. نِعْمَةِ رَبِّكَ is an idafa: "the favor of your Lord". al-Jadwal ties the phrase to حَدِّثْ and says the فَ in between does not stop this. The فَ links the command حَدِّثْ to أَمَّا, and حَدِّثْ is imperative (مَبْنِي عَلَى السُّكُون) with a hidden subject أَنْتَ. وَ is a joining وَ (عَاطِفَة): this third condition-and-answer is joined to the first.

**Sarf (Morphology):** نِعْمَةِ is from root ن-ع-م, on the فِعْلَة pattern, a noun meaning "favor, blessing". Its plural is نِعَم. حَدِّثْ is the Form II imperative from root ح-د-ث. The doubled middle letter (دّ) shows Form II, and the imperative is built from the present tense by removing the prefix and adding sukun.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The last verse turns the surah from comfort to duty. Verses 9-10 forbade specific harms; verse 11 commands thanks in words. حَدِّثْ means to speak about the favor, not only to feel thankful in private. The preposition بِ in بِنِعْمَةِ رَبِّكَ makes the favor the thing spoken about: "speak about the favor". The surah ends not with a warning but with a joyful command: speak about what Allah has done for you.

## Practice Exercises

<ExerciseBox question="Trace the oath-answer structure of this surah. Identify the two oaths (vv.1-2) and their jawab al-qasam (v.3). Then explain: what grammatical marker confirms that verse 3 is the answer to the oaths? How does the مَا negation in the jawab interact with the positive imagery of the oaths?">

**The two oaths:**
1. وَٱلضُّحَىٰ — "By the morning brightness" (oath particle وَ + muqsam bihi in genitive)
2. وَٱلَّيْلِ إِذَا سَجَىٰ — "And by the night when it settles" (joining وَ + muqsam bihi + a time word with its clause)

**The jawab al-qasam (answer):**
مَا وَدَّعَكَ رَبُّكَ وَمَا قَلَىٰ — "Your Lord has not abandoned you, nor has He been displeased"

**Grammatical confirmation:**
The jawab al-qasam has no special start of its own. It is the sentence that comes right after the oaths and answers them. Here it is negated with مَا. An answer that says something positive takes the oath لَ. This is why verses 4 and 5 start with لَ: al-Jadwal says they are also part of the answer, and the لَ is used because a positive statement follows a negative one.

**Interaction between oaths and answer:**
The oaths swear by positive natural phenomena (bright morning, peaceful night), while the answer *negates* an accusation (abandonment, displeasure). The contrast is deliberate: the certainty and beauty of morning and night (which never fail to appear) guarantee the certainty of Allah's continued care (which also never fails). Just as night *always* gives way to morning, the pause in revelation will *always* give way to its return.

</ExerciseBox>

<ExerciseBox question="Analyze the triple parallel structure in verses 6-8 and its mirror in verses 9-11. For each of the three pairs, identify: (a) the second object (accusative) of وَجَدَ in vv.6-8, (b) the corresponding أَمَّا topic in vv.9-11, and (c) the logical connection between the blessing and the command.">

**Pair 1: Verses 6 and 9**
- **(a) Second object of وَجَدَ:** يَتِيمًا (an orphan) — describes the state in which Allah found the Prophet
- **(b) أَمَّا topic:** ٱلْيَتِيمَ (the orphan) — same word, now as the object of the command
- **(c) Connection:** Because Allah sheltered you when you were an orphan (فَآوَى), you must not oppress orphans (فَلَا تَقْهَرْ). Personal experience of vulnerability → empathy toward the vulnerable.

**Pair 2: Verses 7 and 10**
- **(a) Second object of وَجَدَ:** ضَالًّا (lost/seeking) — describes spiritual searching before revelation
- **(b) أَمَّا topic:** ٱلسَّائِلَ (the one who asks/seeks) — someone in a similar state of seeking
- **(c) Connection:** Because Allah guided you when you were seeking (فَهَدَى), you must not repel those who come seeking from you (فَلَا تَنْهَرْ). Experience of being guided → patience with seekers.

**Pair 3: Verses 8 and 11**
- **(a) Second object of وَجَدَ:** عَائِلًا (in need/poor) — describes material deprivation
- **(b) أَمَّا topic:** بِنِعْمَةِ رَبِّكَ (the favor of your Lord) — the blessings that replaced poverty
- **(c) Connection:** Because Allah enriched you (فَأَغْنَى), you must publicly proclaim His favors (فَحَدِّثْ). Experience of provision → gratitude and proclamation.

**Structural note:** The first two commands are prohibitions (لَا تَقْهَرْ, لَا تَنْهَرْ — do not...), while the third is a positive command (حَدِّثْ — do...). This mirrors the progression from protection (preventing harm) to active gratitude (spreading good).

</ExerciseBox>

<ExerciseBox question="Compare the three Form IV verbs in this surah: آوَى (v.6), أَعْطَى/يُعْطِي (v.5), and أَغْنَى (v.8). For each, give the Form I base meaning, explain how Form IV changes the meaning, and identify the root. What pattern do you notice about Form IV's function in this surah?">

**Verb 1: آوَى (āwā) — verse 6**
- **Root:** ء-و-ي
- **Form I meaning:** أَوَى — to take shelter, to seek refuge (intransitive)
- **Form IV meaning:** آوَى — to give shelter, to provide refuge to someone (transitive/causative)
- **Change:** Intransitive → transitive. From "seeking shelter" to "providing shelter"

**Verb 2: أَعْطَى/يُعْطِي (a'tā/yu'tī) — verse 5**
- **Root:** ع-ط-و
- **Form I meaning:** عَطَا — to reach for and take
- **Form IV meaning:** أَعْطَى — to give (literally, to make someone take)
- **Change:** Taking → giving. Takes two objects (recipient + gift)

**Verb 3: أَغْنَى (aghnā) — verse 8**
- **Root:** غ-ن-ي
- **Form I meaning:** غَنِيَ — to be rich, self-sufficient (stative, intransitive)
- **Form IV meaning:** أَغْنَى — to make someone rich/self-sufficient (causative, transitive)
- **Change:** Stative → causative. From "being rich" to "making someone rich"

**The pattern:** In all three cases, Form IV is causative: it turns something the Prophet experienced into something Allah did for him. Allah gave shelter, gave, and made him self-sufficient. (Note that هَدَىٰ in verse 7 is Form I.)

</ExerciseBox>

## Grammar Summary

<Callout type="note" title="Key Grammar Concepts in This Surah">
- **Oath construction (قَسَم)**: وَ as oath particle standing in for بِ, with the genitive on ٱلضُّحَىٰ and ٱلَّيْلِ; the phrase is tied to an unspoken verb "I swear"; إِذَا is a time word with no condition in it, and its clause is its مُضَاف إِلَيْهِ
- **مَا negation of past tense**: مَا وَدَّعَكَ and وَمَا قَلَىٰ (v.3) — مَا النَّافِيَة stands in front of a past tense verb and leaves it as it is (unlike لَمْ which puts a present verb in the jussive)
- **لَام القَسَم (the lam of the oath)**: لَ in verses 4 and 5; al-Jadwal says these sentences are joined to the answer of the oath, and the lam is used because a positive statement follows a negative one
- **لَسَوْفَ (sworn future)**: the lam of the oath + the future سَوْفَ, a sworn promise about the future
- **اسم التَّفْضِيل (comparative)**: خَيْرٌ + مِنْ for comparison — "better than" with irregular elative form
- **أَلَمْ rhetorical interrogation**: أَ (interrogative) + لَمْ (jussive negation) → rhetorical question expecting "yes" as answer
- **Second object of وَجَدَ**: يَتِيمًۭا, ضَآلًّۭا, عَآئِلًۭا — accusative nouns: "He found you [as] an orphan / lost / poor"
- **Triple parallel structure**: Three identical وَجَدَكَ + second object + فَ + verb patterns (vv.6-8) creating rhythmic enumeration of blessings
- **أَمَّا...فَ conditional construction**: three instances (vv.9-11); the noun after أَمَّا is the object of the verb after the فَ, brought forward, and the فَ in the answer is required
- **لَا النَّاهِيَة (prohibitive لَا)**: لَا تَقْهَرْ, لَا تَنْهَرْ — governs jussive mood for negative commands
- **Form IV causative verbs**: آوَى (sheltered), أَعْطَى (gave), أَغْنَى (enriched) — all showing Divine agency through causative Form IV
- **Form II intensive verbs**: وَدَّعَ (said goodbye to), حَدِّثْ (proclaim actively) — doubled middle radical for intensity
- **Blessing-command correspondence**: Vv.6-8 (past blessings) can be lined up with vv.9-11 (present commands), creating ethical imperatives grounded in personal experience
</Callout>
