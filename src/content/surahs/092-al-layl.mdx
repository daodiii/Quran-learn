# Al-Layl (The Night)

## Overview

- **Revelation:** Meccan
- **Verses:** 21
- **Theme:** This surah begins with oaths by the night, day, and the creation of male and female. It teaches that human efforts and paths differ vastly: those who give, are pious, and believe in goodness will be eased toward righteousness, while the miserly who deny goodness will be eased toward hardship. It promises ultimate reward and punishment, and emphasizes God's warning and guidance.
- **Grammar Focus:** Oath constructions with وَ (wa), conditional particles أَمَّا (amma) with فَ (fa-), Form II and Form IV verbs, comparative structures, future tense verbs, negation particles

<Callout type="tip" title="A Masterclass in Parallel Structure and Verb Form Diversity">
Al-Layl is one of the most beautifully structured surahs in the Quran, built on a perfect parallel contrast between two types of people. Verses 5-7 (the giver) mirror verses 8-10 (the miser) in exact grammatical structure: أَمَّا مَنْ + three verbs + فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُ لِ + superlative noun. The surah also showcases remarkable verb form diversity across its 21 verses: Form I (basic: يَغْشَىٰ, بَخِلَ, يَصْلَاهَا), Form II (intensive/causative: صَدَّقَ, كَذَّبَ, نُيَسِّرُ, يُجَنَّبُ), Form IV (transitive: أَعْطَىٰ, يُغْنِي, أَنذَرْتُكُمْ, يُؤْتِي), Form V (reflexive: تَجَلَّىٰ, تَرَدَّىٰ, تَوَلَّىٰ, تَلَظَّىٰ, يَتَزَكَّىٰ), Form VIII (reflexive: اتَّقَىٰ, ابْتِغَاءَ), and Form X (estimative: اسْتَغْنَىٰ). This makes it an extraordinary showcase for Arabic morphology, with Form V alone appearing five times with five different roots.
</Callout>

<Callout type="rule" title="The Oath Construction with وَ">
- Opening oath by the night -- وَ is oath particle taking genitive
- إِذَا means "when" and carries no condition. It is tied to an unspoken "I swear"
- يَغْشَىٰ present tense for recurring action, alif maqsura ending
- يَغْشَىٰ has no spoken object (commentators often supply "the day" or "the earth")
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** The verse opens with an oath construction (jumlat al-qasam). وَ is the oath particle (harf al-qasam) that puts اللَّيْلِ in the genitive case. al-Jadwal ties the phrase to an unspoken verb: "I swear by the night". إِذَا is an adverb (zarf) meaning "when", without any conditional sense, and it is also tied to "I swear". Its mudaf ilayhi is the clause يَغْشَىٰ. The verb has a hidden subject (هُوَ referring to اللَّيْلِ) and no spoken object (commentators often supply "the day" or "the earth").

**Sarf (Morphology):** يَغْشَىٰ is a Form I present tense (mudari') verb from root غ-ش-ي on the pattern يَفْعَل. It ends with alif maqsura, indicating it is a defective verb (mu'tall al-lam). The present tense indicates a recurring, habitual action -- the night covers repeatedly by nature.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The surah opens with cosmic phenomena as witnesses: the night that covers and conceals. The choice of يَغْشَىٰ (covers/envelops) rather than simply "comes" evokes the totality of darkness spreading over the earth. This sets up the contrast of the surah: al-Jadwal notes that the picture of the night fits the miser (verses 8-10) and the picture of the day fits the giver (verses 5-7). The present tense suggests an ongoing event rather than a single one.

<Callout type="rule" title="Form V Reflexive: تَجَلَّىٰ">
- Continuation of oath series
- تَجَلَّىٰ Form V (reflexive): "to reveal itself, become manifest"
- Contrasts with verse 1: night covers, day reveals
- The clause تَجَلَّىٰ is the mudaf ilayhi of إِذَا, as in verse 1
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** Parallel to verse 1. The second وَ joins النَّهَارِ to اللَّيْلِ: both are things sworn by, so النَّهَارِ is genitive like it. al-Jadwal says that إِذَا in both verses is tied to the unspoken "I swear", and that the clause تَجَلَّىٰ is its mudaf ilayhi. The verb changes from present (يَغْشَىٰ in v1) to past (تَجَلَّىٰ); either tense is fine after إِذَا.

**Sarf (Morphology):** تَجَلَّىٰ is Form V (تَفَعَّلَ) from root ج-ل-و. Form V adds the reflexive/middle voice to the intensive Form II (جَلَّى "to reveal/unveil"), yielding "to reveal itself, become manifest." The alif maqsura ending marks it as a defective verb (mu'tall al-lam). The تَ prefix is the hallmark of Form V.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The contrast between يَغْشَىٰ (covers -- active concealment) and تَجَلَّىٰ (reveals itself -- reflexive manifestation) is the first of many antithetical pairs in this surah. Night covers, and day shows itself. al-Jadwal points out that these two opposite pictures match the two groups of people in the surah. This duality prepares the central contrast of the surah.

<Callout type="rule" title="مَا as a Masdar Particle">
- مَا is a masdar particle in al-Jadwal's first reading: "by the creating of the male and the female". al-Jadwal also allows a relative: "by Him who created", meaning Allah
- خَلَقَ الذَّكَرَ وَالْأُنثَىٰ: fundamental duality in creation
- Third and final oath before the answer (jawab al-qasam)
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** The third oath, with a different structure: وَ + مَا + a verb, rather than وَ + a plain noun. In al-Jadwal's first reading مَا is a masdar particle (harf masdari). With its clause it turns into a verbal noun, "the creating of the male and the female". That verbal noun is in a genitive position and is joined by وَ to اللَّيْلِ, the first thing sworn by. The clause خَلَقَ الذَّكَرَ وَالْأُنثَىٰ is the sila of the particle. al-Jadwal also allows مَا as a relative word meaning "He who", Allah, again in a genitive position joined to اللَّيْلِ. الذَّكَرَ is the direct object (maf'ul bihi) in the accusative, and الْأُنثَىٰ is coordinated with it (ma'tuf 'alayhi).

**Sarf (Morphology):** خَلَقَ is Form I past tense from root خ-ل-ق, one of the most fundamental verbs in Quranic Arabic. الذَّكَرَ from root ذ-ك-ر on pattern فَعَل and الْأُنثَىٰ from root أ-ن-ث on pattern فُعْلَى represent the fundamental gender pair in Arabic grammar and creation.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The three oaths form a crescendo: night (covering), day (revealing), and the creating of male and female (a further duality). Each oath introduces a pair of opposites -- night/day, covering/revealing, male/female -- preparing the listener for the surah's central contrast between two types of human beings. The third oath can be read as "by the creating" or as "by the One who created", and al-Jadwal gives both.

<Callout type="rule" title="إِنَّ...لَ Double Emphasis: The Jawab al-Qasam">
- إِنَّ with the lam of the oath on the predicate: a double emphasis
- This is the answer (jawab) to the oath series (verses 1-3)
- سَعْيَكُمْ: human efforts/actions/strivings
- شَتَّىٰ indeclinable adjective meaning "diverse, various, scattered"
- Sets up the contrast in following verses: different types of effort
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** This verse is the jawab al-qasam (answer to the oath) -- the main clause that the three oaths in verses 1-3 were building toward. إِنَّ governs سَعْيَكُمْ as its subject (ism inna) in the accusative. The لَ on شَتَّىٰ is the lam of the oath (lam al-qasam) in al-Jadwal's reading. It takes the place of the usual emphasis lam that moves to the predicate after إِنَّ. Together, إِنَّ and this lam give the answer a double emphasis. شَتَّىٰ is the predicate (khabar inna), nominative in place; the alif maqsura hides the ending.

**Sarf (Morphology):** سَعْيَ is a masdar (verbal noun) from root س-ع-ي on the فَعْل pattern, meaning "striving, effort, endeavor." The attached pronoun كُمْ (2nd person plural) makes it a possessive: "your striving." شَتَّىٰ from root ش-ت-ت on the فَعْلَى pattern is a plural adjective meaning "diverse, scattered, various" -- it indicates complete divergence.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** After three cosmic oaths, the answer is startlingly concise: just four words. The double emphasis (إِنَّ...لَ) indicates this truth is being solemnly declared -- human efforts diverge fundamentally. The word شَتَّىٰ (scattered/diverse) from the root meaning "to scatter" suggests not merely difference but radical divergence -- paths so different they cannot be reconciled. This single verse is the thesis statement that the rest of the surah elaborates.

<Callout type="rule" title="The أَمَّا...فَ Construction and Verb Forms IV and VIII">
- فَأَمَّا...فَ construction: "as for X, then Y" (Y comes in verse 7)
- مَنْ relative pronoun: "whoever"
- أَعْطَىٰ Form IV: "to give [in charity]" -- the hamza prefix (أَ) makes root ع-ط-و transitive

- Both verbs with alif maqsura ending
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** This verse begins the first branch of the أَمَّا...فَ conditional construction. al-Jadwal calls this فَ resumptive (isti'nafiyya): it starts a new sentence after verse 4. أَمَّا is a particle of condition and detail, and its answer takes a linking فَ (verse 7). مَنْ is a relative pronoun and the mubtada' (subject); its predicate is the clause in verse 7. The two verbs أَعْطَىٰ and اتَّقَىٰ are the sila (relative clause) of مَنْ. al-Jadwal explains the sentence as "whatever happens, whoever gave ... We will ease him", with the second فَ dropped for lightness.

**Sarf (Morphology):** أَعْطَىٰ is Form IV (أَفْعَلَ) from root ع-ط-و. Form IV adds transitivity -- while عَطَا means "to extend," أَعْطَى means "to give (something to someone)." اتَّقَىٰ is Form VIII (افْتَعَلَ) from root و-ق-ي. The original form would be اوْتَقَىٰ, but the weak first radical و assimilates into the تَ, yielding اتَّقَىٰ. Form VIII adds reflexivity: "to protect oneself" = "to be God-conscious."

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The three characteristics of the righteous person (giving, fearing God, believing in goodness) come as three verbs: أَعْطَىٰ (outward action -- generosity), اتَّقَىٰ (God-consciousness), and صَدَّقَ بِالْحُسْنَىٰ (belief in the best reward). Each verb ends in alif maqsura, creating a powerful rhythmic unity across the three verses (5-6).

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The verb and its directional object share the same root (ي-س-ر), creating a figura etymologica -- "to ease toward ease" -- emphasizing that the facilitation is total and the destination is its own reward. This verse will be mirrored exactly in verse 10 with one devastating change: الْيُسْرَىٰ becomes الْعُسْرَىٰ.

The one who fears God knows his dependence; the one who thinks he needs nothing is lost. This is not merely behavioral contrast but psychological and spiritual opposition.

- إِذَا تَرَدَّىٰ: "when he falls"; إِذَا holds the sense of a condition and its answer is left out
- The point: his wealth will not help him then
</Callout>

#### Nahw-Sarf-Balagha Synthesis

**Nahw (Syntax):** In al-Jadwal's first reading, مَا is a negation particle: "his wealth does not avail him". al-Jadwal also allows an interrogative مَا as the subject, with the clause as its predicate: "what does his wealth avail him?". The clause is joined by وَ to سَنُيَسِّرُهُ لِلْعُسْرَىٰ in verse 10, so it also has a nominative place. يُغْنِي is the main verb with مَالُهُ as its subject (fa'il) and عَنْهُ tied to it. إِذَا is an adverb (zarf) that carries the sense of a condition, so it is tied to an answer that is left out: "his wealth does not avail him". The clause تَرَدَّىٰ is its mudaf ilayhi.

**Sarf (Morphology):** يُغْنِي is Form IV (يُفْعِلُ) from root غ-ن-ي, the same root as اسْتَغْنَىٰ in verse 8. The two words echo each other: he who "deemed himself self-sufficient" (Form X) discovers that nothing "suffices for him" (Form IV). تَرَدَّىٰ is Form V (تَفَعَّلَ) from root ر-د-ي meaning "to fall, be destroyed." al-Jadwal adds that the last letter of the root, ي, turned into an alif because it has a fatha before it (the original is تَرَدَّيَ).

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The root غ-ن-ي appears twice in the surah: as اسْتَغْنَىٰ (v8, he deemed himself self-sufficient) and يُغْنِي (v11, it avails him). The one who thought he was غَنِيّ (rich/self-sufficient) discovers his مَال (wealth) does not make him غَنِيّ at all. This lexical callback creates poetic justice through morphology. On the negative reading the verse says it plainly: his wealth will do him no good.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The restriction (hasr) through لَا...إِلَّا limits the burning to one kind of person, الْأَشْقَى (the most wretched), and verse 16 says who he is. This pairs with الْأَتْقَى (the MOST righteous) in verse 17, creating another superlative contrast.

In al-Jadwal's first reading, يَتَزَكَّىٰ is a hal clause (circumstantial, "while purifying himself") of the doer of يُؤْتِي. al-Jadwal also allows a badal of the clause يُؤْتِي.

**Sarf (Morphology):** يُؤْتِي is Form IV (يُفْعِلُ) from root أ-ت-ي. While Form I أَتَى means "to come," Form IV آتَى/يُؤْتِي means "to give, to bring." يَتَزَكَّىٰ is Form V (يَتَفَعَّلُ) from root ز-ك-و. Form V of this root means "to purify oneself" -- the reflexive of Form II زَكَّى ("to purify"). The word زَكَاة (the obligatory charity) comes from the same root, connecting charitable giving with purification.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** The verb يُؤْتِي (gives) is in the present tense, unlike أَعْطَىٰ (gave) in verse 5 -- this shift from past to present indicates that the righteous person gives habitually and continuously, not as a one-time act. The connection between يُؤْتِي (giving) and يَتَزَكَّىٰ (self-purification) reveals the Quranic philosophy of wealth: money is not diminished by giving but the soul is purified by it. The root ز-ك-و (growth/purification) embeds in the word the paradox that giving away wealth causes spiritual growth.

**Nahw (Syntax):** This is one of the more complex sentences in the surah. al-Jadwal reads the وَ as resumptive (it starts a new sentence) or as a hal wa. مَا is a negation particle for nominal sentences. لِأَحَدٍ is a prepositional phrase tied to a predicate that comes first and is left unsaid. عِندَهُ is an adverb of place; al-Jadwal ties it to an unspoken hal of نِّعْمَةٍ ("a favor with him"), or alternatively to تُجْزَىٰ. **Sarf (Morphology):** نِّعْمَة from root ن-ع-م on the فِعْلَة pattern means "blessing, favor, grace." تُجْزَىٰ is the passive of Form I (يُفْعَلُ) from root ج-ز-ي meaning "to be repaid/rewarded." The passive means "a favor to be repaid": no one has such a favor with him.

**Balagha (Rhetoric):** This verse clarifies the nature of the righteous person's giving: it is not an exchange. The emphatic negation (مَا...مِن) leaves no room for ulterior motive -- there is not even a TRACE of a favor being repaid. The elaborate syntax (prepositional phrases, adverb, extra مِن, passive verb) creates a thorough, legally precise denial of any motive other than sincerity. In a surah built on contrasts, this verse establishes the purest possible motive for generosity.

Adding الْأَعْلَىٰ at the end elevates the entire construction, reminding the listener that the One whose pleasure is sought is above all things.

**Sarf (Morphology):** يَرْضَىٰ is Form I (يَفْعَلُ) from root ر-ض-ي, a defective verb (mu'tall al-lam) meaning "to be pleased, satisfied." **Balagha (Rhetoric):** The surah's final verse is a masterpiece of concision. After 20 verses of elaborate parallelism, cosmic oaths, and detailed descriptions, the conclusion is four simple words: "And he will surely be satisfied." The contrast between the complexity of the journey and the simplicity of the reward is itself eloquent. The oath (لَ) and the future particle سَوْفَ leave no doubt. No object is spoken for يَرْضَىٰ, so the satisfaction is left open and is not limited to one thing.

- تَلَظَّىٰ (ل-ظ-ي) = "it blazes fiercely" (intensive reflexive burning)

<ExerciseBox question="Examine the oath construction in verses 1-4. Identify: (a) the three objects of the oath, (b) how each creates a duality/contrast, (c) the jawab al-qasam, and (d) why this particular oath sequence leads to this particular answer.">

**(a) The three objects of the oath:**
1. وَاللَّيْلِ إِذَا يَغْشَىٰ -- "By the night when it covers"
2. وَالنَّهَارِ إِذَا تَجَلَّىٰ -- "By the day when it appears"
3. وَمَا خَلَقَ الذَّكَرَ وَالْأُنثَىٰ -- "By the creating of male and female" (al-Jadwal also allows "By the One who created male and female")

The first is introduced by وَ (oath particle), which puts the following noun in the genitive (majrur). In the other two, a joining وَ adds another thing sworn by, so they are genitive too (the third is a masdar clause in a genitive position).

**(b) The dualities/contrasts in each oath:**
1. Night (covers/conceals) vs. Day (reveals/manifests) -- darkness vs. light
2. يَغْشَىٰ (present tense) vs. تَجَلَّىٰ (past tense): two opposite actions, each inside an إِذَا clause
3. الذَّكَرَ (male) vs. الْأُنثَىٰ (female) -- fundamental biological duality

Each oath presents a PAIR of opposites that work together in God's creation.

**(c) The jawab al-qasam (answer to the oath):**
إِنَّ سَعْيَكُمْ لَشَتَّىٰ -- "Indeed, your efforts are diverse"

Marked by إِنَّ and the lam of the oath (لَ): this is the usual shape of an answer to an oath.

**(d) Why this oath sequence leads to this answer:**
The three oaths establish that DUALITY is built into creation itself: night/day, covering/revealing, male/female. These are not defects but divine design. The answer then extends this principle to human effort: just as creation has inherent dualities, human striving (سَعْي) is inherently diverse (شَتَّى). Some strive toward good (giving, fearing God, believing) and some toward evil (withholding, self-sufficiency, denial).

The logical progression: God created the world in contrasting pairs → therefore human effort also diverges into contrasting paths → and the rest of the surah (verses 5-21) details these two divergent paths and their consequences.
</ExerciseBox>

## Grammar Summary

<Callout type="note" title="Key Grammar Concepts in This Surah">
- **Oath constructions with وَ**: Verse 1 uses وَ as the oath particle taking the genitive; in verses 2-3 a joining وَ adds more things sworn by. Verse 21 has an unspoken oath (the lam of the oath)
- **إِذَا clauses**: Verses 1 and 2 use plain "when" tied to "I swear"; in verse 11 إِذَا holds the sense of a condition and its answer is left out
- **إِنَّ emphasis structures**: Verses 4, 12, 13 use إِنَّ for emphasis, with لَ for double emphasis (the lam of the oath in verse 4, a lam of emphasis in verses 12-13)
- **أَمَّا...فَ conditional construction**: Verses 5-7 and 8-10 demonstrate "as for X, then Y" structure with perfect parallelism
- **Future tense particles**: سَ (verses 7, 10, 17) and سَوْفَ (verse 21) indicate future actions
- **Comparative/superlative structures**: الْيُسْرَىٰ vs. الْعُسْرَىٰ (ease vs. hardship), الْأَشْقَى vs. الْأَتْقَى (most wretched vs. most righteous), الْحُسْنَىٰ (the best), الْأَعْلَىٰ (the Most High)
- **Form II verbs (intensive/causative)**: صَدَّقَ, كَذَّبَ, نُيَسِّرُهُ, يُجَنَّبُهَا (passive)
- **Form IV verbs**: أَعْطَىٰ, يُغْنِي, أَنذَرْتُكُمْ, يُؤْتِي
- **Form V verbs (reflexive)**: تَجَلَّىٰ, تَرَدَّىٰ, تَوَلَّىٰ, تَلَظَّىٰ, يَتَزَكَّىٰ
- **Form VIII verbs**: اتَّقَىٰ, ابْتِغَاءَ (verbal noun)
- **Form X verb**: اسْتَغْنَىٰ (considering oneself to possess a quality)
- **Passive voice constructions**: يُجَنَّبُهَا, تُجْزَىٰ
- **Negation particles**: لَا (verse 15), مَا (verses 11, 19)
- **Restriction and exception**: إِلَّا limits the action to one doer in verse 15 and begins a separate exception in verse 20
- **Perfect structural parallelism**: The surah exhibits beautiful symmetry between the two types of people (verses 5-7 vs. 8-10, verse 15 vs. 17)
- **Idafa constructions**: وَجْهِ رَبِّهِ, سَعْيَكُمْ
- **Emphasis patterns**: وَلَسَوْفَ (verse 21) is a new sentence with an unspoken oath (لَ) and a future particle
</Callout>
