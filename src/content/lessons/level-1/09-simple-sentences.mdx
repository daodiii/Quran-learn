Both verses use the word ٱلْحَقُّ, “the truth”. But they are two different kinds of sentence.

In the first, ٱلْحَقُّ is the first word. In the second, the first word is جَآءَ, “has come”. That is an action word, a verb.

This lesson is about that difference. The first word decides the kind of sentence.

</Block>

In L1.04 you learned three kinds of word: noun, verb and little word. Now you use that to sort sentences.

- The first word is a **noun**: the sentence is **nominal**, جُمْلَة اِسْمِيَّة (*jumla ismiyya*). *Ism* means noun.
- The first word is a **verb**: the sentence is **verbal**, جُمْلَة فِعْلِيَّة (*jumla fiʿliyya*). *Fiʿl* means verb.

A pronoun like هُوَ (“he”) counts as a noun. You will study pronouns later (L3.08).

This course uses these two kinds. L2.01 and L2.03 teach them in full.

<Note kind="remember">The first word decides. Not the meaning of the sentence, and not the words that come later.</Note>

</Block>

<Block id="nominal" title="A nominal sentence starts with a noun" level={2}>
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Ash-Shura 42:19 (start)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'ٱللَّهُ', en: 'Allah', say: 'allahu' }] },
        { role: 'khabar', words: [{ ar: 'لَطِيفٌۢ', en: 'kind', say: 'latifun' }] },
        { role: 'tied', hint: 'belongs to لَطِيفٌ', words: [{ ar: 'بِعِبَادِهِۦ', en: 'to his servants', say: 'bi-ibadihi', type: 'p' }] },
      ]}
      en={['Allah ', { gap: 'is' }, ' kind to His servants.']}
    />
  </Stage>

The first word is ٱللَّهُ, a noun. So the sentence is nominal.

A nominal sentence has two parts:

- The **mubtadaʾ**, مُبْتَدَأ (*mubtadaʾ*): “the thing you start with”. Here it is ٱللَّهُ.
- The **khabar**, خَبَر (*khabar*): “the news”. It tells you something about the mubtadaʾ. Here it is لَطِيفٌ, “kind”.

The little phrase بِعِبَادِهِۦ, “to His servants”, goes with لَطِيفٌ. It says who Allah is kind to.

<Note kind="know">English books say **subject** and **predicate**. They mean roughly the same as mubtadaʾ and khabar. L2.01 teaches the nominal sentence in full.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="no-is" title="There is no word for “is”" level={2}>
  <Stage label="The missing word">
    <Tiles
      label="ٱللَّهُ, then nothing, then لَطِيفٌ: Allah is kind"
      items={[
        { ar: 'ٱللَّهُ', en: 'Allah' },
        { ghost: 'is', note: 'English only' },
        { ar: 'لَطِيفٌ', en: 'kind' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The Arabic has two words. The English has three.

English needs *is*, *am* or *are*: “Allah <u>is</u> kind.” Arabic puts the two words side by side, and the reader understands “is”. Nothing has been left out. There is no hidden word, and you do not look for one.

You add “is” when you say the sentence in English. That is all.

<Note kind="warn">Some books say “the verb *is* is implied”. Do not look for a verb in a nominal sentence. The sentence is complete with two nouns.</Note>

</Block>

<Block id="verbal" title="A verbal sentence starts with a verb" level={2}>
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="An-Naml 27:16 (start)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'وَوَرِثَ', en: 'and inherited', say: 'wa-warita', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'fail', words: [{ ar: 'سُلَيْمَٰنُ', en: 'Solomon', say: 'sulaymanu' }] },
        { role: 'maful', words: [{ ar: 'دَاوُۥدَ', en: 'David', say: 'dawuda' }] },
      ]}
      en={['And Solomon inherited from David.']}
    />
  </Stage>

The first word is وَوَرِثَ. Take off the وَ (“and”), and the verb is وَرِثَ, “inherited”. So the sentence is verbal.

A verbal sentence has these parts:

- The **verb**, فِعْل (*fiʿl*): the action. Here it is وَرِثَ.
- The **doer**, فَاعِل (*fāʿil*): who does the action. Here it is سُلَيْمَٰنُ, Solomon.
- Sometimes an **object**, مَفْعُول بِهِ (*mafʿūl bihi*): what the action is done to. Here it is دَاوُۥدَ, David.

The object is optional. “The truth has come” (17:81) has a verb and a doer, and no object.

<Note kind="know">L2.03 teaches the verbal sentence in full, with the three kinds of doer and the object.</Note>

<Check n={4} q="Who is the doer in وَرِثَ سُلَيْمَٰنُ دَاوُۥدَ?" options={['وَرِثَ', 'سُلَيْمَٰنُ', 'دَاوُۥدَ']} answer={1} why="Solomon did the inheriting. The word right after the verb is the doer. David is the object." />
</Block>

<Block id="order" title="The verb comes first" level={2}>
  <Stage label="Arabic order and English order">
    <Tiles
      label="Arabic order: verb, doer, object"
      items={[
        { ar: 'وَرِثَ', en: 'inherited', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'سُلَيْمَٰنُ', en: 'Solomon' },
        { ar: 'دَاوُۥدَ', en: 'David' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

In Arabic the usual order is **verb, then doer, then object**.

English does it the other way round: “Solomon inherited David.” The doer comes first in English.

So when you read Arabic, do not expect the doer in front. After a verb, look for the doer next.

<Note kind="remember">Verbal sentence: verb, doer, object. English puts the doer first. Arabic puts the verb first.</Note>
</Block>
</Step>

<Step id="closer" label="A closer look" title="A closer look at each kind">
<Block id="nominal-parts" title="Nominal: the starting point and the news">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Yunus 10:107 (end)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'وَهُوَ', en: 'and he', say: 'wa-huwa' }] },
        { role: 'khabar', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلْغَفُورُ', en: 'the Forgiving', say: 'al-ghafuru' }] },
        { role: 'khabar2', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلرَّحِيمُ', en: 'the Merciful', say: 'ar-rahimu' }] },
      ]}
      en={['And He ', { gap: 'is' }, ' the Forgiving, the Merciful.']}
    />
  </Stage>

The mubtadaʾ here is a pronoun: هُوَ, “he”. It is a noun, so the sentence is nominal. The little word وَ (“and”) at the front only joins this sentence to the one before it.

The khabar is ٱلْغَفُورُ, and a second khabar, ٱلرَّحِيمُ, follows it. One mubtadaʾ can have more than one khabar. The English puts a comma between them. Arabic needs no extra word.

Look at the last letters of the three words. Each ends in the sound **-u**. Both parts of a nominal sentence take the same kind of ending. The next lesson (L1.10) shows why endings change.

<Note kind="know">The khabar can be a single word, a short phrase (as in 2:147), a verbal sentence, or a nominal sentence. L2.01 teaches all five kinds.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="verbal-parts" title="Verbal: sometimes the doer is inside the verb">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Kafirun 109:1"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'قُلْ', en: 'say', say: 'qul', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'tied', label: 'what is said', hint: 'the words to say', words: [{ ar: 'يَٰٓأَيُّهَا', en: 'O', say: 'ya ayyuha', type: 'p' }, { ar: 'ٱلْكَٰفِرُونَ', en: 'the disbelievers', say: 'al-kafirun' }] },
      ]}
      en={['Say, ', { gap: 'you' }, ': “O disbelievers”.']}
    />
  </Stage>

قُلْ is one word, and it is a full verbal sentence on its own: “Say!” There is no separate word for the doer, because the doer is “you”. The doer is hidden inside the verb.

So a verbal sentence can be just a verb. The doer is always there, but it is not always a separate word.

The rest of the verse, يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْكَٰفِرُونَ, is **what is said**. It is not an ordinary object. Al-Jadwal calls it “the words that are said” (*maqūl al-qawl*).

<Note kind="know">Some learners call this part the object. The grammar books give it its own name, “what is said”.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="little-words" title="Skip the little word at the front">
  <Stage label="One little word, two kinds of sentence">
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Kafirun 109:2 (start)"
      groups={[
        { words: [{ ar: 'لَآ', en: 'not', say: 'la', type: 'p' }] },
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'أَعْبُدُ', en: 'I worship', say: 'abudu', type: 'v' }] },
      ]}
      en={['I do not worship.']}
    />
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Kafirun 109:3 (start)"
      groups={[
        { words: [{ ar: 'وَلَآ', en: 'and not', say: 'wa-la', type: 'p' }] },
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'أَنتُمْ', en: 'you', say: 'antum' }] },
        { role: 'khabar', words: [{ ar: 'عَٰبِدُونَ', en: 'worshippers', say: 'abiduna' }] },
      ]}
      en={['And you ', { gap: 'are' }, ' not worshippers.']}
    />
  </Stage>

Many sentences begin with a little word: وَ (and), فَ (so), or the word for “not”, لَا. A little word is not a noun or a verb, so it does not decide the kind. **Skip it and look at the next word.**

- لَآ أَعْبُدُ: skip لَآ. The next word, أَعْبُدُ (“I worship”), is a verb. The sentence is **verbal**.
- وَلَآ أَنتُمْ عَٰبِدُونَ: skip وَلَآ. The next word, أَنتُمْ (“you”), is a pronoun, so it is a noun. The sentence is **nominal**.

The same لَا stands in front of both. It does not change the kind of sentence.

<Note kind="know">A few words at the front do change the sentence that follows them. *Inna* and *kāna* are the main ones. L2.10 and L2.11 teach them. For now, skip the little word and sort by the next word.</Note>

</Block>
</Step>

<Step id="mistakes-practice" label="Mistakes and practice" title="Four mistakes, then practice">
<Block id="mistake-meaning" title="Mistake 1: judging by meaning, not by the first word">
  <Stage label="An action, but a nominal sentence">
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Ar-Rahman 55:1"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ', en: 'the Most Merciful', say: 'ar-rahmanu' }] },
      ]}
    />
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Ar-Rahman 55:2"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'عَلَّمَ', en: 'taught', say: 'allama', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'maful', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلْقُرْءَانَ', en: 'the Quran', say: 'al-qurana' }] },
      ]}
      en={['The Most Merciful taught the Quran.']}
    />
  </Stage>

Some books say “a nominal sentence describes what something *is*, and a verbal sentence describes what something *does*”. That is a way to remember, but it is not the rule. The rule is the first word.

Read the two verses as one sentence: “The Most Merciful taught the Quran.” It is about an action, teaching. But the first word is ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ, a noun. So it is a **nominal** sentence. The mubtadaʾ is ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ, and the khabar is the whole verbal sentence عَلَّمَ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ. Al-Jadwal reads it this way.

<Note kind="warn">A verb later in the sentence does not make it verbal. Only the first word counts. L2.01 shows a verbal sentence as a khabar, and L2.03 teaches the verbal sentence.</Note>

<Check n={7} q="ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ عَلَّمَ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ: what kind of sentence is it?" options={['Nominal, because the first word is a noun', 'Verbal, because it is about an action']} answer={0} why="The first word decides. ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ is a noun. The verb عَلَّمَ is inside the khabar." />
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-wa" title="Mistake 2: counting the little word as the first word">
  <Stage label="Skip it, then look">
    <Tiles
      label="Skip the little word, then look at the next word"
      items={[
        { ar: 'وَلَآ', en: 'and not (skip)', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'أَنتُمْ', en: 'you' },
        { ar: 'عَٰبِدُونَ', en: 'worshippers' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A learner sees وَلَآ at the front, a word that is neither a noun nor a verb, and does not know what to do. The answer is to skip it. The little word joins or negates. It does not decide.

The next word here is أَنتُمْ, “you”. That is a pronoun, and a pronoun is a noun. So this is a nominal sentence, even though a verb, أَعْبُدُ, appears later in the verse. The first word that counts is أَنتُمْ.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-doer" title="Mistake 3: calling the doer a mubtadaʾ">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Isra 17:81 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'جَآءَ', en: 'has come', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'fail', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلْحَقُّ', en: 'the truth' }] },
      ]}
    />
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Baqarah 2:147 (start)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلْحَقُّ', en: 'the truth' }] },
        { role: 'khabar', words: [{ ar: 'مِن', en: 'from', type: 'p' }, { ar: 'رَّبِّكَ', en: 'your Lord' }] },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Both verses have ٱلْحَقُّ with a -u ending. A noun with -u is not always a mubtadaʾ.

- After a verb, the noun is the **doer** (fāʿil).
- At the start of the sentence, the noun is the **mubtadaʾ**.

The ending is the same. The position tells you the job.

<Check n={8} q="In جَآءَ ٱلْحَقُّ (17:81), what is ٱلْحَقُّ?" options={['The mubtadaʾ', 'The doer (fāʿil)']} answer={1} why="It comes after a verb, and it is the one who comes. That makes it the doer." />
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-order" title="Mistake 4: thinking the doer comes first, as in English">
  <Stage label="Made-up example">
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Made-up example"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'قَالَ', en: 'said', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'fail', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلرَّجُلُ', en: 'the man' }] },
      ]}
      en={['The man said.']}
    />
  </Stage>

English says “The man said”. Arabic says قَالَ ٱلرَّجُلُ, literally “Said the man”.

If you look for the doer first, you may miss the verb. Always read the first word first. A verb first means: the doer comes next.

<Check n={9} q="In قَالَ ٱلرَّجُلُ, which word is first, and which kind is the sentence?" options={['قَالَ, a verb: the sentence is verbal', 'ٱلرَّجُلُ, a noun: the sentence is nominal']} answer={0} why="Read right to left. The first word is قَالَ, “said”. The doer ٱلرَّجُلُ comes after it." />
</Block>

<Block id="practice-1" title="Practice: sort and name the parts">
  <Stage label="For each sentence, say">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>First word:</b> after you skip a little word?</li>
      <li><b>Kind:</b> nominal or verbal?</li>
      <li><b>Parts:</b> mubtadaʾ and khabar, or verb and doer?</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

Work out each answer first. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Is each one nominal or verbal? (a) قُلْ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْكَٰفِرُونَ (Al-Kafirun 109:1) (b) وَلَآ أَنَا۠ عَابِدٌ مَّا عَبَدتُّمْ (Al-Kafirun 109:4) (c) وَقَالَ ٱلرَّسُولُ يَٰرَبِّ (Al-Furqan 25:30, start) (d) وَٱللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ (Al-Ma’idah 5:74, end)">

- (a) **Verbal.** The first word is قُلْ, a command verb: “say”.
- (b) **Nominal.** Skip وَلَآ. The next word, أَنَا۠ (“I”), is a pronoun, so it is a noun.
- (c) **Verbal.** Skip وَ. The next word, قَالَ (“said”), is a verb. The doer is ٱلرَّسُولُ.
- (d) **Nominal.** Skip وَ. The next word, ٱللَّهُ, is a noun.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Find the mubtadaʾ and the khabar: وَٱللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ (Al-Ma’idah 5:74, end). Meaning: “And Allah is Forgiving, Merciful.”">

ٱللَّهُ is the **mubtadaʾ**: the one the sentence is about.

غَفُورٌ is the **khabar**, and رَحِيمٌ is a **second khabar**. They are two separate pieces of news about Allah. Al-Jadwal calls the second one “a second khabar”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Find the verb and the doer: ءَامَنَ ٱلرَّسُولُ بِمَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيْهِ (Al-Baqarah 2:285, start). Meaning: “The Messenger believes in what was sent down to him.”">

The verb is **ءَامَنَ**, “believed”. The doer is **ٱلرَّسُولُ**, “the Messenger”. The words بِمَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيْهِ tell you what he believed in. They are not the doer.

Notice again: the verb comes first, and the doer comes next. English says “the Messenger believes”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="This verse starts with a short phrase, not with a single noun: لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ (Al-Kafirun 109:6, start). Meaning: “You have your religion.” Which kind of sentence is it?">

It is **nominal**. لَكُمْ means “for you”. It is not a verb. دِينُكُمْ, “your religion”, is a noun.

The khabar is لَكُمْ, put first. The mubtadaʾ is دِينُكُمْ, put second. There is no verb “have” in the Arabic: “For you (is) your religion.”

A khabar can come before its mubtadaʾ. L2.01 teaches when and why.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Make two sentences from these words, and name the kind. ٱلْكِتَابُ (the book), جَدِيدٌ (new), قَرَأَ (he read), ٱلطَّالِبُ (the student). These are made-up examples.">

**Nominal:** ٱلْكِتَابُ جَدِيدٌ, “The book is new.” The mubtadaʾ is ٱلْكِتَابُ. The khabar is جَدِيدٌ. There is no word for “is”.

**Verbal:** قَرَأَ ٱلطَّالِبُ ٱلْكِتَابَ, “The student read the book.” The verb is قَرَأَ. The doer is ٱلطَّالِبُ. The object is ٱلْكِتَابَ.

Look at the end of ٱلْكِتَابَ. It ends in -a, not -u. The object takes a different ending from the doer. L1.10 explains why.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Which word decides the kind of sentence in وَلَآ أَنتُمْ عَٰبِدُونَ مَآ أَعْبُدُ (Al-Kafirun 109:3)? There is a verb in it. Is the sentence verbal?">

The word that decides is **أَنتُمْ**. Skip وَلَآ, and the first word is the pronoun “you”, which is a noun. So the sentence is **nominal**.

The verb أَعْبُدُ comes at the end. It belongs to a smaller phrase, مَآ أَعْبُدُ, “what I worship”. It does not change the kind of the main sentence.

The mubtadaʾ is أَنتُمْ, and the khabar is عَٰبِدُونَ, “worshippers”.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The one-second test">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Find the first word.</b> Skip وَ, فَ and لَا.</li>
      <li><b>Noun?</b> Nominal: mubtadaʾ + khabar.</li>
      <li><b>Verb?</b> Verbal: verb + doer (+ object).</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

- Arabic has two kinds of sentence: **nominal** (starts with a noun) and **verbal** (starts with a verb).
- The **first word** decides. A pronoun counts as a noun. Skip a little word like وَ or لَا before you decide.
- A nominal sentence has a **mubtadaʾ** and a **khabar**. Arabic has no word for “is”. You add it in English, and nothing is hidden.
- A verbal sentence has a **verb**, a **doer** and sometimes an **object**. The verb comes first. The doer can be hidden inside the verb.
- The meaning does not decide the kind. A nominal sentence can be about an action.

**Next:** L1.10 shows how Arabic marks the role of each word with the ending of the word: why ٱلْحَقُّ ends in -u here, and why the object ٱلْكِتَابَ ended in -a. Then L2.01 teaches the nominal sentence in full, and L2.03 teaches the verbal sentence in full.
