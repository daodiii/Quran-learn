You can already read this verse. Now look at what kind of word each one is. Read it from right to left.

- **قَدْ** is a **little word**. It adds “surely”.
- **أَفْلَحَ** is a **verb**. It tells what happened: they succeeded.
- **ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ** is a **noun**. It names who did it: the believers.

English has many kinds of word: nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions and more. Arabic grammar books use only **three**. Every Arabic word goes in exactly one of them. Think of sorting post into three boxes.

| Box | Arabic | What it holds | Examples from verses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Noun | اِسْمٌ (*ism*) | A name, a thing, an idea, a quality, a place, a time | ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ *mankind* (103:2), خُسْرٍ *loss* (103:2) |
| Verb | فِعْلٌ (*fiʿl*) | An action, with a time | أَفْلَحَ *succeeded* (23:1), يُعْطِيكَ *gives you* (93:5) |
| Little word | حَرْفٌ (*ḥarf*) | A word that only does a job with other words | قَدْ *surely* (23:1), إِنَّ *indeed* (103:2), مِنْ *from* (96:2) |

This is the first thing to ask about any word you meet: which box? Later rules are easier when you know what kind of word you are looking at.

<Note kind="tip">Open any page of the Quran and look at the first five words. Do not try to sort them yet. Come back after step 3 and sort them with the signs.</Note>

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* is a short grammar book by Ibn Ājurrūm (died 723 AH). For centuries it has been the first grammar book many students learn. It begins with speech (*al-kalām*) and says: “Its parts are three: a noun, a verb, and a little word that comes for a meaning.”

The three boxes are the start of the classical course. The book then gives **signs**, clues you can test, so you can tell which box a word belongs in. Steps 2 and 3 use them.

<Note kind="know">In the book, a word that “comes for a meaning” means a little word has a job. On its own it gives no picture, like “in” or “indeed”. It needs other words.</Note>

The noun box is wider than the English word *noun*. It also holds words you would call adjectives, pronouns and place words.

| What it is | Example | Verse | Corpus tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| a kind of being | ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ *mankind* | 103:2 | N |
| an idea | خُسْرٍ *loss* | 103:2 | N |
| a name | نُوحٍ *Nūḥ* | 26:105 | PN |
| a quality | غَفُورًا *forgiving* | 33:5 | ADJ |
| a place word | عِندَ *with, at* | 33:5 | LOC |
| a pronoun | هُوَ *he* | 33:5 | PRON |
| “this” or “that” | ذَٰلِكَ *that* | 2:2 | DEM |
| “the one who” | ٱلَّذِى *the one who* | 96:1 | REL |

The Quranic Arabic Corpus gives these finer tags. In the three-box view they are all nouns. *Al-Jadwal*, a book that analyses every verse, names alladhī and dhālika in its own words as nouns of a special kind: a “joining noun” (*ism mawṣūl*) and a “pointing noun” (*ism ishāra*). *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* counts the pronouns as nouns too.

<Note kind="know">An Arabic adjective is a noun that describes another noun. L2.09 teaches how it matches the noun. Pronouns come in L3.01 and L3.02. “This” and “who” come in L3.10 and L3.11.</Note>

The book says a noun is known by these signs. It then lists the prepositions and the three oath letters. Here are the signs, with the book’s own word for each.

| Sign | In the book | Example | Verse |
|---|---|---|---|
| The genitive ending, -i or -in | *al-khafḍ* | خُسْرٍ | 103:2 |
| Tanween | *al-tanwīn* | خُسْرٍ | 103:2 |
| Al- at the front | *dukhūl al-alif wa-l-lām* | ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ | 103:2 |
| A preposition before it | *ḥurūf al-khafḍ* | فِى comes before خُسْرٍ | 103:2 |
| An oath letter before it | *ḥurūf al-qasam* | وَٱلتِّينِ | 95:1 |

Test the two nouns in this verse. ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ has **al-**. خُسْرٍ has **tanween**, ends in the genitive -in, and comes after the preposition fī. That is plenty.

A noun needs only one sign. A verb never has any of these five.

<Note kind="know">The book’s word *khafḍ* is the same case that this course calls the genitive (*jarr*). L1.10 shows the three endings. L2.06 and L2.07 teach the genitive.</Note>

Two of the signs are little words that stand in front of a noun.

The first is a **preposition**. In Al-ʿAlaq 96:1 (part), the verse says بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ, “in the name of your Lord”. The little word بِ comes before ٱسْمِ. So ٱسْمِ is a noun, and it takes the genitive -i.

The second is an **oath letter**: a letter that means “by”, as in “by Allah”. In At-Tin 95:1 (start), the verse says وَٱلتِّينِ, “By the fig”. The little word وَ here means “by”. So ٱلتِّينِ is a noun, again with -i.

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* lists three oath letters: wa-, bi- and ta-. L2.07 teaches them.

<Note kind="warn">The letter وَ can also mean “and”. In 95:1 the second one, in وَٱلزَّيْتُونِ, means “and”: “and the olive”. L2.07 shows how to tell the two apart.</Note>

A **verb** is a word for an action. It also carries a **time**. The three forms you will meet most are:

- **Past**: the action happened. خَلَقَ, “he created” (Al-ʿAlaq 96:1, end). L3.05 teaches it.
- **Present or future**: the action is happening or will happen. يُعْطِيكَ, “he gives you” (Ad-Duha 93:5, part). L3.06 teaches it.
- **Command**: you ask for the action. ٱقْرَأْ, “read!” (Al-ʿAlaq 96:1, start). L3.08 teaches it.

The time idea is a helper that this course adds. The book does not use it. It gives signs instead, and the next two blocks show them. Use the signs when you are not sure.

<Note kind="warn">A word that means a time, like يَوْمٌ (a day), is a noun. It names the time. A verb does not name a time. It happens in a time.</Note>

</Block>

The book gives four signs of a verb. The first three are little words that stand **before** the verb. The fourth is a mark at the **end**.

| Sign | In the book | Example | Verse |
|---|---|---|---|
| قَدْ before it: “surely, has” | *qad* | قَدْ أَفْلَحَ | 23:1 |
| سَ joined to the front: “will” | *al-sīn* | سَنَدْعُ | 96:18 |
| سَوْفَ before it: “will, soon” | *sawfa* | لَسَوْفَ يُعْطِيكَ | 93:5 |
| A silent tāʾ at the end: a feminine doer | *tāʾ al-taʾnīth al-sākina* | كَذَّبَتْ | 26:105 |

In 23:1, **qad** comes before أَفْلَحَ. A noun does not take qad, so أَفْلَحَ is a verb.

In 96:18, the letter **sa-** is joined to the front of نَدْعُ. It means “will”. It only goes before verbs, so نَدْعُ, “we call”, is a verb.

Qad and sa- are themselves little words. Their job is to stand in front of a verb.

The other two signs of a verb.

**Sawfa.** In 93:5 the little word سَوْفَ comes before يُعْطِيكَ and means “soon, will”. It goes only before a verb, so يُعْطِيكَ is a verb. The two little words joined to the front, وَ and لَ, are explained in step 3.

**The silent tāʾ.** In 26:105 the verb ends in تْ, a tāʾ with a sukūn. This is the sign of a feminine doer. The doer, قَوْمُ, “the people”, is a group, and *al-Jadwal* says the verb is feminine to match the meaning of the doer: a community. The tāʾ is added to a verb to show who did it.

You have seen four signs of a noun and four of a verb. The next step starts from the third box.

The book defines the third box by what it is not: a little word is a word with **no noun sign and no verb sign**.

Test إِنَّ in 103:2. It has no tanween, no al-, and no genitive ending. No preposition comes before it. Qad, sa- and sawfa do not stand before it either, and it has no silent tāʾ. It fits neither list, so it is a little word.

The same test works on لَ and فِى in لَفِى. Here the Corpus marks لَ as a little word of emphasis and فِى as a preposition.

<Note kind="know">The test works in this order because the noun and verb signs are things you can see. A little word is what is left over. As a helper, ask also: does it need other words to mean anything? “Indeed” and “in” do. This helper is ours, not the book’s.</Note>

<Check n={10} q="Why is إِنَّ in إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ a little word?" options={['It is short', 'It has no noun sign and no verb sign', 'It comes first in the verse']} answer={1} why="Length and place do not decide. A little word is a word with neither kind of sign." />
</Block>

The little-word box holds many jobs, far more than the English *a* and *the*.

| Job | Example | Verse | Corpus tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| a preposition: from, in | مِنْ | 96:2 | P |
| “and” | وَ (joined to the next word) | 103:3 | CONJ |
| “indeed” | إِنَّ | 103:2 | ACC |
| “surely” (emphasis) | لَ (joined to the next word) | 103:2 | EMPH |
| “has surely” | قَدْ | 23:1 | CERT |
| “will” | سَ or سَوْفَ | 96:18, 93:5 | FUT |
| “not” | لَمْ | 33:5 | NEG |
| “except” | إِلَّا | 103:3 | EXP |
| “by” in an oath | وَ (joined to the next word) | 95:1 | P |

You do not need these tag names. They only show how many jobs the box holds. This table has no “the” in it: Arabic says “the” with *al-*, which is joined to the noun and is one of its signs (step 2, and L1.06).

<Note kind="know">Two of these you will study later. The prepositions come in L2.07. *Inna* comes in L2.10.</Note>
</Block>

Many little words are one letter, and they join the word after them: وَ، فَ، بِ، لِ، كَ، لَ، سَ. They are still **separate words**. A written word like وَلَسَوْفَ holds three little words. The Corpus, *al-Jadwal* and the grammar books all treat them as separate.

So when you sort words into boxes, split a joined word first. Sort the pieces:

- بِٱسْمِ (Al-ʿAlaq 96:1, part) is a little word and a noun.
- فَهُوَ (Ash-Shuʿara 26:78, part) is a little word and a noun, the pronoun “he”.
- A pronoun can also join the **end** of a word. In خَلَقَنِى (26:78, part), the verb خَلَقَ is joined to نِى, “me”. That ending is a noun. L3.02 teaches these.

<Note kind="warn">*Al-* is not a fourth kind of word. In this course and in the book, it is a **sign** of a noun. The Corpus writes it as a prefix. Do not count it as a word when you sort.</Note>
</Block>

Two small words can have the same letters and only a different vowel: **مِنْ** (*min*) and **مَن** (*man*). They are in different boxes.

- **مَن**, “who, whoever”, is a **noun**. In Nuh 71:28 (part), the verse says وَلِمَن دَخَلَ, “and for whoever entered”. A preposition, لِ, stands before مَن. That is a noun sign. *Al-Jadwal* names it a joining noun.

The vowel changes the box. So never decide by letters alone. Test the signs.

<Exercise n={1} q="Which kind of word is each one in وَٱلْعَصْرِ (Al-Asr 103:1)? Name the sign. Meaning: “By time.”">

**وَ** is a **little word**: an oath letter that means “by”. It has no noun sign and no verb sign. It is joined to the next word.

**ٱلْعَصْرِ**, “time”, is a **noun**. It has al- at the front. It ends in the genitive -i, and an oath letter stands before it.

So this phrase has two kinds of word, a little word and a noun. It has no verb. The wa- swears by what follows. It does not join the verse to the surah before it. *Al-Jadwal* ties the phrase to a left-out verb: “I swear”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Find the nouns in إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَفِى خُسْرٍ (Al-Asr 103:2). Name the sign for each. Meaning: “Indeed, mankind is in loss.”">

There are **two nouns**.

- **ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ**, “mankind”: it has **al-**.
- **خُسْرٍ**, “loss”: it has **tanween**, it ends in the genitive -in, and the preposition فِى comes before it.

The other words are **little words**: إِنَّ, “indeed”; لَ, “surely”; and فِى, “in”. There is no verb.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Why is ءَامَنُوا۟ a verb and not a noun? (Al-Asr 103:3, part). Meaning: “they believed”.">

Three clues.

1. It fails the noun signs. It has no tanween and no al-, and it takes no genitive ending. A preposition does not stand before it.
2. It names an action in the past: they believed.
3. It follows a verb pattern. The ending ـُوا۟ means “they”. You could put qad before it, as in 23:1. A feminine doer would give the silent tāʾ: ءَامَنَتْ.

Compare the noun from the same root: إِيمَانٌ, “faith”. It takes tanween and names the idea. The verb names the action in a time.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Label every word by kind: وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ (Al-Asr 103:3, part). Meaning: “and they did the good deeds”.">

First split the joined word.

1. **وَ**: a **little word**, “and”.
2. **عَمِلُوا۟**: a **verb** in the past, “they did”. The ending ـُوا۟ is the doer, “they”.
3. **ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ**: a **noun**, “the good deeds”. It has al-.

The kinds in order: little word, verb, noun. The little word joins this action to the one before it. The verb says what they did. The noun says what was done.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Sort every word of قَالَ رَبِّ ٱغْفِرْ لِى (Al-Aʿraf 7:151, start). Meaning: “He said: My Lord, forgive me.”">

1. **قَالَ**: a **verb** in the past, “he said”.
2. **رَبِّ**: a **noun**, “my Lord”. In other verses a preposition stands before it, as in بِرَبِّهِمْ (6:1). Its last vowel comes from the dropped “my”.
3. **ٱغْفِرْ**: a **verb**, a command, “forgive!”. It has no noun sign.
4. **لِى**: two pieces. **لِ** is a **little word**, “for”. **ى** is a **noun**, “me”, an attached pronoun (L3.02).

Order: verb, noun, verb, little word and pronoun. A command is a verb too. It shows an action that you ask for.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Roots do not decide the box. The word ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ (Al-Asr 103:3, part) comes from the root ص ل ح. Name two more words from this root, and say which box each is in.">

The root **ص ل ح** (ṣ-l-ḥ) carries the meaning “good, right”. The Corpus gives this root for ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ. You can find the three letters inside it: ٱل**صَّ**ٰ**لِحَ**ٰتِ.

- صَالِحٌ, “good, righteous”: a **noun** (tanween).
- أَصْلَحَ, “he put right”: a **verb** in the past.
- إِصْلَاحٌ, “putting right, reform”: a **noun** (tanween).

One root gives both nouns and verbs. So you cannot find the box from the root. Use the signs. L1.05 and L3.03 teach roots.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="ٱلْحَقِّ (Al-Asr 103:3, part) is a noun with al-. Name two jobs a noun like this can have in a sentence. These made-up examples help: ٱلْحَقُّ وَاضِحٌ (“The truth is clear”) and قَالَ ٱلْحَقَّ (“He spoke the truth”). Both are made-up examples.">

**ٱلْحَقِّ**, “the truth”, names an idea. It can have several jobs:

- The thing a sentence is about: ٱلْحَقُّ وَاضِحٌ. The ending is **-u**. L2.01 teaches this.
- What the action is done to: قَالَ ٱلْحَقَّ. The ending is **-a**. L2.03 and L2.05 teach this.
- The noun after a preposition: بِٱلْحَقِّ in 103:3. The ending is **-i**. L2.07 teaches this.

The last vowel changes with the job. The box stays the same: it is a noun every time.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Count the three kinds in إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ (Al-Asr 103:3). Split every joined piece.">

- **Little words, 6**: إِلَّا; وَ (before عَمِلُوا۟); وَ (before the first تَوَاصَوْا۟); بِ (before ٱلْحَقِّ); وَ (before the second تَوَاصَوْا۟); بِ (before ٱلصَّبْرِ).
- **Verbs, 4**: ءَامَنُوا۟, عَمِلُوا۟, تَوَاصَوْا۟, تَوَاصَوْا۟.
- **Nouns, 4**: ٱلَّذِينَ, ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ, ٱلْحَقِّ, ٱلصَّبْرِ.

The four -ū endings are doers, “they”, and they are nouns. They are not counted here. For the whole surah, add 103:1 (a little word and a noun) and 103:2 (three little words and two nouns). That makes **10 little words, 4 verbs and 7 nouns**.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Which box is each word in? (a) مَن in وَلِمَن دَخَلَ (Nuh 71:28, part) (b) مِنْ in مِنْ عَلَقٍ (Al-ʿAlaq 96:2, part). Say how you know.">

(a) **مَن**, “whoever”, is a **noun**. The preposition لِ stands before it. That is a noun sign. (The word دَخَلَ after it is a verb in the past, “entered”.)

(b) **مِنْ**, “from”, is a **little word**. Nothing can stand before it, and it has no verb sign. It is a preposition. The noun after it, عَلَقٍ, has tanween and the genitive ending.

The two words differ in one vowel and sit in different boxes. Test the signs.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Sort every word of ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ (Al-Anʿam 6:1, start). Meaning: “Praise belongs to Allah, who created.”">

1. **ٱلْحَمْدُ**, “praise”: a **noun**. It has al-.
2. **لِ**: a **little word**, a preposition, “for, to”.
3. **ٱللَّهِ**: a **noun**, the name of Allah. A preposition stands before it, and it ends in -i.
4. **ٱلَّذِى**, “the one who”: a **noun**, a joining noun. Its ٱل belongs to the word.
5. **خَلَقَ**, “he created”: a **verb** in the past.

Order: noun, little word and noun (joined in لِلَّهِ), noun, verb. *Al-Jadwal* reads the words the same way: the first is the starting noun, لِلَّهِ is a preposition with its noun, alladhī is a joining noun and khalaqa is a past verb.

</Exercise>

- Every Arabic word is a **noun** (ism), a **verb** (fiʿl) or a **little word** (ḥarf). It is in one box only.
- A **noun** has a sign: tanween, al-, the genitive ending, a preposition before it, or an oath letter before it. The box also holds adjectives, pronouns, “this” and “who”, and place words.
- A **verb** has a sign: qad, sa- or sawfa before it, or a silent tāʾ at the end. It names an action with a time.
- A **little word** has neither sign. It does a job: “in”, “and”, “indeed”, “not”, “except”.
- Split joined little words before you sort. Do not decide by length or by the letters alone.
