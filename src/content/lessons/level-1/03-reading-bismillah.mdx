This phrase is the first verse of Al-Fatiha. It is also written at the start of every other surah except At-Tawbah (surah 9). Muslims say it before many things they do.

The Arabic name for the phrase is **Bismillah**, or *basmala* (بَسْمَلَة). It has four words. Read them from right to left.

In this lesson you will read each word slowly. You already know every letter and every mark in it. The aim is to put them together without guessing.

<Note kind="know">Scholars differ on whether the Bismillah is a verse of every surah. Al-Jadwal lists both views. This course cites it as 1:1, the number the Tanzil text gives it.</Note>

</Block>

Every sound is a letter plus its mark. For each word, look at the letters from right to left, then at the mark on each one, then say them together. The Bismillah uses these marks:

| Mark | What it does | Where you meet it |
|---|---|---|
| kasra ـِ | a short “i” | bi, mi, hi, ni |
| sukūn ـْ | no vowel: the letter is said on its own | the س in بِسْمِ, the ح in ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ |
| shadda ـّ | the letter is said twice | the second ل in ٱللَّهِ, the ر in both names |
| dagger alif ـٰ | a small alif above the letter: a long “ā” | ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ |
| ي after a kasra | a long “ī” | ٱلرَّحِيمِ |
| alif waṣla ٱ | an alif that starts a word. Silent when the word joins the one before | ٱللَّهِ, ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ, ٱلرَّحِيمِ |

</Block>

<Step id="words-1-2" label="Words one and two" title="Bismi llāhi">
<Block id="bismi" title="Word one: بِسْمِ">
  <Stage label="Two parts, three letters">
    <Tiles
      label="بِ, the little word, and سْمِ, the noun"
      items={[
        { ar: 'بِ', en: 'bi: in, with', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'سْمِ', en: 'smi: name of' },
      ]}
    />

بِسْمِ is a little word and a noun joined together. **بِ** (*bi*) is a preposition, a little word like “in” or “with”. **سْمِ** is the noun *ism*, “name”. L2.07 teaches بِ in full.

Read the three letters one at a time:

| Letter | Mark | You say |
|---|---|---|
| ب | kasra | bi |
| س | sukūn | s, with no vowel |
| م | kasra | mi |

Say it slowly: *bi – s – mi*. The س has no vowel of its own, so it closes the first beat (syllable). You say *bis*, then *mi*.

<Note kind="warn">Do not put a vowel on the س. The word is *bis-mi*, not *bi-si-mi*.</Note>

<Check n={3} q="How do you say سْ with the sukūn?" options={['si', 's, with no vowel', 'sa']} answer={1} why="A sukūn means no vowel. The letter is said on its own, closing the beat before it." />
</Block>

The noun *ism* (name) is written ٱسْم. It starts with an alif waṣla. In this verse the alif is written after بِ: بِٱسْمِ. In the Bismillah it is not written: بِسْمِ.

The sound is the same in both places: *bismi*. The alif waṣla is silent when a word joins the one before it, so you never say it here. The Tanzil text of the Bismillah simply leaves it out.

<Note kind="warn">The missing alif has nothing to do with the -i ending. The -i comes from the preposition بِ (L2.07). The alif is a matter of spelling.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="join" title="Two words, one flow: bismi llāhi">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      reference="Al-Fatiha 1:1 (start)"
      groups={[
        { words: [{ ar: 'بِسْمِ', en: 'in the name of', say: 'bismi', type: 'p' }] },
        { words: [{ ar: 'ٱللَّهِ', en: 'Allah', say: 'llāhi' }] },
      ]}
      en={['In the name of Allah.']}
    />
  </Stage>

ٱللَّهِ starts with an alif waṣla. This alif has one rule:

- When you **start** your reading on the word, you say the alif: *Allāhi*.
- When the word **follows** another word, the alif is silent. The word joins the one before.

Here ٱللَّهِ follows بِسْمِ, so the alif is silent. The *mi* of *bismi* runs straight into the doubled *l*:

| You write | You say |
|---|---|
| بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ | bismi llāhi, said as *bis-mil-lā-hi* |

<Note kind="warn">Do not say *bismi allāhi*. In the middle of the phrase the alif waṣla is silent.</Note>

<Check n={4} q="How do you say the start of ٱللَّهِ after بِسْمِ?" options={['allāhi, with the alif', 'llāhi, without the alif', 'lāhi, with a single l']} answer={1} why="After another word the alif waṣla is silent. The doubled lām follows straight on from the mi of bismi." />
</Block>

<Block id="allah" title="Word two: ٱللَّهِ and its shadda">
  <Stage label="Four signs">
    <Tiles
      label="The pieces of ٱللَّهِ, read from right to left"
      items={[
        { ar: 'ٱ', en: 'alif waṣla: silent here', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'ل', en: 'lām of al-: joins the next lām', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'لَّ', en: 'llā', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'هِ', en: 'hi', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

| Letter | Mark | You say |
|---|---|---|
| ٱ | alif waṣla | nothing, after another word |
| ل | no mark | joins the next lām |
| ل | fatḥa and shadda | a doubled l, with an “a” |
| ه | kasra | hi |

There are two lāms. The first is the lām of *al-* (“the”). The second is the first letter of the name. Al-Jadwal says the two merge into one doubled lām, and the shadda on the second shows it. So you hold the *l* for two beats, as in the English word “bell”.

The name also has a long ā in the middle: *l-lā-hi*. This text writes only a fatḥa on the lām, so the long ā is not marked. You learn it with the name.

The word ends with a kasra. The preposition بِ acts on the noun right after it, ٱسْم. ٱللَّهِ is genitive for its own reason: it is the second word of an idafah, a pair of nouns meaning “the X *of* Y”. Here the pair is “the name *of Allah*”. L2.08 teaches this.

<Note kind="know">Scholars differ on where the name comes from. Al-Jadwal says it comes from *al-ilāh*, “the one who is worshipped”. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn lists several views and prefers that the name is a proper name, not derived from another word.</Note>

</Block>

<Step id="words-3-4" label="Words three and four" title="Ar-raḥmāni r-raḥīmi">
<Block id="sun" title="ٱلرَّ: the lām you do not say">
  <Stage label="Three signs">
    <Tiles
      label="The start of ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ, read from right to left"
      items={[
        { ar: 'ٱ', en: 'alif waṣla: silent here', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'ل', en: 'lām of al-: written, not said', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'رَّ', en: 'rra: a doubled r', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Both names start with ٱلرَّ. This is the word *al-* (“the”) followed by **ر**. The ر is a sun letter. A sun letter swallows the lām of *al-*. You still write the lām, but you do not say it. The ر is said twice instead, and the shadda on the ر shows this.

So after ٱللَّهِ you say *r-raḥmāni*, not *al-raḥmāni*. If you start your reading on the word, you say *ar-raḥmāni*.

Compare a word where the lām is said:

| Word | After ٱل comes | The lām | You say |
|---|---|---|---|
| ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ (1:1) | رَّ, with a shadda | written, not said | ar-raḥmāni |
| ٱلْحَمْدُ (1:2) | حْ, with a sukūn on the lām | said | al-ḥamdu |

The letter ح is not a sun letter, so the lām keeps its sukūn and you say it. L1.06 teaches the full list.

</Block>

  <Stage label="Four pieces after the article">
    <Tiles
      label="ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ without its article, read from right to left"
      items={[
        { ar: 'رَّ', en: 'rra', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'حْ', en: 'ḥ, no vowel', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'مَٰ', en: 'mā, long', type: 'x' },

| Letter | Mark | You say |
|---|---|---|
| ر | fatḥa and shadda | a doubled r, with an “a” |
| ح | sukūn | ḥ, with no vowel |
| م | fatḥa and a dagger alif | mā, a long ā |
| ن | kasra | ni |

The small alif above the م is the dagger alif. The fatḥa and the dagger alif together make a long ā: hold it for two beats. The full alif is not written in this word.

Say it in four beats: *ar – raḥ – mā – ni*. This one word has a shadda, a sukūn, a long ā and a kasra.

</Block>

<Block id="rahim" title="Word four: ٱلرَّحِيمِ letter by letter">
  <Stage label="Three pieces after the article">
    <Tiles
      label="ٱلرَّحِيمِ without its article, read from right to left"
      items={[
        { ar: 'رَّ', en: 'rra', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'حِي', en: 'ḥī, long', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'مِ', en: 'mi', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

| Letter | Mark | You say |
|---|---|---|
| ر | fatḥa and shadda | a doubled r, with an “a” |
| ح | kasra | ḥi |
| ي | no mark | makes the kasra a long ī |
| م | kasra | mi |

The ح here has a kasra, not a sukūn. The kasra and the ي together make a long ī (L1.02). So the ح belongs with the ī: *ar – ra – ḥī – mi*.

<Note kind="tip">Say the syllables as *ra-ḥī-mi*. If you say *raḥ-ī-mi*, you cut the long ī in two.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="compare" title="Two names, one root">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="The two names side by side"
      items={[
        { ar: 'ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ', en: 'the Most Kind' },
        { ar: 'ٱلرَّحِيمِ', en: 'the Most Merciful' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The two names start the same way, but they split after the ر:

| Part | ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ | ٱلرَّحِيمِ |
|---|---|---|
| the ح | sukūn | kasra |
| the long vowel | ā, from the dagger alif | ī, from the ي |
| the last letter | ن | م |

Both words come from the root **ر ح م**, which is about mercy (L1.05 teaches roots). Al-Jadwal gives each word its pattern. ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ follows the pattern *faʿlān*, and ٱلرَّحِيمِ follows *faʿīl*. Both are intense forms: they say “a lot of mercy”.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal says ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ is the stronger of the two. It is a name that belongs to Allah alone, and it comes first. ٱلرَّحِيمِ follows and adds the finer kinds of mercy.</Note>

<Check n={8} q="Which name has a sukūn on the ح?" options={['ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ', 'ٱلرَّحِيمِ']} answer={0} why="In ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ the ح has a sukūn. In ٱلرَّحِيمِ it has a kasra." />
</Block>

  <Stage label="Read it as one flow">

Each of the first three words ends in a short *-i*. Do not stop on it. Let it run into the next word. Said together, the phrase has ten beats:

*bis – mil – lā – hir – raḥ – mā – nir – ra – ḥī – mi*

The tiles above show where each word's sound lands in the flow. Notice how the last sound of one word starts the next beat: *hir*, *nir*.

<Note kind="know">When you stop at the end of the verse, you drop the last vowel and say *ar-ra-ḥīm*. This stop is called *waqf* in the books on reading the Quran aloud (tajwīd).</Note>

Read at a slow pace first. Get every mark right, then speed up.
</Block>

<Step id="mistakes" label="Mistakes and the same words elsewhere" title="Mistakes, and the same words elsewhere">
<Block id="mistake-shadda" title="Mistake 1: one l in Allah">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="ٱللَّهِ with its doubled lām"
      items={[
        { ar: 'ٱللَّهِ', en: 'llāhi: hold the l', type: 'x' },

Many beginners say *alāhi*, with a single *l*. The shadda says you must double it. Hold the *l* for a full beat before you go on to the *ā*.

<Note kind="tip">Say “bell” and hold the *l* at the end. Then say *bismi llāhi* and hold the same sound.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-wasla" title="Mistake 2: saying the alif waṣla in the middle">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="Two words that join"
      items={[
        { ar: 'بِسْمِ', en: 'bismi', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'ٱللَّهِ', en: 'llāhi, not allāhi', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

When you read *bismi llāhi*, you do not say *bismi allāhi*. The alif waṣla is silent because the word follows another word. 
The same goes for the other two names. After ٱللَّهِ, say *r-raḥmāni* and *r-raḥīmi*, with no alif sound in front.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-pause" title="Mistake 3: stopping between words">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="Three joins in the phrase"
      items={[
        { ar: 'بِسْمِ', en: 'bis-mil', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'ٱللَّهِ', en: 'lā-hir', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ', en: 'raḥ-mā-nir', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'ٱلرَّحِيمِ', en: 'ra-ḥī-mi', type: 'x' },

The Bismillah is one phrase. If you stop after each word, you tend to say each word as if it started a new phrase, and then the silent alif comes back.

Link the end of each word to the start of the next. Stop only at the end of the verse.
</Block>

ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ and ٱلرَّحِيمِ look alike. Check two things: the mark on the ح and the long vowel after it. A sukūn and a long ā mean ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ. A kasra and a long ī mean ٱلرَّحِيمِ.

<Block id="mistake-vowel" title="Mistake 5: adding a vowel to a sukūn">

<Block id="tied" title="What is بِسْمِ tied to? Two views">
  <Stage label="Two readings of one phrase">
    <Tiles
      label="First view: a noun is left out"
      items={[

    <Tiles
      label="Second view: a verb is left out"
      items={[
        { ghost: 'I begin', note: 'left out' },
        { ar: 'بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ', en: 'by the name of Allah, the Most Kind, the Most Merciful', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />

A preposition phrase belongs to a word (L2.07). In the Bismillah no such word is written. Scholars agree that a word is left out. They differ on which kind.

- **A noun** is the view of the Basran grammarians, and al-Jadwal follows it. The phrase بِسْمِ is the khabar, and the mubtadaʾ is left out: “My beginning is by the name of Allah.” This is a **nominal** sentence.
- **A verb** is the view of the Kufan grammarians, as al-Durr al-Maṣūn reports it. The left-out word is a verb: “I begin by the name of Allah.” This is a **verbal** sentence.

Al-Durr al-Maṣūn adds that the بِ here means asking for help: the speaker begins with Allah's help.

Pick one view and keep it. “My beginning is …” is a nominal sentence. “I begin …” is a verbal sentence. If you add a verb to a nominal sentence, the sentence has changed type, and you are no longer following the first view.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal gives the same two readings for the Bismillah in 27:30. It gives the left-out noun first and the left-out verb “I begin” as another way. L2.07 shows both views again in exercise 8.</Note>

<Exercise n={1} q="Break down the word بِسْمِ letter by letter. Which mark does each letter carry?">

- ب has a kasra: *bi*.
- س has a sukūn: *s*, with no vowel.
- م has a kasra: *mi*.

Together: *bis-mi*. The sukūn on the س closes the first beat.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Why does ٱللَّهِ have a shadda on the lām? What does it do to the sound?">

There are two lāms in the word. The first is the lām of *al-*, the second is the first letter of the name. The two merge, and the shadda on the second one shows that you say the lām twice. The sound is *l-lā-hi*, with the *l* held for two beats.

Without the shadda you would say a single *l*, and that is wrong.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Name every reading mark in ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ. Which letters have a sukūn? Which have a shadda? Where is the long vowel?">

- ٱ: alif waṣla. It is silent after another word.
- ل: the lām of *al-*. It is written, not said, because ر is a sun letter.
- رَّ: ر with a shadda and a fatḥa. It is said twice: *rra*.
- حْ: ح with a **sukūn**. It is said with no vowel: *ḥ*.
- مَٰ: م with a fatḥa and a **dagger alif**. This is the long ā: *mā*.
- نِ: ن with a kasra: *ni*.

Sound: *ar-raḥ-mā-ni*. The word has one sukūn, one shadda and one long ā.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="What is the difference between ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ and ٱلرَّحِيمِ when you read them?">

Both start with *ar-ra*. After that:

- ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ has a **sukūn** on the ح, then a **long ā**, then ن: *ar-raḥ-mā-ni*.
- ٱلرَّحِيمِ has a **kasra** on the ح, then a **long ī** from the ي, then م: *ar-ra-ḥī-mi*.

Both words come from the root ر ح م, about mercy. Al-Jadwal gives ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ the pattern *faʿlān* and ٱلرَّحِيمِ the pattern *faʿīl*, and says ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ is the stronger of the two.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Read this aloud and write the sounds: وَإِنَّهُۥ بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ (An-Naml 27:30, end). Which words are the same as in Al-Fatiha 1:1?">

*wa-innahu bismi llāhi r-raḥmāni r-raḥīmi.*

The last four words are the same as in 1:1: بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ. Only وَإِنَّهُۥ is new. You do not say the alif of ٱللَّهِ, because it follows بِسْمِ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Write the sounds of هُوَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ (Al-Hashr 59:22, end). What is different from the Bismillah?">

*huwa r-raḥmānu r-raḥīmu.*

The letters are the same. The endings are **-nu** and **-mu**, with a ḍamma, where the Bismillah has **-ni** and **-mi**, with a kasra. The ر is a sun letter again, so the lām of *al-* is silent. The alif waṣla is silent too, because ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ follows هُوَ.

At the end of the verse you may stop and say *r-raḥīm*, with no last vowel.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Which words of the Bismillah start with an alif waṣla? What do you do with it?">

Three words have a written alif waṣla: ٱللَّهِ, ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ and ٱلرَّحِيمِ. Each one follows another word, so the alif is silent: *bismi llāhi r-raḥmāni r-raḥīmi*.

The noun ٱسْم also starts with an alif waṣla, but the Tanzil text does not write it in بِسْمِ. It is silent there too.

If you started your reading on ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ, as you do at Al-Fatiha 1:3, you would say the alif: *ar-raḥmāni*.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Compare ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ (Al-Fatiha 1:1) with ٱلْحَمْدُ (Al-Fatiha 1:2). Why do you say the lām in one and not in the other?">

In ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ the letter after ال is ر, a sun letter. The lām is written but not said, and the ر has a shadda: *ar-raḥmāni*.

In ٱلْحَمْدُ the letter after ال is ح, which is not a sun letter. The lām has a sukūn and is said: *al-ḥamdu*.

The sukūn on the lām, or the shadda on the next letter, tells you which one you have.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Give the meaning of the Bismillah under each view of what بِسْمِ is tied to. Say whether the sentence is nominal or verbal.">

- **Left-out noun** (Basran, followed by al-Jadwal): “My beginning is by the name of Allah, the Most Kind, the Most Merciful.” A **nominal** sentence. The phrase is the khabar and “my beginning” is the mubtadaʾ.
- **Left-out verb** (Kufan, in al-Durr al-Maṣūn): “I begin by the name of Allah, the Most Kind, the Most Merciful.” A **verbal** sentence.

Either answer is correct if you name the view and keep to it.

</Exercise>

- The Bismillah is four words: *bismi llāhi r-raḥmāni r-raḥīmi*. It is written at the start of every surah except At-Tawbah.
- Each sound is a letter plus its mark. A sukūn means no vowel. A shadda means the letter is said twice. A dagger alif or a ي after a kasra makes a long vowel.

- The two names share the root ر ح م. ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ has a sukūn on the ح and a long ā. ٱلرَّحِيمِ has a kasra on the ح and a long ī.
- All four words end in -i, for different reasons: the preposition, the idafah “name of Allah”, and two adjectives that copy their noun. L2.07 to L2.09 teach each one.
- Scholars differ on what بِسْمِ is tied to: a left-out noun or a left-out verb.
