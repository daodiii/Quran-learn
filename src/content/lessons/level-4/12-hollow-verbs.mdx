Nuh is speaking about his people. Two verbs in this verse have a weak letter in the middle of the root.

The first is فَقُلْتُ, “so I said”. It ends in **-tu**, “I”, the ending you know from L3.05. The second is كَانَ, “he was”. Compare them. كَانَ has a long ā: كَ-ا-نَ. In قُلْتُ the ā is gone.

Both are Form I verbs with a three-letter root. L3.05 called them hollow and promised that this lesson would give the rules.

L3.04 gave a test for Form I: take away every letter that is not in the root. In قَالَ the ā is not a root letter. Only ق and ل are left, and a root has three letters. So the ā stands in for the missing one.

The present tells you which one. يَقُولُ has a **و**, so the root of قَالَ is ق و ل.

A verb whose middle root letter is و or ي is a **hollow verb**, فِعْل أَجْوَف (*fiʿl ajwaf*). The word means “with a hollow inside”: the middle letter hides behind a long vowel, and in some forms it drops out. The letters و and ي are two of the three **weak letters**, حُرُوف الْعِلَّة. The third is the alif.

Compare a sound verb with a hollow verb.

| Form | Sound: كَتَبَ | Hollow: قَالَ |
|---|---|---|
| Past “he” | كَتَبَ | قَالَ |
| Past “I” | كَتَبْتُ | قُلْتُ |
| Present “he” | يَكْتُبُ | يَقُولُ |
| Jazm “he” | يَكْتُبْ | يَقُلْ |
| Command | ٱكْتُبْ | قُلْ |

All three root letters show in every form of كَتَبَ. In قَالَ they do not: the middle letter is a long ā, a long ū or nothing at all. The rest of this lesson says which, and when.

<Note kind="know">The Quranic Arabic Corpus has 5,162 verb words (26.7% of its 19,356) from roots with a weak middle letter. This is the count of L3.05 and L4.11. 4,214 of them are Form I. This lesson leaves out the 89 words of لَيْسَ and 32 words from roots whose last letter is weak too (like هَوَى). That leaves **4,093** Form I words, and the counts below use those. Two verbs make up most of them: قَالَ has 1,618 words and كَانَ has 1,358, together 2,976 of the 4,093 (72.7%). These are Quran Learn's counts.</Note>

The three verbs قَالَ, زَادَ and خَافَ all look the same in the past: a long ā. The present tells them apart.

| Kind | Past “he” | Present “he” | Past, before a sukūn ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present with ū | قَالَ | يَقُولُ | قُلْتُ |
| Present with ī | زَادَ | يَزِيدُ | زِدْنَا |
| Present with ā | خَافَ | يَخَافُ | خِفْتُ |

The Quran has قُلْتُ in 71:10, يَزِيدُ in 35:1 and خِفْتُ in 19:5. The word زِدْنَا is in 18:13 with -hum: زِدْنَٰهُمْ, “we added to them”.

In the ū kind the root has a و: ق و ل. In the ī kind it has a ي: ز ي د. The ā kind has either one: خَافَ is خ و ف, and نَالَ, “he reached”, is ن ي ل.

The Corpus has 992 present words from Form I hollow verbs. 854 of them keep the long vowel in the stem: 553 with ū, 235 with ā and 66 with ī. The other 138 are cut short. The next steps show when.

These are the hollow verbs the Quran uses most, with their Form I words in the Corpus.

The verb is قَالَ (*qāla*), “he said”, root ق و ل. The table is the pattern of L3.05 with this verb.

Look at these five forms. The ending is added after the last root letter, the ل, and the ل keeps its own vowel: a fatḥa in قَالَ, قَالَتْ, قَالَا and قَالَتَا, and a ḍamma in قَالُوا. Nothing closes the syllable, so the **long ā stays**.

In 28:23 two women speak to Musa. قَالَتَا is “the two women said”. The ending -atā is the dual, as in L3.05, and the alif is the doer. All five forms of this table are in the Quran: the Corpus tags them as he, she, the two (men), the two (women) and they (men).

These eight forms have a **short stem**: قُل, not قَال. The ending starts with a letter that has its own vowel (-ta, -tu, -nā), or it is the ending -na. As in كَتَبْتُ (L3.05), the last root letter closes the syllable with a **sukūn**.

Now the long ā would stand in front of that sukūn, and the verb cuts it: قَال + ـْتُ gives قُلْتُ. Five long forms and eight short forms make the 13 forms of L3.05.

In 18:14 the young men in the cave say لَقَدْ قُلْنَآ. The verb is “we said”: the stem قُل and the ending -nā, which is the doer.

The Quran does not use every form. The Corpus has five of the eight short forms of قَالَ: قُلْنَ (12:31, with وَ), قُلْتَ (5:116), قُلْتُمْ (2:55), قُلْتُ (71:10) and قُلْنَا (20:68). The forms for “you (one woman)”, “you two” and “you all (women)” are not in the Quran for this verb. They are teaching forms.

Say قَالْتُمْ slowly. A long ā and then a letter with a sukūn are two silent sounds in a row. Arabic does not keep both, and the long vowel goes: قُلْتُمْ.

Look at the vowel on the ق. It is a ḍamma, and it comes from the و of the root. The short vowel that is left on the first letter shows which weak letter stood behind the ā.

Al-Jadwal explains قُلْتُمْ in 2:55 in these terms: the verb is built on a sukūn, the alif drops where two silent sounds meet, and the ḍamma on the first letter shows that the alif stood for a و. Al-Jadwal gives the same kind of reason for خِفْتُمْ (2:229), زِلْتُمْ (40:34) and ٱخْتَرْتُكَ (20:13).

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal explains each word one at a time. The one-line rule, “a long vowel is cut when a sukūn follows”, and the table of short vowels in the next blocks are Quran Learn's teaching summary of those notes. No approved book in this project states them as one rule.</Note>

To know whether the stem stays long, look at the **last root letter** of the verb.

- If it has a vowel, the long ā stays: قَالَ, قَالَتْ, قَالَا, قَالُوا.
- If it has a sukūn, the stem is short: قُلْتُ, قُلْنَا, قُلْنَ.

In 18:14 the same young men use both. The verbs قَامُوا and فَقَالُوا end in -ū, so the م and the ل have a ḍamma and the ā stays. Later in the verse قُلْنَآ has a sukūn on the ل, and the ā is cut. The verb قَالُوا in 36:52, the example of L3.03, is another long-stem verb.

The same test works for the present, the jussive and the command in the next step.

The command comes from the jazm form of the verb with the prefix taken away (L3.08): تَقُلْ gives قُلْ. So the same rule applies. Two forms end in a sukūn, and their stem is short: **قُلْ** for one man and **قُلْنَ** for women (33:32, وَقُلْنَ). The other three forms end in a vowel, so the stem stays long: قُولَا (20:44), قُولُوا۟ (2:104, 33:70) and قُولِىٓ (19:26). The command تُوبُوٓا۟ (11:3), from تَابَ يَتُوبُ, is the same.

In 21:69 the verse has both kinds of verb. قُلْنَا has a short stem, and كُونِى has a long ī in front of the ending -ī, “you (one woman)”. The fire is feminine in Arabic.

The ī kind and the ā kind work the same way. The ī kind gives زِدْ, as in رَبِّ زِدْنِى (20:114), and the ā kind gives خَفْ (a teaching form: the Quran has لَا تَخَفْ).

The Corpus has 425 commands from Form I hollow verbs. 353 are short (352 for one man and one for women), and 332 of the 352 are قُلْ. 72 keep the long vowel.

When a word that starts with ٱل comes next, the sukūn of قُلْ changes to a kasra: قُلِ ٱللَّهُمَّ (3:26).

L3.09 showed قِيلَ and said that this lesson gives the reason. The pattern of the passive is فُعِلَ. For قَالَ it would be قُوِلَ. The و is a weak letter and cannot carry the kasra. So the kasra moves to the first letter, and the و turns into ي. The result is **قِيلَ**. Al-Jadwal gives this explanation in its note on 2:11.

| Active | Passive | In the Quran |
|---|---|---|
| قَالَ | قِيلَ | قَدْ قِيلَ (41:43) |
| يَقُولُ | يُقَالُ | مَّا يُقَالُ (41:43) |
| سَاقَ | سِيقَ | وَسِيقَ (39:71) |

The present passive يُقَالُ comes from يُقْوَلُ by a similar move: the fatḥa goes to the ق, and the و becomes ā. This step is a teaching summary: al-Jadwal explains only قِيلَ in the verses of this lesson. In the Quran the passive of جَآءَ is written وَجِا۟ىٓءَ (39:69).

In 41:43 the doer is left out. Al-Jadwal reads the word مَا as the stand-in for the doer of يُقَالُ, and explains it as “the like of what was said”.

The Corpus has the passive of قَالَ 52 times: 49 in the past and 3 in the present (the count of L3.09).

The active participle of Form I has the pattern فَاعِل. For a hollow verb the weak letter would stand next to the long ā: قَاوِل. Instead it becomes a **hamza**, written on a ي seat: قَائِل. Al-Jadwal states this as a rule in its note on قَائِمًا (3:18): in the فَاعِل shape every hollow verb turns its weak letter into a hamza.

So قَامَ gives قَائِم, نَامَ gives نَائِم (7:97), فَازَ gives فَائِز (59:20) and قَالَ gives قَائِل (23:100). In Form IV the participle takes a ي instead: مُقِيمَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ (14:40), from أَقَامَ.

The Corpus has 91 active participles of Form I hollow verbs. 79 of them have this hamza. The other 12 are the form تَوَّاب, “one who turns back often”, from تَابَ.

The passive participle has the pattern مَفْعُول. For a hollow verb one و drops and the ū stays: مَقْوُول becomes مَقُول. In 17:29 the word مَلُومًا, “blamed”, comes from لَامَ (root ل و م). Al-Jadwal says it is like مَقُول. A ya root gives مَفِيل: مَكِيدُونَ (52:42), “those who are plotted against”, from كَادَ يَكِيدُ. Al-Jadwal says it is on the pattern of مَبِيع.

The short vowel depends on the kind of verb. خَافَ has a present with ā (يَخَافُ), so its past has a kasra: خِفْتُ. Look at the present first: ū gives a ḍamma, and ī or ā gives a kasra. A very few verbs break the table. مَاتَ has both مُتُّمْ and مِتُّ.

<Block id="mistake-root" title="Mistake 4: reading the root from the short stem">
  <Stage label="Two root letters show">

      label="قُلْتُ: the root is not ق ل ت"

In قُلْتُ only two root letters show, ق and ل, and the ت follows. The root looks like ق ل ت. The ت is the ending, “I”, and the root is ق و ل.

Put the verb back to “he”. قَالَ shows the long ā, and the present يَقُولُ shows the و. Do this whenever a verb has a short stem and you cannot see three root letters.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-at" title="Mistake 5: cutting the stem in -at">

<Exercise n={1} q="Which of these roots are hollow? If a root is hollow, say whether the middle letter is و or ي. (a) ق ت ل (b) ق و ل (c) س ي ر (d) ن ص ر (e) ك و ن (f) ع م ل (g) ب ي ع (h) ف ع ل">

- (a) Not hollow: all three letters are strong.
- (b) Hollow, middle **و**.
- (c) Hollow, middle **ي**.
- (d) Not hollow.
- (e) Hollow, middle **و**.
- (f) Not hollow.
- (g) Hollow, middle **ي**. The Quran has verbs from this root only in Forms III and VI.
- (h) Not hollow.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Build forms from the root ص و م, “fasting”: (a) the past “he” (b) the past “I” (c) the present “he” (d) jazm “he” (e) the command to one man. Then say which of these the Quran has.">

- (a) صَامَ (*ṣāma*): the long ā stays.
- (b) صُمْتُ (*ṣumtu*): the ā is cut, and the present has ū, so a ḍamma is left.
- (c) يَصُومُ (*yaṣūmu*).
- (d) يَصُمْ (*yaṣum*): the sukūn cuts the ū.
- (e) صُمْ (*ṣum*): the same cut.

The Quran has two forms of this root. They are تَصُومُوا۟ (2:184, the present with -ū) and فَلْيَصُمْهُ (2:185, jazm, with -hu). The past and the command are teaching forms. Al-Jadwal explains يَصُمْهُ in 2:185 as the weak letter dropped because of jazm.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Build forms from the root ق و م, “standing”: (a) the past “he” (b) the past “I” (c) the present “he” (d) the present “they (women)” (e) the command to one man">

- (a) قَامَ (*qāma*).
- (b) قُمْتُ (*qumtu*): the ā is cut. The present يَقُومُ has ū, so a ḍamma is left.
- (c) يَقُومُ (*yaqūmu*).
- (d) يَقُمْنَ (*yaqumna*): the ending -na puts a sukūn on the م. This is a teaching form.
- (e) قُمْ (*qum*). The Quran has قُمِ ٱلَّيْلَ (73:2), with a helper kasra in front of ٱل. Al-Jadwal says the weak letter dropped because two silent sounds meet.

The Quran has قُمْتُمْ (“you all stood”) in 5:6, so (b) is the same pattern with another ending.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Look at Al-Ikhlas. Which verbs are hollow? (a) قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ (Al-Ikhlas 112:1) (b) لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ (Al-Ikhlas 112:3) (c) وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ (Al-Ikhlas 112:4). For each hollow verb give the root, the kind and why it is short.">

- (a) **قُلْ** is hollow. The root is ق و ل, the kind is ū, and it is the command to one man. It ends in a sukūn, so the ū is cut.
- (b) **يَلِدْ** and **يُولَدْ** are not hollow. Their root is و ل د, and the weak letter is the first one. L4.14 teaches these verbs.
- (c) **يَكُن** is hollow. The root is ك و ن, the kind is ū, and the verb is in jazm after لَمْ. The sukūn cuts the ū. The Corpus tags it as kāna in the jussive.


</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Is the stem long or short? Say why, and who did it. (a) قَالَتْ (b) قُلْنَا (c) قَالُوا (d) قُلْتَ (e) كُنتُمْ (f) كَانَتْ">

- (a) **Long.** The ending -at has the sukūn on the ت, and the ل keeps a fatḥa. “She said.”
- (b) **Short.** -nā puts a sukūn on the ل. “We said.”
- (c) **Long.** The ل has a ḍamma in front of -ū. “They (men) said.”
- (d) **Short.** -ta puts a sukūn on the ل. “You (one man) said.”
- (e) **Short.** -tum puts a sukūn on the ن. “You all were.” The kind is ū, so the vowel is a ḍamma.
- (f) **Long.** Same as (a). “She was.”

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Write the past “I” (or “we”) of each verb. Look at the present first. (a) يَتُوبُ (b) يَزِيدُ (c) يَخَافُ (d) يَقُومُ (e) يَشَاءُ">

- (a) **تُبْتُ**: ū gives a ḍamma. The Quran has تُبْتُ in 4:18.
- (b) **زِدْتُ**: ī gives a kasra. The Quran has زِدْنَٰهُمْ in 18:13, “we added to them”, which is the same stem with -nā.
- (c) **خِفْتُ**: ā gives a kasra (19:5).
- (d) **قُمْتُ**: ū gives a ḍamma. The Quran has قُمْتُمْ in 5:6.
- (e) **شِئْتُ**: ā gives a kasra. The Quran has شِئْتَ in 7:155.

(b), (d) and (e) are teaching forms with another ending in the Quran.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Put each verb in jazm after لَمْ. (a) يَقُولُ (b) تَكُونُ (c) يَخَافُ (d) يَقُومُونَ (e) تَسِيرُونَ (f) يَكُونُ, in the shortest form">

- (a) **لَمْ يَقُلْ**: the ū is cut.
- (b) **لَمْ تَكُنْ**.
- (c) **لَمْ يَخَفْ**: the ā is cut and a fatḥa is left.
- (d) **لَمْ يَقُومُوا**: the nūn drops and -ū follows, so the long ū stays.
- (e) **لَمْ تَسِيرُوا**: the same. The Quran has لَمْ يَسِيرُوا۟ (12:109).
- (f) **لَمْ يَكُ**: the sukūn cuts the ū and the nūn drops as well (19:9, 8:53).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Write the command from the present. (a) تَقُولُ, to one man (b) تَقُولُونَ (c) تَقُولِينَ (d) تَكُونِينَ (e) تَقُومُ, to one man (f) تَخَافُ, to one man">

- (a) **قُلْ**.
- (b) **قُولُوا**: -ū follows, so the ū stays (2:104, 33:70).
- (c) **قُولِي**: -ī follows (19:26).
- (d) **كُونِي**: the Quran has كُونِى in 21:69, said to the fire.
- (e) **قُمْ**: the Quran has قُمِ in 73:2.
- (f) **خَفْ**: the ā is cut and a fatḥa is left. This is a teaching form. The Quran has the jazm form لَا تَخَفْ (20:68).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Passive and participles. Write (a) the passive past “he” of قَالَ (b) the passive past “he” of سَاقَ (c) the active participle of قَامَ (d) the active participle of نَامَ (e) the passive participle of لَامَ">

- (a) **قِيلَ**: the kasra moves to the ق and the و turns into ي (2:11, 41:43).
- (b) **سِيقَ**: the same change. The Quran has وَسِيقَ (39:71).
- (c) **قَائِم**: the و becomes a hamza (3:18).

- (e) **مَلُوم**: one و drops and the ū stays (17:29).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Find the hollow verbs in this verse. For each one say whether the stem is long or short, and why. (a) وَلَقَدْ جَآءَكُمْ يُوسُفُ مِن قَبْلُ بِٱلْبَيِّنَٰتِ فَمَا زِلْتُمْ فِى شَكٍّ (Ghafir 40:34, part) (b) إِذَا هَلَكَ قُلْتُمْ لَن يَبْعَثَ ٱللَّهُ (Ghafir 40:34, part)">

- **جَآءَكُمْ** (a): root ج ي ء, hollow with a hamza. The ending is “he”, so the stem is long. The -kum is the object.
- **زِلْتُمْ** (a): the Corpus root is ز ي ل, and the verb is of the ā kind (يَزَالُ). The ending -tum puts a sukūn on the ل, so the stem is short, with a kasra. Al-Jadwal says the weak letter is cut because the verb is built on a sukūn.
- **قُلْتُمْ** (b): root ق و ل. The same cut, with a ḍamma. “You said.”

The verb هَلَكَ is not hollow: its root ه ل ك is strong.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={11} q="Five of these forms are wrong. Say what is wrong and write the right form. (a) قَالْتُ (b) قُلُوا (c) خُفْتُ (d) قُلَتْ (e) لَمْ يَقُولْ">

- (a) **قُلْتُ**. The ā cannot stand in front of the sukūn on the ل.
- (b) **قَالُوا**. The ل has a ḍamma here, so nothing cuts the ā.
- (c) **خِفْتُ**. The present is يَخَافُ, so the vowel is a kasra.
- (d) **قَالَتْ**. The sukūn is on the ending ت, and the ل keeps its fatḥa.
- (e) **لَمْ يَقُلْ**. Jazm puts a sukūn on the ل, so the ū is cut.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={12} q="Say the root, the form and the kind of each word, and why the stem is long or short. (a) لَقَدْ كِدتَّ تَرْكَنُ (Al-Isra 17:74, part) (b) فَٱسْتَجَبْتُمْ لِى (Ibrahim 14:22, part) (c) وَهُمْ نَآئِمُونَ (Al-Aʿraf 7:97, part)">

- (b) **ٱسْتَجَبْتُمْ**: root ج و ب, Form X. The ā of ٱسْتَجَابَ is cut in front of -tum, and the vowel is a fatḥa, as in Forms IV, VIII and X.

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="a long vowel in front of a sukūn is cut short"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'قَال', small: 'the stem, with a long ā' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'ـْتُ', small: 'an ending that puts a sukūn on the ل' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'قُلْتُ', small: '“I said”: the ā is cut' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A **hollow verb** has و or ي in the middle of the root. The weak letter shows as a long vowel: ā in the past, and ū, ī or ā in the present. The present tells the kind.
- The long vowel is **cut** when a sukūn follows: the past with -ta, -tu, -nā, -na and the other consonant endings (قُلْتُ), the present with -na (يَقُلْنَ), jazm (لَمْ يَقُلْ) and the command (قُلْ).
- The short vowel that is left shows the weak letter: a ḍamma for ū, a kasra for ī, and a kasra for ā in the past (خِفْتُ) but a fatḥa in jazm (تَخَفْ). Forms IV, VIII and X keep a fatḥa.
- The long vowel **stays** in front of a vowel: قَالُوا, قَالَتْ, تَقُولُونَ, لَمْ يَسِيرُوا۟, قُولُوا۟.
- كَانَ can lose its nūn in jazm: لَمْ تَكُ.
- The passive is قِيلَ (the kasra moves, the و becomes ي). The active participle has a hamza, قَائِل, and the passive participle drops one و, مَلُوم.
- The iʿrāb line follows the ending: a past verb is built on a sukūn when the ending starts with a consonant (قُلْنَا), jazm is a sukūn (تَخَفْ), and the command is built on a sukūn (كُنْ).
