This is the middle of Al-Anfal 8:29. Read it from right to left.

It has two halves. **إِن تَتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ** says “if you are mindful of Allah”. **يَجْعَل لَّكُمْ فُرْقَانًا** says what follows: “he will give you a way to tell right from wrong”. English says the same with “if” and “will”.

Look at the two verbs. تَتَّقُوا۟ has lost the nūn of the plain present, تَتَّقُونَ. يَجْعَل ends without the ḍamma of the plain present, يَجْعَلُ. L3.07 taught this ending: it is **jazm**. In a conditional sentence the word إِنْ puts both verbs in jazm.

English has two halves in “If you help, he will help you”. Arabic grammar names them.

- **شَرْط** (*sharṭ*) is the condition, the “if” half. Its verb is the **فِعْل الشَّرْط** (*fiʿl ash-sharṭ*), “the verb of the condition”.
- **جَوَاب الشَّرْط** (*jawāb ash-sharṭ*) is “the answer to the condition”, the “then” half. Some books call it **جَزَاء** (*jazāʾ*), “result”.
- **أَدَاة الشَّرْط** (*adāt ash-sharṭ*) is the word of condition: the little word in front. In this lesson it is إِنْ. L4.04 teaches the other words.

Together they make a **جُمْلَة شَرْطِيَّة** (*jumla sharṭiyya*), a conditional sentence. The usual order is the word of condition, then the condition, then the answer.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal uses these same names: “the verb of the condition” and “the answer”. When an answer starts with فَ, he calls that فَ “the one that ties the answer” (الفاء الرابطة لجواب الشرط). Step 3 is about it.</Note>

</Block>

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah*, the short grammar book you met in L3.07, says there are eighteen words of jazm. إِنْ is on the list, with مَا and مَنْ.

The book does not say that a word of condition puts **two** verbs in jazm. It only names the words. The verses show the two verbs, and al-Jadwal reads both of them in jazm.

Two little words look the same when the vowel marks are missing.

- **إِنْ** has a sukūn on the nūn. It means “if”, and a verb follows it.
- **إِنَّ** has a shadda. It means “indeed”, and a noun follows it. L2.10 taught this word.

In 31:8 the verbs ءَامَنُوا۟ and وَعَمِلُوا۟ come after ٱلَّذِينَ, not after إِنَّ. They tell who “those who” are. The sentence is a plain statement. It has no condition and no answer, so nothing is in jazm.

The next word is a past verb in 384 of them, a present verb tagged jussive in 116 and another present verb in 39. In 31 it is a negative word, in 6 a noun, and in 2 something else. Some tags are unreliable and this was not checked verse by verse.</Note>

In 64:17 the two verbs show jazm in two different ways.

- **تُقْرِضُوا۟** is the verb of the condition. The plain present is تُقْرِضُونَ. The nūn is gone.
- **يُضَٰعِفْهُ** is the answer. The plain present is يُضَٰعِفُ, with a ḍamma. Now the ف has a sukūn, and the attached **هُ**, “it”, follows.

These are the signs from L3.07. Here they are again on the verb يَكْتُبُ, used only as a pattern.

| Plain present | In jazm | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| يَكْتُبُ | يَكْتُبْ | the ḍamma becomes a sukūn |
| تَكْتُبُونَ | تَكْتُبُوا۟ | the nūn is gone |
| يَكْتُبَانِ | يَكْتُبَا | the nūn is gone |
| تَكْتُبِينَ | تَكْتُبِي | the nūn is gone |
| تَكْتُبْنَ | تَكْتُبْنَ | nothing: it already ends in a sukūn, and the nūn is the doer |

Every form for one person takes the sukūn: he, she, you (one man), I and we. In this lesson you will see يَسْرِقْ (he), تَكُ (she), تَجْهَرْ (you, 20:7) and نَعُدْ (we, 8:19).

One condition can have several answers. In 8:29 there are three. The first is يَجْعَل. The next two are joined to it with وَ: **يُكَفِّرْ** and **يَغْفِرْ**.

A verb joined with “and” to the answer takes the same ending as the answer. So both stay in jazm, and both end in a sukūn. Al-Jadwal reads them as “joined to” يَجْعَل.

</Block>

In 4:78 the verb of the condition comes first and its doer comes after it, as in any verbal sentence (L2.03). تُصِبْهُمْ is “it befalls them”. The doer is **حَسَنَةٌ**, “something good”. The verb is feminine because the doer is. The **-hum** is the object.

The verb shows jazm by a sukūn on the ب. The plain present is تُصِيبُ, so the long ī is gone as well. L4.12 teaches why.

The answer is يَقُولُوا۟, “they say”, and it lost its nūn. The words after it, هَٰذِهِۦ مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ, are what they say. The next part of the verse repeats the pattern with سَيِّئَةٌ, “something bad”.

A past verb can follow إِنْ too. The Corpus count in step 1 shows it is the more common choice: the word after إِنْ is a past verb 384 times and a present verb in jazm 116 times.

In 17:7 both the verb of the condition and the answer are past: أَحْسَنتُمْ … أَحْسَنتُمْ. In 17:8 they are عُدتُّمْ … عُدْنَا. A past verb never changes its ending (L3.05: it is built). So you cannot see the jazm. The books say the verb is **in the place of jazm** (فِي مَحَلِّ جَزْم). Al-Jadwal writes this for the verb of the condition, and says the second أَحْسَنتُمْ is “like the first”.

Compare 17:8 with 8:19. The verb is the same, عَادَ, “return”, and the English is the same. In 8:19 the verbs are present, so the jazm shows: تَعُودُوا۟ lost its nūn and نَعُدْ has a sukūn. In 17:8 the verbs are past, so it does not.

In these verses the English is “if you do”, not “if you did”. The action has not happened yet.

كَانَ often opens the condition: إِن كُنتُمْ, “if you are”. كُنتُمْ is a past verb, so it shows no jazm. It is built on a sukūn and stands in the place of jazm. Al-Jadwal reads it that way in 3:31.

Then comes the khabar of kāna (L2.11). In 3:31 the khabar is a verb: تُحِبُّونَ, “you love”. This is **not** the verb of the condition. It is the khabar of كُنتُمْ, so it keeps the nūn of the plain present.

The answer is a command, فَٱتَّبِعُونِى, tied with فَ. After it comes يُحْبِبْكُمُ, in jazm. Al-Jadwal reads it as the answer to the command: it is as if the verse said “if you follow me, Allah will love you”.

<Step id="fa" label="The answer and فَ" title="The answer, and the فَ that ties it">
<Block id="fa-why" title="When does the answer need a فَ?">

    <Tiles
      label="One shows jazm. One cannot."
      items={[
        { ar: 'يَنصُرْكُمْ', en: 'a verb in jazm: no فَ (47:7)', type: 'v' },

So far the answer was a verb that shows jazm, or a past verb. Nothing else is needed to link it to the condition.

Some answers cannot show jazm. A command has no jazm form. A noun sentence has no verb to cut. Then the answer starts with **فَ**. Al-Jadwal calls it “the one that ties the answer”, الفاء الرابطة.

In the verses of this lesson al-Jadwal gives the answer sentence a different place in the two cases. With a فَ the whole answer sentence stands **in the place of jazm**. Without a فَ it has no place of its own (لا محل لها).

The condition usually comes first. In 39:13 it comes later: “I fear the punishment of a great day, if I disobey my Lord.”

The verb of the condition is عَصَيْتُ, a past verb in the place of jazm. The answer is not written after it. Al-Jadwal says the answer is left out and the words before إِنْ point to it, so the sentence needs no second answer.

<Note kind="know">Some grammarians read it differently: they call the earlier words the answer itself, put first. This lesson follows al-Jadwal.</Note>

Almost every answer so far showed jazm, or sat in a place that hid it. In 3:120 the answer verb يَضُرُّكُمْ ends in a ḍamma, the ending of the plain present.

Al-Jadwal explains it with a فَ that is understood but not written: “then their scheming will not harm you”. After a فَ the verb takes the plain ending, as in 9:28.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal says this reading is the one of al-Mubarrad. Sibawayh read it differently: the verb only points to an answer that is put first in meaning, so it keeps its plain ending. This lesson follows al-Jadwal’s first reading.</Note>

So an answer with a plain ending is not always a mistake. When you write your own answer without a فَ, use jazm.

<Step id="irab" label="Mistakes, word by word" title="Five mistakes, and four verses word by word">
<Block id="mistake-nun" title="Mistake 1: looking for a lost nūn on every verb">
  <Stage label="Three verbs after إِنْ">
    <Tiles
      label="Only one of them is cut"
      items={[
        { ar: 'تَتَّقُوا۟', en: 'verb of the condition: the nūn is gone', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'كُنتُمْ', en: 'past verb: no change to see', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'تُحِبُّونَ', en: 'khabar of kāna: the nūn stays', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

After إِنْ you do not always find a lost nūn. There are three reasons.

- The verb is **past**, like كُنتُمْ in 2:23 and 3:31. It cannot change, so it stands in the place of jazm.
- The verb is the **khabar of kāna**, like تُحِبُّونَ in 3:31. It is not the verb of the condition.
- The verb comes after a **فَ**, like يُغْنِيكُمُ in 9:28. It is an ordinary sentence.

Find the verb of the condition first, then look at its ending.

<Check n={19} q="In إِن كُنتُمْ تُحِبُّونَ ٱللَّهَ, why does تُحِبُّونَ keep its nūn?" options={['It is the khabar of kāna, not the verb of the condition', 'It is a past verb', 'The nūn is the doer']} answer={0} why="كُنتُمْ is the verb of the condition. تُحِبُّونَ is its khabar, and only the verb of the condition and the answer are put in jazm." />
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-fa" title="Mistake 2: leaving out the فَ">
  <Stage label="Which one is right?">
    <Tiles
      label="The فَ is part of the answer"
      items={[
        { ar: 'فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ', en: 'the verse (8:19 and 2:271)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'هُوَ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ', en: 'made up: no فَ', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

When the answer is a command, a noun sentence or starts with قَدْ, سَوْفَ or لَن, it needs the فَ. Without it the answer is not tied to the condition.

The فَ is easy to skip because English does not need a word there. In English the answer can follow straight after: “if you stop, it is better for you”. Arabic ties it: وَإِن تَنتَهُوا۟ فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ (8:19).

<Note kind="warn">Not every فَ near a condition is the tying فَ. The فَ in front of إِنْ, as in فَإِنْ, “so if”, joins the sentence to the one before it. Al-Jadwal reads it that way in 3:20. The tying فَ is the first word of the answer.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-in" title="Mistake 3: reading every إن as “if”">
  <Stage label="One spelling, three words">
    <Tiles
      label="إِنْ, إِنَّ and a third إِنْ"
      items={[
        { ar: 'إِنْ', en: 'if: a verb follows', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'إِنَّ', en: 'indeed: a noun follows', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'إِنْ', en: 'not: a noun follows, and إِلَّا later', type: 'p' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

You met إِنْ and إِنَّ in the first step. There is a third word that is spelled like إِنْ: the negative إِنْ, “not”. L4.04 teaches it.

It appears in the verse of Exercise 7. Right after the condition فَإِن تَوَلَّيْتُمْ comes إِنْ أَجْرِىَ إِلَّا عَلَى ٱللَّهِ, “my reward is only from Allah”. Al-Jadwal calls this إِنْ a negative word. It is not a condition: nothing follows it that needs an answer.

To tell them apart, look at the vowel marks, then at the next word. A verb after إِنْ means “if”. A noun after إِنَّ means “indeed”.

<Check n={20} q="Which little word has a shadda?" options={['إِنْ, “if”', 'إِنَّ, “indeed”']} answer={1} why="إِنَّ has a shadda on the nūn. إِنْ has a sukūn." />
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-words" title="Mistake 4: thinking every “if” word cuts the verbs">
  <Stage label="Words of condition">
    <Tiles
      label="Some cut the verbs, and some do not"
      items={[
        { ar: 'إِنْ', en: 'cuts both verbs', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'مَن', en: 'cuts both verbs (29:6, 48:17)', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'لَوْ', en: 'does not cut (8:19)', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'إِذَا', en: 'does not cut (5:6)', type: 'p' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Arabic has more than one word of condition. This lesson uses إِنْ, with مَن in 29:6. They put the verbs in jazm.

Other words do not. In 8:19, after the conditions with إِنْ, the verse says وَلَوْ كَثُرَتْ, “even if it is many”. Al-Jadwal calls لَوْ here a word of condition that does not cut. In 5:6 he reads إِذَا as a word for future time that carries the meaning of a condition. Neither puts a verb in jazm there.

In 48:17, وَمَن يُطِعِ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥ يُدْخِلْهُ جَنَّٰتٍ, the word مَن does cut: يُطِعِ and يُدْخِلْهُ are both in jazm.

So do not expect jazm after every “if”. L4.04 gives the full list of the words and what each one does.

</Block>

<Block id="mistake-own" title="Mistake 5: forcing jazm onto your own answer">
  <Stage label="Made-up examples">
    <Tiles
      label="Made-up example: two good answers"
      items={[
        { ar: 'إِن تَدْرُسُوا۟ تَنْجَحُوا۟', en: 'jazm: the nūn is gone from both', type: 'v' },

      ]}

Jazm shows on an answer only when the answer can carry it. An answer that starts with فَ keeps the plain ending, as in 9:28. An answer without فَ shows jazm when it can.

When you write your own answer, choose one of the two shapes above. Do not put a فَ in front of a verb in jazm, and do not leave a plain ending on an answer that has no فَ.

For the real verses that do not fit, see 3:120 in step 3.
</Block>

        { at: [6, 7], role: '“before”: min and a time word. It belongs to the verb.', case: 'قَبْلُ has a fixed ending, a ḍamma; it stands in the place of the genitive', mark: 'a ḍamma', why: 'It says when the brother stole.', irab: 'جار ومجرور، وقبل اسم ظرفي مبني على الضم في محل جر متعلق بسرق' },
        { at: [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7], role: 'the whole answer: فَقَدْ سَرَقَ أَخٌ لَّهُۥ مِن قَبْلُ', why: 'It starts with فَ, so it stands in the place of jazm.', irab: 'جملة قد سرق أخ في محل جزم جواب الشرط مقترنة بالفاء' },

  </Stage>

This is the speech of Yusuf's brothers about Binyamin. The condition has a verb that shows its jazm. The answer is a past verb, so it needs قَدْ and the فَ.

Compare this verse with 17:7. There the past answer needed no فَ. Here it has قَدْ, so it does.

<Step id="practice" label="Practice" title="Practice">
<Block id="practice-1" title="Practice: find the parts">
  <Stage label="For each sentence, say">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Word:</b> which word of condition?</li>
      <li><b>Condition:</b> which verb, and how does it show jazm?</li>
      <li><b>Answer:</b> where does it start, and is there a فَ?</li>
      <li><b>Hidden:</b> is a past verb in the place of jazm?</li>
    </ol>

Work out each answer first, on paper or in your head. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Find the three parts in وَإِن تُطِيعُوهُ تَهْتَدُوا۟ (An-Nur 24:54, part): the word of condition, the condition and the answer. Say how each verb shows jazm.">

- The word of condition is **وَإِن**, “and if”.
- The condition is **تُطِيعُوهُ**, “you obey him”. The plain present is تُطِيعُونَهُ. The nūn is gone, and -hu is the object.
- The answer is **تَهْتَدُوا۟**, “you will be guided”. The plain present is تَهْتَدُونَ. The nūn is gone here too.

The meaning is “and if you obey him, you will be guided”. There is no فَ, because the answer shows jazm.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Look at إِن تُقْرِضُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ قَرْضًا حَسَنًا يُضَٰعِفْهُ لَكُمْ (At-Taghabun 64:17, part). Which word is the verb of the condition and which is the answer? How does each show jazm?">

The verb of the condition is **تُقْرِضُوا۟**. It shows jazm by the lost nūn (the plain present is تُقْرِضُونَ).

The answer is **يُضَٰعِفْهُ**. It shows jazm by the sukūn on the ف (the plain present is يُضَٰعِفُ), and the attached -hu, “it”, follows. The word of condition is **إِن**.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Analyse إِن تَجْتَنِبُوا۟ كَبَآئِرَ مَا تُنْهَوْنَ عَنْهُ نُكَفِّرْ عَنكُمْ سَيِّـَٔاتِكُمْ (An-Nisa 4:31, start). Find the word of condition, the verb of the condition and the answer. Give the root, the form and the sign of jazm for each verb.">

- The word of condition is **إِن**.
- The verb of the condition is **تَجْتَنِبُوا۟**, “you all keep away from”. Root ج ن ب, Form VIII (L3.14). The nūn is gone (the plain present is تَجْتَنِبُونَ).
- Its object is كَبَآئِرَ مَا تُنْهَوْنَ عَنْهُ, “the great sins that you are forbidden”. The verb تُنْهَوْنَ keeps its nūn. It is a passive verb (L3.09) inside the clause that completes مَا (L3.11), so it is not cut.
- The answer is **نُكَفِّرْ**, “we will wipe out”. Root ك ف ر, Form II (L3.12). The sukūn is on the ر (the plain present is نُكَفِّرُ). The doer is “we”.

The verse goes on with وَنُدْخِلْكُم, “and we will bring you in”. It is joined to the answer with وَ and is also in jazm: a sukūn on the ل, from Form IV.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Made-up example. Finish the sentence with an answer in jazm: إِن تَدْرُسُوا۟ بِٱجْتِهَادٍ … (“If you study hard, …”). Then write a second answer that needs a فَ.">

A good answer in jazm is **تَنْجَحُوا۟**, “you will succeed”. The plain present is تَنْجَحُونَ, so the nūn is gone: إِن تَدْرُسُوا۟ بِٱجْتِهَادٍ تَنْجَحُوا۟. The verb of the condition, تَدْرُسُوا۟, has lost its nūn too.

An answer with a فَ can be a noun sentence, such as **فَٱلنَّجَاحُ لَكُمْ**, “then success is yours”. It can also be a command, such as **فَٱسْتَمِرُّوا۟**, “then keep going”. Neither could show jazm, so each starts with فَ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Say whether each verb is past, in jazm, or has a plain ending: (a) كُنتُمْ (Al-Baqarah 2:23) (b) تُحِبُّونَ (Al-Imran 3:31) (c) يُحْبِبْكُمُ (Al-Imran 3:31) (d) عُدْنَا (Al-Isra 17:8) (e) نَعُدْ (Al-Anfal 8:19) (f) يَضُرُّكُمْ (Al-Imran 3:120)">

- (a) **past**, built on a sukūn, in the place of jazm: it is the verb of the condition.
- (b) **plain ending**: the nūn stays, because it is the khabar of kāna.
- (c) **in jazm**: the answer to the command فَٱتَّبِعُونِى, with a sukūn on the last ب.
- (d) **past**, “we returned”, built on a sukūn: the answer, with its jazm hidden.
- (e) **in jazm**: a sukūn on the د, in the answer.
- (f) **plain ending**: a ḍamma. Al-Jadwal reads an unwritten فَ before it.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Block id="practice-2" title="Practice: the فَ, the verses and the lines">
  <Stage label="When the answer needs a فَ">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>A command:</b> فَٱطَّهَّرُوا۟</li>
      <li><b>A noun sentence:</b> فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ</li>
      <li><b>قَدْ, سَوْفَ, لَن:</b> فَقَدْ, فَسَوْفَ, فَلَن</li>
      <li><b>Starts with إِنَّ or مَا:</b> فَإِنَّ, فَمَا</li>
      <li><b>No فَ:</b> a verb in jazm, or a plain past verb</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

<Exercise n={6} q="Does the answer need a فَ? (a) يَنصُرْكُمْ (Muhammad 47:7) (b) هُوَ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ (Al-Baqarah 2:271) (c) أَحْسَنتُمْ (Al-Isra 17:7) (d) ٱطَّهَّرُوا۟ (Al-Maidah 5:6) (e) لَن يَغْفِرَ (At-Tawbah 9:80) (f) نَعُدْ (Al-Anfal 8:19)">

- (a) **No.** A verb in jazm shows the link.
- (b) **Yes.** A noun sentence: the verse has فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ.
- (c) **No.** A plain past verb needs no tie.
- (d) **Yes.** A command: the verse has فَٱطَّهَّرُوا۟.
- (e) **Yes.** After لَن: the verse has فَلَن يَغْفِرَ.
- (f) **No.** A verb in jazm.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Look at فَإِن تَوَلَّيْتُمْ فَمَا سَأَلْتُكُم مِّنْ أَجْرٍ (Yunus 10:72, part). Find the verb of the condition and the answer. What is the tense of the verb of the condition? Why does the answer start with فَ?">

The word of condition is **إِن**. The verb of the condition is **تَوَلَّيْتُمْ**, “you turned away” (Form V). It is a **past** verb, so it is built on a sukūn and stands in the place of jazm.

The answer is **مَا سَأَلْتُكُم مِّنْ أَجْرٍ**, “I have not asked you for any reward”. It starts with the negative مَا and a past verb. Neither can show jazm, so the فَ ties it. Al-Jadwal marks it as the فَ of the answer.

The verse goes on with إِنْ أَجْرِىَ إِلَّا عَلَى ٱللَّهِ. That إِنْ means “not” (see Mistake 3).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Find the two conditions and their answers in فَإِن تُطِيعُوا۟ يُؤْتِكُمُ ٱللَّهُ أَجْرًا حَسَنًا وَإِن تَتَوَلَّوْا۟ كَمَا تَوَلَّيْتُم مِّن قَبْلُ يُعَذِّبْكُمْ عَذَابًا أَلِيمًا (Al-Fath 48:16, part). Say how each verb shows jazm.">

- First condition: **تُطِيعُوا۟**, “you obey”. The nūn is gone. Its answer is **يُؤْتِكُمُ ٱللَّهُ أَجْرًا حَسَنًا**, “Allah will give you a good reward”. The verb يُؤْتِكُمُ shows jazm by the dropped final yā of يُؤْتِي.
- Second condition: **تَتَوَلَّوْا۟**, “you turn away”. The nūn is gone. Its answer is **يُعَذِّبْكُمْ عَذَابًا أَلِيمًا**, “he will punish you with a painful punishment”. The verb يُعَذِّبْكُمْ has a sukūn on the ب.

Inside the second condition, كَمَا تَوَلَّيْتُم مِّن قَبْلُ means “as you turned away before”. Its verb, تَوَلَّيْتُم, is past and belongs to that phrase, not to the condition.

Neither answer has a فَ, because both show jazm.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Which one is it: “if”, “indeed” or “not”? Say what follows each. (a) إِن تَنصُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ (Muhammad 47:7) (b) إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ (Luqman 31:8) (c) إِنْ أَجْرِىَ إِلَّا عَلَى ٱللَّهِ (Yunus 10:72)">

- (a) **“If”.** A verb follows, تَنصُرُوا۟, and it has lost its nūn.
- (b) **“Indeed”.** A noun follows: ٱلَّذِينَ. It has a shadda.
- (c) **“Not”.** A noun follows, أَجْرِىَ, and later إِلَّا. The meaning is “my reward is only from Allah”. L4.04 teaches this إِنْ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Write the iʿrāb line, as in the cards, for: (a) كُنتُمْ (Al-Baqarah 2:23) (b) يَقُولُوا۟ (An-Nisa 4:78) (c) the second أَحْسَنتُمْ (Al-Isra 17:7)">

- (a) فعل ماض ناقص مبني على السكون في محل جزم فعل الشرط، والتاء اسم كان
- (b) فعل مضارع مجزوم جواب الشرط وعلامة الجزم حذف النون، والواو فاعل
- (c) فعل ماض مبني على السكون في محل جزم جواب الشرط، والتاء فاعل

The line names the sound the verb is built on or the sign of jazm, the part it plays (verb of the condition or answer), and its doer.

</Exercise>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="condition plus answer equals a conditional sentence"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'condition', small: 'the “if” half' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'answer', small: 'the “then” half' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'conditional sentence', small: 'جُمْلَة شَرْطِيَّة' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A conditional sentence has a **word of condition**, a **condition** and an **answer**: إِن تَتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ يَجْعَل لَّكُمْ.
- With إِنْ both verbs take **jazm** when they can show it: a lost nūn, a sukūn or a dropped weak letter. Answers joined with وَ stay in jazm.
- A **past verb** cannot show jazm. The books say it is **in the place of jazm**: كُنتُمْ, أَحْسَنتُمْ.
- An answer that cannot show jazm starts with **فَ**: a command, a noun sentence, or a sentence with قَدْ, سَوْفَ or لَن.
- Look for the **verb of the condition** first. The khabar of kāna keeps its nūn (3:31).
- إِنْ “if” has a sukūn and a verb after it. إِنَّ “indeed” has a shadda and a noun after it.
