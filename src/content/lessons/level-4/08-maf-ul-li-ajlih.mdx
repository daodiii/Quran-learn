The verse describes people caught in a storm with darkness, thunder and lightning. Read the line from right to left.

يَجْعَلُونَ means “they put” and أَصَٰبِعَهُمْ means “their fingers”. The line already says what they do and where. One question is left: why?

The verse answers it twice. مِّنَ ٱلصَّوَٰعِقِ says “because of the thunderbolts”. حَذَرَ ٱلْمَوْتِ says “for fear of death”. The word حَذَرَ ends in -a and gives the reason. It is a **mafʿūl li-ajlih** (مَفْعُول لِأَجْلِهِ).

L2.05 showed this verse and said Level 4 would teach the role.

<Block id="name" title="What the grammar book says">
  <Stage label="From al-Ajurrumiyyah">

      <p>قَامَ زَيْدٌ إِجْلَالًا لِعَمْرٍو</p>
      <p>وَقَصَدْتُكَ ابْتِغَاءَ مَعْرُوفِكَ</p>
    </blockquote>
    <p class="lx-quote-note">Vowel marks added by Quran Learn.</p>

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* has a chapter with this name. It says the mafʿūl li-ajlih is the **accusative noun** that is mentioned to make clear the **reason** (سَبَب) the action happened.

The book gives two examples.

- قَامَ زَيْدٌ إِجْلَالًا لِعَمْرٍو: “Zayd stood up to honour ʿAmr.” إِجْلَالًا is the reason.
- قَصَدْتُكَ ابْتِغَاءَ مَعْرُوفِكَ: “I came to you, seeking your kindness.” ابْتِغَاءَ is the reason.

The role has three names. Al-Jadwal and the book use **mafʿūl li-ajlih**. *Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* writes **mafʿūl min ajlihi** (in its notes on 2:19 and 2:265) and **mafʿūl lahu** (on 21:107). All three mean the same role.

</Block>

Look at the three reasons. They share three marks.

- **A noun in naṣb.** Each ends in -a (the first word of an idafah) or -an (a bare word with tanwīn). L2.05 taught these endings.
- **A verbal noun.** حَذَرَ names the action of being careful, ٱبْتِغَآء names seeking, حَسَد names envying. L3.20 taught that a verbal noun is a noun, so it takes a case. The Corpus tags حَذَرَ and ٱبْتِغَآءَ as verbal nouns. Al-Jadwal calls حَسَدًا a maṣdar.
- **It answers “why?”.** It does not answer “what?”, “where?” or “how?”.

<Note kind="tip">Ask the question. After the verb and its doer, ask “why did they do it?”. If a verbal noun in naṣb answers, you have found a mafʿūl li-ajlih.</Note>

The verse says that many of the People of the Book wish to turn the believers back into disbelievers. Ask who does each thing. Who wishes? Who would do the turning? Who feels the envy? The same people. One group is the doer of the verb and the owner of the feeling.

That is the first condition that grammar teachers give for a mafʿūl li-ajlih: **the reason belongs to the doer of the verb**. The book’s own example fits it: Zayd stands, and Zayd honours ʿAmr.

Al-Jadwal says this plainly in a note on 30:24: the doer of the verb and the doer of the reason must be the same. *Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* says it in its note on 13:12. For 2:109, al-Jadwal says the verb that works on حَسَدًا is يَرُدُّونَ (or وَدَّ, earlier in the verse). Both verbs have the same doers.

This verse forbids killing children and names the reason people might give: fear. خَشْيَةَ is the feeling and ends in -a. إِمْلَٰقٍ is what is feared. It is the second word of the idafah, so it is genitive. The Corpus tags it as a Form IV verbal noun. It tags خَشْيَةَ only as a noun, from the root خ ش ي, the root of the verb خَشِيَ, “he feared”.

Collect the reasons so far: fear of death, seeking Allah’s pleasure, envy, fear of poverty. Each is something that happens inside the person. Both examples in *al-Ajurrumiyyah* are the same kind: honouring and seeking.

Teachers add two conditions to the doer. The reason is a **feeling or a wish** of the doer, a “heart action” (فِعْل قَلْبِيّ). And it is there **at the same time** as the action: the fear is present while the children are in danger.

<Note kind="know">The feeling and the same time are a teaching summary. Quran Learn could not confirm them in *al-Ajurrumiyyah*, al-Jadwal or al-Durr, which only name the same doer. Use them as a tendency: the reasons in this lesson are all feelings and wishes.</Note>

The reason can look three ways.

- **A bare word with tanwīn.** بَغْيًا ends in -an. حَسَدًا (2:109) is the same.
- **The first word of an idafah.** حَذَرَ ٱلْمَوْتِ (2:19), خَشْيَةَ إِمْلَٰقٍ (17:31) and ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ ٱللَّهِ (2:207). The first word ends in -a and has **no tanwīn**. The second word is genitive.
- **Two reasons joined by وَ.** بَغْيًا وَعَدْوًا. Al-Jadwal reads عَدْوًا as joined to بَغْيًا, so it is a mafʿūl li-ajlih too.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal gives a second reading of بَغْيًا in a note: a verbal noun standing for a ḥāl, “being unjust”. This lesson follows its main reading, the reason.</Note>

A line can give more than one reason. Here the people spend for two reasons: ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ ٱللَّهِ and وَتَثْبِيتًا. Al-Jadwal reads the first as a mafʿūl li-ajlih and the second as joined to it. تَثْبِيتًا is a Form II verbal noun on the pattern تَفْعِيل (L3.12, L3.20). Al-Jadwal says the phrase مِّنْ أَنفُسِهِمْ describes it: a steadying that comes from themselves.

*Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* says the same about 2:19: two reasons do no harm, because a verb can be given more than one.

Look at the first word. The Corpus has ٱبْتِغَآء 14 times. It is accusative 12 times: in 2:207, 2:265, 2:272, 3:7 (twice), 4:114, 13:17, 13:22, 17:28, 57:27, 60:1 and 92:20. It is genitive once (4:104, after فِى) and nominative once (30:23). So the word usually stands in naṣb, but its job is read from the sentence.

<Note kind="know">Both words have a second reading in al-Jadwal and in *al-Durr al-Maṣūn*: a ḥāl, “seeking” and “making firm”. *Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* also records that Ibn ʿAṭiyya refused the reason reading for ٱبْتِغَآءَ. He thought the joined word تَثْبِيتًا cannot be a reason for spending. Al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī answers that this depends on how تَثْبِيتًا is read. This lesson follows al-Jadwal’s main reading.</Note>

“Fear and hope” looks like a reason: why does Allah show the lightning? Now ask who feels what. Allah shows the lightning. People feel the fear and the hope. The doers differ.

Al-Jadwal reads the pair as a verbal noun standing for a ḥāl said of “you”: “fearing and hoping”. It records another reading in a note: al-ʿUkbarī read it as a mafʿūl li-ajlih, and al-Zamakhshari refused that because the doers differ. *Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* records answers to al-Zamakhshari. Two of them are these. One is that يُرِيكُمُ means “makes you see”, so the one who sees is the one who fears. The other is that some scholars, such as Ibn Kharūf, do not require the same doer at all.

The same two words in 30:24 and 32:16 are a mafʿūl li-ajlih in al-Jadwal. So a pair of words can be read one way in one verse and another way in the next.

<Note kind="know">Where the doers differ, scholars read the word as a ḥāl, or they explain the verb so that the doers become one. Both answers are old and both are in the books. This lesson teaches the same doer as the usual rule and names the other view here.</Note>

Three slips are common.

- **Tanwīn on the first word of the idafah.** حَذَرًا ٱلْمَوْتِ is wrong. The idafah takes the tanwīn away.
- **The wrong case on the reason.** The reason is a mafʿūl li-ajlih, so its ending is -a. A -i ending would mean it is the noun after a preposition.
- **Giving naṣb to the second word.** ٱلْمَوْتِ is the muḍāf ilayh. It is genitive. Only the first word is the mafʿūl li-ajlih.

The reason is often a chain of words, as in ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ ٱللَّهِ. The role belongs to the first word. The words after it are muḍāf ilayh, one inside the next.

<Step id="practice" label="Practice" title="Practice">
<Block id="practice-1" title="Practice: find it and say what it does">
  <Stage label="For each word, ask">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Is it in naṣb?</b> -a or -an.</li>
      <li><b>Is it a verbal noun?</b> An action or a feeling.</li>
      <li><b>Does it say why?</b> Not “how?”.</li>
      <li><b>Is there a preposition in front?</b> Then it is a phrase.</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

Work out each answer first, on paper or in your head. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Find the mafʿūl li-ajlih in خَرَجُوا۟ مِن دِيَٰرِهِمْ وَهُمْ أُلُوفٌ حَذَرَ ٱلْمَوْتِ (Al-Baqarah 2:243, part). Say who has the fear.">

- **The reason:** حَذَرَ, in naṣb with -a. ٱلْمَوْتِ is the muḍāf ilayh, genitive.
- **Who has the fear:** the people who went out. They are the doers of خَرَجُوا۟, so the doers match.
- **Beside it:** وَهُمْ أُلُوفٌ, “and they were thousands”, is a state clause. Al-Jadwal reads the wāw as a wāw of state (L4.01).

It is the same two words as in 2:19. The Corpus has this word (حَذَر) only in these two verses.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Look at وَمَن يَفْعَلْ ذَٰلِكَ ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ ٱللَّهِ (An-Nisa 4:114, part). Find the mafʿūl li-ajlih, give the root and pattern of its first word, and say what ٱللَّهِ is.">

- **The reason:** ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ ٱللَّهِ. The mafʿūl li-ajlih is the first word, ٱبْتِغَآءَ.
- **Root and pattern:** root ب غ ي, pattern اِفْتِعَال (Form VIII).
- **Why -a:** it is in naṣb and is the first word of an idafah, so it has no tanwīn.
- **ٱللَّهِ:** the muḍāf ilayh of مَرْضَاتِ, genitive.

The one who does “that” is the one who seeks, so the doers match.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Which of these is a mafʿūl li-ajlih? (a) وَٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا۟ ٱبْتِغَآءَ وَجْهِ رَبِّهِمْ (Ar-Rad 13:22, part) (b) إِنَّمَا نُطْعِمُكُمْ لِوَجْهِ ٱللَّهِ (Al-Insan 76:9, part) (c) مِنْ أَجْلِ ذَٰلِكَ كَتَبْنَا (Al-Maidah 5:32, start) (d) فَأَتْبَعَهُمْ فِرْعَوْنُ وَجُنُودُهُۥ بَغْيًا وَعَدْوًا (Yunus 10:90, part)">

- (a) **yes.** ٱبْتِغَآءَ is in naṣb and says why they were patient.
- (b) **no.** لِوَجْهِ ٱللَّهِ is lām and a noun, genitive. It is a phrase.
- (c) **no.** مِنْ أَجْلِ ذَٰلِكَ is min and a noun. أَجْلِ is genitive.
- (d) **yes, both.** بَغْيًا is the reason and عَدْوًا is joined to it. Al-Jadwal has a ḥāl reading in a note.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Check the conditions for each. (a) قَامَ زَيْدٌ إِجْلَالًا لِعَمْرٍو (the example in al-Ajurrumiyyah) (b) دَخَلَ ٱلطَّالِبُ بِسَبَبِ ٱلْمُعَلِّمِ (made up) (c) يُنفِقُونَ أَمْوَٰلَهُمُ ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ ٱللَّهِ (Al-Baqarah 2:265, part) (d) يُرِيكُمُ ٱلْبَرْقَ خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا (Ar-Rad 13:12, part)">

- (a) **all met.** إِجْلَالًا is accusative and says why. Zayd stands and Zayd honours: one doer, one time, and a feeling.
- (b) **not a mafʿūl li-ajlih.** بِسَبَبِ ٱلْمُعَلِّمِ is a preposition and its noun, and the noun is genitive. The teacher is the cause. The student’s own feeling is not named. (دَخَلَ is read dakhala.)
- (c) **met.** The doers of spending and seeking are the same, and the word is in naṣb.
- (d) **the doers differ.** Allah shows, people fear. Al-Jadwal reads a ḥāl. Scholars who read a reason explain the verb as “makes you see”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Name each accusative word. Made-up: (a) كَتَبَ رِسَالَةً (b) كَتَبَ كِتَابَةً (c) كَتَبَ رَغْبَةً فِي ٱلْخَيْرِ. (d) The three accusatives in كَتَبَ ٱلطَّالِبُ ٱلدَّرْسَ كِتَابَةً جَمِيلَةً رَغْبَةً فِي ٱلتَّفَوُّقِ. And from a verse: (e) حَذَرَ in 2:19.">

- (a) **mafʿūl bihi.** رِسَالَةً is what was written. It comes from another root.
- (b) **mafʿūl muṭlaq.** كِتَابَةً comes from the same root as كَتَبَ and repeats the action. L4.07 teaches it.
- (c) **mafʿūl li-ajlih.** رَغْبَةً is a feeling and says why he wrote. The phrase فِي ٱلْخَيْرِ goes with it: desire for good.
- (d) **Three jobs in one sentence.** ٱلدَّرْسَ is the mafʿūl bihi: what was written. كِتَابَةً جَمِيلَةً is a mafʿūl muṭlaq with its adjective: how it was written. رَغْبَةً is the mafʿūl li-ajlih: why. The sentence means “the student wrote the lesson beautifully, out of desire for excellence”.
- (e) **mafʿūl li-ajlih.** The feeling is fear, and it says why they put their fingers in their ears.

The sentences in (a) to (d) are made up and are not in the Quran.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Exercise n={6} q="Made up: (a) جِئْتُ طَلَبَ ٱلْعِلْمِ (b) جِئْتُ لِطَلَبِ ٱلْعِلْمِ (c) جِئْتُ طَلَبًا لِلْعِلْمِ. Which contain a mafʿūl li-ajlih, and in what shape?">

- (a) **yes.** طَلَبَ is in naṣb and is the first word of an idafah, so no tanwīn.
- (b) **no.** لِطَلَبِ is lām and a noun. The noun is genitive, so it is a phrase.
- (c) **yes.** طَلَبًا is in naṣb with tanwīn. The phrase لِلْعِلْمِ says what is sought, as لِعَمْرٍو does in the book’s example.

All three say “I came to seek knowledge”. Only (a) and (c) have the word in naṣb.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="In كُفَّارًا حَسَدًا مِّنْ عِندِ أَنفُسِهِم (Al-Baqarah 2:109, part), who has the envy, and what does مِّنْ عِندِ أَنفُسِهِم add?">

- **Who:** the same people who would turn the believers back. The doers match.
- **What the phrase adds:** “from themselves”. Al-Jadwal ties it to حَسَدًا. The envy starts inside them, so it is a feeling of the doer.

كُفَّارًا is not the reason. It is the second object: turned into what?

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Look at يُنفِقُونَ أَمْوَٰلَهُمْ رِئَآءَ ٱلنَّاسِ (An-Nisa 4:38, part). Say what al-Jadwal reads رِئَآءَ as and what the note allows. Which question does each answer?">

- **Al-Jadwal’s main reading:** a ḥāl, a verbal noun in the place of “showing off”. It answers “in what state do they spend?”.
- **The note:** a mafʿūl li-ajlih. It answers “why do they spend?”.
- **What stays the same:** the ending (-a), the first word of an idafah, and the meaning.

The same two readings apply to بَطَرًا in 8:47.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Give the root and pattern of each: (a) ٱبْتِغَآءَ (Al-Baqarah 2:207, part) (b) تَثْبِيتًا (Al-Baqarah 2:265, part) (c) حَذَرَ (Al-Baqarah 2:19, part) (d) مَرْضَاتِ (Al-Baqarah 2:207, part)">

- (a) root ب غ ي, pattern اِفْتِعَال (Form VIII). The last letter turned into a hamza.
- (b) root ث ب ت, pattern تَفْعِيل (Form II).
- (c) root ح ذ ر, pattern فَعَل. A verbal noun learned by hearing.
- (d) root ر ض و (Corpus), pattern مَفْعَلَة. A mīm verbal noun of رَضِيَ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Look at إِلَّا ٱبْتِغَآءَ وَجْهِ رَبِّهِ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ (Al-Layl 92:20). Al-Jadwal reads ٱبْتِغَآءَ in two ways. What are they, and what do they share?">

- **Main reading:** an exception cut off from what came before: “but he gives, seeking the face of his Lord”. L4.05 teaches the exception.
- **Note:** a mafʿūl li-ajlih, with a verb understood, and إِلَّا meaning “but”.
- **What they share:** the word is in naṣb, the first word of an idafah, and means “seeking the face of his Lord”.

Both names describe one accusative word, as in 4:38 and 8:47.

</Exercise>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="a complete action plus a reason in naṣb equals a mafʿūl li-ajlih"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'a complete action', small: 'verb and doer' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'a reason in naṣb', small: 'a verbal noun: why?' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'mafʿūl li-ajlih', small: 'مَفْعُول لِأَجْلِهِ' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A **mafʿūl li-ajlih** is a noun in naṣb that says why the action was done. It is usually a verbal noun for a feeling or a wish: حَذَرَ ٱلْمَوْتِ, ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ ٱللَّهِ, حَسَدًا.
- *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* defines it: the accusative noun that makes clear the reason the action happened.
- The usual condition is the same doer as the verb. Al-Jadwal and al-Durr name it. Scholars argue about it in 13:12.
- It can be a bare word with tanwīn (بَغْيًا), the first word of an idafah (حَذَرَ ٱلْمَوْتِ), or joined to another reason by وَ.
- A reason given with min (مِّنْ إِمْلَٰقٍ), lām (لِوَجْهِ ٱللَّهِ) or min ajli (مِنْ أَجْلِ ذَٰلِكَ) is a preposition and its noun, in the genitive. It is not a mafʿūl li-ajlih.
- After a verb, a verbal noun in naṣb can be a reason, a ḥāl or an absolute object. The question decides. Scholars read some of them two ways (8:47, 4:38, 21:107).

**Next:** L4.09 teaches the object of accompaniment, which says with whom or with what an action happened. L4.07 teaches the absolute object, the other job of an accusative verbal noun.
