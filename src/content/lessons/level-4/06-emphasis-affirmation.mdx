This verse is strengthened in two ways. The sentence says only “We showed him signs”. The rest makes it firm.

At the start stands **وَلَقَدْ**: وَ, then لَ, then قَدْ. Al-Jadwal reads the لَ as the lām of an oath that is not spoken (لَام الْقَسَم), and قَدْ as a word of certainty (حَرْف تَحْقِيق). In English both come out as “certainly”.

The second way is **كُلَّهَا**, “all of them”. It comes right after ءَايَٰتِنَا and copies its accusative ending. Al-Jadwal calls it tawkid of ءَايَٰتِنَا. Take it away and the sentence is still whole. With it, no sign is left out.

</Block>

<Block id="map" title="The map: five ways to strengthen">
  <Stage label="Five ways">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">

      <li><b>The oath:</b> “By …”, then a strengthened answer. Step 3.</li>
      <li><b>The nūn of emphasis:</b> an extra nūn on a present verb. Step 3.</li>
      <li><b>Repeating:</b> say the same word again. Step 4.</li>

    </ol>

Arabic has no italics or capital letters. It strengthens with words. Here is where each way is taught, and what it looks like.

| Way | Arabic | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Little words | حُرُوف التَّوْكِيد | A small word in front of the sentence or the word | إِنَّ رَبِّى لَسَمِيعُ (14:39) |
| The oath | قَسَم | “By …”, then an answer that carries emphasis | وَتَٱللَّهِ لَأَكِيدَنَّ (21:57) |
| The nūn of emphasis | نُون التَّوْكِيد | An extra nūn at the end of a present verb | لَنَسْفَعًۢا (96:15) |
| Repeating | تَوْكِيد لَفْظِيّ | The same word or sentence said again | كَلَّا … ثُمَّ كَلَّا (102:3 and 102:4) |
| Words of meaning | تَوْكِيد مَعْنَوِيّ | A following word: كُلّ, أَجْمَع, نَفْس, عَيْن | كُلُّهُمْ أَجْمَعُونَ (15:30) |

The first three go **in front of** or **onto** a word or a sentence. The last two are tawkid in the narrow sense: a **following word** (تَابِع). It stands after the word it strengthens and copies its case, like the adjective and the “and” word in L2.06. *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* names four following words: the adjective, the “and” word, tawkid and the replacement word.

<Note kind="know">The books use the word tawkid in two ways. Narrowly, it is the following word (the last two rows). Al-Jadwal also calls inna “a word of tawkid” (حَرْف تَوْكِيد), and speaks of “the lām of tawkid” and “the nūn of tawkid”. This lesson says “emphasis” for the whole map and “tawkid” for the following word. The Corpus file has no tag for the following word, so we could not count it there. The counts below are for little words, the nūn and repeated words.</Note>

</Block>
</Step>

The plain sentence is ٱللَّهُ تَوَّابٌ رَّحِيمٌ (a made-up example, “Allah accepts repentance and is merciful”). Put إِنَّ in front and the first word moves to the accusative: إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ. The khabar keeps its ḍamma. L2.10 teaches how inna works, with the lām of emphasis on the khabar.

Here inna is only the first strengthener. The second verse has a لَ on the khabar as well: لَسَمِيعُ. Al-Jadwal calls it the lām “that slid away” (اللَّام الْمُزَحْلَقَة), moved from the front of the sentence to the khabar. It is a second strengthener on the same statement, and it changes no ending.

In the Corpus, 1,682 words are tagged as inna. We counted the words with the lemma inna, and we did not count her sisters.

The little لَ is the most common strengthener. Three places are new here. On a **noun that starts a sentence**, as in لَأَنتُمْ أَشَدُّ رَهْبَةً (59:13). Al-Jadwal calls it “the beginning lām” (لَام الِابْتِدَاء). In front of **قَدْ**, which is the next block. In front of **سَوْفَ**: فَلَسَوْفَ تَعْلَمُونَ (26:49). Al-Jadwal reads this لَ as the lām of an oath that is not spoken. The Corpus has four such words: 19:66, 26:49, 92:21 and 93:5.

On a **verb**, the لَ comes with an oath and the nūn of emphasis. That is Step 3.

<Note kind="know">The Corpus tags 1,001 words that begin with a prefixed لَ as emphasis. We counted them by what the لَ is attached to: nouns 283, قَدْ 183, present verbs 226 (176 of them also end in the nūn of emphasis), past verbs 135, the condition word إِن 63 (as in لَئِن), prepositions 63, pronouns 26, joining nouns 13, سَوْفَ 4, names 3, place words 2. The tag is wider than the grammar books. Some of the 135 past verbs carry the لَ that ties the answer to لَوْ (see Mistake 3), which al-Jadwal does not call emphasis. So read the 1,001 as “words that begin with this لَ”, and not as 1,001 strengthened sentences.</Note>

**قَدْ** goes in front of a verb. Al-Jadwal calls it a word of certainty (حَرْف تَحْقِيق) in both verses. It changes no ending. In English it often comes out as “certainly” or just as a strong “has”.

In the Corpus, قَدْ is tagged as a word of certainty 406 times, and the word right after it is a verb every time. It is a **past** verb 398 times (217 after قَدْ alone and 181 after لَقَدْ). It is a **present** verb 8 times: 2:144 (نَرَىٰ), 6:33, 15:97 and 16:103 (نَعْلَمُ), 24:63 and 24:64 (يَعْلَمُ), 33:18 (يَعْلَمُ) and 61:5 (تَعْلَمُونَ). All eight verbs mean “see” or “know”.

You may read that قَدْ with a present verb can mean “maybe”. None of these eight needs it. For 33:18, al-Jadwal says the knowledge of Allah is sure at all times, so قَدْ here is certainty.

**لَقَدْ** is two little words written as one: the strengthening لَ and قَدْ. Al-Jadwal reads the لَ as the lām of an oath (لَام الْقَسَم). Here the oath is spoken in the three verses before: وَٱلتِّينِ وَٱلزَّيْتُونِ, وَطُورِ سِينِينَ and وَهَٰذَا ٱلْبَلَدِ ٱلْأَمِينِ. Al-Jadwal calls the sentence of verse 4 the answer of the oath (جَوَاب الْقَسَم). Where no oath is spoken, as in 20:56, al-Jadwal says the oath is understood.

In the Corpus, قَدْ comes with this لَ 183 times out of 406, and the word after it is a past verb 181 times and a present verb twice (15:97 and 16:103).

<Note kind="know">The Corpus tags the لَ of لَقَدْ simply as a word of emphasis. Al-Jadwal reads it as the lām of an oath, spoken or understood. The two readings describe the same word, and both treat the sentence as strengthened. This lesson follows al-Jadwal’s name for it.</Note>

An oath has two parts. The first names what you swear by. It is a **letter** and a noun in the genitive: وَ (wāw), بِ (bāʾ) or تَ (tāʾ). In these verses tāʾ goes with the name of Allah. In 38:82 al-Jadwal says the oath phrase belongs to a verb left out, “I swear”.

The second part is the **answer** (جَوَاب الْقَسَم). It is the sentence that is sworn to, and it carries its own strengthener. In this lesson the answers use لَقَدْ (95:4), لَ with a verb and the nūn (21:57) and the same with the light nūn in the next block. The Corpus has no tag for the oath, so we could not count oaths.

Surah Ad-Duha (L4.19) opens with oath wāws. Later it uses the لَ on a noun and لَسَوْفَ, both from Step 2.

The nūn comes in two forms. The **heavy** nūn is ـَنَّ, a nūn with a shadda: لَيُسْجَنَنَّ. The **light** nūn is ـَنْ, a nūn with a sukūn, spelled like a tanwin on an alif in the script: وَلَيَكُونًا. Both do the same job.

We read the first seven in the text (2:132, 2:147, 3:102, 3:169, 3:178, 3:180 and 3:188), and all seven are bans.

These four are teaching forms. The last one is the form of 14:12, which you meet word by word below.

A present verb ends in -u, or in -a after a word of naṣb, or in a sukūn after a word of jazm (L3.07). When the nūn of emphasis comes right after the verb, the verb ends in a **fatḥa** and does not change again. The books call it built on the fatḥa (مَبْنِيّ عَلَى الْفَتْح). Al-Jadwal says so for نَصْبِرَنَّ in 14:12 and for نَسْفَعَنْ in 96:15. For تَحْسَبَنَّ in 3:169 it says the verb is built on the fatḥa **in the place of jazm**, because of the لَا of the ban.

So the nūn does **not** make the verb subjunctive. The mood is whatever the sentence has: plain in the oath answer, jazm after the ban. The fatḥa you see is the sign of the “built” verb. The nūn of emphasis is also a different nūn from the nūn of the five verbs (تَكْتُبُونَ) and from the -na of the women’s plural (تَكْتُبْنَ). Mistake 4 sorts them.

The plural forms and “you (f)” change the ending again before the nūn. This lesson keeps to “he”, “we”, “I” and “you (m)”.

The book says tawkid is a following word. It copies the case of the word it strengthens: nominative, accusative or genitive. Then the book lists the words. They are **نَفْس** (self), **عَيْن** (the very one), **كُلّ** (all) and **أَجْمَع** (all together). It adds three words that go after أَجْمَع: أَكْتَعُ, أَبْتَعُ and أَبْصَعُ. None of the three is in the Corpus text.

| Word | Meaning | After it | Strengthens |
|---|---|---|---|
| نَفْس | self | a pronoun that matches the word | the person or thing itself |
| عَيْن | the very one | a pronoun that matches the word | the person or thing itself |
| كُلّ | all | a pronoun that matches the word | the whole of it |
| أَجْمَع | all together | no pronoun | the whole, after كُلّ |

The pronoun after نَفْس, عَيْن and كُلّ matches the strengthened word in gender and number. This is the usual way the examples are given. Quran Learn has not checked the rule in an approved source. The Corpus has 17 words where كُلّ has a pronoun attached.

A test helps you spot real tawkid. **Take the word away.** The sentence must stay whole and keep its meaning. If it falls apart, the word is a doer, an object or something else.

<Block id="mistake-nun" title="Mistake 4: three kinds of nūn">
  <Stage label="Teaching forms, not verses">
    <Tiles
      label="Three endings that look alike"
      items={[
        { ar: 'تَكْتُبْنَ', en: 'you (women) write: the -na of the doers', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'تَكْتُبُونَ', en: 'you all write: the nūn of the five verbs', type: 'v' },

The three forms end in a nūn, and each nūn is a different thing. In **تَكْتُبْنَ**, -na is the ending for “they (f)” or “you (f)”: it is the doer. L3.05 and L3.07 taught it. In **تَكْتُبُونَ** the nūn belongs to the five verbs. It drops after a word of naṣb or jazm. In **لَتَكْتُبَنَّ** the nūn is the nūn of emphasis. The verb ends in a fatḥa and the nūn has a shadda.

Two checks tell them apart. The nūn of emphasis comes with a **لَ** in front (176 times out of 243 in the Corpus) or after لَا of a ban. And it always strengthens: you can say “surely” in English. Do not say that the nūn makes the verb subjunctive. It does not (see the block “What the nūn does to the verb”).

<Block id="find" title="How to find the strengthener">
  <Stage label="Look in this order">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Start of the sentence:</b> إِنَّ, قَدْ, لَقَدْ, لَ, سَوْفَ.</li>
      <li><b>An oath:</b> وَ, بِ or تَ and a genitive noun, then the answer.</li>
      <li><b>End of a verb:</b> the nūn of emphasis, with a fatḥa before it.</li>
      <li><b>After a noun:</b> كُلّ, أَجْمَع, نَفْس or عَيْن. Remove it: does the sentence stay whole?</li>
      <li><b>The same words again:</b> repeating.</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

Use this order on every verse. The little words and the oath come first because they stand in front. The following words come last because you have to check them against the noun before.

<Note kind="know">Teachers often say “the more strengtheners, the stronger the sentence”. This is a teaching summary, and Quran Learn has found no approved source for it. Treat it as a way to notice the words. L4.17 looks at how the Quran chooses between plain and strengthened speech.</Note>
</Block>

<Exercise n={1} q="Name every strengthener in each. (a) قَدْ أَفْلَحَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ (Al-Muʾminun 23:1) (b) إِنَّ رَبَّكَ لَبِٱلْمِرْصَادِ (Al-Fajr 89:14) (c) وَلَقَدْ نَعْلَمُ أَنَّكَ يَضِيقُ صَدْرُكَ (Al-Hijr 15:97, part) (d) قَدْ نَعْلَمُ إِنَّهُۥ لَيَحْزُنُكَ (Al-Anam 6:33, part)">

- (a) **قَدْ**, in front of the past verb أَفْلَحَ: “have certainly succeeded”.
- (b) **إِنَّ** and a **لَ** on the khabar. The khabar is the phrase بِٱلْمِرْصَادِ. The first word becomes accusative: رَبَّكَ.
- (c) **لَقَدْ**: the لَ and قَدْ, in front of a present verb. This is one of the two cases in the Corpus where لَقَدْ comes before a present verb.
- (d) Three: **قَدْ**, **إِنَّ** and a **لَ** on the verb يَحْزُنُكَ. The verse piles three strengtheners on one statement: “We certainly know that it truly grieves you”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="The Corpus has eight words where قَدْ stands before a present verb: 2:144 قَدْ نَرَىٰ; 6:33, 15:97 and 16:103 نَعْلَمُ; 24:63, 24:64 and 33:18 يَعْلَمُ; 61:5 تَعْلَمُونَ. What do the eight verbs have in common, and what does that say about the meaning “maybe”?">

All eight verbs mean “see” or “know”. In each the speaker or Allah is sure: “We see”, “We know”, “He knows”, “you know”. So the reading “certainly” fits each one, and none needs “maybe”.

Eight is a small number. This does not prove that قَدْ with a present verb never means “maybe” anywhere in Arabic. It says the Quran, as the Corpus tags it, gives no case where “maybe” is needed.

</Exercise>

- (a) Oath: **تَٱللَّهِ**, with **tāʾ**. Answer: لَقَدْ ءَاثَرَكَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيْنَا, strengthened with لَقَدْ.
- (b) Oath: **وَٱلتِّينِ** with **wāw**, and three oath phrases in a row (95:1 to 95:3). Answer: verse 4, strengthened with لَقَدْ.
- (c) Oath: **بِعِزَّتِكَ** with **bāʾ**. Answer: لَأُغْوِيَنَّهُمْ with لَ and the heavy nūn, and أَجْمَعِينَ after the object.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="For each verb say the nūn (heavy or light), the ending before it, and the word in front. (a) لَيُسْجَنَنَّ (Yusuf 12:32, part) (b) لَنَسْفَعًۢا (Al-Alaq 96:15, part) (c) وَلَا تَحْسَبَنَّ ٱللَّهَ غَٰفِلًا (Ibrahim 14:42, part) (d) لَأُقَطِّعَنَّ أَيْدِيَكُمْ (Ash-Shuara 26:49, part)">

- (a) **Heavy**. A fatḥa on the nūn that is a root letter, then the nūn of emphasis. In front: لَ of an oath. “He will surely be put in prison.”
- (b) **Light**, spelled like a tanwin on an alif. A fatḥa on the ع. In front: لَ. “We will surely seize.”
- (c) **Heavy**. A fatḥa on the ب. In front: لَا of a ban. “Do not think at all.” 3:169 has the same pattern, and al-Jadwal says there the verb stands in the place of jazm.
- (d) **Heavy**. A fatḥa on the ع. In front: لَ. “I will surely cut off your hands.”

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Tawkid or not? Take the word away and see. (a) وَيُحَذِّرُكُمُ ٱللَّهُ نَفْسَهُۥ (Al ʿImran 3:28, part) (b) فَطَوَّعَتْ لَهُۥ نَفْسُهُۥ قَتْلَ أَخِيهِ (Al-Maʾidah 5:30, part) (c) أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَلِيمٌ (Al-Baqarah 2:231, part) (d) كُلُّهُمْ جَمِيعًا (Yunus 10:99, part) (e) فَسَجَدَ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ كُلُّهُمْ أَجْمَعُونَ (Al-Hijr 15:30)">

- (a) **Not tawkid.** نَفْسَهُۥ is the second object of يُحَذِّرُ. Without it the sentence does not say what we are warned of.
- (b) **Not tawkid.** نَفْسُهُۥ is the doer of طَوَّعَتْ. Without it there is no doer.
- (c) **Not tawkid.** كُلِّ is the first word of an idafah, with شَىْءٍ.
- (d) **كُلُّهُمْ** is tawkid. **جَمِيعًا** is, in al-Jadwal, a state that strengthens, so it is not tawkid in the narrow sense.
- (e) **Both tawkid**: كُلُّهُمْ, then a second tawkid أَجْمَعُونَ. Take both away and the sentence is whole.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Exercise n={6} q="What is said twice, and what is new the second time? (a) كَلَّا سَوْفَ تَعْلَمُونَ (At-Takathur 102:3) (b) ثُمَّ كَلَّا سَوْفَ تَعْلَمُونَ (At-Takathur 102:4) Which kind of tawkid is it? Then say what the question of 55:13 does 31 times.">

The whole sentence كَلَّا سَوْفَ تَعْلَمُونَ is said twice. The second time begins with **ثُمَّ**, and al-Jadwal says this shows the second is stronger. It is **tawkid lafẓī**, tawkid by the same words.

The question of 55:13 is the same kind. It is a whole sentence repeated 31 times in one surah, each time after something new.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Mend each made-up sentence. All four are wrong. (a) جَاءَ ٱلْمَلِكُ نَفْسَهُ (b) رَأَيْتُ ٱلْقَوْمَ كُلُّهُمْ (c) مَرَرْتُ بِٱلْقَوْمِ كُلُّهُمْ (d) جَاءَ ٱلْمَلِكُ نَفْسُ">

All four are made-up, and each breaks the rule of the case or the pronoun.

- (a) **جَاءَ ٱلْمَلِكُ نَفْسُهُ**. ٱلْمَلِكُ is the doer, nominative, so the tawkid is nominative: -u.
- (b) **رَأَيْتُ ٱلْقَوْمَ كُلَّهُمْ**. ٱلْقَوْمَ is the object, accusative, so كُلَّهُمْ has a fatḥa.
- (c) **مَرَرْتُ بِٱلْقَوْمِ كُلِّهِمْ**. ٱلْقَوْمِ is genitive after بِ, so كُلِّهِمْ is genitive. The pronoun also changes to -him after the kasra.
- (d) **جَاءَ ٱلْمَلِكُ نَفْسُهُ**. نَفْس needs its pronoun pointing back to the king.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Make each plain sentence stronger. All four are made-up. (a) ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ فِى جَنَّةٍ, with إِنَّ and لَ (b) نَصَرَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ, with لَقَدْ (c) نَصْبِرُ, as an oath answer with the nūn (d) جَاءَ ٱلْقَوْمُ, with كُلّ">

- (a) **إِنَّ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ لَفِى جَنَّةٍ**. After إِنَّ the first word moves to the accusative: -īna. The لَ goes on the khabar, here the phrase فِى جَنَّةٍ.
- (b) **لَقَدْ نَصَرَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ**. No ending changes.
- (c) **لَنَصْبِرَنَّ**. A fatḥa on the ر, then the heavy nūn. Compare 14:12.
- (d) **جَاءَ ٱلْقَوْمُ كُلُّهُمْ**. Nominative, with a pronoun that points back to the people.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="List the strengtheners and name the way of each. (a) وَلَقَدْ أَرَيْنَٰهُ ءَايَٰتِنَا كُلَّهَا (Ta-Ha 20:56, part) (b) كَلَّا لَئِن لَّمْ يَنتَهِ لَنَسْفَعًۢا (Al-Alaq 96:15, part)">

- (a) Three words. **لَ** (an oath that is understood) and **قَدْ**, both little words, in front. **كُلَّهَا**, a following word: tawkid of ءَايَٰتِنَا, accusative.
- (b) Three. The **لَ** in لَئِن prepares an oath. The **لَ** in لَنَسْفَعًۢا starts the answer. The **light nūn** ends the verb. كَلَّا is a word of rebuke and is not part of the count.

Counting shows you where to look. It does not measure how strong a verse is.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Check the book’s list. Which of these are in the list of tawkid words in al-Ajurrumiyyah: نَفْس, جَمِيع, كُلّ, عَيْن, أَجْمَع? Where does جَمِيع belong in 10:99?">

In the book: **نَفْس, عَيْن, كُلّ, أَجْمَع**, and the three words that go after أَجْمَع (أَكْتَعُ, أَبْتَعُ, أَبْصَعُ). **جَمِيع** is not in the list.

In 10:99, كُلُّهُمْ is the tawkid. Al-Jadwal reads جَمِيعًا as a **state that strengthens** (حَال مُؤَكِّدَة).

</Exercise>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="a strengthener makes a plain sentence stronger"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'a plain sentence', small: 'جُمْلَة' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'a strengthener', small: 'five ways' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'a stronger sentence', small: 'same facts, more certain' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- There are **five ways** to strengthen: little words, the oath, the nūn of emphasis, repeating, and words of meaning.
- **Little words:** إِنَّ (L2.10), قَدْ, لَقَدْ and the لَ. قَدْ stands before a verb: 398 of 406 before a past verb, 8 before a present verb, all of seeing or knowing.
- **The oath** is a letter (وَ, بِ or تَ) and a genitive noun. The **answer** is strengthened by لَقَدْ, or by لَ with a verb and the nūn.
- **The nūn of emphasis** is heavy (241 in the Corpus) or light (2). The verb before it ends in a fatḥa and is built. The nūn does not make a verb subjunctive.
- **Repeating** is tawkid lafẓī: كَلَّا twice in 102:3 and 102:4, and 31 times the question of 55:13.
- **Words of meaning** (تَوْكِيد مَعْنَوِيّ) follow the noun and copy its case: كُلّ, أَجْمَع, نَفْس, عَيْن. Take the word away: the sentence must stay whole. نَفْس can be a doer or an object, and كُلّ in front of a noun is the first word of an idafah.
- Not every لَ strengthens: the لَ of the answer to لَوْ only ties it.

**Next:** L4.07 teaches the absolute object, one more way to strengthen a verb. L4.10 puts the words of negation side by side. L4.17 looks at emphasis as one of the arts of speech. Surah Ad-Duha (L4.19) uses the oath wāw, the لَ on a noun and لَسَوْفَ.
