Three words of negation stand in this verse, and each one does something different to the verb after it.

- **لَمْ** stands before تَسْتَغْفِرْ. The verb ends in a sukūn. That is jazm (L3.07).
- **لَن** stands before يَغْفِرَ. The verb ends in a fatḥa. That is naṣb.
- **لَا** stands before يَهْدِى. The verb keeps its plain ending, so لَا changed nothing.

All three say “not”. What tells them apart is the ending of the word after them. This lesson takes eight words that say “not” and shows what each one does.

Al-Jadwal gives each clause its own sentence and reads the last لَا as a plain negative. It does not parse the verbs word by word in this verse. The words لَمْ and لَن are the ones L3.07 parsed in other verses.

Five of these stand before a verb: لَمْ, لَمَّا, لَن, لَا and مَا. Others stand before a noun: لَيْسَ, مَا, إِنْ and غَيْر. لَا can stand before either.

Ask what each word does to the thing after it. There are only a few answers.

| What it does | Words |
|---|---|
| Jazm on a present verb | لَمْ, لَمَّا, and لَا when it makes a ban |
| Naṣb on a present verb | لَن |
| Nothing to a verb | لَا (a plain “not”), مَا, إِنْ |
| Turns the khabar accusative | لَيْسَ, and مَا when it works like laysa |
| Is a noun itself | غَيْر |

The next two steps go through them one by one. The fourth step puts them side by side, with counts.

L3.07 showed these two lists. They hold the little words that change the ending of a present verb. Four of our words are in them. لَن is in the list of naṣb. لَمْ, لَمَّا and the لَا “in the ban” are in the list of jazm. أَلَمْ and أَلَمَّا are the same two words with the question أَ in front.

The lists are about verbs, so they leave out the words that change no verb: the plain لَا, the negative مَا and the negative إِنْ. The مَا and إِنْ that are in the list of jazm are the condition words (L4.04).

The book adds “and the request” to the ban. A ban spoken to God is a request, and it takes the same jazm: رَبَّنَا لَا تُؤَاخِذْنَآ (2:286), “our Lord, do not hold us to account”. The verb تُؤَاخِذْنَآ ends in a sukūn on the ذ.

لَا is the most common word that says “not”. In 20:52 it stands before two present verbs, يَضِلُّ and يَنسَى. Both keep the plain ending they would have without لَا. So لَا changed nothing. The sentence says “does not”: something that is true now, or always.

Quran Learn counted in the Corpus file. 1,406 words are tagged as the negative لَا. A present verb follows right away in 1,017 of them. A noun follows in 300, and the next step deals with those.

<Note kind="know">A past verb can come after لَا, but only six times, in five verses: 6:148, 10:16, 16:35, 75:31 and 90:11. In فَلَا صَدَّقَ وَلَا صَلَّىٰ (75:31), “he neither believed nor prayed”, the verbs keep their past form. Al-Jadwal calls the لَا a plain negative both times.</Note>

Here the same letters لَا mean “do not”. The verb after it shows jazm. تَقُل ends in a sukūn, and the plain form is تَقُولُ: the long ū is gone. تَنْهَرْهُمَا is a sukūn on the ر, and the plain form is تَنْهَرُ. L3.07 taught the ban. What is new here is the comparison: in 20:52 the verb kept its -u and the sentence said “does not”. Here the verb is cut short and the sentence says “do not”.

Al-Jadwal calls this لَا “prohibiting and bringing jazm”, and the doer is a hidden “you”.

The Corpus tags 332 words as the لَا of prohibition, and a present verb follows 330 of them. About 110 more words tagged as a plain negative stand before a verb that is tagged jussive, and many of those are bans too, such as فَلَا تَكْفُرْ (2:102). The tags do not draw a clean line. Trust the verb’s ending.

<Note kind="know">The verse starts with أَلَّا تَعْبُدُوٓا۟ إِلَّآ إِيَّاهُ, “that you worship none but Him”. Its verb has lost its nūn, which is naṣb, but أَنْ did that. أَلَّا is أَنْ plus لَا, and the لَا only says “not”.</Note>

لَمْ is the word of jazm you meet most. It changes the ending and moves the meaning to the past (L3.07). The plain form of تَرَ is تَرَىٰ, “you see”. After لَمْ the last letter ىٰ is gone.

The أَ in front is a question. أَلَمْ تَرَ asks “have you not seen?”, and it is meant as a reminder: you have seen it.

The Corpus tags 353 words as لَمْ. Of these, 347 are لَمْ itself and 6 are the لَمَّا of “not yet”, which comes later in this step. 125 of the لَمْ have a question أَ on the same word, like أَلَمْ here and أَوَلَمْ in 7:184. All of them stand before a present verb. The Corpus tags 350 of these verbs as jussive. The other three are 5:41, 18:82 and 62:3, and the text shows jazm in each.

<Note kind="know">لَمْ points to the past in a plain sentence, but the time can follow the sentence. After a condition word it looks ahead. In فَإِن لَّمْ تَفْعَلُوا۟ وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا۟ (2:24), “and if you do not, and you will not”, the لَمْ speaks about what is still to come. L4.03 teaches the condition.</Note>

</Block>

لَن goes before a present verb and gives it a fatḥa. أَبْرَحَ ends in -a, and the plain form is أَبْرَحُ. Al-Jadwal calls لَن a word of negation and naṣb. The meaning looks ahead: “will not”.

The Corpus tags 106 words as لَن, and the verb after it is tagged as naṣb in all 106. The verb after حَتَّىٰ is in naṣb too, because of a hidden أَنْ (L3.07).

Quran Learn did not check who holds the other view in an approved source.</Note>

لَمَّا before a present verb says “not yet”. It brings jazm like لَمْ: يَلْحَقُوا۟ lost its nūn. The plain form is يَلْحَقُونَ.

Al-Jadwal calls this لَمَّا a word of negation, of turning to the past, and of jazm. Teachers add that it points to something still expected. That extra sense is a teaching summary, and it fits this verse: the others have not joined them, and they are expected to.

L4.04 met the other لَمَّا, “when”, before a past verb. It found the “not yet” one in eight verses: 2:214, 3:142, 9:16, 10:39, 38:8, 49:14, 62:3 and 80:23. The Corpus tags six of them with the same tag as لَمْ, which is why the count of لَمْ above includes them. The letters are the same, so look at the verb. A present verb in jazm after لَمَّا means “not yet”.

مَا before a verb changes nothing. A past verb stays past (ضَلَّ, غَوَىٰ, عَلَّمْنَٰهُ) and a present verb keeps its plain ending (يَنۢبَغِى). So مَا does not set the time. The verb does. In 53:2 the sentence is about the past, and in 36:69 the second clause is about what is fitting now.

Al-Jadwal calls the مَا of 53:2 and of 36:69 a plain negative.

The Corpus tags 705 words as the negative مَا. A past verb follows 334 of them (47%) and a present verb 141 (20%). A noun, a pronoun, a pointing word or a name follows 207 (29%), and the next step deals with those. A verse with إِلَّا later in it holds 262 of the 705 (37%).

<Note kind="warn">مَا does not always say “not”. L3.11 showed the joining noun, “what”. L4.04 showed the condition word, “whatever”. In the Corpus the negative tag is on 705 words, the joining noun on 1,476 and the condition on 23. It is a negative when the sentence says “not” and no returning pronoun points back to it.</Note>

L2.11 taught laysa. It is a verb with only a few forms. The first noun after it is the ism, in the nominative. The khabar is accusative. Nothing else changes when it says “not”.

Here the khabar begins with an extra بِ. The noun after it, أَحْكَمِ, ends in a kasra, because of the بِ. Al-Jadwal says it is “genitive in form and accusative in place” (مَجْرُور لَفْظًا مَنْصُوب مَحَلًّا). The extra بِ adds strength and has no meaning of its own. In the Corpus, 27 of the 89 laysa stand before a phrase, and often that phrase is the khabar, put first, as in لَّيْسَ عَلَيْكَ هُدَىٰهُمْ (2:272), “their guidance is not on you”.

laysa has no tense of its own. L2.11 called it a verb that just means “is not”. It takes its time from the sentence.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal calls laysa a verb (jāmid, with only a few forms). Some grammarians call it a little word. Quran Learn did not check who holds each view. It makes no difference to the endings you read.</Note>

laysa has the endings of a past verb (L3.05), the same ones that كَتَبَ takes. One difference: when the ending starts with a consonant, the ي of the stem is gone and a sukūn stands on the س. So you get لَسْتُ, not لَيْسْتُ.

Not all 13 forms are in the Quran. The Corpus tags 89 words as laysa, in 7 forms: “he” 74 times, “you” (man) 4, “you all” (men) 3, “she” 3, “I” 2, “they” (men) 2 and “you all” (women) once. The form لَسْتَ is the one in 88:22 below.

The ism is hidden inside the verb in “he”, “she” and “they”, as in a past verb. In the endings -ta, -tum and -tu, the ending itself is the ism: لَسْتَ, “you are not”.

Both sentences say “no …” with a noun after لَا. The difference is in the ending of the noun.

In 2:256 إِكْرَاهَ has a fatḥa and no tanwīn. This is the **lā that denies the kind** (L4.05). Al-Jadwal says the noun is built on a fatḥa, in the place of the accusative, and that the phrase after it is the khabar.

In 2:38 خَوْفٌ has -un, the ending of an ordinary mubtadaʾ. Al-Jadwal calls this لَا non-working. A footnote adds that it may also be read as working like laysa, with خَوْفٌ as its ism and عَلَيْهِمْ as its khabar. The second لَا, in وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ, is called an extra one that strengthens the “no”.

So before a noun, look at the ending. A fatḥa with no tanwīn marks the kind. A ḍamma or tanwīn marks a plain negative. A pronoun can follow لَا too: وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ (2:38) and وَلَآ أَنتُمْ عَٰبِدُونَ مَآ أَعْبُدُ (109:3). There لَا is a plain negative, and the khabar stays nominative. Al-Jadwal reads the لَا of Al-Kafirun as a plain negative.

In the Corpus, 300 of the negative لَا stand before a noun, 40 before a pronoun and 27 before a preposition.

غَيْر is not a little word of negation. It is a noun. It means “other than”, and in front of a describing word it comes out as “not”: غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ, “not cut off”. L4.05 taught it as a word of exception and showed that the word after it is genitive. That is مَمْنُونٍ here, the second part of an idafah.

The case of غَيْر itself comes from the noun it describes. In 84:25 it describes أَجْرٌ (nominative), so it ends in -u. Al-Jadwal calls it an adjective of أَجْرٌ. In 11:108 it describes عَطَآءً (accusative), so it is غَيْرَ.

The Corpus tags غَيْر 147 times, 144 as a noun and 3 as an adjective. L4.05 noted that not all of them mean “other than”. Many only mean “not”. They change nothing except that the next word is genitive.

<Block id="choose" title="Choosing the word, and what you see">
  <Stage label="Four questions">

      <li><b>What comes next?</b> A present verb, a past verb or a noun.</li>
      <li><b>What does the ending show?</b> A sukūn, a lost nūn or a lost weak letter (jazm). A fatḥa (naṣb). The plain ending.</li>
      <li><b>What is the time?</b> The past, now, the future, or a ban.</li>
      <li><b>Is it “not”?</b> Check that the word is not a joining or condition مَا.</li>

| After the word you see | The word is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a verb with a sukūn, a lost nūn or a lost weak letter | لَمْ, لَمَّا, or a ban لَا | تَسْتَغْفِرْ (63:6) |
| a verb with a fatḥa | لَن | يَغْفِرَ (63:6) |
| a verb with its plain ending | لَا, مَا or إِنْ | يَهْدِى (63:6), يَنۢبَغِى (36:69) |

| a noun with a fatḥa and no tanwīn | the lā that denies the kind | إِكْرَاهَ (2:256) |

The five verbs (يَفْعَلُونَ, تَفْعَلُونَ and the others in L3.07) lose their nūn after لَمْ, لَمَّا, لَن and a ban. They look the same after all four. Then the word in front decides, and the time of the sentence helps.

<Block id="mistake-form" title="Mistake 1: the wrong verb form after the word">
  <Stage label="Made-up examples">
    <Tiles
      label="Which form goes with which word"
      items={[
        { ar: 'لَمْ يَذْهَبْ', en: 'right: present form, jazm', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'لَمْ ذَهَبَ', en: 'wrong: a past verb after لَمْ', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'مَا ذَهَبَ', en: 'right: a past verb after مَا', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'مَا يَذْهَبْ', en: 'wrong: jazm after مَا', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'لَنْ يَذْهَبُ', en: 'wrong: the plain -u after لَنْ', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'لَنْ يَذْهَبَ', en: 'right: naṣb', type: 'v' },

لَمْ, لَمَّا and لَن take a present verb in a fixed ending: jazm for the first two and naṣb for لَن. A past verb cannot follow them. This is the point of L3.07: the word in front sets the ending.

مَا is the other way round. It changes nothing, so it takes the verb as it is: past, or present with its plain ending. A sukūn after مَا would be a mistake.

The English “did not” can be لَمْ يَذْهَبْ or مَا ذَهَبَ. Pick the verb form that goes with the word.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-time" title="Mistake 2: reading the time from the wrong word">
  <Stage label="Real verbs">
    <Tiles
      label="Who sets the time?"
      items={[
        { ar: 'مَا يَنۢبَغِى', en: 'the verb: now (36:69)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'مَا ضَلَّ', en: 'the verb: the past (53:2)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'لَمْ تَرَ', en: 'the word: the past (105:1)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'فَلَنْ أَبْرَحَ', en: 'the word: the future (12:80)', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

لَمْ and لَن set the time. The verb after them is a present form in both, and the meaning is the past after لَمْ and the future after لَن.

مَا does not set the time. A past verb after it is past, and a present verb is present. A learner who thinks “مَا means the past” will misread مَا يَنۢبَغِى (36:69) and the other 140 Corpus cases of مَا before a present verb.

لَا does not set the time either. The ending of its verb tells a statement from a ban, and the sentence gives the time.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-claims" title="Mistake 3: claims that are too strong">
  <Stage label="Four common claims">
    <Tiles
      label="What the verses show"
      items={[
        { ar: 'لَن', en: 'not always “never” (12:80)', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'لَمْ', en: 'not the only word with jazm and a past sense', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'مَا', en: 'not only for the past', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'لَيْسَ', en: 'not only for a noun that names a state', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Four claims often seen in summaries do not hold up.

- **“لَن means never.”** In 12:80 it is “until my father gives me leave”. See the note in step 2.
- **“لَمْ is the only word that gives a present form a past meaning.”** لَمَّا does the same, with the sense “not yet”. (The ban and the lām of command also give jazm, but not a past sense.)
- **“مَا is for the past.”** 141 of the 705 negative مَا stand before a present verb, and 207 before a noun.
- **“لَمْ is formal and مَا is casual.”** Quran Learn found nothing in al-Jadwal or in the Corpus to support this. The Corpus has 705 مَا and 347 لَمْ. لَمْ occurs in 77 surahs and the negative مَا in 73.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-ma" title="Mistake 4: every مَا is a negative">
  <Stage label="Three مَا, three jobs">
    <Tiles
      label="Three مَا"

        { ar: 'مَا ضَلَّ', en: 'negative: not (53:2)', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ', en: 'joining noun: what is in (2:284)', type: 'n' },
        { ar: 'وَمَا تَفْعَلُوا۟', en: 'condition: whatever (2:197, L4.04)', type: 'n' },

L3.11 showed that مَا does many jobs. In the Corpus, مَا is a negative in 705 tags, a joining noun in 1,476 and a condition word in 23. So a مَا is a negative only if the sentence says “not”.

The same is true of إِنْ (condition, negative, or the إِنَّ of L2.10) and of لَا (a plain “not”, a ban, or the lā of the kind). Look at the words around it, and at the endings, before you decide.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-laysa" title="Mistake 5: the wrong case for the khabar">
  <Stage label="Made-up examples">
    <Tiles
      label="The khabar after laysa"
      items={[
        { ar: 'لَيْسَ مُؤْمِنًا', en: 'right: khabar accusative', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'لَيْسَ مُؤْمِنٌ', en: 'wrong: khabar nominative', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'مَا هُوَ مُؤْمِنًا', en: 'right: مَا like laysa, khabar accusative', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'وَلَآ أَنتُمْ عَٰبِدُونَ', en: 'right: a plain لَا, khabar nominative (109:3)', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The khabar of laysa is accusative. So is the khabar of a مَا that works like laysa. A khabar after إِنْ is nominative, because إِنْ changes nothing.

To say “he is not a believer” you can use لَيْسَ مُؤْمِنًا or مَا هُوَ مُؤْمِنًا. The plain لَا is different. In وَلَآ أَنتُمْ عَٰبِدُونَ (109:3) it stands before a pronoun and leaves the khabar in the nominative.

So the case of the khabar tells you which word came before it: the accusative for laysa and a مَا like it, the nominative for إِنْ and a plain لَا.
</Block>
</Step>

  <Stage label="For each word, say">

      <li><b>The time:</b> the past, now, the future or a ban?</li>

Work out each answer first, on paper or in your head. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Say which word says “not”, what it does to the word after it, and the time. (a) لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ (Al-Ikhlas 112:3) (b) لَآ أَعْبُدُ مَا تَعْبُدُونَ (Al-Kafirun 109:2) (c) وَلَن نُّؤْمِنَ لِرُقِيِّكَ (Al-Isra 17:93, part) (d) لَّيْسَ ٱلْبِرَّ أَن تُوَلُّوا۟ وُجُوهَكُمْ (Al-Baqarah 2:177, part)">

- (a) **لَمْ**: jazm. يَلِدْ and يُولَدْ end in a sukūn. The plain forms are يَلِدُ and يُولَدُ. The time is the past: “He did not beget, and was not begotten.”
- (b) **لَا**: nothing. أَعْبُدُ keeps its plain -u. “I do not worship”: a statement about now. The مَا in the verse is the joining noun, “what”, not a negative.
- (c) **لَن**: naṣb. نُؤْمِنَ ends in a fatḥa. It looks ahead: “we will not believe”. حَتَّىٰ sets an end: “until you bring down …”.
- (d) **لَيْسَ**: it works on nouns. ٱلْبِرَّ is accusative (-a), so it is the khabar, put first. The ism is the clause أَن تُوَلُّوا۟ وُجُوهَكُمْ (L2.11).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Change the sentence to use the word in brackets, and say what changes. The sentences are made-up examples. (a) لَمْ يَفْعَلْ, “he did not do”, with مَا (b) لَا يَفْعَلُ, “he does not do”, with لَن (c) هُوَ مُؤْمِنٌ, “he is a believer”, made negative with لَيْسَ (d) تَفْعَلُ, “you do”, made into a ban">

- (a) **مَا فَعَلَ**. The verb changes from a present form with a sukūn to a past verb. Both say “he did not do”. Neither is more formal.
- (b) **لَنْ يَفْعَلَ**. The ending changes from -u to -a (naṣb). The time moves ahead: “he will not do”.
- (c) **لَيْسَ مُؤْمِنًا**. The khabar changes from -un to -an. The ism “he” is hidden inside لَيْسَ. You can also write لَيْسَ هُوَ مُؤْمِنًا.
- (d) **لَا تَفْعَلْ**. The ending changes from -u to a sukūn, and the sentence becomes a ban: “do not do”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Choose the word for each meaning, and say what the verb or noun looks like. All five sentences are made-up examples. (a) “They will not enter the garden.” (b) “The messenger is not a liar.” (c) “I do not lie.” (d) “Do not lie.” (e) “He has not come yet.”">

- (a) **لَنْ يَدْخُلُوا ٱلْجَنَّةَ**. لَن, the future, with naṣb: يَدْخُلُونَ lost its nūn.
- (b) **لَيْسَ ٱلرَّسُولُ كَاذِبًا**. laysa: ٱلرَّسُولُ is the ism (-u) and كَاذِبًا the khabar (-an). مَا هُوَ كَاذِبًا, with مَا like laysa, would also do.
- (c) **لَا أَكْذِبُ**. A plain “not” with the plain -u.
- (d) **لَا تَكْذِبْ**. A ban: the verb ends in a sukūn.
- (e) **لَمَّا يَأْتِ**. “Not yet”, with jazm: the ي of يَأْتِي is gone.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Say the word, the effect and the time for each phrase, then name the root and the form of the verb. (a) أَمْ لَمْ تُنذِرْهُمْ (Al-Baqarah 2:6, part) (b) لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:6, end) (c) وَمَا يَسْتَوِى ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ وَٱلْبَصِيرُ (Fatir 35:19) (d) لَن تَنَالُوا۟ ٱلْبِرَّ حَتَّىٰ تُنفِقُوا۟ (Al-Imran 3:92, part)">

- (a) **لَمْ**: jazm, the past. تُنذِرْهُمْ has a sukūn on the ر. The root is ن ذ ر and it is Form IV (أَنذَرَ). The -hum is the object.
- (b) **لَا**: nothing. يُؤْمِنُونَ keeps its nūn, the plain ending. “They do not believe.” The root is ا م ن, Form IV.
- (c) **مَا**: nothing. يَسْتَوِى is a present verb and the sentence says “are not equal”. The root is س و ي and it is Form VIII. The -u is hidden on the long ī.
- (d) **لَن**: naṣb, the future. تَنَالُوا۟ lost its nūn. The Corpus gives the root as ن ي ل. The verb is Form I. حَتَّىٰ sets an end, and تُنفِقُوا۟ lost its nūn too.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Write each verb as it looks after لَمْ, after لَن and after a plain لَا. The plain forms are: (a) يَكْتُبُ (b) تَكْتُبُونَ (c) يَأْتِي (d) تَكْتُبْنَ">

These are teaching forms, not verses.

| Plain | After لَمْ | After لَن | After a plain لَا |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) يَكْتُبُ | يَكْتُبْ | يَكْتُبَ | يَكْتُبُ |
| (b) تَكْتُبُونَ | تَكْتُبُوا | تَكْتُبُوا | تَكْتُبُونَ |
| (c) يَأْتِي | يَأْتِ | يَأْتِيَ | يَأْتِي |
| (d) تَكْتُبْنَ | تَكْتُبْنَ | تَكْتُبْنَ | تَكْتُبْنَ |

- In (b) لَمْ and لَن give the same form, the lost nūn. The word in front tells which is which.
- In (c) the ي is gone after لَمْ and carries a visible fatḥa after لَن.
- (d) is the women’s plural. Its nūn is part of the form, so nothing changes (L3.07).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Plain “not” or ban? Say which, and what the verb shows. (a) فَأَمَّا ٱلْيَتِيمَ فَلَا تَقْهَرْ (Ad-Duha 93:9) (b) وَلَا تَقْتُلُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ (An-Nisa 4:29, part) (c) إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يَظْلِمُ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ (An-Nisa 4:40, part) (d) رَبَّنَا لَا تُؤَاخِذْنَآ (Al-Baqarah 2:286, part)">

- (a) **A ban**: “do not oppress”. تَقْهَرْ ends in a sukūn. The plain form is تَقْهَرُ.
- (b) **A ban**: “do not kill yourselves”. تَقْتُلُوا۟ lost its nūn. The plain form is تَقْتُلُونَ.
- (c) **A plain “not”**: يَظْلِمُ keeps its plain -u. “Allah does not wrong, even by the weight of an atom.”
- (d) **A ban, spoken as a request**: “our Lord, do not hold us to account”. تُؤَاخِذْنَا ends in a sukūn on the ذ, with the object -nā.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Negative, joining noun or condition? Decide for the مَا in each. (a) مَا وَدَّعَكَ رَبُّكَ (Ad-Duha 93:3, part) (b) لِّلَّهِ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ (Al-Baqarah 2:284, part) (c) وَمَا تُنفِقُوا۟ مِن شَىْءٍ (Al-Imran 3:92, part) (d) مَا هَٰذَا بَشَرًا (Yusuf 12:31, part)">

- (a) **Negative**: “your Lord has not left you”. A past verb follows, and nothing points back to “what”.
- (b) **Joining noun**: “to Allah belongs what is in the heavens”. مَا means “what”.
- (c) **Condition**: “whatever you spend”. تُنفِقُوا۟ lost its nūn, and an answer follows (L4.04).
- (d) **Negative, working like laysa**: بَشَرًا is the khabar, in the accusative.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Give the second word the right ending. The sentences are made-up examples, except (a) and (b), which come from 12:31. (a) مَا هَٰذَا ____ (بَشَرٌ) (b) إِنْ هَٰذَا إِلَّا ____ (مَلَكٌ) (c) لَيْسَ ٱلْوَلَدُ ____ (مَرِيضٌ) (d) لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ (Al-Baqarah 2:2, part) and فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ (Al-Baqarah 2:38, part): which noun has tanwīn?">

- (a) **بَشَرًا**. After مَا that works like laysa, the khabar is accusative.
- (b) **مَلَكٌ**. After إِنْ the khabar is nominative.
- (c) **مَرِيضًا**. After laysa the khabar is accusative: لَيْسَ ٱلْوَلَدُ مَرِيضًا.
- (d) **خَوْفٌ** has tanwīn, so it follows a plain negative. **رَيْبَ** has a fatḥa and none, so it follows the lā of the kind (L4.05).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Is the word غَيْر nominative, accusative or genitive, and why? (a) لَهُمْ أَجْرٌ غَيْرُ (Al-Inshiqaq 84:25, part) (b) عَطَآءً غَيْرَ مَجْذُوذٍ (Hud 11:108, end) Then say the case of the word after غَيْر in each.">

- (a) **Nominative.** It describes أَجْرٌ, which is nominative. The word after it is مَمْنُونٍ, genitive.
- (b) **Accusative.** It describes عَطَآءً, which is accusative. The word after it is مَجْذُوذٍ, genitive.

The word after غَيْر is genitive in both, because غَيْر starts an idafah (L4.05).

</Exercise>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="a word of negation changes what comes after it"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'word of negation', small: 'eight words' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'the word after it', small: 'jazm, naṣb or nothing' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'negative sentence', small: 'جُمْلَة مَنْفِيَّة' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- **Jazm:** لَمْ (the past), لَمَّا (“not yet”) and the لَا of a ban. **Naṣb:** لَن (the future). **Nothing:** the plain لَا, مَا and إِنْ.
- **On nouns:** لَيْسَ gives the khabar the accusative, and so does a مَا that works like laysa. The lā that denies the kind goes with a noun built on a fatḥa. إِنْ and a plain لَا leave the nouns alone.
- **Time:** لَمْ looks back and لَن looks ahead. مَا and the plain لَا take the time from the verb and the sentence.
- غَيْر is a noun. It follows the case of the word it describes, and the word after it is genitive.
- The same letters can be different words: مَا (negative, joining noun, condition), إِنْ (condition, negative, inna), لَا (plain “not”, ban, the lā of the kind) and لَمَّا (“when”, “not yet”). The ending of the next word and the sentence decide.
