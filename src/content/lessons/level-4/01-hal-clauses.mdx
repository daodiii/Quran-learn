This is Musa, leaving a city. Read the verse from right to left.

The verb فَخَرَجَ means “so he left”. With the words مِنْهَا, “from it”, the sentence is already complete: “So he left it.”

Two more words say **how** he left: خَآئِفًا (afraid) and يَتَرَقَّبُ (watching out). Each one is a **ḥāl**, حَال, “state”. A ḥāl says in what state the doer was while the action happened.

English does this with an -ing word or with “while”: “he left afraid”, “he left while watching out”.

<Block id="name" title="What the grammar book says">
  <Stage label="From al-Ajurrumiyyah">
    <blockquote class="lx-quote" lang="ar" dir="rtl">
      <p>اَلْحَالُ هُوَ الِاسْمُ الْمَنْصُوبُ الْمُفَسِّرُ لِمَا انْبَهَمَ مِنَ الْهَيْئَاتِ، نَحْوُ قَوْلِكَ: جَاءَ زَيْدٌ رَاكِبًا</p>
    </blockquote>
    <p class="lx-quote-note">Vowel marks added by Quran Learn.</p>
  </Stage>

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* is a short grammar book by Ibn Ājurrūm (died 723 AH). It has a chapter on the ḥāl. Here is its definition in plain English: the ḥāl is a noun in the accusative that makes clear a state (هَيْئَة) that was left unclear.

Its example is جَاءَ زَيْدٌ رَاكِبًا, “Zayd came riding”. Without رَاكِبًا you know that Zayd came. You do not know how. The ḥāl fills that gap.

The word ḥāl means “state” or “condition”. It names a job in the sentence, like the doer or the object.

<Note kind="know">The book speaks about a **noun** as a ḥāl. The Quran also uses a whole sentence or a phrase for the same job. Step 2 shows all three.</Note>

</Block>

Every ḥāl describes someone. That someone is the **ṣāḥib al-ḥāl**, صَاحِب الْحَال, “the owner of the state”.

In 28:21 the owner of خَآئِفًا is the doer of the verb: the hidden “he”. In 2:119 the owner of بَشِيرًا is the **object**: the -ka of أَرْسَلْنَٰكَ, “you”. L1.06 parsed this verse and called بَشِيرًا a ḥāl. Al-Jadwal says it is a ḥāl of the object.

So the owner can be the doer or the object. To find it, ask: who was in this state while the action happened?

The ḥāl usually matches its owner in number and gender. One person, so بَشِيرًا is singular. Step 2 has a ḥāl for many men.

<Block id="conditions" title="Four rules from the book">
  <Stage label="Al-Ajurrumiyyah, in four words">
    <Tiles
      label="What the book says about a ḥāl"
      items={[
        { ar: 'اسْمٌ', en: 'a noun', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'مَنْصُوبٌ', en: 'accusative', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'نَكِرَةٌ', en: 'indefinite', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'بَعْدَ تَمَامِ الْكَلَامِ', en: 'after the sentence is complete', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The definition and the lines after it give four rules. Test them on خَآئِفًا in 28:21.

- **The ḥāl is accusative.** خَآئِفًا ends in -an (tanwīn fatḥa).
- **The ḥāl is indefinite.** خَآئِفًا has no ٱلْ. The tanwīn shows it.
- **It comes after the sentence is complete.** فَخَرَجَ مِنْهَا is already a full sentence. The ḥāl is an extra.
- **The owner is definite.** The owner of خَآئِفًا is the hidden “he”, a pronoun. A pronoun is definite. The owner of بَشِيرًا is -ka, also a pronoun.

“Complete” gives you a quick test: take the ḥāl out. If a full sentence is left, it was a ḥāl. If the sentence breaks, the word was needed.

A whole sentence or a phrase has no ending to show the accusative. The grammar books say it stands “in the place of the accusative” (فِي مَحَلِّ نَصْبٍ). Step 2 shows this.

<Note kind="know">The book says the owner is definite. This lesson teaches that normal case. Other grammar books list a few exceptions. They are not taught here.</Note>

<Check n={4} q="Which can be a ḥāl, by the book's rules: ضَاحِكًا or ٱلضَّاحِكُ?" options={['ضَاحِكًا', 'ٱلضَّاحِكُ']} answer={0} why="A ḥāl is indefinite and accusative. ضَاحِكًا has no ٱلْ and ends in -an. ٱلضَّاحِكُ has ٱلْ, so it would be an adjective." />
</Block>
</Step>

The first kind is **one word**. In 39:73 the word is خَٰلِدِينَ, “staying for ever”. It is a doer word (L3.19) from the root خ ل د.

Look at its ending. The owner is “you all” (men), so the ḥāl is plural. A doer word in the plural ends in -ūna when it is nominative, and in **-īna** when it is accusative. خَٰلِدِينَ has -īna. That is the accusative of L2.05.

The made-up sentence has the same pattern as the Quran: verb, doer, ḥāl. مُسْرِعًا is from أَسْرَعَ, “he hurried”, and it ends in -an. It is not a verse.

<Note kind="know">Some words end in an alif, like كُسَالَىٰ, “lazy”, in 4:142. The alif hides the fatḥa, so you cannot see the ending. Al-Jadwal says the fatḥa is there but not seen. It is a ḥāl all the same.</Note>

</Block>

        { role: 'hal', label: 'ḥāl: a sentence', hint: 'a present verb, and its doer is “they”', words: [{ ar: 'يَبْكُونَ', en: 'weeping', say: 'yabkuna', type: 'v' }] },
      ]}
      en={['And they came to their father in the evening, weeping.']}
    />

The second kind is a **whole sentence**. يَبْكُونَ is a present verb. Its doer, “they”, is inside the verb, so it is a full sentence: “they are weeping”.

Al-Jadwal says this sentence is a ḥāl of the doer of جَآءُوٓ. A sentence has no ending, so it stands in the place of the accusative.

This sentence has **no وَ**. The hidden “they” inside يَبْكُونَ goes back to the brothers, the owner. That link joins the ḥāl to its owner.

The root of يَبْكُونَ is ب ك ي. The last root letter ي drops before the ending -ūna. You can see it in يَبْكِي, “he weeps”. The form without it is يَبْكُونَ, on the pattern يَفْعُونَ. L4.13 teaches this kind of verb.

A verse can have more than one ḥāl. In 28:21 Al-Jadwal calls يَتَرَقَّبُ a second ḥāl, after خَآئِفًا.

The word عِشَآءً is not a ḥāl. It is a time word: it says when. Step 3 comes back to it.

</Block>

A ḥāl can also be a **nominal sentence**: a starting point and its news (L2.01). In 4:43 it is أَنتُمْ سُكَٰرَىٰ, “you are drunk”.

The sentence starts with a وَ. This is the **wāw al-ḥāl**, وَاو الْحَال. It means “while”, not “and”. Al-Jadwal calls it “wāw ḥāliyya”, and the Quranic Arabic Corpus tags it as a circumstantial wāw. Here the sentence is a ḥāl of the doer of تَقْرَبُوا.

The pronoun أَنتُمْ goes back to the owner, “you all”. With nominal sentences, the pattern is: **وَ, then a pronoun for the owner, then the news.**

The made-up sentence has the same pattern: وَهُوَ يَضْحَكُ. Here the news is a verb sentence, يَضْحَكُ, “laughs”.

L2.02 and L1.08 showed this pattern before it had a name: وَأَنتُم مُّسْلِمُونَ (3:102) and وَأَنتُم مُّعْرِضُونَ (2:83). Both mean “while you are …”.

A ḥāl can start with a **past verb**. In 5:61 it is دَّخَلُوا۟, “they entered”. It comes after وَ and the little word قَد, which here means “already”.

The sentence says what was true while they spoke: they had already come in with unbelief. Al-Jadwal says it is a ḥāl of the doer of قَالُوٓا۟. The Corpus also tags the وَ as circumstantial, and قَد as a word of certainty.

The wāw al-ḥāl with a past verb comes with قَد. This is the usual pattern in these verses. It is a teaching summary, not a rule taken from a source.

The part ءَامَنَّا, “we believed”, is what they said. It is not a ḥāl.

<Note kind="know">The verse goes on: وَهُمْ قَدْ خَرَجُوا۟ بِهِۦ, “and they have gone out with it”. Al-Jadwal joins this to the first ḥāl, so there are two.</Note>

With the wāw al-ḥāl, the sentence says what was already true." />
</Block>

The third kind is a **phrase**: a preposition and its noun. The Arabic grammar books call it شِبْه جُمْلَة, “like a sentence”.

In 28:79 the phrase is فِى زِينَتِهِۦ, “in his finery”. It says how Qarun went out. Al-Jadwal reads it as a ḥāl of the doer of خَرَجَ, and he paraphrases it with the word مُتَزَيِّنًا, “adorned”.

That paraphrase is a good test. A phrase is a ḥāl when you can swap it for a describing word and the sentence still says the same.

The other phrase in the verse is different. عَلَىٰ قَوْمِهِۦ, “to his people”, is tied to the verb خَرَجَ. It says where he went, so it completes the verb. It is not a ḥāl.

Most ḥāls name a state that changes: afraid, laughing, hurrying. A few name a state that stays.

In 2:91 the Quran is the truth, and it always confirms the earlier books. Al-Jadwal calls مُصَدِّقًا a **ḥāl muʾakkida**, حَال مُؤَكِّدَة, a “confirming” ḥāl. It does not describe a passing state. It says again what is already true.

The same is true in وَخُلِقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ ضَعِيفًا (4:28): “and the human being was created weak”. Al-Jadwal calls ضَعِيفًا a ḥāl. A person is weak from the start, so this state does not pass. L3.09 met this verse.

So a ḥāl is not always a **temporary** state. The better test is the job: it describes its owner while the action happens.

An adjective (naʿt) and a ḥāl can look alike. Both describe and both can end in -an. The difference is in the definiteness.

An adjective **copies** its noun (L2.09). ٱلرَّجُلُ has ٱلْ, so ٱلْكَرِيمُ has ٱلْ too, and it copies the ending: it is a naʿt. In جَآءَ رَجُلٌ ضَاحِكٌ the noun is indefinite, so the describing word is indefinite too: also a naʿt.

A ḥāl does **not** copy. The owner is definite (ٱلرَّجُلُ, a pronoun, a name) and the ḥāl is indefinite and accusative. So a describing word without ٱلْ after a definite noun is read as a ḥāl. L2.09 warned that كَبِيرًا without ٱلْ would be read this way.

In 12:2, قُرْءَٰنًا is a ḥāl and عَرَبِيًّا is its naʿt: the adjective describes the ḥāl. Al-Jadwal also says قُرْءَٰنًا is a fixed word (not made from a verb), and that Arabic allows a fixed word as a ḥāl when it can be read as a describing word. He calls it a ḥāl muwaṭṭiʾa. Here the sense is “as a Quran, Arabic”. L2.10 met this verse.

<Block id="tamyiz" title="Another look-alike: tamyīz">
  <Stage label="Two accusative words">
    <Tiles
      label="Both are indefinite and accusative"
      items={[
        { ar: 'حَال', en: 'answers: in what state?', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'تَمْيِيز', en: 'answers: of what? in what way? (L4.02)', type: 'x' },

    />

A **tamyīz** (تَمْيِيز, “specifier”) is another word that is indefinite and accusative. It makes a vague word clear. L4.02 teaches it.

The two jobs ask different questions. A ḥāl says **in what state** the owner was while the action happened. A tamyīz says **of what, or in what way** something is true.

An example is in 28:78. The words أَشَدُّ مِنْهُ قُوَّةً mean “stronger than him in strength”. Al-Jadwal calls قُوَّةً a tamyīz. It does not describe someone while he acts. It says in what way he is stronger.

Some words can be read both ways. In 4:69 Al-Jadwal reads رَفِيقًا as a tamyīz, and al-Durr al-Maṣūn gives a second reading as a ḥāl. L3.04 has the note. L4.02 teaches the test.

Not every word after the verb is a ḥāl. Four look-alikes:

- **A time word.** عِشَآءً in 12:16 says when. Al-Jadwal calls it a time word (ظَرْف) tied to the verb. L2.05 said the same of يَوْمًا in 2:19. A ḥāl says how, not when.
- **A place phrase.** فِي ٱلْفَصْلِ says where. It completes the verb جَلَسَ, so it is tied to the verb and is not a ḥāl. You cannot swap it for a word like “adorned”.
- **The answer of “when”.** In 12:63, قَالُوا۟ is the answer of فَلَمَّا: “when they returned, they said”. Al-Jadwal says it is a new sentence with no place of its own. It does not describe how they returned.
- **A new sentence.** In 12:19 the sentence قَالَ يَٰبُشْرَىٰ starts after the drawer lets down his bucket. It tells what he said next. It is not a ḥāl (see exercise 6).

The rule of thumb: a ḥāl is part of the first sentence. It describes the owner **while** the action happens. A new sentence says what happens **next**.

<Step id="irab" label="Mistakes and parsing" title="Four mistakes, and four verses word by word">
<Block id="mistake-definite" title="Mistake 1: a ḥāl with ٱلْ, or an owner without it">
  <Stage label="Look at ٱلْ">
    <Tiles
      label="Made-up examples"
      items={[
        { ar: 'جَآءَ ٱلطَّالِبُ ضَاحِكًا', en: 'ḥāl: the student came laughing', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'جَآءَ ٱلطَّالِبُ ٱلضَّاحِكُ', en: 'naʿt: the laughing student came', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A ḥāl is indefinite. If you add ٱلْ and copy the ending, the word becomes an adjective. The meaning changes: not “came laughing” but “the laughing one came”, which tells you which student.

The other side matters too. The owner is normally definite. With an indefinite noun, the describing word copies it and is a naʿt: جَآءَ رَجُلٌ ضَاحِكٌ, “a laughing man came”.

Check the two sides together. A ḥāl has no ٱلْ and is accusative. Its owner has ٱلْ or is a pronoun or a name.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-ending" title="Mistake 2: the wrong ending on a one-word ḥāl">
  <Stage label="Word or sentence">
    <Tiles
      label="Made-up examples"
      items={[
        { ar: 'خَرَجَ ٱلْقَوْمُ فَرِحِينَ', en: 'one word, accusative: the people left rejoicing', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'خَرَجَ ٱلْقَوْمُ وَهُمْ فَرِحُونَ', en: 'a sentence: the people left while they were rejoicing', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A one-word ḥāl is accusative: فَرِحِينَ, with -īna. If you want the nominative فَرِحُونَ, the word is the news of a nominal sentence. Then it needs the wāw and the pronoun: وَهُمْ فَرِحُونَ.

Do not write خَرَجَ ٱلْقَوْمُ فَرِحُونَ. It looks like a ḥāl, but it has the nominative ending of a doer word and nothing to join it to the sentence.

For a word like كُسَالَىٰ, where an alif hides the ending, remember that the fatḥa is there but not seen.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-waw" title="Mistake 3: reading the wāw al-ḥāl as “and”">
  <Stage label="Two meanings of وَ">
    <Tiles
      label="One pattern: وَ + pronoun + news"
      items={[
        { ar: 'وَأَنتُمْ سُكَٰرَىٰ', en: 'while you are drunk (4:43)', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'وَهُوَ يَضْحَكُ', en: 'while he laughs (made up)', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A وَ can mean “and”: it joins two things. It can also be the wāw al-ḥāl: it starts a sentence that says in what state the owner was.

Look for the pattern: **وَ, then a pronoun that goes back to the owner, then the news.** وَأَنتُمْ سُكَٰرَىٰ is “while you are drunk”, not “and you are drunk”. The prayer verse does not say “come near the prayer and be drunk”.

Translate the wāw al-ḥāl as “while”, and the sentence reads right.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-temporary" title="Mistake 4: ruling out a ḥāl because the state lasts">
  <Stage label="States that stay">
    <Tiles
      label="Two lasting states"
      items={[
        { ar: 'ضَعِيفًا', en: 'weak (4:28)', type: 'n' },
        { ar: 'مُصَدِّقًا', en: 'confirming (2:91)', type: 'n' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Many books say “a ḥāl is a passing state”. That is the usual case, not the rule. Al-Jadwal calls ضَعِيفًا in 4:28 a ḥāl, and مُصَدِّقًا in 2:91 a ḥāl muʾakkida.

So do not throw out a word because its state lasts. Ask the real questions: is it indefinite and accusative (or a sentence or phrase in that place)? Does it describe an owner while the action happens? Is the sentence complete without it?
</Block>

<Step id="practice" label="Practice" title="Practice">
<Block id="practice-1" title="Practice: find it and name it">
  <Stage label="For each sentence, ask">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Complete?</b> Is there a full sentence without it?</li>
      <li><b>Owner:</b> who was in this state?</li>
      <li><b>Kind:</b> a word, a sentence or a phrase?</li>
      <li><b>Not a ḥāl?</b> Time, place, adjective or new sentence?</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

Try each question first, on paper or in your head. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Is the last word a ḥāl or a naʿt? جَآءَ ٱلْمُعَلِّمُ ٱلْجَدِيدُ (made-up sentence)">

**ٱلْجَدِيدُ**, “the new one”, is a **naʿt** (adjective).

- It has ٱلْ, like ٱلْمُعَلِّمُ. A ḥāl is indefinite.
- It copies the nominative ending of ٱلْمُعَلِّمُ. A ḥāl is accusative.
- It says which teacher: the new one. It does not say in what state he came.

“The new teacher came.” It does not mean “the teacher came newly”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Find the ḥāl and its owner: جَآءَ ٱلطُّلَّابُ مُسْرِعِينَ (made-up sentence)">

**مُسْرِعِينَ**, “hurrying”, is the ḥāl.

- It has no ٱلْ, and the ending -īna is accusative (the doer is nominative).
- The owner is **ٱلطُّلَّابُ**, “the students”. It is masculine plural and so is the ḥāl.
- You can add “while”: “the students came while hurrying”.
- The root is س ر ع. مُسْرِعِينَ is a doer word from أَسْرَعَ (Form IV), on the pattern مُفْعِلِينَ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Name the ḥāl, its kind and its owner: وَدَخَلَ ٱلْمَدِينَةَ عَلَىٰ حِينِ غَفْلَةٍ مِّنْ أَهْلِهَا (Al-Qasas 28:15, part)">

The ḥāl is **عَلَىٰ حِينِ غَفْلَةٍ**, “at a time of heedlessness”: a phrase (shibh jumla). It says in what state he entered.

Al-Jadwal reads it as a ḥāl of the **doer** of دَخَلَ: the hidden “he”, Musa. The heedlessness belongs to the people, as مِّنْ أَهْلِهَا, “from its people”, says. The ḥāl still describes the one who enters: he enters the city while its people are not paying attention.

Al-Durr al-Maṣūn calls it a ḥāl too. It allows the owner to be the doer, and also the object, ٱلْمَدِينَةَ. The role is the same in both readings. The lesson follows Al-Jadwal.

The object ٱلْمَدِينَةَ is not a ḥāl. It is the thing he enters.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Change the one-word ḥāl into a sentence ḥāl with the wāw al-ḥāl: خَرَجَ ٱلْقَوْمُ فَرِحِينَ (made-up sentence)">

**خَرَجَ ٱلْقَوْمُ وَهُمْ فَرِحُونَ**, “the people left while they were rejoicing”.

- **وَ** is the wāw al-ḥāl, “while”.
- **هُمْ** is a pronoun that goes back to ٱلْقَوْمُ.
- **فَرِحُونَ** is now the news of a nominal sentence, so it is nominative (-ūna), not accusative (-īna).
- The root is ف ر ح, “joy”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Find the ḥāl and say its kind: إِنَّا ذَهَبْنَا نَسْتَبِقُ (Yusuf 12:17, part)">

**نَسْتَبِقُ**, “racing”, is a ḥāl: a sentence with a present verb. Al-Jadwal reads it as a ḥāl of the doer of ذَهَبْنَا.

- The owner is the -nā of ذَهَبْنَا, “we”.
- The doer of نَسْتَبِقُ is “we”, so the verb goes back to the owner. There is no wāw.
- The root is س ب ق. The verb is Form VIII (L3.14): ٱسْتَبَقَ, on the pattern نَفْتَعِلُ in the present for “we”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Which look-alike is it? In فَأَدْلَىٰ دَلْوَهُۥ قَالَ يَٰبُشْرَىٰ هَٰذَا غُلَٰمٌ (Yusuf 12:19, part), is قَالَ يَٰبُشْرَىٰ a ḥāl?">

**No.** It is a new sentence: the drawer lets down his bucket, then he says “Good news!”.

- It does not describe how he let down the bucket while he did it.
- It tells what happened next.
- Al-Jadwal says the sentence قَالَ is an explaining sentence with no place of its own. It does not call it a ḥāl.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Exercise n={7} q="Is ضَعِيفًا a ḥāl? وَخُلِقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ ضَعِيفًا (An-Nisa 4:28, part). Say its owner.">

**Yes.** Al-Jadwal calls ضَعِيفًا a ḥāl.

- It is indefinite and accusative (-an), and the sentence is complete without it.
- The owner is **ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ**, “the human being”. The verb is passive (L3.09), so ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ stands in for the doer.
- The state is not temporary. A person is weak from creation. So a ḥāl does not have to pass.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Find the two ḥāls: وَإِذَا قَامُوٓا۟ إِلَى ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ قَامُوا۟ كُسَالَىٰ يُرَآءُونَ ٱلنَّاسَ (An-Nisa 4:142, part)">

- **كُسَالَىٰ**, “lazy”: a one-word ḥāl. It ends in an alif, so the fatḥa is not seen. The owner is the doer of قَامُوا۟ the second time: “they”.
- **يُرَآءُونَ ٱلنَّاسَ**, “showing off to people”: a sentence ḥāl with a present verb. Its doer, “they”, goes back to the owner. There is no wāw.

Al-Jadwal reads the second one as a ḥāl, and allows a second reading: a new sentence with no place of its own. The first, كُسَالَىٰ, is clear.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="In وَجَآءُوٓ أَبَاهُمْ عِشَآءً يَبْكُونَ (Yusuf 12:16), which word is the ḥāl and which is not? Say which question each answers.">

- **يَبْكُونَ** is the ḥāl. It answers “in what state did they come?”: weeping.
- **عِشَآءً** is a time word, not a ḥāl. It answers “when?”: in the evening. Al-Jadwal ties it to the verb.
- **أَبَاهُمْ** is the object. It answers “whom?”.

Remove each and see: without يَبْكُونَ or عِشَآءً the sentence is still complete. The question each answers tells them apart.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Which kind of ḥāl is each? (a) بَشِيرًا (Al-Baqarah 2:119) (b) وَأَنتُمْ سُكَٰرَىٰ (An-Nisa 4:43) (c) فِى زِينَتِهِۦ (Al-Qasas 28:79) (d) وَقَد دَّخَلُوا۟ بِٱلْكُفْرِ (Al-Maidah 5:61) (e) يَبْكُونَ (Yusuf 12:16)">

- (a) **One word**. It describes the object -ka.
- (b) **A sentence**, a nominal sentence with wāw al-ḥāl.
- (c) **A phrase** (shibh jumla). It is like “adorned”.
- (d) **A sentence** with a past verb, wāw al-ḥāl and qad.
- (e) **A sentence** with a present verb and no wāw.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={11} q="Build it. Start from جَآءَ ٱلرَّجُلُ (made-up). Add a one-word ḥāl, “laughing”, and then a sentence ḥāl with wāw, “while he was laughing”.">

- One word: **جَآءَ ٱلرَّجُلُ ضَاحِكًا**. The ḥāl has no ٱلْ and ends in -an.
- A sentence with wāw: **جَآءَ ٱلرَّجُلُ وَهُوَ يَضْحَكُ**. وَ is the wāw al-ḥāl, هُوَ goes back to ٱلرَّجُلُ, and يَضْحَكُ is the news.
- A sentence without wāw is also possible with a present verb: **جَآءَ ٱلرَّجُلُ يَضْحَكُ**, as in يَبْكُونَ in 12:16.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={12} q="What is the root of يَبْكُونَ in 12:16, and where did the last root letter go?">

The root is **ب ك ي**. The last root letter ي does not show in يَبْكُونَ. It drops before the ending -ūna. You see it in the singular يَبْكِي, “he weeps”.

The pattern is يَفْعُونَ. The root has three letters, but the third one does not show, and the ḍamma stays before the wāw. L4.13 teaches this kind of verb.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="a sentence plus a state"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'the action', small: 'who did what' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'the state', small: 'how, while it happened' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'a ḥāl', small: 'حَال' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A **ḥāl** says in what state the owner (ṣāḥib al-ḥāl) was while the action happened. The owner is the doer or the object.
- Al-Ajurrumiyyah gives the rules: a noun, accusative, indefinite, after a complete sentence, and a definite owner.
- It has **three kinds**: one word (خَٰلِدِينَ), a whole sentence (يَبْكُونَ, وَأَنتُمْ سُكَٰرَىٰ, وَقَد دَّخَلُوا۟) and a phrase (فِى زِينَتِهِۦ). A sentence or a phrase stands in the place of the accusative.
- A nominal sentence ḥāl starts with the **wāw al-ḥāl**, “while”, and a pronoun that goes back to the owner.
- A ḥāl is not always a passing state: ضَعِيفًا and مُصَدِّقًا name lasting states.
- It is not an adjective (the adjective copies the noun), not a time or place word (when, where), and not a new sentence (what happened next).
