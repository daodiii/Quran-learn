Zakariyya speaks to his Lord about his old age. Read the line from right to left.

ٱشْتَعَلَ means “lit up”, like a fire. ٱلرَّأْسُ is “the head”. “The head lit up” is a full sentence, but it leaves a question: lit up with what? The last word answers it: شَيْبًا, “white hair”.

That answering word is a **tamyīz** (تَمْيِيز). The word means “making a distinction”. L2.05 showed this verse and said Level 4 would teach it.

<Block id="name" title="What the grammar book says">
  <Stage label="From al-Ajurrumiyyah">
    <blockquote class="lx-quote" lang="ar" dir="rtl">
      <p>اَلتَّمْيِيزُ هُوَ الِاسْمُ الْمَنْصُوبُ الْمُفَسِّرُ لِمَا انْبَهَمَ مِنَ الذَّوَاتِ</p>
    </blockquote>
    <p class="lx-quote-note">Vowel marks added by Quran Learn.</p>
  </Stage>

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* has a chapter on the tamyīz. It says that the tamyīz is the **accusative noun** that explains something **vague** (انْبَهَمَ) among **things** (الذَّوَات).

The same book defines the ḥāl in almost the same words. The difference is one word: the ḥāl explains what is vague among **states** (الْهَيْئَات). Step 4 comes back to this.

The vague thing is called the **mumayyaz**. Sometimes it is one word, like a number. In 19:4 it is the whole statement “the head lit up”.

Al-Jadwal separates two kinds. In a **tamyīz mufrad** one word is vague (a number, a measure). In a **tamyīz jumla** the sentence is vague, as in 19:4. Step 2 teaches the first kind and step 3 the second.

<Check n={2} q="What does the tamyīz explain, in the book’s definition?" options={['Something vague among things', 'Something vague among states', 'Something vague among sounds']} answer={0} why="The tamyīz explains what is vague among things (الذَّوَات). The ḥāl explains what is vague among states." />
</Block>

<Block id="rules" title="Three marks of a tamyīz">
  <Stage label="Three words, one ending">
    <Tiles
      label="Three tamyīz words from this lesson"
      items={[
        { ar: 'شَيْبًا', en: 'white hair' },
        { ar: 'ذَهَبًا', en: 'gold' },
        { ar: 'كَوْكَبًا', en: 'a star' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The same chapter adds two rules in one line: a tamyīz is always indefinite, and it comes only after a sentence is complete (لَا يَكُونُ إِلَّا نَكِرَةً وَلَا يَكُونُ إِلَّا بَعْدَ تَمَامِ الْكَلَامِ; vowel marks added by Quran Learn).

So a tamyīz has three marks.

- **Accusative.** The three words above all end in -an: the accusative with tanwīn (L2.05).
- **Indefinite.** It has no ٱلْـ. It names a kind of thing, not a known one.
- **After a complete sentence.** “The head lit up” already makes sense. The tamyīz comes last and adds the answer.

<Note kind="know">A tamyīz is usually singular, even when English needs a plural. Step 2 shows this with numbers.</Note>

<Check n={3} q="Which mark does a tamyīz NOT have?" options={['Accusative ending', 'No ٱلْـ', 'Genitive ending']} answer={2} why="A tamyīz is accusative and indefinite. The genitive is not its case." />
</Block>
</Step>

    />

The verse is about people who die as unbelievers. مِلْءُ ٱلْأَرْضِ means “enough to fill the earth”. That is a measure, and a measure is vague: enough of what? The tamyīz answers: ذَهَبًا, “gold”.

English puts “of” between the two words: “a pound of honey”. Arabic puts the tamyīz straight after, in the accusative. Weights, volumes, lengths and areas work in the same way. Al-Jadwal lists them in a note on this topic: a number, a weight, a volume, an area and a length can each be explained by a tamyīz.

- **Made-up example:** اشْتَرَيْتُ رِطْلًا عَسَلًا, “I bought a pound of honey”. رِطْلًا is the measure and عَسَلًا is the tamyīz.

Al-Jadwal adds that after a measure you can also say it with **min** (رِطْلًا مِنْ عَسَلٍ) or as an **idafah** (رِطْلَ عَسَلٍ). The accusative tamyīz is the one this lesson is about.

</Block>

This is Yusuf telling his father about his dream. أَحَدَ عَشَرَ is “eleven”. It is two words, and both end in -a. The ending does not change with the case, so the pair is fixed on fatḥa. Here it is the object of رَأَيْتُ.

“Eleven” alone is vague, so the tamyīz follows: كَوْكَبًا, “a star”. Look at the form. It is **singular**: literally “eleven star”. English says “stars”, but Arabic keeps the singular.

The rule for 11 to 19 is: the counted word is a singular noun in the accusative.

<Note kind="know">The number word changes with the gender of the counted thing, and the teens have their own rules. L4.16 (<a href="/learn/level-4/16-numbers-counting/">numbers and counting</a>) teaches them. Here you only need the case and the singular.</Note>

The tens (عِشْرُونَ, ثَلَاثُونَ, أَرْبَعُونَ and so on up to تِسْعُونَ) look like the sound masculine plural of L1.08: -ūna in the nominative, -īna in the accusative and genitive. In 2:51 أَرْبَعِينَ ends in -īna. Al-Jadwal calls it the second object of the verb.

After the tens, and for every number up to ninety-nine, the tamyīz is again a **singular accusative** noun: لَيْلَةً, “a night”. The idea is the same as in 12:4. *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* gives two examples of its own: عِشْرِينَ غُلَامًا, “twenty boys”, and تِسْعِينَ نَعْجَةً, “ninety ewes”.

      ]}
      en={['…and he shaped them into seven heavens.']}

With 3 to 10 the counted word looks different. سَمَٰوَٰتٍ is **plural** and **genitive**, the second word of an idafah: “seven of heavens”. Al-Jadwal parses it this way in 2:29: the number is the first word and سَمَٰوَٰتٍ is the muḍāf ilayh.

So the three marks of step 1 do not fit. This counted word is genitive and plural. The pattern is easy to keep: 3 to 10, an idafah with a plural; 11 to 99, a singular accusative tamyīz.

<Note kind="know">Names differ here. Al-Jadwal parses سَمَٰوَٰتٍ as muḍāf ilayh in 2:29, but its own overview of the tamyīz (a note on 99:7) also lists this genitive plural after 3 to 10 as a kind of tamyīz. The definition in *al-Ajurrumiyyah* (an accusative noun) does not cover this genitive word. This lesson follows the book’s definition and al-Jadwal’s parse of the verse.</Note>

This verse has both patterns side by side. Nuh stayed among his people “a thousand years, except fifty years”.

- **أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ**: 1000 takes a **singular genitive** word, an idafah. So does 100. Al-Jadwal parses سَنَةٍ as the muḍāf ilayh.
- **خَمْسِينَ عَامًا**: 50 takes a **singular accusative** tamyīz. Al-Jadwal parses عَامًا as tamyīz.

Check them with the marks from step 1: عَامًا is accusative and indefinite, so it is the tamyīz. سَنَةٍ is genitive, so it is not.

This is the verse about the People of the Cave. The last word, تِسْعًا, is the number “nine”. The thing counted, “years”, is **not written**. The reader knows it from سِنِينَ, three words earlier.

So a tamyīz can be understood without being said. Look for a number with no counted word after it, and ask what was counted.

<Note kind="know">Scholars differ on two words in this verse.<br />
**سِنِينَ.** Al-Jadwal and *al-Durr al-Maṣūn* both read it as a *badal* (or *ʿaṭf bayān*) of ثَلَٰثَ مِا۟ئَةٍ, not as a tamyīz. A tamyīz of “hundred” should be a singular genitive in an idafah, and the tanwīn on مِا۟ئَةٍ rules that out. Two readers (Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī) read مِا۟ئَةِ with an idafah to سِنِينَ.<br />
**تِسْعًا.** Al-Jadwal calls it a tamyīz. *Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* calls it the object (mafʿūl bihi) of ٱزْدَادُوا۟, with the counted word “years” left out. Both readings keep the accusative and the meaning “nine years”.</Note>

كَمْ is a word for an unknown or large number, and it needs a tamyīz as well. In 19:74 al-Jadwal reads كَمْ as a word that says “many” (it is not a question here), and it reads مِّن قَرْنٍ as its tamyīz. The tamyīz comes with **min** and is genitive.

Two more examples, made up for practice:

- كَمْ كِتَابًا قَرَأْتَ؟ “How many books did you read?” In a real question with كَمْ, the tamyīz is a singular accusative noun.
- كَذَا دِرْهَمًا, “so many dirhams”. كَذَا also stands for an unspecified number, and its tamyīz is accusative too.

<Note kind="warn">The two made-up examples show the usual pattern, as teachers give it. Quran Learn did not check it against *al-Ajurrumiyyah* or al-Jadwal, so treat it as a tendency.</Note>

<Block id="object" title="Tamyīz or object?">
  <Stage label="Three questions, three jobs">
    <Tiles
      label="Ask the question"
      items={[
        { ar: 'مَفْعُول بِهِ', en: 'whom? what?', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'تَمْيِيز', en: 'of what? in what way?', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'حَال', en: 'how was it?', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

In وَفَجَّرْنَا ٱلْأَرْضَ عُيُونًا two words are accusative after the verb. Ask the questions in order.

- *What did we make burst?* ٱلْأَرْضَ. That is the object.
- *Burst in what way?* عُيُونًا. That is the tamyīz.

The object is the thing the verb acts on. The tamyīz does not take the action. It says of what, or in what way, the statement is true.

The two can be close. The teaching tip from step 3 helps: a tamyīz turned from the doer or the object can often be turned back into “X of Y”.

<Step id="practice" label="Practice" title="Practice">
<Block id="practice-1" title="Practice: find it and say what it does">
  <Stage label="For each word, ask">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Is it a tamyīz?</b> Accusative, indefinite, after a complete sentence.</li>
      <li><b>What is vague?</b> A number, a measure, or the whole statement.</li>
      <li><b>Which question?</b> Of what? In what way?</li>
      <li><b>Not a ḥāl?</b> Does it say how, or of what?</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

Work out each answer first, on paper or in your head. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Made-up example: عِنْدِي عِشْرُونَ كِتَابًا. Find the mumayyaz and the tamyīz, and say why the tamyīz is singular.">

- **Mumayyaz:** عِشْرُونَ, “twenty”. It is vague: twenty of what?
- **Tamyīz:** كِتَابًا, “a book”. It answers the question.
- **Why singular:** after 20 to 99 the counted word is a singular accusative noun. English says “books”.

عِنْدِي means “I have”, literally “at me”. It is not part of the tamyīz.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Tamyīz or ḥāl? (a) جَاءَ ٱلطُّلَّابُ مُسْرِعِينَ (made up) (b) جَاءَ عِشْرُونَ طَالِبًا (made up) (c) رَأَيْتُهُمْ لِى سَٰجِدِينَ (Yusuf 12:4, part) (d) وَضَاقَ بِهِمْ ذَرْعًا (Hud 11:77, part)">

- (a) **ḥāl.** مُسْرِعِينَ says how they came: hurrying. Nothing in “the students came” is vague.
- (b) **tamyīz.** طَالِبًا says what عِشْرُونَ counts: twenty of what? Students.
- (c) **ḥāl.** سَٰجِدِينَ says how the sun and moon were when I saw them.
- (d) **tamyīz.** Al-Jadwal reads ذَرْعًا as a tamyīz. “He was distressed for them” is vague, and ذَرْعًا says in what respect.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Made-up example: ثَلَاثَةُ كِلَابٍ, “three dogs”. Why is كِلَابٍ genitive and plural, and is it a tamyīz?">

كِلَابٍ is the second word of an idafah, so it is **genitive**. After 3 to 10 the counted word is **plural**.

By the definition of *al-Ajurrumiyyah* it is not a tamyīz, because a tamyīz is accusative. Al-Jadwal’s overview also lists this pattern as a kind of tamyīz, so some books use the name more widely. Compare عِشْرُونَ كَلْبًا, “twenty dogs”: كَلْبًا is a singular accusative tamyīz.

The number ثَلَاثَةُ has the ة even though كِلَابٌ is a masculine word. The number follows its own rule (L4.16).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Look at أَنَا۠ أَكْثَرُ مِنكَ مَالًا وَأَعَزُّ نَفَرًا (Al-Kahf 18:34, part). Find the two tamyīz words, say what each makes clear, and say which kind of tamyīz it is.">

- **مَالًا** makes clear أَكْثَرُ: more in what? In wealth.
- **نَفَرًا** makes clear أَعَزُّ: mightier in what? In men.

Both are a **tamyīz jumla** in al-Jadwal’s words: the vague thing is the statement “I am more than you”. Both are accusative and indefinite. The speaker is the owner of the two gardens.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Find the tamyīz and say what it makes clear: (a) هَلْ نُنَبِّئُكُم بِٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ أَعْمَٰلًا (Al-Kahf 18:103, part) (b) فَهِىَ كَٱلْحِجَارَةِ أَوْ أَشَدُّ قَسْوَةً (Al-Baqarah 2:74, part) (c) إِنَّ هَٰذَآ أَخِى لَهُۥ تِسْعٌ وَتِسْعُونَ نَعْجَةً (Sad 38:23, part)">

- (a) **أَعْمَٰلًا**: the losers in what? In deeds. It follows ٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ, a “most” word. Al-Jadwal reads it as a tamyīz.
- (b) **قَسْوَةً**: harder in what? In hardness (of heart). It follows أَشَدُّ.
- (c) **نَعْجَةً**: ninety-nine of what? Ewes (female sheep). It is singular and accusative, as after 99.

(a) and (b) explain a statement; (c) explains a number.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Turn each plain sentence into one with a tamyīz. (a) طَابَتْ نَفْسُ زَيْدٍ, “Zayd’s heart was pleased” (b) مَالِي أَكْثَرُ مِنْ مَالِكَ, “my wealth is more than your wealth”. Then look at وَفَجَّرْنَا ٱلْأَرْضَ عُيُونًا (Al-Qamar 54:12, part): what was its plain form?">

- (a) **طَابَ زَيْدٌ نَفْسًا.** “Zayd” is now the doer, and نَفْسًا is the tamyīz.
- (b) **أَنَا أَكْثَرُ مِنْكَ مَالًا.** “I” is now the topic, and مَالًا is the tamyīz. This is the shape of 18:34.
- 54:12: the plain form would be **فَجَّرْنَا عُيُونَ ٱلْأَرْضِ** (made-up, not in the verse), “we made the springs of the earth burst”. The object “springs” became the tamyīz.

These plain forms are rebuilt for practice, not verses.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Exercise n={7} q="Put the counted word after each number (made-up): (a) twenty men (b) three men (c) eleven days (d) a hundred years (e) fifty years. Say which are a tamyīz.">

- (a) **عِشْرُونَ رَجُلًا**: tamyīz, singular accusative.
- (b) **ثَلَاثَةُ رِجَالٍ**: idafah, plural genitive. Not a tamyīz by the book’s definition.
- (c) **أَحَدَ عَشَرَ يَوْمًا**: tamyīz, singular accusative. (Both parts of the number end in -a.)
- (d) **مِائَةُ سَنَةٍ**: idafah, singular genitive.
- (e) **خَمْسُونَ سَنَةً**: tamyīz, singular accusative.

Only (a), (c) and (e) are a tamyīz in the strict sense.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Look at وَلَبِثُوا۟ فِى كَهْفِهِمْ ثَلَٰثَ مِا۟ئَةٍ سِنِينَ وَٱزْدَادُوا۟ تِسْعًا (Al-Kahf 18:25). Say what سِنِينَ and تِسْعًا are, as al-Jadwal reads them, and what al-Durr al-Maṣūn says about تِسْعًا.">

- **سِنِينَ** is a badal (or ʿaṭf bayān) of ثَلَٰثَ مِا۟ئَةٍ. It is not a tamyīz, because a tamyīz of “hundred” is a singular genitive in an idafah and the tanwīn on مِا۟ئَةٍ blocks that.
- **تِسْعًا** is “nine”. Al-Jadwal calls it a tamyīz. The counted word, years, is understood.
- *Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* calls تِسْعًا the object of ٱزْدَادُوا۟ and says the counted word “years” is left out.

Both books give the same meaning: nine years.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="In إِلَّا مَن سَفِهَ نَفْسَهُۥ (Al-Baqarah 2:130, part) the word نَفْسَهُۥ is accusative. Why is it not read as a tamyīz by the usual rule, and what does al-Jadwal call it?">

A tamyīz is indefinite, and نَفْسَهُۥ has a pronoun on it, so it is definite. The usual rule says no.

Al-Jadwal calls it a **mafʿūl bihi**, an object. *Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* gives seven readings, prefers the object, and says some Kufan grammarians read it as a tamyīz.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Which word is the tamyīz, and in which case is it? (a) كَمْ كِتَابًا قَرَأْتَ؟ (made up) (b) وَكَمْ أَهْلَكْنَا قَبْلَهُم مِّن قَرْنٍ (Maryam 19:74, part)">

- (a) **كِتَابًا**: the tamyīz of كَمْ, a singular accusative noun (the usual pattern; a tendency, not checked).
- (b) **مِّن قَرْنٍ**: the tamyīz of كَمْ in al-Jadwal’s reading. It comes with min, so قَرْنٍ is genitive.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="a vague part plus a tamyīz equals a clear meaning"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'a vague part', small: 'a number, a measure, a statement' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'tamyīz', small: 'accusative, indefinite' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'a clear meaning', small: 'تَمْيِيز' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A **tamyīz** is an accusative, indefinite noun that explains something vague. It comes after a complete sentence. It answers “of what?” or “in what way?”.
- **One vague word** (tamyīz mufrad): a measure, or a number from 11 to 99. After 11 to 99 the tamyīz is a **singular accusative** noun.
- After **3 to 10, 100 and 1000** the counted word is the second word of an idafah (genitive), not a tamyīz in the strict sense.
- **A vague statement** (tamyīz jumla): after “more” words, “became good” and similar verbs. It was often turned from the doer or the object: شَيْبًا, عُيُونًا.
- A **ḥāl** explains a state (how?). A tamyīz explains a thing (of what?).
- Scholars differ on رَفِيقًا (4:69), سِنِينَ and تِسْعًا (18:25) and نَفْسَهُۥ (2:130). The lesson gives the common reading and names the other.
