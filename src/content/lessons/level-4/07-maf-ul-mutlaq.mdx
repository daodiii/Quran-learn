Read the line from right to left. كَلَّمَ is “spoke to”. ٱللَّهُ is the doer. مُوسَىٰ is the one who was spoken to, the object.

The last word, تَكْلِيمًا, is a noun made from the same root as the verb: ك ل م. Word for word the verse says “spoke a speaking”. English has this habit too: “she smiled a big smile”, “he slept a deep sleep”. English usually turns the noun into a word like “really”.

Arabic names this word a **mafʿūl muṭlaq** (مَفْعُول مُطْلَق). L2.05 showed this verse and said Level 4 would teach it.

*Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* calls تَكْلِيمًا a verbal noun that stresses the verb and rules out a figurative reading. Without it, “spoke to Musa” could mean something looser, like sending him a message. With it, the speaking was real speaking.

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* has a chapter called **the maṣdar**. It says that the maṣdar is the **accusative noun** that comes **third** in the verb’s conjugation. Its example is the verb ضَرَبَ يَضْرِبُ: past (ضَرَبَ), present (يَضْرِبُ), then the verbal noun (ضَرْبًا). This is the verbal noun of L3.20, here in naṣb.

Later books, and al-Jadwal, call this job **mafʿūl muṭlaq**. Two terms help.

- **Mafʿūl bihi** (مَفْعُول بِهِ) is the thing the verb acts on. The names of the other objects each carry a tie: bihi (“to it”), fīhi (“in it”), li-ajlihi (“for its sake”) and maʿahu (“with it”).
- **Muṭlaq** means “free”. This object has no tie in its name. Teachers explain the name this way. The lesson did not check that explanation in a book.

The usual mafʿūl muṭlaq is the verb’s own verbal noun, in naṣb. Verb and noun share the root.

| Verb | Verbal noun in naṣb | Meaning | In the Quran text? |
|---|---|---|---|
| ضَرَبَ | ضَرْبًا | struck, a striking | yes (2:273, 37:93) |
| كَتَبَ | كِتَابَةً | wrote, a writing | no |
| فَرِحَ | فَرَحًا | rejoiced, a rejoicing | no |
| نَصَرَ | نَصْرًا | helped, a helping | yes (7:192, 25:19, 48:3) |

These four are teaching forms. A search of the Tanzil text in the Corpus file finds ضَرْبًا and نَصْرًا, and does not find كِتَابَةً or فَرَحًا. The pattern is real all the same. Each verb has a verbal noun, and L3.20 shows how to find it.

The root is the usual sign, not the whole test. Step 3 shows a mafʿūl muṭlaq that is not the verb’s own verbal noun, and step 4 shows a same-root word that is an object.

A mafʿūl muṭlaq has three signs.

- **It is in naṣb.** The ending is -an (تَكْلِيمًا), -a (before a pronoun or in an idafah), -ayni (a dual) or -īna. Step 4 shows each.
- **It usually has the root of the verb.** Step 3 shows the stand-ins that do not.
- **It does not name the one acted on.** It says how the action happened.

Teachers sort the “how” into three jobs, and step 2 takes them one by one. **Stress** (تَوْكِيد): the verb is said again, so it is certain. **Kind** (بَيَان ٱلنَّوْع): what sort of action it was. **Number** (بَيَان ٱلْعَدَد): how many times. Al-Jadwal uses the words “its kind” and “its number” in its notes, and al-Durr al-Maṣūn uses “stress”.

</Block>

Two verses, the same pattern: a verb, its doer, and the verb’s own verbal noun with **nothing added**. تَمُورُ with مَوْرًا, تَسِيرُ with سَيْرًا. Al-Jadwal reads both as مَفْعُول مُطْلَق.

A bare verbal noun does one job: it **stresses** the verb. The action is certain. English adds “really” or “truly”.

*Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* says the two verbal nouns here are used to rule out a figurative reading: the sky and the mountains do move, in fact.

A verbal noun can also be said twice, as in 89:21: دُكَّتِ ٱلْأَرْضُ دَكًّا دَكًّا, “the earth is crushed, crushed”. Exercise 1 looks at it.

The verse says that the believers were tested and shaken. زِلْزَالًا is the verbal noun of the verb زُلْزِلُوا۟ (root ز ل ز ل). Then an adjective follows: شَدِيدًا, “severe”.

Now the verbal noun does a second job. It does not only say “shaken”. It says **what kind** of shaking. The adjective is a naʿt (L2.09) and agrees with it in naṣb: -an and -an. Al-Jadwal reads زِلْزَالًا as مَفْعُول مُطْلَق.

The two jobs differ in what follows the noun. A bare noun stresses. A noun with an adjective names the kind. 33:41 and 3:37 in the tiles show the same shape.

This is the verse that opens Surat az-Zalzalah. The verbal noun زِلْزَال has a pronoun attached: -hā, “its”. It is the same root as the verb زُلْزِلَتِ.

Look at the ending. The word before -hā ends in a **visible** fatḥa: زِلْزَالَ. There is no tanwīn, because a word that is the first part of an idafah loses its tanwīn (L2.08), and a pronoun makes the same kind of pair.

Al-Jadwal reads زِلْزَالَهَا as مَفْعُول مُطْلَق. *Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* adds that the verbal noun is attached to its doer, and that the meaning is “the shaking that fits the earth”: the one that its size and greatness call for. So the owner tells you the kind.

- **Made-up example:** أَكْرِمِ ٱلتَّقِيَّ إِكْرَامَهُ, “honour the pious man the honour that is his”. Al-Durr quotes this pattern from al-Zamakhshari.

The third job counts. In 69:14 the noun دَكَّةً means “one crushing”: one time, not the action in general. وَٰحِدَةً, “one”, repeats the count and follows it as an adjective. Al-Jadwal reads دَكَّةً as مَفْعُول مُطْلَق.

In 9:101 the noun is مَرَّتَيْنِ, “two times”. It is a **dual**, and the dual in naṣb ends in -ayni (L2.05). Al-Jadwal reads it as a mafʿūl muṭlaq that stands in for the verbal noun and gives its number.

The same chapter of *al-Ajurrumiyyah* divides the maṣdar in two. The **lafẓī** kind has the same wording as the verb: قَتَلْتُهُ قَتْلًا. The **maʿnawī** kind has the same meaning but not the same wording: جَلَسْتُ قُعُودًا and قُمْتُ وُقُوفًا. The verb جَلَسَ has the root ج ل س, and قُعُود has ق ع د.

So the word in this job does not have to be the verb’s own verbal noun, and it does not always have the same root. Al-Jadwal calls such a word a **nāʾib ʿan al-maṣdar**, a stand-in for the verbal noun. The next four blocks show stand-ins in the Quran.

The verb أَنۢبَتَ is Form IV, root ن ب ت. The regular verbal noun of Form IV is إِنْبَات (L3.20). The verse uses نَبَاتًا instead. It has the same root, but it is another noun. “He made you grow a growth.” The Corpus tags it as a plain noun, while it tags تَكْلِيمًا in 4:164 as a verbal noun.

Al-Jadwal reads نَبَاتًا as a mafʿūl muṭlaq that stands in for the verbal noun, because it meets the verb in its root. In this verse the object is the -kum in أَنۢبَتَكُم, and the mafʿūl muṭlaq is نَبَاتًا. Both are in the same sentence, as in 4:164.

3:37 has the same pair with an adjective: وَأَنۢبَتَهَا نَبَاتًا حَسَنًا, “and made her grow a good growth”.

<Note kind="know">*Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* gives two readings of نَبَاتًا in 71:17. It is a *ism maṣdar*, a noun that stands for the verbal noun (the extra letters of إِنْبَات are dropped). Or it belongs to an understood verb, نَبَتُّمْ, “you grew”. Both readings keep the noun in naṣb with the same meaning.<br />
The same book says about 73:8, وَتَبَتَّلْ إِلَيْهِ تَبْتِيلًا, that تَبْتِيلًا is not the regular verbal noun of Form V (that is تَبَتُّل). It follows the meaning “cut yourself off”, and it suits the end of the verse. Al-Jadwal calls it a stand-in as well.</Note>

Al-Jadwal says كُلّ is a mafʿūl muṭlaq that stands in for the verbal noun, **because it is attached to the verbal noun**. *Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* says the same: كُلّ takes its job from the word it is attached to. The proof is the question. “Write what?” fits the object. “Write how?” or “how many times?” fits the mafʿūl muṭlaq.

99:1 and 99:2 look alike: a verb, the earth, and a word with -hā. In 99:1 the question “shaken how?” gives the mafʿūl muṭlaq. In 99:2 the question “brings out what?” gives the object, أَثْقَالَهَا. Its root, ث ق ل, is different from the verb’s root, خ ر ج.

The mafʿūl muṭlaq is always in naṣb, but naṣb has more than one mark.

- **-an** on a single word: تَكْلِيمًا, مَوْرًا, زِلْزَالًا.
- **-a** when a pronoun or a second noun follows: زِلْزَالَهَا. The tanwīn is gone, and the fatḥa is visible.
- **-ayni** on a dual: مَرَّتَيْنِ.
- **-īna** on the tens: ثَمَٰنِينَ.

When a verbal noun is in the genitive, like ٱلْمَيْلِ in كُلَّ ٱلْمَيْلِ, it is not the mafʿūl muṭlaq. The word before it is.

<Step id="practice" label="Practice" title="Practice">
<Block id="practice-1" title="Practice: find it and say its job">
  <Stage label="For each word, ask">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Is it in naṣb?</b> A mafʿūl muṭlaq always is.</li>
      <li><b>Which question does it answer?</b> To whom (object), or how, what kind, how many times?</li>
      <li><b>Is it the verb’s own verbal noun?</b> Or a stand-in: another form, كُلّ, a number?</li>
      <li><b>Is the verb said?</b> Or left out?</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

Work out each answer first, on paper or in your head. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Find the mafʿūl muṭlaq and say its job (stress, kind or number): (a) وَتَسِيرُ ٱلْجِبَالُ سَيْرًا (At-Tur 52:10) (b) ٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ ذِكْرًا كَثِيرًا (Al-Ahzab 33:41) (c) فَدُكَّتَا دَكَّةً وَٰحِدَةً (Al-Haqqah 69:14) (d) كَلَّآ إِذَا دُكَّتِ ٱلْأَرْضُ دَكًّا دَكًّا (Al-Fajr 89:21)">

- (a) **سَيْرًا**: stress. It is bare and comes from the root of تَسِيرُ.
- (b) **ذِكْرًا**: kind. The adjective كَثِيرًا, “much”, follows it. “Remember Allah with much remembering.”
- (c) **دَكَّةً**: number. It means one crushing, and وَٰحِدَةً, “one”, repeats the count.
- (d) **دَكًّا** (the first): stress. Al-Jadwal says the second دَكًّا repeats the first, to stress it further: “crushed, crushed”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Object or mafʿūl muṭlaq? (a) كَتَبَ ٱلطَّالِبُ رِسَالَةً (made up) (b) كَتَبَ ٱلطَّالِبُ كِتَابَةً (made up) (c) يَكْتُبُونَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ (Al-Baqarah 2:79, part) (d) وَأَخْرَجَتِ ٱلْأَرْضُ أَثْقَالَهَا (Az-Zalzalah 99:2)">

- (a) **object.** رِسَالَةً is what was written. Its root, ر س ل, is different from the verb’s.
- (b) **mafʿūl muṭlaq.** كِتَابَةً has the root of the verb and says how: “the student wrote a writing”.
- (c) **object.** ٱلْكِتَٰبَ has the root ك ت ب, but it is the thing written. Al-Jadwal: mafʿūl bihi.
- (d) **object.** أَثْقَالَهَا is what the earth brings out. Its root is different.

Only (b) is a mafʿūl muṭlaq. The same root appears in (b) and (c), so the root is not enough.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Which job: stress, kind or number? (a) ضَرَبَ ٱلْمُعَلِّمُ ٱلطَّالِبَ ضَرْبًا (b) ضَرَبَ ٱلْمُعَلِّمُ ٱلطَّالِبَ ضَرْبًا شَدِيدًا (c) ضَرَبَ ٱلْمُعَلِّمُ ٱلطَّالِبَ ضَرْبَتَيْنِ (all made up)">

- (a) **stress.** ضَرْبًا is bare. It makes the striking certain.
- (b) **kind.** The adjective شَدِيدًا, “severe”, says what kind of striking.
- (c) **number.** ضَرْبَتَيْنِ is a dual, “two strikes”. It says how many times. The dual ends in -ayni in naṣb.

In all three the root is ض ر ب, the same as the verb.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Check the root. In each pair, say whether the noun has the verb’s root and which kind of verbal noun it is. (a) زُلْزِلَتِ and زِلْزَالَهَا (b) كَلَّمَ and تَكْلِيمًا (c) نَصَرَ and عَوْنًا (made up) (d) أَنۢبَتَ and نَبَاتًا">

- (a) Same root ز ل ز ل. The verbal noun of the verb.
- (b) Same root ك ل م. The regular Form II verbal noun on تَفْعِيل.
- (c) Different roots: ن ص ر and ع و ن. The meaning is the same, so this is the book’s second kind, the maʿnawī, like جَلَسْتُ قُعُودًا. A stand-in, and not an error.
- (d) Same root ن ب ت, but the regular verbal noun of Form IV is إِنْبَات. نَبَات is a stand-in.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Which verb is left out? (a) فَضَرْبَ ٱلرِّقَابِ (Muhammad 47:4) (b) قَالَ مَعَاذَ ٱللَّهِ (Yusuf 12:23) (c) غُفْرَانَكَ رَبَّنَا (Al-Baqarah 2:285)">

- (a) **ٱضْرِبُوا**, “strike”. The verbal noun takes the place of the command.
- (b) **أَعُوذُ**, “I seek protection”.
- (c) A verb like “we ask”, “give us”. Al-Jadwal reads غُفْرَانَكَ as a mafʿūl muṭlaq of a left-out verb, and رَبَّنَا is “our Lord”, a call.

The word before the genitive noun in (a) and (b) is the first word of an idafah.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Name the stand-in and the other word. (a) فَلَا تَمِيلُوا۟ كُلَّ ٱلْمَيْلِ (An-Nisa 4:129, part) (b) فَٱجْلِدُوهُمْ ثَمَٰنِينَ جَلْدَةً (An-Nur 24:4, part)">

- (a) **كُلَّ** is the stand-in, in naṣb, because it is attached to a verbal noun. **ٱلْمَيْلِ** is the muḍāf ilayh, in the genitive.
- (b) **ثَمَٰنِينَ** is the stand-in: it says how many times. **جَلْدَةً** is the tamyīz: it says what is counted.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Exercise n={7} q="Add a mafʿūl muṭlaq to each verb (made up): (a) ذَكَرَ ٱلرَّجُلُ ٱللَّهَ, for stress (b) صَبَرَ ٱلرَّجُلُ, for kind, with the adjective جَمِيلًا (c) قَرَأْتُ ٱلسُّورَةَ, for number: twice">

<Exercise n={8} q="ḥāl or mafʿūl muṭlaq? (a) ٱدْعُوا۟ رَبَّكُمْ تَضَرُّعًا (Al-Araf 7:55, part) (b) وَكَلَّمَ ٱللَّهُ مُوسَىٰ تَكْلِيمًا (An-Nisa 4:164, end)">

- (a) **ḥāl** in the usual reading: “humbly”. Al-Jadwal and al-Durr al-Maṣūn both give it first, and both allow a mafʿūl muṭlaq reading (“a call of humility”). The noun does not come from the verb’s root.
- (b) **mafʿūl muṭlaq.** Same root as كَلَّمَ, and it stresses the verb.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Same root, not the usual form. For each noun give the verb’s form, its regular verbal noun and the word the verse uses. (a) وَٱللَّهُ أَنۢبَتَكُم مِّنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ نَبَاتًا (Nuh 71:17) (b) فَإِنِّىٓ أُعَذِّبُهُۥ عَذَابًا (Al-Maidah 5:115, part)">

- (b) Form II (shadda on the second letter). The regular verbal noun is تَعْذِيب. The verse uses عَذَاب. Al-Jadwal calls it a stand-in mafʿūl muṭlaq.

L3.20 teaches the regular patterns.

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="a verb plus its own verbal noun equals a stronger or clearer action"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'the verb', small: 'said, or left out' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'its verbal noun in naṣb', small: 'or a stand-in' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'a stronger or clearer action', small: 'مَفْعُول مُطْلَق' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A **mafʿūl muṭlaq** is the verb’s own verbal noun in naṣb. It says how the action happened, and it does not name the one acted on.
- It has **three jobs**: stress (bare), kind (with an adjective or an owner), number (one time, a dual or a number).
- **Stand-ins** do the job when the verb’s own verbal noun is not there: another form of the verbal noun (نَبَات, عَذَاب, خُسْرَان), كُلّ before a verbal noun, a number. *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* allows a verbal noun with the same meaning but another root.
- The verb can be **left out**: فَضَرْبَ ٱلرِّقَابِ, مَعَاذَ ٱللَّهِ.
- The same root does not prove a mafʿūl muṭlaq: ٱلْكِتَٰبَ in 2:79 is an object. A ḥāl can be a verbal noun too: تَضَرُّعًا in 7:55.
- It always is in naṣb, with -an, -a, -ayni or -īna. After كُلَّ the verbal noun is genitive.

**Recognition checklist.**

1. Find a noun in naṣb after the verb, or a stand-in such as كُلّ or a number.
2. Ask the question: to whom or what (object), or how, what kind, how many times?
3. Check the root. The same root is the usual sign.
4. Name the job: stress, kind or number.

|---|---|---|---|
| Stress | كَلَّمَ | تَكْلِيمًا | 4:164 |
| Stress | تَمُورُ | مَوْرًا | 52:9 |
| Kind | زُلْزِلُوا۟ | زِلْزَالًا شَدِيدًا | 33:11 |
| Kind | زُلْزِلَتِ | زِلْزَالَهَا | 99:1 |
| Number | دُكَّتَا | دَكَّةً وَٰحِدَةً | 69:14 |
| Number | سَنُعَذِّبُهُم | مَّرَّتَيْنِ | 9:101 |
| Stand-in | أَنۢبَتَكُم | نَبَاتًا | 71:17 |
