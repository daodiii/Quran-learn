The two verses stand side by side in Surah ad-Duha. Both verbs come from one root, **و ج د**, “finding”. Look at how each verb starts.

وَجَدَكَ starts with a و. In وَوَجَدَكَ there are two: the first is the little word “and”, the second is the root letter. يَجِدْكَ has no و at all.

The root is the same in both verbs. The past shows the first root letter, and the present has lost it. The rest of the lesson shows where this happens and where it does not.

Every verb has a root of three letters (L3.03). When the **first** letter is و, the verb is an assimilated verb, مِثَال (*mithāl*). Al-Jadwal writes *muʿtall mithāl*, “a weak verb of the mithāl kind”. L4.11 listed the kinds of weak verbs, and this is the kind with a weak first letter. The other kinds are in L4.12 (middle), L4.13 (last) and L4.15 (a hamza).

| Root | Past | Present | Meaning | Form I verb words |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| و ج د | وَجَدَ | يَجِدُ | he found | 106 |
| و ع د | وَعَدَ | يَعِدُ | he promised | 70 |
| و ه ب | وَهَبَ | يَهَبُ | he gave | 22 |
| و ض ع | وَضَعَ | يَضَعُ | he put down | 21 |
| و ق ي | وَقَىٰ | يَقِي | he protected | 16 |
| و ر ث | وَرِثَ | يَرِثُ | he inherited | 13 |
| و ق ع | وَقَعَ | يَقَعُ | it fell, it happened | 12 |
| و ص ل | وَصَلَ | يَصِلُ | he reached | 10 |
| و ل د | وَلَدَ | يَلِدُ | she gave birth | 9 |

Take وَصَلَ, “he reached”, root **و ص ل**. Its present should be يَوْصِلُ: the prefix يَ, then the root letter و with a sukūn, then ص with a kasra. That form is not used. The و falls out, and you read **يَصِلُ**. In 4:90 the “they” form is يَصِلُونَ.

Al-Jadwal gives the reason in his notes on 66:6 and 6:145. The first letter falls because it stands **between يَ and a kasra**. For أَجِدُ in 6:145 he gives the starting form أَوْجِدُ. In his note on 17:64 he adds that the first letter falls “in the present and the command”. The next step takes the command. You met the same verb in L3.03: of يَعِدُكُمُ (2:268) al-Jadwal says that it is a mithāl verb with a kasra in the present, and that its first letter has fallen.

What is left looks like a verb with two root letters: يَصِلُ shows ص and ل. The root keeps its three letters, and the و is hidden. Put a sound verb next to an assimilated one:

| Form | Sound verb, ن ص ر | Assimilated verb, و ج د |
|---|---|---|
| Past (he) | نَصَرَ | وَجَدَ |
| Present (he) | يَنْصُرُ | يَجِدُ |
| Command (you, one man) | ٱنصُرْ | جِدْ |
| Root letters you see in the present | three | two |

The Quran has no command of وَجَدَ, so جِدْ is a teaching form.

<Note kind="know">In the pattern, the missing letter is the ف of فَعَلَ (L3.03). Al-Jadwal writes the pattern of يَعِدُ as يَعِلُ, and the pattern of the command عِدْ as عِلْ.</Note>

Most of these verbs take a **kasra** on the middle letter in the present: يَجِدُ, يَعِدُ, يَصِلُ. A few take a **fatḥa**: يَهَبُ, يَضَعُ, يَقَعُ. The و falls in both groups.

| Present vowel | Past and present | Words in the Quran (present and command) |
|---|---|---|
| kasra | وَجَدَ, يَجِدُ | 67 |
| kasra | وَعَدَ, يَعِدُ | 20 |
| kasra | وَصَفَ, يَصِفُ | 13 |
| kasra | وَرِثَ, يَرِثُ | 9 |
| kasra | وَصَلَ, يَصِلُ | 7 |
| kasra | وَلَدَ, يَلِدُ | 3 |
| fatḥa | وَضَعَ, يَضَعُ | 11 |
| fatḥa | وَهَبَ, يَهَبُ | 10 |
| fatḥa | وَقَعَ, يَقَعُ | 3 |
| fatḥa | وَذَرَ, يَذَرُ | 45 |

In the fatḥa verbs the last or middle root letter is a throat letter: ع in وَضَعَ and وَقَعَ, ه in وَهَبَ. Al-Jadwal says this about the verbal noun سَعَة (2:247): the fatḥa shows “in place of the kasra” because the last letter is ع. So the starting form had a kasra too, and the و fell before the fatḥa came. This is how the books explain the group. The reverse does not hold: وَهَنَ has a ه and still takes a kasra, as in تَهِنُوا۟ (3:139). Of the four fatḥa verbs, only يَذَرُ has no throat letter.

The past of يَذَرُ is not used. Al-Jadwal says on 2:234 that the Arabs did not use وَذَرَ, and that the language uses تَرَكَ for the past. The Quran has only the present and the command of this verb (45 words in the Corpus). Learn يَذَرُ as it is.

The command comes from the present, with the three moves of L3.08. Take تَعِدُ. End it in a sukūn: تَعِدْ. Take off the prefix: عِدْ. L3.08 added a helping alif when the word began with a silent letter, as in ٱنصُرْ. Here the word begins with ع, and the ع has a kasra. Nothing needs a helper, so there is **no ٱ**.

Al-Jadwal says in his notes on 37:24 and 66:6 that the و falls from the command too. In 37:24 he gives the reason: the middle letter has a kasra in the present, so the first letter falls from the command as well.

|---|---|---|---|

| قَعُوا | وَقَعَ | fall! | فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ (15:29) |

The Corpus has 44 Form I commands from the verbs of this lesson. All of them are “you”: 28 for one man and 16 for you all (men). In the verses, a و at the start of the written word is often the little word “and”. In وَعِدْهُمْ you read و + عِدْ + هُمْ, and the root letter is gone. The Quran forms in 37:24 and 15:29 are “you all”.

The root **و ق ي** has a weak letter at **both ends**: و first and ي last. The verb is assimilated and defective at once (L4.13 teaches the last letter).

The first و falls in the present, like any assimilated verb: يَقِي. In the command the last letter falls too, because the command is built on dropping it. Only ق is left, with its kasra: **قِ**. Al-Jadwal calls this a “doubled dropping” in his note on 2:201 (the first letter, then the last). With نَا, “us”, you read قِنَا. With the “you all” ending, the last letter is gone and the ending is ـُوا: قُوا.

Six of the 16 Form I words of this root are commands. Parse 3 below goes through قِنَا in 2:201.

You will meet these words in 4:90 (مِّيثَٰقٌ, in the same verse as يَصِلُونَ), 3:9 (ٱلْمِيعَادَ) and 6:152 (وَٱلْمِيزَانَ). مِيزَان and مِيرَاث have the same shape as the other two. Al-Jadwal's notes that were read here did not give them.

A noun of place or time keeps the و: مَوْعِد, “a promised time or place” (12 words), is the noun L3.21 showed. The ي appears only when the و is silent after a kasra.

So a ي in the middle of a word can hide a root that starts with و. Look at the root in a dictionary: the root of مِيثَاق is و ث ق.

</Block>

<Block id="mistake-write" title="Mistake 1: writing the و in the present">
  <Stage label="Which one is a word?">
    <Tiles
      label="يَجِدُ is a word. يَوْجِدُ is the starting form."
      items={[
        { ar: 'يَجِدُ', en: 'he finds: correct', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'يَوْجِدُ', en: 'not a word: the starting form', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

When you build a present from a root that starts with و, do not keep the و. Say the prefix, then the second root letter: يَ + جِدُ. The و stays in the past, the passive and the other forms. Two verbs are the exception, وَجِلَ and وَدَّ: the present يَوْجَلُ keeps its و.

<Note kind="warn">Do not drop the و everywhere. وَجَدَ, وُجِدَ (12:75) and يُوعَدُونَ keep it.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-root" title="Mistake 2: reading the root from the letters you see">
  <Stage label="Two verbs, two roots">
    <Tiles
      label="يَعِدُ and يَعُدُّ"
      items={[
        { ar: 'يَعِدُ', en: 'he promises: root و ع د', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'يَعُدُّ', en: 'he counts: root ع د د (تَعُدُّونَ, 22:47)', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

يَعِدُ shows ع and د, so it is easy to give it the root ع د د. That root belongs to يَعُدُّ, “he counts”, which has a ḍamma on the ع and a shadda on the د. To find the root of a present verb that seems to have two letters, add a و to the front and look it up. A kasra on the middle letter is a clue, not proof.

A second trap is the و you see at the start of a written word. In وَعِدْهُمْ (17:64) the و is “and”. The root letter has fallen. Compare وَوَجَدَكَ in 93:7, where the first و is “and” and the second is the root letter.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-helper" title="Mistake 3: adding a helper to the command">
  <Stage label="Which one is a command?">
    <Tiles
      label="عِدْ is right. ٱعِدْ has a helper it does not need."
      items={[
        { ar: 'عِدْ', en: 'right: promise!', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ٱعِدْ', en: 'wrong: no helper is needed', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'ٱنصُرْ', en: 'right: help! This verb needs it', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

L3.08 added the helping alif ٱ when the command began with a silent letter. ٱنصُرْ needs it, because نْصُرْ starts with a sukūn. عِدْ, جِدْ, صِلْ and قِفْ start with a letter that already has a vowel, so they take no helper. In وَقِنَا and وَهَبْ the و you see is “and”.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-all" title="Mistake 4: thinking the rule has no exceptions">
  <Stage label="Count them">
    <Tiles
      label="the rule and what it leaves out"
      items={[
        { ar: 'يَجِدُ', en: 'the و falls', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'يَوَدُّ', en: 'the و stays', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'يُوعَدُ', en: 'the و stays (passive)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ٱتَّقَىٰ', en: 'the و turns into ت', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

It is easy to think that the و simply drops and that is all. It is more than that. The و falls in the Form I active present and command, with a kasra or a fatḥa that replaced it. It stays in the past, the passive and Forms II to VI and X. It stays in وَجِلَ and وَدَّ. It turns into ت in Form VIII and into ي in nouns like مِيثَاق.

Four things to ask of a verb that starts with a و, or seems to be missing one letter: Which form is it? Which tense? Is it active or passive? Does a kasra follow the و?
</Block>

<Exercise n={7} q="In لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ (Al-Ikhlas 112:3), which verb is active and which is passive? What is the root? Why does one lose the و and the other keep it?">

The root is و ل د, “giving birth”. **يَلِدْ** is active: “He has not begotten”. The و has fallen between يَ and the kasra, and the jazm after لَمْ shows as a sukūn on the د. **يُولَدْ** is passive: “He was not born”. The prefix has a ḍamma, the و is read as a long ū, and no kasra follows it. A passive verb keeps the و.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Give the root, and say what happened to the first و. (a) يَصِلُونَ (An-Nisa 4:90) (b) وَيَذَرُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:234) (c) أَجِدُ (Al-Anam 6:145) (d) ٱتَّسَقَ (Al-Inshiqaq 84:18) (e) مِّيثَٰقٌ (An-Nisa 4:90)">

- (a) Root و ص ل. The و fell from the present, because a kasra follows.
- (b) Root و ذ ر. The و fell. This verb has no past in the Quran: it is used only in the present and the command (al-Jadwal on 2:234).
- (c) Root و ج د. The و fell. Al-Jadwal gives the starting form أَوْجِدُ.
- (d) Root و س ق. The و turned into ت and joined the added ت of Form VIII. The starting form is ٱوْتَسَقَ.
- (e) Root و ث ق. The و turned into ي after the kasra of م. The starting form was مِوْثَاق (al-Jadwal on 2:27).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="تَوْجَلْ (Al-Hijr 15:53) keeps its و, but تَجِدْ does not. Why?">

Both are presents in jazm. The present of وَجَدَ takes a **kasra**: يَجِدُ. The و falls between يَ and the kasra. The present of وَجِلَ takes a **fatḥa**: تَوْجَلُ. Al-Jadwal says in his note on 15:52 that وَجِلَ belongs to the door of فَرِحَ. No kasra follows the و, so it stays. يَوَدُّ (2:96) works in the same way.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Read وَقِنَا عَذَابَ ٱلنَّارِ (Al-Baqarah 2:201). Which letters of the root و ق ي are gone from قِنَا, and when did each fall?">

Two letters are gone: the **و** and the **ي**. The و fell in the present, يَقِي, because a kasra follows it, and the fall carries into the command. The ي fell because the command is built on dropping the last weak letter (L4.13). Only ق is left, with its kasra, and نَا is the object “us”. Al-Jadwal says the pattern is عِنَا.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={11} q="Undo the change and give the starting form and the root. (a) ٱتَّسَقَ (Al-Inshiqaq 84:18) (b) ٱتَّعَدَ (a teaching form) (c) ٱتَّقَوا۟ (Ar-Raad 13:35)">

- (a) Starting form ٱوْتَسَقَ. Root و س ق.
- (b) Starting form ٱوْتَعَدَ. Root و ع د. The Quran does not use this verb.
- (c) Starting form ٱوْتَقَىٰ (al-Jadwal, 2:21), then the ending -ū for “they”. Root و ق ي.

In each the و turned into ت and joined the added ت of Form VIII. Look for a doubled ٱتَّ.

</Exercise>

    <Formula
      label="in the present of وَعَدَ, the و falls away"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'يَوْعِدُ', small: 'starting form' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'the و falls', small: 'between يَ and a kasra' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'يَعِدُ', small: 'he promises' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- An **assimilated verb** (مِثَال) has the root letter **و** first. The Quran has 957 such verb words, from 50 roots.
- In the **present** of Form I the و falls: يَجِدُ, يَعِدُ, يَصِلُ. The same happens with a fatḥa that replaced the kasra: يَهَبُ, يَضَعُ. The reason in al-Jadwal: it stands between يَ and a kasra.
- In the **command** it falls too, and there is no helper: عِدْ, جِدْ, قِفْ, هَبْ. وَقَى loses both ends: قِنَا, قُوا.
- The و **stays** in the past, the passive (وُعِدَ, يُوعَدُ) and Forms II to VI and X. It stays in the keepers وَجِلَ (تَوْجَلْ) and وَدَّ (يَوَدُّ).
- It **changes** in Form VIII (ٱوْتَسَقَ becomes ٱتَّسَقَ, and ٱتَّقَىٰ) and in nouns (مِيثَاق). Some verbal nouns lose it: سِنَة, دِيَة, سَعَة.
- A verb with a ي first keeps it: يَيْـَٔسُ.
- Several rules in this lesson are teaching summaries. The sources name each change case by case.
