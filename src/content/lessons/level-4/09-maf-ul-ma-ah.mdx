The verse is about Dāwūd (David). God tells the mountains to echo his praise, and the birds do it with them.

Look at the last word. وَٱلطَّيْرَ is a wāw and ٱلطَّيْرَ, “the birds”, and it ends in a fatḥa. Al-Jadwal reads this wāw as “along with”. The birds do not get a command of their own. They go along with the mountains in the same action.

The word that carries this meaning is the **mafʿūl maʿah**. It is the topic of the lesson.

<Block id="book" title="What al-Ajurrumiyyah says">
  <Stage label="From al-Ajurrumiyyah">
    <blockquote class="lx-quote" lang="ar" dir="rtl">

    </blockquote>
    <p class="lx-quote-note">From the Wikisource text. Vowel marks added by Quran Learn, not checked against a printed edition.</p>

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* has a short chapter on the mafʿūl maʿah (*bāb al-mafʿūl maʿah*). In plain words the line says: it is the accusative noun that is mentioned to show who the action was done with.

The book gives two examples. جَاءَ ٱلْأَمِيرُ وَٱلْجَيْشَ means “the commander came along with the army”. ٱسْتَوَى ٱلْمَاءُ وَٱلْخَشَبَةَ means “the water rose level with the wood”.

The book lists fifteen accusative jobs in all. The mafʿūl maʿah is one of them. L2.05 promised it, and this lesson teaches it.

</Block>

  <Stage label="The three parts">
    <Tiles

      ]}

Each part has a name.

- **The action** is usually a verb. In 34:10 it is أَوِّبِى, “echo”. In the book it is جَاءَ.
- **The wāw** is **wāw al-maʿiyya**, “the wāw of togetherness”. The word مَعِيَّة comes from مَعَ, “with”.
- **The companion** is the **mafʿūl maʿah**, “the object with it”. The -hu stands for the action: the thing the companion goes along with.

The companion is not the doer and it is not the object of the verb. It is a third kind of noun that comes in beside the action. It is always accusative.

</Block>

The mafʿūl maʿah is an old and well-known job in Arabic grammar. The Quran has very few clear cases of it. Here is what two sources show.

- The Quranic Arabic Corpus tags three wāws as **comitative**, the word it uses for “along with”: 3:142, 5:36 and 10:71. One of them stands before a verb. Two stand before a noun.
- Al-Jadwal reads a noun after a wāw of togetherness as its first reading in one verse without a doubt, 34:10. It gives the same first reading, with a doubt, in 6:112 and in 6:137, which repeats it.
- The two sources do not agree on a single verse with a noun.

Step 3 counts every case and shows the doubts. This lesson does not stretch a verse to make the list longer.

<Step id="with-or-and" label="With, or and?" title="Tell “along with” from “and”">
<Block id="and" title="The wāw of “and”">
  <Stage label="Made-up example">
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Made-up example"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'جَاءَ', en: 'came', say: 'jaa', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'fail', label: 'fāʿil', hint: 'the first doer: nominative, -un', words: [{ ar: 'زَيْدٌ', en: 'Zayd', say: 'zaydun' }] },
        { role: 'matuf', label: 'maʿṭūf', hint: 'wāw “and” and a second doer: nominative, -un', words: [{ ar: 'وَعَمْرٌو', en: 'and ʿAmr', say: 'wa-amrun' }] },
      ]}
      en={['Zayd came, and ʿAmr came.']}
    />

Two men came. Both did the coming. ʿAmr is joined to Zayd by “and”, so he gets the same job and the same case: a doer in the nominative.

This is **wāw al-ʿaṭf**, the wāw of joining. The word after it copies the case of the word before it.

</Block>

<Block id="with" title="The wāw of “along with”">
  <Stage label="Made-up example">
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Made-up example"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', label: 'fiʿl + fāʿil', hint: 'the ending -tu is the doer: “I”', words: [{ ar: 'سِرْتُ', en: 'I walked', say: 'sirtu', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'maful', label: 'mafʿūl maʿah', hint: 'wāw “along with” and the river: accusative, -a', words: [{ ar: 'وَٱلنَّهْرَ', en: 'along with the river', say: 'wa-n-nahra' }] },
      ]}
      en={['I walked alongside the river.']}
    />
  </Stage>

One person walked. The river did not walk. It went along with the walking, as a companion to the action.

The wāw means “along with”, and the word after it ends in a fatḥa. This is the same shape as وَٱلطَّيْرَ in 34:10.

Put the two sentences side by side. One doer and an accusative companion: wāw of togetherness. Two doers in the same case: wāw of joining.

</Block>

  <Stage label="From al-Ajurrumiyyah">
    <Sentence

      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'ٱسْتَوَىٰ', en: 'rose level', say: 'istawa', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'fail', label: 'fāʿil', hint: 'the doer: nominative, -u', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلْمَاءُ', en: 'the water', say: 'al-maau' }] },
        { role: 'maful', label: 'mafʿūl maʿah', hint: 'accusative, -a', words: [{ ar: 'وَٱلْخَشَبَةَ', en: 'along with the wood', say: 'wa-l-khashabata' }] },
      ]}
      en={['The water rose level with the wood.']}
    />
  </Stage>

This is the book’s second example. Try “and”: “the water and the wood rose level”. That would make the wood a second doer. Wood does not rise. Only “along with” makes sense, so the wood is the companion.

That is the clearest case. When the second noun cannot do what the verb says, the wāw is the wāw of togetherness and the noun is accusative.

<Check n={7} q="Why is ٱلْخَشَبَةَ a companion and not a second doer?" options={['Wood cannot do the action of rising level', 'It ends in a ḍamma', 'It comes before the verb']} answer={0} why="The meaning rules out “and”. The wood goes along with the water. It does not do the verb." />
</Block>

<Block id="both" title="When both work">
  <Stage label="Two readings of the book’s sentence">
    <Tiles
      label="One letter changes the meaning"
      items={[
        { ar: 'جَاءَ ٱلْأَمِيرُ وَٱلْجَيْشُ', en: '-u: the commander and the army came (two doers)', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'جَاءَ ٱلْأَمِيرُ وَٱلْجَيْشَ', en: '-a: the commander came along with the army', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Sometimes both readings are possible, and the ending tells you which one is meant.

With a ḍamma, ٱلْجَيْشُ is a second doer: the commander and the army both came. With a fatḥa, ٱلْجَيْشَ is the companion: the commander came, accompanied by the army.

Al-Jadwal gives another example in a note, أَكْرَمْتُكَ وَزَيْدًا. The wāw can join زَيْدًا to the object -ka, “I honoured you and Zayd”. It can also mean “along with”, “I honoured you together with Zayd”. Both readings are allowed there.

<Note kind="know">This block is a teaching summary. The book gives only the definition and its two examples. The note on أَكْرَمْتُكَ وَزَيْدًا is al-Jadwal’s, in its notes to 9:82, where it passes on a list from Ibn Hishām’s *Mughnī*.</Note>

<Block id="decide" title="How to decide: three questions">
  <Stage label="The decision">
    <Tiles
      label="Ask these about a wāw and the noun after it"
      items={[
        { ar: '١', en: 'Is there one action, or two doers?', type: 'x' },
        { ar: '٢', en: 'Can the second noun do the action too?', type: 'x' },
        { ar: '٣', en: 'Does the meaning say “and”, or “along with”?', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Ask these questions in order.

1. **Is there one action, with a companion beside it?** Then think of “along with”.
2. **Can the second noun do the action too?** If it cannot, only “along with” is left. If it can, the wāw can be “and”.
3. **Read both meanings.** Choose the one that fits. When both fit, the ending is the clue: -a for the companion, the same case as the first word for “and”.

The ending helps only when the first word has a different case. If the first word is also accusative, the ending cannot tell the two apart. Mistake 1 in step 4 shows this.

</Block>
</Step>

The Quran says “with” most often with the word **مَعَ**, with no wāw. The Corpus tags مَعَ 159 times in 152 verses.

مَعَ is a **noun of place** (*ẓarf*), not a preposition. It is accusative itself, and the pronoun after it is genitive, because it is the second word of an idafah. Al-Jadwal reads مَعَكُمَا that way: an accusative place word and a genitive pronoun.

Because مَعَ already says “with”, it needs no wāw and it is not a mafʿūl maʿah. The mafʿūl maʿah is the noun that comes in after a wāw.

</Block>

Al-Jadwal’s first reading of this verse is the wāw of togetherness: the word مَا is the companion, “in the place of the accusative”. You cannot see an ending, because a joining noun keeps one shape (L3.11).

The same words come in 6:137, and al-Jadwal sends the reader back to 6:112.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal also names the other reading in the same line and in a footnote: the wāw means “and”, and مَا is joined to the object -hum. Then the verse says “leave them and leave what they invent”. The sense is close. The difference is only in the name of the job.</Note>

</Block>

<Block id="shuraka" title="The famous doubt: وَشُرَكَآءَكُمْ">

      en={['So settle your plan, and your partners too.']}

This is the best-known doubt. Nūḥ speaks to his people and tells them to settle their plan against him. The Corpus tags the wāw of وَشُرَكَآءَكُمْ as comitative.

Al-Jadwal reads it as plain “and”, with a note: you settle a plan, not people, so a word is missing, “the affair of your partners”. The two nouns are then both accusative objects. The other reading is the mafʿūl maʿah: “settle your plan together with your partners”. Al-Jadwal says Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī allowed it, and Ibn Hishām after him. It also names al-ʿUkbarī as giving it.

<Note kind="know">Both readings agree on the case, which is accusative. The fatḥa is the same, so the verse cannot settle the question. This lesson follows al-Jadwal’s first reading in the word-by-word panel and gives the other reading here.</Note>

The Corpus gives the tag “comitative” to exactly three wāws. Here is how al-Jadwal reads each.

- **3:142** has a verb after the wāw: وَيَعْلَمَ. Both sources call the wāw “along with”. It is not a noun, so it is not a mafʿūl maʿah. The next block shows it.
- **5:36** says وَمِثْلَهُۥ مَعَهُۥ, “and the like of it with it”. Al-Jadwal reads plain “and”. In a footnote it says al-Zamakhsharī allowed the mafʿūl maʿah, and Abū Ḥayyān refused it because the meaning does not hold.
- **10:71** is the verse of the last block.

So the Corpus’s list is short, and al-Jadwal reads two of its three wāws as plain “and”.

The wāw of togetherness can also stand before a **verb**. The verb then ends in -a, the subjunctive (L3.07), because a hidden **أَنْ** is understood after the wāw. The word after such a wāw is a verb, so it is not a mafʿūl maʿah. It is a cousin of it.

Al-Jadwal says this hidden أَنْ comes only after a negation or after a request, such as a command, a prohibition or a wish. In 3:142 the negation is لَمَّا. In the made-up sentence the request is لَا تَأْكُلِ.

A noun after the wāw of togetherness needs no negation and no request. That is a second difference.

Al-Jadwal reads the same construction in 2:188, 4:19 and 6:27 as the first reading, and in 2:42, 7:127 and 8:27 as a second reading.

The accusative has more jobs than any one lesson holds. *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* names fifteen. These seven are the ones Level 2 and Level 4 teach so far.

| Job | Question | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Object, مَفْعُول بِهِ | What? Whom? | [L2.05](/learn/level-2/05-accusative-case/) |
| State, حَال | How? In what state? | [L4.01](/learn/level-4/01-hal-clauses/) |
| Specifier, تَمْيِيز | Of what? | [L4.02](/learn/level-4/02-tamyiz/) |
| Word set apart after إِلَّا, مُسْتَثْنَى | Except whom? | [L4.05](/learn/level-4/05-exception-particles/) |
| The verb’s own noun, مَفْعُول مُطْلَق | How? (repeats the verb) | [L4.07](/learn/level-4/07-maf-ul-mutlaq/) |
| Reason, مَفْعُول لِأَجْلِهِ | Why? | [L4.08](/learn/level-4/08-maf-ul-li-ajlih/) |
| Companion, مَفْعُول مَعَهُ | Along with what? | this lesson |

All seven are accusative, and the mark follows the word, not the job: -a or -an on a singular noun, -ayni on a dual, -īna on a sound masculine plural, a kasra on a sound feminine plural (L2.05). The job is decided by the question.

<Note kind="tip">The question helps. Ask what the word adds to the action. A companion adds “along with whom?”. A ḥāl adds “in what state?”. An object adds “what?”.</Note>

<Block id="mistake-case" title="Mistake 1: thinking an accusative after a wāw proves “along with”">
  <Stage label="Both nouns are accusative">
    <Tiles
      label="Same ending, two different wāws"
      items={[
        { ar: 'أَكْرَمْتُ زَيْدًا وَعَمْرًا', en: 'made-up: “and”: two objects', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'سِرْتُ وَٱلنَّهْرَ', en: 'made-up: “along with”: a companion', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

In the first sentence عَمْرًا is accusative, and it is joined by “and”. Both Zayd and ʿAmr are objects of أَكْرَمْتُ. The accusative did not come from a wāw of togetherness.

An accusative noun after a wāw can be a second object, a second ḥāl, and so on. First ask what the word before the wāw is. Then ask whether the wāw means “and” or “along with”.

<Note kind="warn">An accusative noun after a wāw is not enough. Check the meaning and the word before it.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-verb" title="Mistake 2: calling a verb after the wāw a mafʿūl maʿah">
  <Stage label="Noun or verb?">
    <Tiles
      label="What follows the wāw"
      items={[
        { ar: 'وَٱلنَّهْرَ', en: 'a noun: mafʿūl maʿah', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'وَتَشْرَبَ', en: 'a verb: hidden أَنْ, not a mafʿūl maʿah', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The mafʿūl maʿah is a **noun**. A verb after the same wāw is in the subjunctive, with a hidden أَنْ. The job has another name, and it needs a negation or a request before it.

Look at what comes after the wāw. A noun can be the mafʿūl maʿah. A verb cannot.

<Note kind="warn">“After a negation or a command the wāw means along with” is only half true. That holds for a verb after the wāw, with a hidden أَنْ. It is not a rule for the noun.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-maa" title="Mistake 3: counting مَعَ and بِ as the mafʿūl maʿah">
  <Stage label="Three ways to say “with”">
    <Tiles
      label="Three different things"
      items={[
        { ar: 'مَعَ', en: 'a noun of place, “with”: 20:46', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'بِ', en: 'a preposition: “with” a tool, 29:48', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'وَ', en: 'a wāw of togetherness: the mafʿūl maʿah', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Three small things can mean “with”.

- **مَعَ** is a noun of place. It is accusative, and the next word is genitive (20:46).
- **بِ** is a preposition. In وَلَا تَخُطُّهُۥ بِيَمِينِكَ (29:48), “you did not write it with your right hand”, the bāʾ shows the tool. It does not show a companion.
- **وَ**, the wāw of togetherness, brings in the companion. Only this one makes a mafʿūl maʿah.

<Note kind="warn">Meaning “with” is not enough. A mafʿūl maʿah has a wāw and a noun that goes along with the action.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-force" title="Mistake 4: forcing a verse into the pattern">
  <Stage label="Three readings of one wāw">
    <Tiles
      label="Same words, three possible readings"

In the Corpus, 803 words are an accusative noun with an ordinary joining wāw in front, and two more (5:36 and 10:71) carry the comitative tag. In almost all of them the wāw means “and”.

Even the same word can be read in two ways. ٱلطَّيْرَ in 34:10 is a companion in al-Jadwal’s first reading. In 21:79, وَسَخَّرْنَا مَعَ دَاوُۥدَ ٱلْجِبَالَ يُسَبِّحْنَ وَٱلطَّيْرَ, it reads “and the birds”, joined to the mountains, with “along with” as a second reading. The Corpus tags both as plain “and”.

When you meet such a wāw, read the meaning first. Call it a mafʿūl maʿah only when “along with” is clearly what the verse says, and mention the other reading.

<Note kind="warn">Do not look for the mafʿūl maʿah in every wāw. The grammar books allow it in very few verses.</Note>
</Block>

Al-Jadwal says the clause “He has not yet known those who struggled” stands in the place of the accusative as a ḥāl. A footnote cites Ibn Hishām’s *Shudhūr al-Dhahab*, and gives another way to read the fatḥa: a jussive verb that got a fatḥa because two sukūns meet. We follow the first reading.

<Block id="practice-1" title="Practice: find the wāw, find the companion">
  <Stage label="For each wāw, say">

      <li><b>The meaning:</b> “and”, or “along with”?</li>

Work out each answer first, on paper or in your head. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Say whether the wāw is the wāw of “and” (ʿaṭf) or of “along with” (maʿiyya). All are made-up examples, except (c), which is from al-Ajurrumiyyah. (a) جَاءَ زَيْدٌ وَعَمْرٌو (b) سِرْتُ وَٱلنَّهْرَ (c) ٱسْتَوَى ٱلْمَاءُ وَٱلْخَشَبَةَ">

- (a) **“And”.** Both Zayd and ʿAmr are doers in the nominative (-un). Two doers, two comings.
- (b) **“Along with”.** One walker. The river is a companion in the accusative (-a).
- (c) **“Along with”.** The wood cannot rise level, so it cannot be a second doer. It is accusative (-a).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Look at Saba 34:10 (part): يَٰجِبَالُ أَوِّبِى مَعَهُۥ وَٱلطَّيْرَ. (a) Which word is the companion? (b) What is its case, and which mark shows it? (c) What is مَعَهُۥ, and is it a companion? (d) What would the word look like if the birds were a second doer in the nominative?">

- (a) **وَٱلطَّيْرَ**: the wāw and ٱلطَّيْرَ.
- (b) Accusative. The mark is the **fatḥa** on the last letter.
- (c) مَعَ is a noun of place (*ẓarf*), “with”, accusative. The -hu is genitive. It is **not** a companion: it has no wāw of togetherness.
- (d) It would end in a ḍamma: ٱلطَّيْرُ. The two readings differ only in the last vowel.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Al-Anam 6:112 (end) says فَذَرْهُمْ وَمَا يَفْتَرُونَ. (a) What is مَا? (b) Why can you not see its case? (c) Give the two readings of the wāw. (d) Does the meaning change?">

- (a) A **joining noun** (*ism mawṣūl*): “what”. The sentence يَفْتَرُونَ finishes it (the ṣila).
- (b) It keeps one shape (L3.11), so the case is “in the place of the accusative”.
- (c) Al-Jadwal’s first reading is the wāw of **togetherness**: مَا is a companion, “leave them along with what they invent”. The other reading is “and”: مَا is joined to the object -hum, “leave them and what they invent”.
- (d) Almost not at all. The sense is close. Only the name of the job changes.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Which accusative job is the word named after each sentence? All are made-up examples. (a) كَتَبَ رِسَالَةً: رِسَالَةً (b) جَاءَ رَاكِبًا: رَاكِبًا (c) عِشْرُونَ كِتَابًا: كِتَابًا (d) ضَرَبَ ضَرْبًا: ضَرْبًا (e) قَامَ إِجْلَالًا: إِجْلَالًا (f) سِرْتُ وَٱلنَّهْرَ: ٱلنَّهْرَ">

- (a) **Object** (مَفْعُول بِهِ): what was written.
- (b) **Ḥāl**: in what state he came.
- (c) **Tamyīz**: twenty of what?
- (d) **Mafʿūl muṭlaq**: the verb’s own noun, repeated.
- (e) **Mafʿūl li-ajlih**: why he stood.
- (f) **Mafʿūl maʿah**: along with what.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Find all the accusative words in each made-up sentence and name their jobs. (a) جَاءَ ٱلْأَمِيرُ وَٱلْجَيْشَ رَاكِبًا (b) كَتَبَ ٱلطَّالِبُ ٱلدَّرْسَ كِتَابَةً جَيِّدَةً رَغْبَةً فِي ٱلنَّجَاحِ (c) ضَرَبَ ٱلْمُعَلِّمُ ٱلطَّالِبَ ضَرْبًا شَدِيدًا غَضَبًا">

- (a) **ٱلْجَيْشَ**: mafʿūl maʿah (came along with the army). **رَاكِبًا**: ḥāl (how he came: riding).
- (b) **ٱلدَّرْسَ**: object (what he wrote). **كِتَابَةً جَيِّدَةً**: mafʿūl muṭlaq and its adjective (how he wrote, from the same root ك-ت-ب). **رَغْبَةً**: mafʿūl li-ajlih (why he wrote: out of desire).
- (c) **ٱلطَّالِبَ**: object (whom he struck). **ضَرْبًا شَدِيدًا**: mafʿūl muṭlaq and its adjective (the verb’s noun from ض-ر-ب). **غَضَبًا**: mafʿūl li-ajlih (why: out of anger).

In (a) the first accusative noun comes after a wāw of togetherness. Remember Mistake 1: check the meaning, not only the ending.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Say what follows the wāw (noun or verb), and which kind of wāw it is. (a) سِرْتُ وَٱلنَّهْرَ (made-up) (b) لَا تَأْكُلِ ٱلسَّمَكَ وَتَشْرَبَ ٱللَّبَنَ (made-up) (c) وَيَعْلَمَ ٱلصَّٰبِرِينَ (Al-Imran 3:142, end) (d) وَذَرْنِى وَٱلْمُكَذِّبِينَ (Al-Muzzammil 73:11, start)">

- (a) A **noun**. Wāw of togetherness: a mafʿūl maʿah, accusative.
- (b) A **verb**, تَشْرَبَ, in the subjunctive. Wāw of togetherness before a verb, with a hidden أَنْ, after the request لَا تَأْكُلْ. Not a mafʿūl maʿah.
- (c) A **verb**, يَعْلَمَ, with a fatḥa. Wāw of togetherness before a verb, after the negation لَمَّا.
- (d) A **noun**, ٱلْمُكَذِّبِينَ, accusative, -īna. Al-Jadwal’s first reading is plain “and”: the deniers are joined to the object -nī, “leave me and the deniers”. It also names “along with” as a possible reading. So this is **not** a clear mafʿūl maʿah.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Write the iʿrāb line of the word named in each. (a) ٱلطَّيْرَ in 34:10 (b) مَا in 6:112 (c) يَعْلَمَ in 3:142 (d) ٱلنَّهْرَ in سِرْتُ وَٱلنَّهْرَ (made-up) (e) شُرَكَآءَ in 10:71, al-Jadwal’s first reading">

- (a) مفعول معه منصوب وعلامة نصبه الفتحة
- (b) اسم موصول مبني في محل نصب مفعول معه
- (c) مضارع منصوب بأن مضمرة بعد واو المعية
- (d) مفعول معه منصوب وعلامة نصبه الفتحة
- (e) معطوف على أمر منصوب مثله

The line follows the kind of word: a visible noun, a joining noun, a verb, or “and”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Find the mistake in each statement and correct it. (a) “An accusative noun after a wāw is always a mafʿūl maʿah.” (b) “مَعَكُمَا in 20:46 is a mafʿūl maʿah.” (c) “The Quran is full of clear mafʿūl maʿah verses.” (d) “A verb after a wāw of togetherness is a mafʿūl maʿah.”">

- (a) An accusative noun after a wāw can also be a second object, a second ḥāl and so on, joined by “and”. Check the meaning and the word before the wāw.
- (b) مَعَ is a noun of place, accusative, and the pronoun after it is genitive. There is no wāw of togetherness.
- (c) The Corpus tags three wāws as comitative. Al-Jadwal’s first reading is a companion noun in only one verse without a doubt, 34:10.
- (d) The mafʿūl maʿah is a noun. A verb after the wāw is in the subjunctive, with a hidden أَنْ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Why can this lesson not hand you one clean list of Quran verses with a mafʿūl maʿah? Name the two sources and what they say.">

The two sources do not agree on a single verse with a noun. The **Corpus** tags three wāws as comitative (3:142, 5:36, 10:71), and one of them is before a verb. **Al-Jadwal** reads two of those three as plain “and”. Its own first readings for a noun are 34:10, 6:112 and 6:137, where the Corpus tags plain “and”. For ten more verses it names “along with” only as a second reading.

So a learner must read the verse, look at the meaning, and give the other reading too.

</Exercise>

  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula

      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'an action', small: 'سِرْتُ' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'wāw: along with', small: 'وَاو ٱلْمَعِيَّة' },

- The **mafʿūl maʿah** is an accusative noun after a wāw that means “along with”. It shows who or what the action went along with (*al-Ajurrumiyyah*).
- The wāw of **togetherness** is a little word with no case. The wāw of **“and”** joins a word to an earlier word and gives it the same job.
- When the second noun cannot do the action, only “along with” is left, as in ٱسْتَوَى ٱلْمَاءُ وَٱلْخَشَبَةَ. When both readings work, the ending is the clue.
- An accusative after a wāw does not prove “along with”. Check the meaning.
- مَعَ is a noun of place, not a mafʿūl maʿah. بِـ is a preposition.
- The Quran has very few clear cases: 34:10 without a doubt, 6:112 and 6:137 with a doubt. The Corpus tags 3:142, 5:36 and 10:71 as comitative. Where scholars differ, the lesson names both readings.
- The same wāw can stand before a verb, which then ends in -a because of a hidden أَنْ (3:142).
- The accusative jobs so far: object, ḥāl, tamyīz, the word after إِلَّا, mafʿūl muṭlaq, mafʿūl li-ajlih and mafʿūl maʿah.
