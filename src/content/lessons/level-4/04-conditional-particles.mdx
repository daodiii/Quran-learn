The verse opens with **فَمَن**, “so whoever”. Two verbs follow, and both are cut short. يَعْمَلْ ends in a sukūn. يَرَهُۥ lost the last letter of يَرَىٰ.

The plain forms are يَعْمَلُ and يَرَىٰ. L3.07 called this cut jazm. Here the word مَنْ brings it. مَنْ is a **condition word**. The first verb is the condition. The second is the answer. L4.03 teaches that pattern. This lesson teaches the words that open it.

    </blockquote>
    <p class="lx-quote-note"><i>Al-Ajurrumiyyah</i>, chapter on the verbs. Vowel marks added by Quran Learn.</p>
  </Stage>

L3.07 showed this list. *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* counts 18 words of jazm. The first six are not conditions: لَمْ, لَمَّا, أَلَمْ, أَلَمَّا, the lām of command and لَا of a ban. The other **twelve** are condition words: إِنْ, مَا, مَنْ, مَهْمَا, إِذْمَا, أَيّ, مَتَى, أَيَّانَ, أَيْنَ, أَنَّى, حَيْثُمَا and كَيْفَمَا. Al-Jadwal calls each one a condition word that cuts (شَرْط جَازِم). The book adds إِذَا, but only for poetry.

That makes **two families** of words that open a condition.

| Family | Words in the Quran | What happens to the verbs |
|---|---|---|
| Jazm (جَازِم, “cuts”) | إِنْ, مَنْ, مَا, مَهْمَا, أَيْنَمَا, حَيْثُمَا, أَيّ | The condition and the answer are in jazm |
| No jazm (غَيْر جَازِم) | إِذَا, لَوْ, لَوْلَا, لَمَّا | The verbs keep their usual ending |

Al-Jadwal names the second family in the same way: a condition word that does not cut (شَرْط غَيْر جَازِم).

<Note kind="know">Seven of the twelve words of jazm are in the Quran as condition words: إِنْ, مَنْ, مَا, مَهْمَا, أَيْنَمَا, حَيْثُمَا and أَيّ. In the Corpus file مَتَىٰ (9 times), أَيَّانَ (6) and أَنَّىٰ (28) are tagged as question words, or as a plain noun or time word, and never as conditions. إِذْمَا and كَيْفَمَا are not in the text at all. The Corpus misses a few conditions that al-Jadwal reads (17:110 below, أَيْنَمَا in 33:61, and مَّا in 35:2 from L3.11), so the Corpus counts in this lesson are Quran Learn counts of tags, and some tags are wrong.</Note>

</Block>

        { ar: 'يَرَهُۥ', en: 'the last letter is gone (99:7)', type: 'v' },

L3.07 taught three signs of jazm on a present verb. A sound verb takes a **sukūn**: يَعْمَلْ. The five verbs lose their **nūn**: تَصْبِرُونَ becomes تَصْبِرُوا۟. A verb that ends in a weak letter loses it: يَرَىٰ becomes يَرَ.

A **past verb** cannot show jazm. كُنتُمْ stays كُنتُمْ, and جَآءَ stays جَآءَ. Past verbs are built on a fixed sound (L3.05). The books say such a verb stands **in the place of jazm** (فِي مَحَلّ جَزْم). It counts as being in jazm, and you cannot see it.

So the rule “the condition word puts both verbs in jazm” is true of every verb after إِنْ, مَنْ and the others. You can see it only on the present ones.

إِنْ is the plainest condition word. It means “if”. It is a little word (حَرْف), so it has no case and no job in the sentence. It cuts both verbs: تَمْسَسْكُمْ and تَسُؤْهُمْ each end in a sukūn.

In the Corpus file, إِنْ is tagged as a condition word **578** times. The word right after it is a past verb 384 times, a present verb 186 times (31 of them after لَمْ), and a noun or another word 8 times. Most of the time you will not see the jazm after إِنْ, because most of the verbs are past. The next block shows what that looks like.

<Note kind="know">A little later in the same verse, the condition is وَإِن تَصْبِرُوا۟ وَتَتَّقُوا۟. Both verbs lose their nūn. The answer is لَا يَضُرُّكُمْ, and its last letter has a ḍamma, not a sukūn. Al-Jadwal reads it as a plain present verb, with a فَ understood in front of it. So an answer does not always show jazm. L4.03 teaches the answer.</Note>

Here the verb after إِنْ is **جَآءَكُمْ**, a past verb. Al-Jadwal says it stands in the place of jazm. Nothing in its shape changes: it keeps its fatḥa, because past verbs are built (L3.05).

The answer is a command with a فَ in front. Al-Jadwal says the whole answer sentence stands in the place of jazm too. L4.03 explains why this answer needs the فَ. The word-by-word panel for this verse is below.

إِنْ with a past verb is very common. The Quran uses it again and again with كَانَ: إِن كُنتُمْ, “if you are”. The verb كُنتُمْ ends in a sukūn, which belongs to every past verb with the ending -tum. It is not the sign of jazm.

مَنْ and مَا are **nouns** (اسْم شَرْط). A noun has a job in the sentence. مَنْ means “whoever” and is for people. In فَمَن فَرَضَ it is the mubtadaʾ. مَا means “whatever” and is for things. In وَمَا تَفْعَلُوا۟ it is the object of تَفْعَلُوا۟: ask “you do what?”

Both verses sit side by side in 2:197. The first condition verb is past, so no jazm shows. In the second, both verbs show it: تَفْعَلُوا۟ lost its nūn and يَعْلَمْهُ ends in a sukūn.

In the Corpus, the word right after a conditional مَنْ is a past verb 86 times and a present verb 98 times (184 in all). After a conditional مَا it is a past verb 9 times and a present verb 14 times (23 in all).

<Note kind="know">What is the khabar of مَنْ? Al-Jadwal gives the common reading: the condition clause is the khabar, so فَمَن يَعْمَلْ… has يَعْمَلْ… as its khabar. It adds that the condition and the answer together can be the khabar.</Note>

The same family has words for “where” and “which”.

| Word | Means | Its job | In the Quran |
|---|---|---|---|
| مَهْمَا | whatever (things) | object | 7:132 |
| أَيّ | whichever | object, here before its verb | 17:110, 28:28 |
| أَيْنَمَا, أَيْنَ مَا | wherever | place word (ẓarf makān) | 2:115, 2:148, 4:78, 33:61 |

The Corpus tags مَهْمَا once, أَيْنَ three times, حَيْثُ مَا twice and أَيّ once (28:28). Al-Jadwal reads 17:110 and 33:61 as conditions too. The Corpus tags the أَيًّا of 17:110 as a noun.

The words are written in more than one way. Tanzil writes مَا as a separate word in أَيْنَ مَا (2:148) and حَيْثُ مَا (2:150), and joined in أَيْنَمَا (2:115). Al-Jadwal reads them the same either way.

In the Corpus, إِذَا is tagged as a time word **405** times. A past verb follows it right away 321 times (79%), a present verb 19 times, a command 2 times, and a noun or another word 63 times. No present verb is in jazm because of إِذَا: the one jussive in these 405, in 7:203, comes after لَمْ.

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* agrees. It lists إِذَا among the words of jazm for poetry only.

<Note kind="know">When a noun comes right after إِذَا, as in إِذَا ٱلسَّمَآءُ ٱنفَطَرَتْ (82:1), al-Jadwal reads the noun as the doer of a hidden verb. The verb that follows explains it. So “when the sky splits” has the same shape as the verses above.</Note>

لَوْ opens a condition about something that is **not so**, or that did not happen. Both verbs here are past: كَانَ and لَفَسَدَتَا. The لَ in front of the answer is the mark of the answer of لَوْ (L4.03). Al-Jadwal calls لَوْ a condition word that does not cut.

Some summaries call this “impossible”. That word is too strong. In فَلَوْ شَآءَ لَهَدَىٰكُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ (6:149), “if He had willed, He would have guided you all”, the guidance did not happen. Nothing says it could not.

In the Corpus, لَوْ is tagged as a condition word 185 times. A past verb follows right away 121 times (65%). A present verb follows 31 times. The other 33 are a noun, أَنَّ and similar words.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal lists more uses of لَوْ. It can be a polite offer, a wish (فَلَوْ أَنَّ لَنَا كَرَّةً, 26:102, “if only we had a return”), and a little word that turns what follows into a noun (وَدُّوا۟ لَوْ تُدْهِنُ, 68:9, “they wish you would soften”). The Corpus tags 16 more لَوْ as subordinating words, which is close to that last kind.</Note>

A common shortcut says that لَوْ always takes a past verb, so that لَوْ أَكُونُ would be wrong. The Quran does not agree. Other verses have the same shape: لَوْ نَشَآءُ لَجَعَلْنَٰهُ حُطَٰمًا (56:65, “if We wished, We would make it chaff”) and وَلَوْ يُؤَاخِذُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلنَّاسَ بِظُلْمِهِم (16:61, “if Allah took people to account for their wrong”).

So after لَوْ you may meet a past verb or a present one. Neither is in jazm.

The noun is the mubtadaʾ, and its khabar is left out, like “if you had not been there”. Al-Jadwal says the khabar is left out here, and must be. The answer is past, with لَ.

It is not a condition and has no answer.

<Block id="mistake-jazm" title="Mistake 2: looking for jazm in the wrong place">
  <Stage label="Made-up examples">
    <Tiles
      label="Where jazm can and cannot be"
      items={[
        { ar: 'إِذَا يَذْهَبْ', en: 'jazm after إِذَا: not in prose', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'إِذَا ذَهَبَ', en: 'correct', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'إِنْ ذَهَبْ', en: 'a sukūn on a past verb: not a word', type: 'x' },

      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

There are two mix-ups here.

- **Jazm after إِذَا.** *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* lists إِذَا for poetry only. In ordinary Arabic, and in the Quran, the verb after إِذَا is not in jazm. If you see a cut-short verb after إِذَا, another word is doing it, like لَمْ in 7:203.
- **A sukūn on a past verb.** After إِنْ a past verb keeps its fatḥa: إِنْ ذَهَبَ. The jazm is in the place of the verb, not on it.

Do not think that only three words cause jazm: إِنْ, مَنْ and مَا. The book counts twelve condition words, and the Quran uses seven of them.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-law" title="Mistake 3: “لَوْ is impossible, and always past”">
  <Stage label="Look at the verbs after لَوْ">
    <Tiles

      items={[

      ]}
    />

Two common claims are too strong.

- **“لَوْ means impossible.”** It says what is not so, or did not happen. “If He had willed” (6:149) is not impossible. It is unreal.
- **“لَوْ always needs a past verb.”** 31 of the 185 conditional لَوْ in the Corpus are followed by a present verb, and 49:7 is one of them.

Both verbs after لَوْ are in the usual ending, and the answer often starts with لَ. L4.03 teaches that part.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-man-ma" title="Mistake 4: reading every مَنْ and مَا as a condition">
  <Stage label="Three words, three jobs">
    <Tiles
      label="Three مَنْ"

L3.11 showed that مَنْ and مَا do many jobs. In the Corpus, مَنْ is tagged as a joining noun 650 times, a condition 184 times and a question 37 times. مَا is a joining noun 1,476 times, a negation 705 times, and a condition only 23 times.

So a مَنْ or مَا is not a condition until the sentence says so. Look for the signs from this lesson: two verbs in a row that are cut short, or stand in the place of jazm, and an answer clause, often opened by فَ. Without them it is probably the joining noun, the question or the “not”.

<Exercise n={1} q="Choose the word: إِنْ, إِذَا, لَوْ or مَنْ. All four sentences are made-up examples. (a) ____ جَاءَ الشِّتَاءُ لَبِسْنَا الْمِعْطَفَ: “When winter comes, we put on the coat.” (b) ____ دَرَسْتَ لَنَجَحْتَ: “If you had studied, you would have passed.” (c) ____ تَدْرُسْ تَنْجَحْ: “If you study, you will pass.” (d) ____ يَدْرُسْ يَنْجَحْ: “Whoever studies passes.”">

- (a) **إِذَا**. Winter will come, and the verb is past with no jazm. إِنْ would also be good Arabic, but it would leave the arrival open.
- (b) **لَوْ**. You did not study, so the case is unreal. The verb is past and the answer starts with لَ.
- (c) **إِنْ**. Both verbs show jazm: تَدْرُسْ and تَنْجَحْ end in a sukūn.
- (d) **مَنْ**. The verbs look the same as in (c). The difference is the meaning: “whoever”, and مَنْ is the mubtadaʾ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Three phrases from verses. For each say the condition word, its family, and what you see on the verbs: (a) وَإِن تَصْبِرُوا۟ وَتَتَّقُوا۟ (Al-Imran 3:120, part) (b) حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا جَآءَ أَحَدَهُمُ ٱلْمَوْتُ (Al-Muʾminun 23:99) (c) فَلَوْ شَآءَ لَهَدَىٰكُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ (Al-Anʿam 6:149)">

- (a) **إِنْ**, the jazm family. Both verbs lost their nūn: تَصْبِرُونَ and تَتَّقُونَ became تَصْبِرُوا۟ and تَتَّقُوا۟. The answer, لَا يَضُرُّكُمْ, keeps its ḍamma in al-Jadwal's reading.
- (b) **إِذَا**, the second family. جَآءَ is past and shows nothing. The death is sure, so the word fits the usual tendency.
- (c) **لَوْ**, the second family. شَآءَ is past, and the answer هَدَىٰكُمْ is past with لَ. The guidance did not happen: it is unreal, not impossible.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Two conditions from Surah Yusuf. For each, name the condition word and the condition verb, and say what you see on it: (a) قَالُوٓا۟ إِن يَسْرِقْ فَقَدْ سَرَقَ أَخٌ لَّهُۥ مِن قَبْلُ (Yusuf 12:77, part) (b) فَإِن لَّمْ تَأْتُونِى بِهِۦ فَلَا كَيْلَ لَكُمْ عِندِى (Yusuf 12:60, part)">

- (a) **إِنْ** and **يَسْرِقْ**, a present verb with a sukūn on the ق: jazm. The answer, فَقَدْ سَرَقَ, is a past verb with قَدْ, so it starts with فَ. “If he steals, a brother of his stole before.”
- (b) **إِنْ** and **تَأْتُونِى**. The verb comes after لَمْ. Its own nūn is gone, as in تَأْتُوا۟. The nūn you see is the protecting nūn (نُون الوِقَايَة) in front of the ي of “me”. Al-Jadwal says the verb is the condition verb in jazm, and that لَمْ only says “not” here. The answer, فَلَا كَيْلَ لَكُمْ, is a sentence with فَ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Compare إِن تَنصُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ يَنصُرْكُمْ (Muhammad 47:7, part) with إِذَا جَآءَ نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلْفَتْحُ (An-Nasr 110:1). Why إِنْ in one and إِذَا in the other? How do the verbs differ?">

In 47:7 the help of the believers is open: it depends on them. The verse uses إِنْ. In 110:1 the victory is promised, and the sura uses إِذَا. This is the usual tendency, not a rule, and 21:34 breaks it.

The verbs differ more clearly. After إِنْ both verbs show jazm: تَنصُرُوا۟ lost its nūn and يَنصُرْكُمْ ends in a sukūn. After إِذَا, جَآءَ is a past verb and nothing is cut.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Say what jazm did to each verb: (a) وَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ شَرًّا يَرَهُۥ (Az-Zalzalah 99:8) (b) وَإِن تَعُودُوا۟ نَعُدْ (Al-Anfal 8:19, part) (c) وَمَا تُنفِقُوا۟ مِنْ خَيْرٍ يُوَفَّ إِلَيْكُمْ (Al-Baqarah 2:272, part)">

- (a) **يَعْمَلْ** has a sukūn. **يَرَهُۥ** lost the last letter of يَرَىٰ.
- (b) **تَعُودُوا۟** lost its nūn. **نَعُدْ** is a verb with a weak middle letter: نَعُودُ became نَعُودْ with a sukūn, and the long ū dropped. L4.12 gives the rule.
- (c) **تُنفِقُوا۟** lost its nūn. **يُوَفَّ** lost the last letter of يُوَفَّىٰ. Do not read the missing end vowel as a sign of the passive. The passive is shown by the vowels inside the verb (L3.09). The jazm is the lost letter.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Exercise n={6} q="Which verbs show jazm after إِنْ, and which only stand in its place? (a) إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ (Al-Imran 3:175, end) (b) إِن تَسْتَفْتِحُوا۟ (Al-Anfal 8:19, start) (c) إِن جَآءَكُمْ فَاسِقٌۢ (Al-Hujurat 49:6, part) (d) إِن يَسْرِقْ (Yusuf 12:77, part)">

- (a) **كُنتُم**: past, in the place of jazm. Nothing shows.
- (b) **تَسْتَفْتِحُوا۟**: jazm shows, because the nūn is gone.
- (c) **جَآءَكُمْ**: past, in the place of jazm. Nothing shows.
- (d) **يَسْرِقْ**: jazm shows, as a sukūn on the ق.

Two of the four show it, and two stand in its place. The Corpus counts agree: past verbs are the majority after إِنْ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Condition, joining noun, question or negative? Decide for the word مَنْ or مَا in each: (a) وَمَن يَفْعَلْ ذَٰلِكَ (Al-Munafiqun 63:9, part) (b) وَلَهُۥ مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ (Ar-Rum 30:26) (c) مَن ذَا ٱلَّذِى يَشْفَعُ عِندَهُۥٓ (Al-Baqarah 2:255, part) (d) وَمَا تُنفِقُونَ إِلَّا ٱبْتِغَآءَ وَجْهِ ٱللَّهِ (Al-Baqarah 2:272, part)">

- (a) **Condition**: “whoever”. يَفْعَلْ ends in a sukūn, and an answer follows with فَ.
- (b) **Joining noun**: “those who are in the heavens and the earth”. No condition, no answer, and the verb is not cut.
- (c) **Question**: “who is it that…?”
- (d) **Negative**: مَا with إِلَّا later, “you spend only to seek the face of Allah”. Compare the condition مَا in the same verse: وَمَا تُنفِقُوا۟ مِنْ خَيْرٍ يُوَفَّ. One uses تُنفِقُونَ with its nūn, the other تُنفِقُوا۟ without it.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Which لَوْلَا is a condition and which is urging? How do you tell? (a) لَوْلَآ أَنتُمْ لَكُنَّا مُؤْمِنِينَ (Saba 34:31, end) (b) لَوْلَآ أَخَّرْتَنِىٓ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍ قَرِيبٍ (Al-Munafiqun 63:10, part)">

- (a) **Condition**: “if it were not for you”. A pronoun follows, and the answer is past with لَ.
- (b) **Urging**: “why did You not give me more time?” A verb follows, and there is no answer. Al-Jadwal calls it a word of urging (حَرْف تَحْضِيض).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Write the verb as it looks after إِنْ. The plain forms are: (a) يَكْتُبُ (b) تَكْتُبُونَ (c) يَأْتِي (d) يَدْعُو (e) تَكْتُبْنَ">

- (b) تَكْتُبُوا۟: the nūn is gone.
- (c) يَأْتِ: the ي is gone.
- (d) يَدْعُ: the و is gone.
- (e) تَكْتُبْنَ: no change. This is the women's plural, and its nūn is part of the form (L3.07).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Two of these made-up sentences are wrong. Find them, say what is wrong, and mend them. (a) إِذَا يَذْهَبْ زَيْدٌ أَذْهَبْ (b) إِنْ ذَهَبْ زَيْدٌ أَذْهَبْ (c) لَوْ يَدْرُسُ لَنَجَحَ">

- (a) **Wrong.** إِذَا does not cut the verb in prose. Write إِذَا ذَهَبَ زَيْدٌ ذَهَبْتُ.
- (b) **Wrong.** A past verb cannot take a sukūn in place of its fatḥa. Write إِنْ ذَهَبَ زَيْدٌ أَذْهَبْ.
- (c) **Correct.** لَوْ may take a present verb. The meaning is unreal: “if he studied, he would have passed”. Compare 49:7.

</Exercise>

- A condition word opens a condition sentence. There are **two families**. إِنْ, مَنْ, مَا, مَهْمَا, أَيْنَمَا, حَيْثُمَا and أَيّ cut both verbs short (jazm). إِذَا, لَوْ, لَوْلَا and لَمَّا leave the verbs alone.
- Jazm shows as a sukūn, a lost nūn or a lost weak letter. A past verb after a jazm word stands **in the place of jazm**, and nothing shows. Most verbs after إِنْ are past.
- مَنْ and مَا are condition **nouns**: “whoever”, “whatever”. They have a job in the sentence. إِنْ is a little word and has none.
- إِذَا is “when”: usually a past verb for something to come. It is used for what you expect, and إِنْ for what is open. This is a tendency, not a rule.
- لَوْ is for what is not so or did not happen. It can take a past or a present verb. لَوْلَا is “if it were not for”, with a noun after it.
- There is a second لَمَّا, “not yet”, before a verb in jazm. إِنْ also means “not”, and مَنْ and مَا are often joining nouns.
