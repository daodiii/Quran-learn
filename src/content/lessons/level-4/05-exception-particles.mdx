The verse is about Ṭālūt and his soldiers at a river. Read it from right to left.

A group does something: they drank. Then comes إِلَّا, “except”. After it comes قَلِيلًا, “a few”. The few are taken out of the group of drinkers.

The group is only the ending **-ū** on فَشَرِبُوا۟. L3.05 taught that the ending names the doer, so no separate word for “they” is written.

<Block id="parts" title="Three parts, three names">
  <Stage label="The three parts">
    <Tiles

The grammar books name each part.

- **The group** is مُسْتَثْنَى مِنْهُ (*mustathnā minhu*), “the one something is taken out of”. In 2:249 it is the “they” inside فَشَرِبُوا۟.
- **The little word** is أَدَاة ٱلِٱسْتِثْنَاء (*adāt al-istithnāʾ*), “the tool of exception”. In this step it is إِلَّا. Step 3 adds غَيْر and سِوَى.
- **The word set apart** is مُسْتَثْنَى (*mustathnā*). L3.22 named it in Al-ʿAsr (103:3). This lesson teaches how its case is chosen.

إِلَّا is a little word (L1.04), so it has no case. The word after it does, and the case changes from sentence to sentence. That is the subject of step 2.

</Block>

Compare the two sentences.

In the first one, أَحَدٌ names the group: “anyone”. The grammar books call this a **complete** sentence (*tāmm*). The group is there, and one word is taken out of it.

In the second one, nobody is named. The sentence is **incomplete** (*nāqiṣ*). Zayd is not taken out of a group. He is the doer of جَاءَ, and إِلَّا adds the meaning “only”.

“Complete” does not mean that the sentence has a verb and a doer. Both sentences have a verb and a doer. It means that the group is named.

<Block id="book" title="What al-Ajurrumiyyah says">
  <Stage label="From al-Ajurrumiyyah">
    <blockquote class="lx-quote" lang="ar" dir="rtl">
      <p>فَالْمُسْتَثْنَى بِإِلَّا يُنْصَبُ إِذَا كَانَ الْكَلَامُ تَامًّا مُوجَبًا</p>
    </blockquote>
    <p class="lx-quote-note">From the Wikisource text. Vowel marks not checked against a printed edition.</p>
  </Stage>

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* has a chapter on the exception (*bāb al-istithnāʾ*). The line above says: the word set apart by إِلَّا is accusative when the sentence is complete and positive. “Positive” (*mūjab*) means the sentence is not negative.

The chapter goes on with two more rules, which this lesson gives in its own words.

1. A **complete negative** sentence: the word set apart can be a substitute (*badal*) or accusative.
2. An **incomplete** sentence: the word set apart takes the case that the sentence needs, “according to the operators”.

The chapter also says that the word after غَيْر and سِوَى is genitive. Step 3 returns to that.

Step 2 shows each rule in verses. Al-Jadwal names a fourth situation in its notes on verses, the “cut off” exception. You will meet it in step 2.

</Block>
</Step>

The verse is about Nūḥ. The group is “a thousand years”, and it is named. The sentence is positive. So the word set apart, خَمْسِينَ, is **accusative**.

You cannot see a fatḥa on خَمْسِينَ. It ends in **-īna**. L2.05 taught that a sound masculine plural takes -īna in the accusative. Al-Jadwal reads the word that way.

The verse of 2:249 in the first block is the same type: فَشَرِبُوا۟ مِنْهُ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا. The group is “they”. The sentence is positive. قَلِيلًا ends in **-an**, the accusative.

L3.22 promised this verse. The verse before it, 103:2, says إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَفِى خُسْرٍ, “mankind is in loss”. The group is named: mankind. The sentence is positive. So the word set apart is accusative.

But ٱلَّذِينَ shows no ending. L3.11 taught that a joining noun keeps one shape. A word with a fixed shape still has a case, and the books say it is **in the place of** that case. Al-Jadwal says ٱلَّذِينَ here is “in the place of the accusative, as the word set apart”.

The same happens in 2:249, a few words earlier: إِلَّا مَنِ ٱغْتَرَفَ, “except whoever scooped”. The joining word مَنِ is also in the place of the accusative.

<Note kind="tip">إِنَّ and إِلَّا look almost the same. إِنَّ has a nūn with a shadda. إِلَّا has a lām with a shadda and a final alif. After inna the first word is accusative (L2.10). After illā the word set apart is accusative here, for another reason.</Note>

The group in 4:66 is again “they”: the ending -ū of فَعَلُوهُ. The sentence is complete. This time it is **negative**: مَّا.

In a complete negative sentence the word set apart is a **substitute** (*badal*). It takes the place of the group and takes its case. The group is the doer -ū, and a doer is nominative. So قَلِيلٌ is **nominative**, with -un. Al-Jadwal reads it that way.

Look at the two sentences under the verse. The word is the same, قَلِيل. The case is different, because one sentence is positive and the other is negative.

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* says that in a complete negative sentence both the substitute and the accusative are allowed. The verse has the substitute.

لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ is a complete negative sentence. The group is named: إِلَٰهَ, “a god”.

The little word لَا here denies a whole kind. The books call it **lā al-nāfiya lil-jins**, “the lā that denies the kind”. It goes with a noun that has no tanwīn, here إِلَٰهَ. The noun is built on a fatḥa. That fatḥa is a fixed ending, not a case ending, and the books say the word is “in the place of the accusative”. L4.10 puts the words of negation side by side.

The sentence needs a news part, a khabar. The Arabic leaves it out, and the books supply **مَوْجُود**, “existing”: “there is no god existing”. The word set apart, ٱللَّهُ, is a substitute for the hidden pronoun inside that missing khabar. A pronoun in the khabar is nominative, so ٱللَّهُ is **nominative**. Al-Jadwal reads 47:19, 2:255 and 20:8 this way.

<Note kind="know">Grammarians read this phrase in more than one way. Al-Jadwal also gives a reading where ٱللَّهُ is a substitute for the place of لَا and its noun, which is nominative. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn gives a second way for 3:62 too. All the readings agree on the case of ٱللَّهُ: nominative. This lesson follows the first reading, the substitute for the pronoun in the missing khabar.</Note>

</Block>

This verse says the same words in another shape. The little word **مِنْ** is an extra, and it makes the sense wider: “any god at all”. The books call it *zāʾida*, “added”.

إِلَٰهٍ has a kasra, the mark of the genitive after مِنْ. In the sentence it is the mubtadaʾ, so it is “in the place of nominative”. The word set apart, ٱللَّهُ, is a substitute for that place. It is nominative.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal first reads ٱللَّهُ here as the khabar of إِلَٰهٍ, with إِلَّا as a word of restriction: “there is no god but Allah”. Its footnote gives the substitute reading above, and says Abū Ḥayyān chose it. This lesson follows the substitute reading, so that 3:62 and 47:19 match. Both readings give a nominative ٱللَّهُ.</Note>

No word names a group here. The sentence is **incomplete**, and إِلَّا means “only”. Al-Jadwal calls it a word of **restriction** (*ḥaṣr*). The sentence says that Muhammad is a messenger and nothing more.

Take away وَمَا and إِلَّا. What is left is مُحَمَّدٌ رَسُولٌ, “Muhammad is a messenger”. In that sentence مُحَمَّدٌ is the mubtadaʾ and رَسُولٌ is the khabar. Both are nominative (L2.01).

That is the rule for an incomplete sentence: the word after إِلَّا takes the case of its **job**. Here the job is the khabar, so it is nominative. *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* says the same: the case is “according to the operators”.

<Block id="choose" title="Choose the case: four questions">
  <Stage label="The decision">
    <Tiles
      label="Four situations, four cases"
      items={[
        { ar: 'تَامّ مُوجَب', en: 'accusative', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'تَامّ مَنْفِيّ', en: 'substitute: its case', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'مُنْقَطِع', en: 'accusative', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'نَاقِص', en: 'the case of its job', type: 'x' },

Ask these questions in order about the word after إِلَّا.

1. **Is the group named?** If not, the sentence is incomplete. Take away the negation and إِلَّا, and give the word the case of its job.
2. **Is the word set apart a different kind from the group?** If yes, it is cut off, and it is accusative.
3. **Is the sentence positive?** If yes, it is accusative.
4. **Is the sentence negative?** Then the word is a substitute for the group and takes its case. The accusative is also allowed.

| Situation | Example | Case of the word set apart |
|---|---|---|
| Complete, positive | فَشَرِبُوا۟ مِنْهُ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا (2:249) | accusative |
| Complete, negative | مَّا فَعَلُوهُ إِلَّا قَلِيلٌ (4:66) | the case of the group (substitute) |
| Cut off | إِلَّا سَلَٰمًا (19:62) | accusative |
| Incomplete | وَمَا مُحَمَّدٌ إِلَّا رَسُولٌ (3:144) | the case of its job |

The tags do not follow the four situations of this lesson. For example, 9:39 and 29:14 get the same tag, and they are different. Use the sentence, not the tag.</Note>

غَيْر is a **noun**, not a little word. It means “other than” (*ghayr*). It does the work of إِلَّا, but it is built differently.

The word after غَيْر is its second word in an idafah (L2.08), so it is always **genitive**. In 7:59 the second word is the pronoun -hu, “Him”. *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* says the word after غَيْر is genitive.

غَيْر is a noun, so it has a case of its own. In 7:59 it describes إِلَٰهٍ, so it follows the case of that word: nominative. Al-Jadwal reads it as an adjective of إِلَٰهٍ that follows its place.

<Note kind="know">The Quran text has غَيْر 147 times, in Quran Learn's count of the Corpus file. Not every one means “other than” as an exception. Many mean only “not” or “another”, as in 9:39, غَيْرَكُمْ, “other than you”.</Note>

سِوَىٰ (*siwā*) works like غَيْر. It is a noun, and the word after it is genitive. *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* names it with غَيْر.

The difference is the ending. سِوَىٰ ends in an alif (alif maqṣūra), and an alif cannot show a vowel. The accusative is there, and it is hidden. You know it from the sentence and not from the letters.

We did not find the word سِوَىٰ in the Quran text, by a letter search of the Corpus file. The word سُوًى in 20:58 is a different word, “middle”. So سِوَىٰ here is taught from the book and from made-up sentences. This is a tendency of the language, not a pattern you will meet in verses.

The iʿrāb line of **إِلَّا** is short. In a complete sentence al-Jadwal writes أداة استثناء, “a word of exception”. In an incomplete sentence it writes أداة حصر, “a word of restriction”. Both are little words and have no case.

The line of the word set apart follows the situation.

| Situation | The line, as the books write it |
|---|---|
| Complete, positive (قَلِيلًا, 2:249) | مستثنى بإلا منصوب وعلامة نصبه الفتحة |
| A word with a fixed ending (ٱلَّذِينَ, 103:3) | اسم موصول مبني في محل نصب على الاستثناء |
| Complete, negative (قَلِيلٌ, 4:66) | بدل من ضمير الفاعل مرفوع وعلامة رفعه الضمة |

| Incomplete (رَسُولٌ, 3:144) | خبر المبتدأ مرفوع وعلامة رفعه الضمة |
| After غَيْر (زَيْدٍ) | مضاف إليه مجرور وعلامة جره الكسرة |

In an incomplete sentence the line is the line of the job: khabar, object, doer, or a phrase with a preposition.

Sometimes the letters إِلَّا are two words joined: إِنْ, “if”, and لَا, “not”. Al-Jadwal reads 9:39 that way: إِنْ is a conditional, and لَا is a negation. There is no group and no word set apart.

It is easy to see the accusative غَيْرَ and put the next word in the accusative too. The case of غَيْر and the case of the word after it are two different things.

غَيْر takes the case of the sentence. The word after it is the second part of an idafah, so it is genitive every time.
</Block>

<Exercise n={1} q="Name the group, إِلَّا and the word set apart in each. (a) فَشَرِبُوا۟ مِنْهُ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا (Al-Baqarah 2:249, part) (b) فَلَبِثَ فِيهِمْ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ إِلَّا خَمْسِينَ عَامًا (Al-Ankabut 29:14, part) (c) Made-up example: مَا رَأَيْتُ أَحَدًا إِلَّا عَلِيًّا">

- (b) Group: **أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ**, “a thousand years”. Word set apart: **خَمْسِينَ**.
- (c) Group: **أَحَدًا**, “anyone”. Word set apart: **عَلِيًّا**, “Ali”.

In all three the group is named, so the sentence is complete. (a) and (b) are positive: accusative. (c) is negative: عَلِيًّا is a substitute for أَحَدًا, which is accusative (the object), so it is accusative too.

<Exercise n={2} q="Say the situation and the case of the word after إِلَّا. (a) إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ (Al-Hijr 15:31, part) (b) مَآ أَنتُمْ إِلَّا بَشَرٌ مِّثْلُنَا (Ya-Sin 36:15, part) (c) وَمَا يُضِلُّ بِهِۦٓ إِلَّا ٱلْفَٰسِقِينَ (Al-Baqarah 2:26, part) (d) وَمَا ٱلنَّصْرُ إِلَّا مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ (Al-Imran 3:126, part) (e) إِنْ هَٰذَآ إِلَّا مَلَكٌ كَرِيمٌ (Yusuf 12:31, part)">

- (a) The group is the angels of the verse before. The sentence is positive. **Accusative**: إِبْلِيسَ, with -a. Scholars disagree whether it is connected or cut off, and the case is the same.
- (b) Incomplete. بَشَرٌ is the khabar of أَنتُمْ: **nominative**, -un.
- (c) Incomplete. Take away وَمَا and إِلَّا: يُضِلُّ بِهِ ٱلْفَٰسِقِينَ, “He misleads the wrongdoers”. ٱلْفَٰسِقِينَ is the object: **accusative**, with -īna (the mark of a sound masculine plural).
- (d) Incomplete. Take away وَمَا and إِلَّا: ٱلنَّصْرُ مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ. The khabar is the phrase مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ. عِندِ is **genitive**, after مِنْ.
- (e) Incomplete. مَلَكٌ is the khabar of هَٰذَآ: **nominative**, -un. كَرِيمٌ describes it and is also nominative.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Write the word set apart with its case ending. All are made-up examples, and the word is Zayd (زَيْد). (a) جَاءَ ٱلْقَوْمُ إِلَّا … (b) مَا جَاءَ أَحَدٌ إِلَّا … (c) مَا جَاءَ إِلَّا … (d) مَا رَأَيْتُ إِلَّا … (e) مَا مَرَرْتُ إِلَّا بِ… (f) مَا مَرَرْتُ بِأَحَدٍ إِلَّا …">

- (a) **زَيْدًا**: complete and positive, accusative.
- (b) **زَيْدٌ**: complete and negative, a substitute for the doer أَحَدٌ. (زَيْدًا is also allowed, as the book says.)
- (c) **زَيْدٌ**: incomplete, and Zayd is the doer.
- (d) **زَيْدًا**: incomplete, and Zayd is the object.
- (e) **زَيْدٍ**: incomplete. The whole phrase بِزَيْدٍ is set apart, and Zayd is the noun after the preposition بِ, so it is genitive.
- (f) **زَيْدٍ**: complete and negative. Zayd is a substitute for أَحَدٍ, which is genitive after بِ. (زَيْدًا is also allowed.)

Sentence (f) looks like (e), but it names the group أَحَدٍ, so it is complete.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Answer about لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ (Muhammad 47:19, part). (a) What is لَا? (b) What is إِلَٰهَ, and why is there no tanwīn? (c) Which word is left out? (d) What case is ٱللَّهُ, and why? (e) Two books read the phrase in different ways. What do they agree on?">

- (a) **lā al-nāfiya lil-jins**: the little word that denies a whole kind, “no god at all”.
- (b) إِلَٰهَ is the noun of لَا and the group. It has a fixed fatḥa and no tanwīn, because that is how the noun after this لَا is built. The books say it is “in the place of the accusative”.
- (c) The khabar, **مَوْجُود**, “existing”.
- (d) **Nominative**, with -u. The sentence is complete and negative, so ٱللَّهُ is a substitute for the hidden pronoun in the missing khabar, and a pronoun in a khabar is nominative.
- (e) They agree on the case: nominative. Some books call ٱللَّهُ a substitute for the place of لَا and its noun, and some call it the khabar. The case is the same.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Say the case and the job of the word named after each verse. (a) مِّنْ إِلَٰهٍ غَيْرُهُۥٓ (Al-Araf 7:59, part): غَيْرُ (b) إِنِ ٱلْحُكْمُ إِلَّا لِلَّهِ (Yusuf 12:40, part): ٱللَّهِ (c) وَمَا مُحَمَّدٌ إِلَّا رَسُولٌ (Al-Imran 3:144, start): what does إِلَّا mean?">

- (b) **ٱللَّهِ** is genitive, after the preposition لِ. The phrase لِلَّهِ is the khabar.
- (c) **“Only”**. No group is named, so the sentence says that Muhammad is a messenger and nothing more.

<Exercise n={7} q="The word قَلِيل is accusative in 2:249 and nominative in 4:66. Say why.">

In 2:249, فَشَرِبُوا۟ مِنْهُ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا, the sentence is complete and positive. The word set apart is accusative: **قَلِيلًا**.

In 4:66, مَّا فَعَلُوهُ إِلَّا قَلِيلٌ, the sentence is complete and negative. The word set apart is a substitute for the doer -ū. The doer is nominative, so **قَلِيلٌ** is nominative.

The group is the same “they”. The type of sentence is what changes the case.

</Exercise>

- (b) بدل من ضمير الفاعل مرفوع وعلامة رفعه الضمة

- (e) نعت لإله تبع محله فهو مرفوع

<Exercise n={9} q="Find the mistake in each statement and correct it. (a) “A sentence with a verb and a doer is complete.” (b) “The word after إِلَّا is always accusative.” (c) “The word after غَيْر is accusative.” (d) “إِلَّا always means except.”">

- (a) **Complete** means the group is named. A sentence can have a verb and a doer and still be incomplete: مَا جَاءَ إِلَّا زَيْدٌ.
- (b) It is accusative in a complete positive sentence and in a cut off exception. In a complete negative sentence it takes the case of the group, and in an incomplete sentence it takes the case of its job.
- (c) The word after غَيْر is **genitive**. غَيْر itself takes the case of the sentence.
- (d) In an incomplete sentence it means “only” (3:144). It can also be two words, إِنْ and لَا, “if not” (9:39).

</Exercise>

- (b) **Yes.** A noun follows, قَلِيلٌ. The sentence is complete and negative.
- (c) **Yes.** A joining noun follows, مَنِ. The sentence is complete and positive, so the joining noun is in the place of the accusative.

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="a group, except, one word taken out"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'the group', small: 'مُسْتَثْنَى مِنْهُ' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'إِلَّا', small: '“except”, or “only”' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'the word set apart', small: 'مُسْتَثْنَى' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- An exception has three parts: the group, the little word, and the word set apart. إِلَّا has no case. The word after it does.
- **Complete** means that the group is named. **Incomplete** means that it is not.
- **Complete and positive:** the word set apart is accusative (2:249, 29:14, 103:3).
- **Complete and negative:** the word is a substitute and takes the case of the group (4:66, 3:62, 47:19). The accusative is also allowed.
- **Cut off:** the word is a different kind from the group, and it is accusative (19:62, 4:157).
- **Incomplete:** the word takes the case of its job, and إِلَّا means “only” (3:144, 12:40).
- لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ is complete and negative. ٱللَّهُ is nominative: a substitute for the hidden pronoun in the missing khabar.
- **غَيْر** and **سِوَىٰ** are nouns. The word after them is genitive. غَيْر takes the case that the word after إِلَّا would take.
- إِلَّا can also be إِنْ and لَا, “if not” (9:39).
