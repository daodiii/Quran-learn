A cold read means you do the work before you see the answer. Each verse of this lesson has two blocks. The first block shows the verse and nothing else, with a short task. Write your answer, then open **Show the answer**. The second block is called Check your work, and it goes through the verse word by word.

The tasks tell you what to look for: the verb, the doer, the object. They never give the answer. Each task block also lists a few words to look up, with their meaning only. A real cold read lets you use a dictionary, and a meaning does not give the grammar.

Write first, even if you are unsure. Then you can see exactly where you and the model differ.

<Note kind="know">The five moves of L5.01 are Quran Learn’s teaching order, built from Levels 1 to 4. No source in this project gives a numbered method. The order of this lesson (try, compare, check, mark) is a teaching method too.</Note>

Read these eight verses aloud once, from top to bottom, right to left in each line. Do not analyse yet. The title of this lesson says whose counsel it is.

The Corpus counts **146 written words** in the eight verses. Verse 15 is the longest, with 29 words, and verse 19 the shortest, with 11. You will take the verses one at a time, in this order: 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19.

**Your task.** Write your answer, then open the model.

- Find the first verb. Say its root, its form, its doer and its object or objects.
- The verb يَشْكُرْ is in the verse twice, with a different ending each time. Say what each ending is and what caused it.
- Find the condition sentences. For each one, name the word that opens it, the condition, the answer and the word that joins the answer.
- Place each sentence (move 5 of L5.01).

**Words to look up.** ٱلْحِكْمَةَ is “wisdom”. لِنَفْسِهِۦ is “for himself”. غَنِىٌّ is “free of need”. حَمِيدٌ is “praised”.

<Exercise n={1} q="Take Luqman 31:12 apart. (a) The first verb: root, form, doer and objects. (b) The two condition sentences. (c) Why the two يَشْكُرْ endings differ. (d) The place of each sentence.">

**Meaning (ours):** “We gave Luqman wisdom: be thankful to Allah. Whoever is thankful is thankful only for himself. And whoever is ungrateful, Allah is free of need and praised.”

| Task | Model answer |
|---|---|
| (a) First verb | **ءَاتَيْنَا**: past, Form IV, root أ ت ي. The doer is -nā, “we”. It takes two objects: **لُقْمَٰنَ** (the one given to) and **ٱلْحِكْمَةَ** (what is given), both accusative ([L3.13](/learn/level-3/13-verb-form-iv/), [L5.15](/learn/level-5/15-nahw-synthesis/#objects)). |
| (b) Conditions | The first: **وَمَن يَشْكُرْ** is the condition and **فَإِنَّمَا** **يَشْكُرُ** **لِنَفْسِهِۦ** is the answer. The second: **وَمَن كَفَرَ** is the condition and **فَإِنَّ** **ٱللَّهَ** **غَنِىٌّ** **حَمِيدٌ** is the answer. Both open with **مَن**, “whoever” ([L4.04](/learn/level-4/04-conditional-particles/)). Each answer is joined by **فَ**, because it starts with إِنَّمَا or إِنَّ and not with a verb in jazm ([L4.03](/learn/level-4/03-conditional-sentences/)). |
| (c) Two endings | The first يَشْكُرْ follows مَن and ends in a sukūn: jazm ([L3.07](/learn/level-3/07-subjunctive-jussive/)). The second يَشْكُرُ stands inside the answer, after إِنَّمَا. Nothing cuts it short, so it keeps its ḍamma. |
| (d) Places | The first sentence is the answer of an oath that is not spoken (the لَ and قَدْ, [L4.06](/learn/level-4/06-emphasis-affirmation/)). ٱشْكُرْ لِلَّهِ explains it: أَنِ means “that is”. The two answers stand in the place of jazm. |

If you missed (b), go to L4.03 and L4.04. If you missed (c), go to L3.07.

Hold your answer next to the cards. Three places are worth a second look.

**The unspoken oath.** وَلَقَدْ holds an oath lām and قَدْ. L4.06 taught both. Al-Jadwal says the oath itself is left out, and ءَاتَيْنَا لُقْمَٰنَ ٱلْحِكْمَةَ is its answer.

**أَنِ.** A little word with the sense “that is”. It joins a verb of giving, which holds the sense of saying, to the words that say what was said. New in this lesson.

**A Corpus error.** The Corpus tags لُقْمَٰنَ as genitive. It is the object of ءَاتَيْنَا, so it is accusative. The name has no tanwīn, which is why the ending looks plain (L2.06).

Four points to compare with your answer.

**One run of sentences.** Al-Jadwal joins the condition sentence of this verse to وَصَّيْنَا in verse 14. Verses 14 and 15 are one run that sits between two parts of Luqman’s speech (Mistake 5).

**A ban and a command.** تُطِعْهُمَا and وَصَاحِبْهُمَا both end in a sukūn plus هُمَا. One has a prefix and follows لَا. The other has neither (Mistake 2).

**Two words in -an.** مَعْرُوفًا is not a ḥāl in al-Jadwal (Mistake 3).

**A figure.** Al-Jadwal names a figure in مَا لَيْسَ لَكَ بِهِۦ عِلْمٌ. It calls it “reversing the apparent” (ʿaks al-ẓāhir), also “denying a thing by affirming it”. Its reading: the wording denies the knowledge where it could have denied the thing known, so the phrase means “what is nothing”. L4.17 and L4.18 do not define this figure. This is al-Jadwal’s reading only.

Two places to compare.

**A new kind of noun.** أَنكَرَ is on the pattern أَفْعَل and means “most …”. Levels 1 to 4 do not teach this pattern as a lesson. You can read it from the pattern, the root ن ك ر and the idafah that follows it.

**A figure.** Al-Jadwal names the last words, صَوْتُ ٱلْحَمِيرِ, an open metaphor (istiʿāra taṣrīḥiyya, [L4.18](/learn/level-4/18-figures-of-speech/)). The word “like” is left out, and the voice of those who raise their voices is called the voice of donkeys. Its reason, in our words: it makes the blame, and the warning against a loud voice, stronger.

<Block id="mis-1" title="Mistake 1: a sukūn always means a ban">
  <Stage label="Three sukūns, three causes">
    <Tiles

A present verb that ends in a sukūn is in jazm, but the ban is only one cause. The cause is the word in front of the verb: the لَا of a ban, a condition word such as مَن or إِن, or a فَ that joins a verb to one already in jazm (فَتَكُن).

Ask what stands before the verb. Then look for the same verb in a place where nothing cuts it short: يَشْكُرُ in the answer of verse 12 keeps its ḍamma.

  <Stage label="One ending, two kinds of verb">
    <Tiles
      label="both end in a sukūn and هُمَا (31:15)"
      items={[

Both verbs end in a sukūn and are followed by هُمَا, “both of them”. They are different verbs. تُطِعْهُمَا is a present verb in jazm: it has the prefix تُ and comes after لَا. صَاحِبْهُمَا is a command: it has no prefix, and it is built on a sukūn.

The test is the front of the verb. A prefix and a لَا mean a ban. A bare stem means a command (L3.08).

A لَ at the start of a word can be three different things. At the start of a verse, before قَدْ, al-Jadwal reads it as the lām of an oath that is not spoken (L4.06). - You took eight verses of Surah Luqman apart, one at a time, before you saw an answer. Two of them (31:16 and 31:17) held words you had met in L1.10 and L4.13. Six were new.
- Verse 12: an oath that is not spoken, two objects, an explanation with أَنِ, and two conditions with مَن. The verb يَشْكُرْ is in jazm after مَن and is raised inside the answer.
- Verses 13 to 17: a call, a ḥāl clause, a ban, a reason; sentences put in between the words of Luqman; a condition with Form III and a ban beside a command; an inna sentence whose news is a condition; four commands of four kinds.
- Verses 18 and 19: two bans and a plain negative, a word in -an with two readings, an elative, a doubled command, the moved lām.
- Al-Jadwal names three figures in the passage: “reversing the apparent” (31:15), *tatmīm* (31:16) and an open metaphor (31:19). Only the last is defined in a lesson of this course (L4.18).
- The Corpus has two tag errors in the passage: لُقْمَٰنَ as genitive (31:12) and تُطِعْهُمَا as 3FS (31:15).
