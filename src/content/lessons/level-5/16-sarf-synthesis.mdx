Nuh tells his people to ask for forgiveness. Four words in the verse are worth a closer look: فَقُلْتُ, ٱسْتَغْفِرُوا۟, كَانَ and غَفَّارًا. فَقُلْتُ shows no و, and كَانَ has a long ā where a root letter should be. ٱسْتَغْفِرُوا۟ starts with ٱسْتَ, and غَفَّارًا has a doubled letter. Ask these four questions about a word, one after the other.

1. **Root.** Peel off the little words, the article, the endings and the prefixes. Count what is left. Pour it back into ف ع ل to test it. ([L3.03](/learn/level-3/03-root-system/))
2. **Kind.** Is the root sound, or does it hold a weak letter, a hamza or a repeated letter? ([L4.11](/learn/level-4/11-weak-verbs-intro/))
3. **Form.** For a verb, is it Form I (the bare verb) or one of II to X? ([L3.04](/learn/level-3/04-verb-form-i/), [L3.12](/learn/level-3/12-verb-form-ii/) to [L3.18](/learn/level-3/18-verb-forms-vi-vii-ix/))
4. **Shape.** For a verb: past, present or command, which mood, and active or passive. For a noun: doer word, done-to word, verbal noun, or noun of place. ([L3.05](/learn/level-3/05-past-tense/) to [L3.09](/learn/level-3/09-passive-voice/), [L3.19](/learn/level-3/19-active-passive-participles/) to [L3.21](/learn/level-3/21-nouns-place-time/))

<Note kind="know">This order of four questions is a teaching summary. No classical book gives it as one method. Each question uses a tool that a lesson of Levels 1 to 4 taught.</Note>

A word is a root with things added. [L3.03](/learn/level-3/03-root-system/) gave the list of what to peel off.

| Piece | Examples | Taught in |
|---|---|---|
| A joining word or little prefix | وَ، فَ، بِ، لِ، كَ | [L1.04](/learn/level-1/04-three-word-types/), [L2.07](/learn/level-2/07-prepositions-genitive/) |

| A pronoun ending | ـهُ، ـهُمْ، ـكُمْ، ـنَا | [L3.02](/learn/level-3/02-attached-pronouns/) |
| A tense prefix or doer ending on a verb | يَ، تَ، نَ، أَ and ـُوا، ـُونَ، ـْتُ | [L3.05](/learn/level-3/05-past-tense/), [L3.06](/learn/level-3/06-present-tense/) |
| A plural, dual or feminine ending | ـِينَ، ـُونَ، ـَانِ، ـَاتٌ، ـَةٌ | [L1.08](/learn/level-1/08-singular-dual-plural/) |
| A case ending | ـُ، ـَ، ـِ، ـٌ | [L1.10](/learn/level-1/10-case-endings/) |

Now peel the three words in the tiles.

| Word | Peeled off | Left | Root |
|---|---|---|---|
| لِيَعْبُدُونِ | لِ (“so that”), يَ (prefix), the doer ending ـُوا, and نِ (the protecting nūn, with “me” left out) | عْبُد | ع ب د |
| فَأَخْرَجَ | فَ (“and then”), and the أَ that Form IV adds | خْرَجَ | خ ر ج |
| ٱلْمَرْعَىٰ | ٱلْ, and the مَ of a noun of place | رْعَىٰ | ر ع ي |

The last root letter of ٱلْمَرْعَىٰ is a ي. It shows as the ending ىٰ, a long ā. The Corpus gives the root ر ع ي. Where a word shows fewer than three letters, or a long vowel where a letter should be, the Corpus root is the answer.

The kind depends on where the special letter sits in the root. The root decides, in every form of the word ([L4.11](/learn/level-4/11-weak-verbs-intro/)).

| Kind | The root has | Example root | Example | Verb words in the Corpus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sound | no weak letter, no hamza, no repeat | خ ل ق | يَخْلُقُ | 8,030 (41.5%) |
| Hollow | a weak letter in the middle | ق و ل | قَالَ | 5,162 (26.7%) |
| Defective | a weak letter at the end | ق ض ي | قَضَىٰٓ | 3,481 (18.0%) |
| Assimilated | a weak letter at the start | و ق ع | وَقَعَتِ | 999 (5.2%) |
| Hamzated | a hamza as one of the letters | أ م ر | يَأْمُرُ | 2,955 (15.3%) |
| Doubled | the last two letters the same | م س س | مَسَّ | 872 (4.5%) |

The counts are Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus, over 19,356 verb words. A word can fall in two kinds, so the shares add up to more than 58.4%, the share of verb words that are not sound. Some roots hold two kinds: جَآءَ (ج ي ء) is hollow and hamzated, and ءَاتَى (أ ت ي) is hamzated and defective.

To find the kind: peel, count the letters, look at the Corpus root, and name the kind from where the special letter sits. Three clues from L3.03 and L4.11 help before you look it up.

- Only two letters left after peeling means a letter is hidden or has dropped.
- A long ā, ū or ī where a root letter should be often stands for a weak letter.
- A shadda on the last root letter can mean a doubled root.

<Note kind="know">The clues are a teaching summary. They fit most verbs, and L4.12 to L4.15 give the cases where they do not. No approved book states the weak-verb rules as one list. When a clue is not enough, the Corpus root tells you what is true.</Note>

</Block>

[L3.03](/learn/level-3/03-root-system/) taught the scale ف ع ل. The ف stands for the first root letter, the ع for the second, the ل for the third. Extra letters and a doubled letter show in the scale word.

| Scale word | Root poured in | Word | The clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| فَعَلَ | ك ت ب | كَتَبَ | Only vowels. No extra letter. |
| فَعَّلَ | ع ل م | عَلَّمَ | The second letter is doubled. |
| يُفَعِّلُ | ع ل م | يُعَلِّمُ | يُ in front, and the second letter doubled. |
| مَفْعُول | ك ت ب | مَكْتُوب | An extra م in front and an extra و after the second letter. |

To test a root, pour it back into the pattern. If the word you get is the word you started with, the root is right. A few roots have four letters (L3.03 and L3.04). Their scale word has a fourth ل. They are rare, so test three letters first.

The Quran has ten forms. The root stays the same in all ten. Only the shape around it changes. The table gives the past and the present scale word, what the shape adds, one Quran verb, and the count of verb words (Quran Learn’s count from the Corpus: 19,356 verb words in all).

| Form | Past and present | What the shape adds | A Quran verb | Verb words |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | فَعَلَ، يَفْعَلُ / يَفْعِلُ / يَفْعُلُ | nothing: the bare verb | كَتَبَ (6:12) | 12,356 |
| II | فَعَّلَ، يُفَعِّلُ | the middle letter is doubled | عَلَّمَ (96:5) | 1,300 |
| III | فَاعَلَ، يُفَاعِلُ | a long ā after the first letter | قَٰتَلَهُمُ (9:30) | 334 |
| IV | أَفْعَلَ، يُفْعِلُ | أَ in front | أَنزَلَ (2:22) | 3,487 |
| V | تَفَعَّلَ، يَتَفَعَّلُ | تَ in front and the middle letter doubled | تَوَكَّلْتُ (11:56) | 414 |
| VI | تَفَاعَلَ، يَتَفَاعَلُ | تَ in front and a long ā | تَبَٰرَكَ (67:1) | 77 |

The shape of a form often brings an idea. The form lessons all say how far to trust it.

| Form | An idea that often comes with it | What the lesson says |
|---|---|---|
| II | A new doer makes it happen, calls it true or false, or does it many times | “It does not follow one rule.” 84 of the 171 Form II verbs have no Form I verb in the Quran ([L3.12](/learn/level-3/12-verb-form-ii/)). |
| III | A second party: with, against, towards | Some Form III verbs have nobody there, like هَاجَرَ ([L3.17](/learn/level-3/17-verb-form-iii/)). |
| IV | Carries the action over to someone | Many meanings are learned as they are, like ءَامَنَ ([L3.13](/learn/level-3/13-verb-form-iv/)). |
| V | Shows the receiver: teach and learn | “A tendency, not a law.” 41 of the 81 Form V roots have no Form II verb ([L3.15](/learn/level-3/15-verb-form-v/)). |
| VI | “One another”: تَعَاوَنُوا۟ | Not always: تَبَارَكَ ([L3.18](/learn/level-3/18-verb-forms-vi-vii-ix/)). |
| VII | It happens to the doer or by itself | The lesson aims at recognising the form ([L3.18](/learn/level-3/18-verb-forms-vi-vii-ix/)). |
| VIII | Read the verse | “The shape alone does not give it” ([L3.14](/learn/level-3/14-verb-form-viii/)). |
| IX | Colours: ٱسْوَدَّتْ, ٱبْيَضَّتْ | Only five verb words ([L3.18](/learn/level-3/18-verb-forms-vi-vii-ix/)). |

Use the idea as a first guess and then read the verse. The meaning of the verb in its verse always has the last word.

The past tense names the doer in its ending ([L3.05](/learn/level-3/05-past-tense/)). The root stays the same, and the ending changes. This is the whole past of كَتَبَ, root ك ت ب.

| Group | Endings | Who | With كَتَبَ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | -a, -at | he, she | كَتَبَ، كَتَبَتْ |
| 2 | -ā, -atā | the two (men), the two (women) | كَتَبَا، كَتَبَتَا |
| 3 | -ū, -na | they (men), they (women) | كَتَبُوا، كَتَبْنَ |
| 4 | -ta, -ti, -tumā, -tum, -tunna, -tu | you (five ways), I | كَتَبْتَ، كَتَبْتِ، كَتَبْتُمَا، كَتَبْتُمْ، كَتَبْتُنَّ، كَتَبْتُ |
| 5 | -nā | we | كَتَبْنَا |

Count them: six forms of the third person, five of “you”, and “I” and “we” make 13. Four points from L3.05 are worth keeping.

- The sukūn falls on the **last root letter**: كَتَبْتُ, not a vowel before the ending.
- The ت of -at is an ordinary tāʾ, the sign of the feminine, and not a tāʾ marbūṭa.
- -na (they, women) and -nā (we) differ by one alif.
- A past verb has no case. Its last sound is fixed, and the books call it built (مَبْنِيّ): on fatḥa for -a, -at and -ā, on ḍamma for -ū, and on sukūn for the consonant endings.

Read it like a map of the earlier lessons. The rows for “the two” and “you two” are left out; they take the endings -ā and -atā in the past and -āni in the present.

- **After لَمْ** the verb is in jazm. One person takes a sukūn, the five verbs lose their nūn, and the women’s plural does not change.

A present verb has three endings: the plain form, naṣb and jazm. A verb has no genitive ([L3.07](/learn/level-3/07-subjunctive-jussive/)). A little word in front brings naṣb or jazm. The sign differs from verb to verb.

|---|---|---|---|

The grammar book that L3.07 follows counts ten words of naṣb and eighteen of jazm. L3.07 teaches five words of naṣb (أَنْ, لَن, كَيْ, لِ “so that”, حَتَّىٰ) and three of jazm (لَمْ, لَا of a ban, the lām of command). A weak last letter shows a visible -a in naṣb (on ي and و), a hidden -a on a long ā, and drops in jazm.

The passive leaves the doer out. The consonants stay and the vowels change ([L3.09](/learn/level-3/09-passive-voice/)).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

<Block id="short-commands" title="Short commands and the weak letter that falls">

A weak letter gives way in three ways ([L4.11](/learn/level-4/11-weak-verbs-intro/)). It can turn into another letter (قَضَىٰٓ), get a sukūn (نَسْتَعِينُ) or drop out (قُلْ). Al-Jadwal names this change *iʿlāl*. The five commands that are shorter than their pattern show the drop at its clearest.

| Command | From | Root | Kind | What is missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| قُلْ | قَالَ | ق و ل | hollow | the middle و |
| ٱدْعُ | دَعَا | د ع و | defective | the last و |
| ٱهْدِ | هَدَىٰ | ه د ي | defective | the last ي |
| كُلْ | أَكَلَ | أ ك ل | hamzated | the first hamza |
| خُذْ | أَخَذَ | أ خ ذ | hamzated | the first hamza |

The weak letter comes back when an ending follows: قُولُوا۟ (33:70), “say!” to a group. The Quran has no كُلْ, “eat!” to one man. It has كُلُوا۟ to a group, and كُلْ here is the base form that L3.08 uses.

In the present of Form I, an assimilated verb loses its first root letter: وَجَدَ gives يَجِدُ, وَعَدَ gives يَعِدُ and وَصَلَ gives يَصِلُ, and the command is عِدْ, قِفْ, هَبْ ([L4.14](/learn/level-4/14-assimilated-verbs/)). Al-Jadwal’s reason is that the و stands between يَ and a kasra. The و stays in the past and the passive, in Forms II to VI and X, and in two verbs that keep it in the present, وَجِلَ and وَدَّ.

<Note kind="know">Several rules in L4.14 are teaching summaries. The sources name each change case by case, and L4.14 says so where it matters.</Note>

<Block id="hamza-doubled" title="The hamza, the seat and the doubled letter">
  <Stage label="Where a hamza sits">
    <Tiles
      label="The hamza at the end of a word sits on the letter before it"
      items={[
        { ar: 'بَدَأَ', en: 'fatḥa before: an alif', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'قُرِئَ', en: 'kasra before: a yāʾ seat', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'شَىْءٍ', en: 'sukūn before: alone', type: 'n' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A hamzated verb has a hamza as one of its three root letters ([L4.15](/learn/level-4/15-hamzated-verbs/)). It is a full consonant, unlike a weak letter, so it usually stays through the forms: أَخَذْنَا, يَأْخُذُ. What changes is the **seat**, the letter it is written on.

- At the start of a word it sits on an alif: أَخَذَ.
- At the end of a word, the vowel on the letter before decides: fatḥa gives an alif (بَدَأَ), kasra a yāʾ seat (قُرِئَ), ḍamma a wāw, and a sukūn or a long vowel the line, alone (شَىْءٍ, جَآءَ).
- In the middle the strongest vowel wins, from strongest to weakest: kasra, ḍamma, fatḥa, sukūn. So سُئِلَ has a yāʾ seat, يُؤْمِنُونَ a wāw and سَأَلَ an alif.
- Two hamzas join into one long vowel: ءَامَنَ (ā), إِيمَٰن (ī), أُوتُوا۟ (ū).
- More than half of the hamzated verb words are in roots that also hold a weak letter, like جَآءَ and أَتَىٰ.

<Note kind="know">The spelling rules are a Quran Learn teaching summary of the standard rules for writing the hamza. L4.15 found no line in the approved sources that states them, and checked each rule against words in the Tanzil text.</Note>

A **doubled** root has a repeated last letter, like م س س. When the second letter carries a vowel, the two merge into one letter with a shadda: مَسَّ. When the last letter has a sukūn, they stay apart: يَمْسَسْكُمْ after إِن, and ضَلَلْتُ before -tu ([L4.11](/learn/level-4/11-weak-verbs-intro/)). A shadda on a verb does not always mean a doubled root: in كَذَّبَ it is Form II.

Both words at the end of 56:51 are doer words (*ism fāʿil*), and the Corpus tags both as active participles. ٱلضَّآلُّونَ is a Form I doer word, on the pattern فَاعِل, from the doubled root ض ل ل. ٱلْمُكَذِّبُونَ is a Form II doer word. Al-Jadwal names it as a doer word from the verb كَذَّبَ, on the pattern مُفَعِّل ([L3.19](/learn/level-3/19-active-passive-participles/)).

The two kinds of participle are easy to build.

- **Form I:** the doer word is **فَاعِل** and the done-to word is **مَفْعُول**: كَاتِبٌ, “writer”, and مَكْتُوبًا, “written”.
- **Forms II to X:** a مُ plus the present verb. A **kasra** before the last letter gives the doer word, a **fatḥa** gives the done-to word.

| Form | Present verb | Doer word | Done-to word |
|---|---|---|---|
| II | يُصَدِّقُ | مُصَدِّقًا (2:41) | مُّكَرَّمَةٍ (80:13) |
| IV | يُؤْمِنُ | مُؤْمِنِينَ (2:8) | ٱلْمُرْسَلِينَ (2:252) |
| V | يَتَكَبَّرُ | ٱلْمُتَكَبِّرِينَ (16:29) | none in this table |
| VIII | يَخْتَلِفُ | مُخْتَلِفًا (6:141) | مُّتَّبَعُونَ (26:52) |
| X | يَسْتَكْبِرُ | مُّسْتَكْبِرُونَ (16:22) | ٱلْمُسْتَضْعَفِينَ (4:75) |

Both are nouns. They take case endings, ٱلْ, tanwīn, gender and number, and they have no time of their own. The sentence tells you when. A doer word can take an object, and the sentence stays nominal. Not every describing word is a participle: ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ, ٱلرَّحِيمِ and عَلِيمٌ are not.

A noun of place or time is made from a verb root with مَ in front. It names where or when an action happens ([L3.21](/learn/level-3/21-nouns-place-time/)).

| Present of the Form I verb | Pattern | Noun | In the Quran |
|---|---|---|---|
| قَعَدَ يَقْعُدُ (ḍamma) | مَفْعَل | مَقْعَد | its plural مَقَٰعِدَ (3:121) |
| جَمَعَ يَجْمَعُ (fatḥa) | مَفْعَل | مَجْمَع | مَجْمَعَ (18:60) |
| جَلَسَ يَجْلِسُ (kasra) | مَفْعِل | مَجْلِس | its plural ٱلْمَجَٰلِسِ (58:11) |

- **The rule:** look at the present, not the past. Kasra there gives مَفْعِل. Any other vowel gives مَفْعَل. Al-Jadwal states the rule in its notes on 17:80.
- **Eleven exceptions:** eleven words break the rule, among them مَشْرِق, مَغْرِب and مَسْجِد. They have a kasra, and you learn them as they are.
- **Longer verbs** (Forms II to X) have no choice of vowel. The noun takes the shape of the passive participle: مُدْخَل (17:80) from أَدْخَلَ, and مُسْتَقَرٌّ (2:36) from ٱسْتَقَرَّ.
- **One shape, three jobs:** the same shape can name a place, a time or the action. The sentence decides.
- **The plural** is مَفَاعِل, a diptote. A noun of place is not the same as a place word (ẓarf).

Each verse has a Form I verb and a Form II verb. The Corpus tags سَوَّىٰ and قَدَّرَ as Form II. They have a doubled middle letter and nothing in front. خَلَقَ and هَدَىٰ are Form I.

سَوَّىٰ and هَدَىٰ are both defective, and both end in ىٰ ([L4.13](/learn/level-4/13-defective-verbs/)). The weak last letter shows as a long ā. The ىٰ spelling points to a ي, but the spelling does not always show the root, so check the “I” form. Here the Corpus gives the roots س و ي and ه د ي. L3.12 names the same pattern in صَلَّىٰ, وَفَّىٰ and سَمَّىٰ: ىٰ in the past with the shadda.

<Exercise n={1} q="Find the root and the kind of each word. (a) مُسْلِمُونَ (Al Imran 3:64) (b) وَٱسْتَغْفَرَ (An-Nisa 4:64) (c) وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ (Al-Asr 103:3) (d) مَقَامًا (Al-Isra 17:79)">

- (a) مُسْلِمُونَ: peel the ending ـُونَ and the مُ. Root **س ل م**, sound. The Corpus tags it a Form IV doer word (pattern مُفْعِل). The verb is أَسْلَمَ.
- (b) وَٱسْتَغْفَرَ: peel the وَ and the ٱسْتَ. Root **غ ف ر**, sound. Form X, the past “he”.
- (c) وَتَوَاصَوْا۟: peel the وَ, the تَ, the long ā and the ending. Root **و ص ي**: assimilated and defective, two kinds in one root. The Corpus tags it Form VI. The last root letter drops in front of the wāw of “they” (L4.13).
- (d) مَقَامًا: peel the مَ. Root **ق و م**, hollow. Al-Jadwal says it can be a verbal noun that begins with مَ (a *maṣdar mīmī*), or a noun of place.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Name the form (I to X) of each verb and its root. (a) عَلَّمَ (b) أَنزَلَ (c) تَوَاصَوْا۟ (d) ٱسْتَكْبَرَ (e) ٱنفَطَرَتْ">

- (a) عَلَّمَ: **Form II**. The middle letter ل is doubled and nothing is in front. Root ع ل م. Next to عَلِمَ, “knew”, it means “taught”. L3.12 says the doubling does not follow one rule, so read the verse.
- (b) أَنزَلَ: **Form IV**. The أَ is in front of ن ز ل. Form IV often carries the action over: نَزَلَ, “came down”, and أَنزَلَ, “sent down” (L3.13).
- (c) تَوَاصَوْا۟: **Form VI**. A تَ in front, then a long ā after the first root letter. Root و ص ي. The idea “one another” fits the verse, but Form VI does not always mean that (L3.18).
- (d) ٱسْتَكْبَرَ: **Form X**. ٱسْتَ in front of ك ب ر. The idea of counting oneself as big fits one of the groups of L3.16, which Quran Learn made for teaching and which are not a rule of Arabic.
- (e) ٱنفَطَرَتْ: **Form VII**. ٱنْ in front of three root letters ف ط ر, and the ending -at, “she”. It has four letters before the ending, which is the clue against a Form I command with ن (L3.18).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Name the kind of root and say what happens to the weak letter. (a) قَالَ (b) هَدَىٰ (c) وَجَدَ">

- (a) قَالَ: **hollow**, root ق و ل. The و shows as a long ā in the past and ū in the present: يَقُولُ. The long vowel is cut in front of a sukūn: قُلْتُ, قُلْنَا. Jazm and the command also cut it: لَمْ يَقُلْ, قُلْ.
- (b) هَدَىٰ: **defective**, root ه د ي. The ي shows as ىٰ in the past, and as a long ī with a hidden ḍamma in the present: يَهْدِي. Jazm and the command drop it: لَمْ يَهْدِ, ٱهْدِ. This is why ٱهْدِنَا (1:6) has no ي before the -nā: it dropped before the ending came on (L4.11).
- (c) وَجَدَ: **assimilated**, root و ج د. The و stays in the past: وَجَدَ, وَجَدْتُ. It falls in the present and the command of Form I: يَجِدُ, جِدْ. It stays in the passive and in Forms II to VI and X (L4.14).

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Block id="practice-2" title="Practice: verb shapes">

<Exercise n={4} q="Make the forms of the root ن ص ر, door 1 (نَصَرَ يَنْصُرُ). (a) past “I” (b) present “he” (c) after لَمْ, “he” (d) command to one man (e) past passive “he” (f) present passive “he” (g) doer word (h) done-to word">

- (a) نَصَرْتُ: the sukūn falls on the last root letter (L3.05).
- (b) يَنْصُرُ: the prefix يَ and the middle vowel ḍamma of door 1 (L3.04, L3.06).
- (c) لَمْ يَنْصُرْ: a sukūn in jazm (L3.07).
- (d) ٱنصُرْ: end like jazm, take off the تَ, add ٱ. It sounds u after a ḍamma (L3.08).
- (e) نُصِرَ: ḍamma on the first letter, kasra on the letter before the last (L3.09).
- (f) يُنْصَرُ: ḍamma on the prefix, fatḥa on the second root letter (L3.09).
- (g) نَاصِرٌ: the pattern فَاعِل (L3.19).
- (h) مَنْصُورٌ: the pattern مَفْعُول (L3.19).

These are teaching forms that follow the patterns of the lessons. The Quran has some of them and not all.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Name the kind of root of each form, and say where the weak letter or the hamza went. (a) قُلْنَا (b) دَعَوْا (c) لَمْ يَخْشَ (d) يَعِدُ (e) خُذْ (f) مَسَّ">

- (a) قُلْنَا: **hollow**, ق و ل. The ending -nā puts a sukūn on the ل, so the long ā of قَالَ is cut (L4.12).
- (b) دَعَوْا: **defective**, د ع و. This is “they (men)”: the alif of دَعَا dropped, and the و is the doer (L4.13, 25:13).
- (c) لَمْ يَخْشَ: **defective**, خ ش ي. The plain present is يَخْشَىٰ. After لَمْ the weak letter drops, and al-Jadwal calls the drop the sign of jazm (9:18).
- (d) يَعِدُ: **assimilated**, و ع د. The first root letter و fell in the present (2:268, L4.14).
- (e) خُذْ: **hamzated**, أ خ ذ. The command loses the hamza. The same happens to كُلْ (L4.15).
- (f) مَسَّ: **doubled**, م س س. The two س merged into one with a shadda. It is not Form II, because the shadda sits on a doubled root (L4.11).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Name the tense, mood and voice, and the root. (a) كُتِبَ (b) يُظْلَمُونَ (c) لِتَشْقَىٰٓ (d) أَقِيمُوا۟ (e) قِيلَ">

- (a) كُتِبَ: past passive, “it was written”. Root ك ت ب. A ḍamma on the first letter and a kasra before the last (L3.09, 2:183).
- (b) يُظْلَمُونَ: present passive, plain form, “they are wronged”. Root ظ ل م. The nūn stays, so no word of naṣb or jazm acts on it (L3.09, 6:160).
- (c) لِتَشْقَىٰٓ: present in naṣb after the lām of reason. Root ش ق ي, defective. The naṣb -a is hidden on the long ā (L3.07, L4.13, 20:2).
- (d) أَقِيمُوا۟: a command to “you all”, Form IV. Root ق و م, hollow. It starts with أَ, a hamza that is said (L3.08, L3.13, 2:110).
- (e) قِيلَ: past passive of قَالَ, “it was said”. Root ق و ل. The kasra moved to the ق and the و turned into ي (L3.09, 2:11).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Sort these roots into the six kinds. A root can belong to two. (a) ق ت ل (b) ب ي ع (c) ر م ي (d) و ع د (e) أ م ر (f) م س س (g) ج ي ء (h) و ق ي">

- (a) ق ت ل: sound.
- (b) ب ي ع: hollow (the middle letter is ي).
- (c) ر م ي: defective.
- (d) و ع د: assimilated.
- (e) أ م ر: hamzated.
- (f) م س س: doubled.
- (g) ج ي ء: hollow and hamzated (L4.11).
- (h) و ق ي: assimilated and defective (L4.11).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Take apart the first word of ٱقْرَأْ بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ (Al-Alaq 96:1). Find its root, its form and its tense. Where does the ٱ come from? Is the hamza at the start?">

The root is **ق ر أ**, hamzated. The hamza is the **last** root letter, not the first. The word is a Form I command to one man. The ٱ is the helping alif of a command (L3.08): from تَقْرَأُ end in a sukūn, تَقْرَأْ, take off the تَ, قْرَأْ, and add ٱ because the word would start with a silent letter. The hamza sits on an alif after the fatḥa (L4.15).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Take apart كَتَبَ and لَأَغْلِبَنَّ in كَتَبَ ٱللَّهُ لَأَغْلِبَنَّ أَنَا۠ وَرُسُلِىٓ (Al-Mujadila 58:21).">

**كَتَبَ**: root ك ت ب, sound, Form I, past “he”, door 1. **لَأَغْلِبَنَّ**: root **غ ل ب**, sound. A Form I present verb, “I”, with the prefix أَ. In front is the lām of an oath, and at the end the nūn of emphasis (L4.06). The verb has a kasra on the second root letter, so it is door 2 (يَغْلِبُ), but that is a reading of the vowel, not a line of al-Jadwal.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Block id="practice-3" title="Practice: nouns, words from Ayat al-Kursi and a mixed verse">

<Exercise n={10} q="Name the noun shape of each word and its root. (a) مَكْتُوبًا (b) كَاتِبٌ (c) مَجْلِس (d) ٱلْمُكَذِّبُونَ (e) مُدْخَل (f) شُرْبَ (g) قَاضٍ">

- (a) مَكْتُوبًا: done-to word, pattern مَفْعُول. Root ك ت ب (L3.19).
- (b) كَاتِبٌ: doer word, pattern فَاعِل. Root ك ت ب.
- (c) مَجْلِس: noun of place on مَفْعِل, because the present is يَجْلِسُ with a kasra. Root ج ل س (L3.21).
- (d) ٱلْمُكَذِّبُونَ: Form II doer word, pattern مُفَعِّل. Root ك ذ ب (56:51).
- (e) مُدْخَل: noun of place from the Form IV verb أَدْخَلَ, in the shape of the done-to word. Root د خ ل (17:80).
- (f) شُرْبَ: verbal noun on فُعْل, heard from شَرِبَ. Root ش ر ب (56:55).
- (g) قَاضٍ: doer word of a defective verb. The last ي dropped in front of the tanwīn. Root ق ض ي (L4.13, 20:72).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={11} q="Take apart three words of لَا تَأْخُذُهُۥ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ (Al-Baqarah 2:255): تَأْخُذُهُۥ, سِنَةٌ and نَوْمٌ. Then take apart ٱلْقَيُّومُ from the same verse.">

- **تَأْخُذُهُۥ**: peel the prefix تَ (“she”, because سِنَةٌ is feminine) and the attached هُ (“him”). Root **أ خ ذ**, hamzated with the hamza first. A Form I present verb, door 1 (أَخَذَ يَأْخُذُ). The hamza has a sukūn after a fatḥa, so it sits on an alif (L4.15).
- **سِنَةٌ**: root **و س ن**, assimilated. Al-Jadwal says the first letter dropped, as in عِدَة, and gives the pattern عِلَة. It is the verbal noun of وَسَنَ يَسِنُ, door 2 (L4.14).
- **نَوْمٌ**: root **ن و م**, hollow. Al-Jadwal: a heard verbal noun of نَامَ يَنَامُ, pattern فَعْل (L3.20).
- **ٱلْقَيُّومُ**: root **ق و م**, hollow. Al-Jadwal reads it as an intensive form on فَيْعُول. The original قَيْوُوم changed when the ي and the و met, and the و turned into ي and merged with the other ي.

L5.03 reads the whole verse. This exercise only takes the shapes of four words apart.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={12} q="Take apart the words of فَجَعَلَهُۥ غُثَآءً أَحْوَىٰ (Al-Aʿla 87:5).">

- **فَجَعَلَهُۥ**: peel فَ and the attached هُ (“it”). Root **ج ع ل**, sound. A Form I past verb, “He made”.
- **غُثَآءً**: a noun, accusative with tanwīn. The Corpus root is غ ث و. It is the second object of جَعَلَ (al-Jadwal).
- **أَحْوَىٰ**: root **ح و ي**, defective. Al-Jadwal: a describing word on the pattern أَفْعَل, from حَوِيَ يَحْوَى, meaning dark with green. It describes غُثَآءً. The ىٰ shows the weak last letter.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={13} q="Take apart the first word of مَٰلِكِ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ (Al-Fatiha 1:4): root, shape, and what al-Jadwal says it is.">

Root **م ل ك**, sound. The shape is **فَاعِل**, a Form I doer word. The Corpus tags it an active participle. Al-Jadwal adds that it is a doer word that has the meaning of a describing word, for the lasting nature of ownership. It comes from مَلَكَ يَمْلِكُ, door 2. The Tanzil text writes it with a small alif, مَٰلِكِ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={14} q="Take apart عَلَّمَ and يَعْلَمْ in عَلَّمَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ (Al-Alaq 96:5).">

**عَلَّمَ**: root ع ل م, Form II, past “he”, “taught”. **يَعْلَمْ**: the same root, Form I, a present verb in jazm after لَمْ, with a sukūn. The plain form is يَعْلَمُ (door 4, L3.04). The two verbs come from one root and show two shapes.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={15} q="In قَالَ رَبِّ ٱشْرَحْ لِى صَدْرِى (Ta-Ha 20:25), say what kind of root قَالَ has, what ٱشْرَحْ is, and what the ى in صَدْرِى is.">

**قَالَ**: root ق و ل, hollow, a long stem, the past “he”. It looks as if it has only two root letters, ق and ل, because the و is the long ā. **ٱشْرَحْ**: a Form I command to one man, root ش ر ح, sound. It is made from تَشْرَحُ: end in a sukūn, take off the تَ and add ٱ (L3.08). **صَدْرِى**: a noun, root ص د ر, with the attached ى, “my”.

</Exercise>

- **Root:** peel the word, count the letters, pour the root into ف ع ل. Six kinds of root: sound, hollow, defective, assimilated, hamzated, doubled.
- **Form:** Form I is the bare verb, 64% of the verb words. Forms II to X add a shape. The idea of a form is a tendency, so read the verse.
- **Past, present, command:** the past ends in 13 endings in five groups. The present has a prefix and an ending. The command is made from the present in three moves.
- **Moods and voice:** naṣb, jazm and the plain form; the passive changes the vowels, not the consonants.
- **Weak letters:** a long vowel is cut in front of a sukūn; the alif drops or the letter shows; the first و falls in the present; the hamza stays and changes its seat.
- **Nouns:** the doer word, the done-to word, the verbal noun and the noun of place all come from the root and the pattern.
- Quran Learn’s four questions and the review tables are teaching summaries. The rules of word shape in L4.11 to L4.15 are teaching summaries too, and this lesson says so where it uses them.
