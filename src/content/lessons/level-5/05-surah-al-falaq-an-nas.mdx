Read each verse from right to left. The English under each verse is our own plain meaning, not a translation. A marked word is one that English needs and the Arabic leaves out.

Surah 113 has **5 verses** and **23 written words**, as the Quranic Arabic Corpus counts them. Verse 1 asks for refuge. Verses 2 to 5 each hold the phrase مِن شَرِّ, “from the evil of”, and say what the refuge is from.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal’s page for these five verses has no note on why the surah came down and no paragraph on figures of speech. This lesson teaches grammar, so it adds neither. Where al-Jadwal says nothing about a single word, the card gives the standard formula and says so.</Note>

Surah 114 has **6 verses** and **20 written words**. Verses 1 to 3 call for refuge in three parts. Verse 4 names what the refuge is from, and verses 5 and 6 go on about it.

Every verse ends in the sound -āsi: ٱلنَّاسِ five times and ٱلْخَنَّاسِ once. You can hear that without any book.

**قُلْ** is a command, and its doer is a hidden “you”. **أَعُوذُ** is a present verb with a hidden “I” as its doer. Both roots have a weak middle letter, so both are hollow verbs (L4.12).

Al-Jadwal reads everything after قُلْ as **what is said**. The phrase **بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ** belongs to أَعُوذُ and answers “with whom?”. Inside it, رَبِّ is the first word of an idafah and ٱلْفَلَقِ is the second.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal glosses ٱلْفَلَقِ as the dawn. In a footnote it adds that the books of tafsir give many other meanings, such as a prison in hell, a valley in it, low ground, or the womb. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn lists some of them too. This lesson writes “daybreak” and does not choose.</Note>

Verses 2 to 5 follow one frame. First comes مِن, then شَرِّ, then a second word that completes the idafah. شَرِّ is genitive because مِن pulls it (L2.07), and it is the first word of an idafah (L2.08).

Al-Jadwal ties every one of these phrases to أَعُوذُ, “in the four places”. So the verses say: “I seek refuge with the Lord of the daybreak, from the evil of …, and from the evil of …”.

Verse 2 has no وَ. Verses 3, 4 and 5 start with وَ, which joins each phrase to the one before it. That there are four مِن شَرِّ phrases in Surah 113 is a Quran Learn count from the Corpus, made by script.

The two words end in the same sound, and each has a long ā before the س. They are different. Al-Jadwal calls ٱلْخَنَّاسِ an intensive form of the doer word, and it calls ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ a noun for the one who whispers, on the pattern فَعْلَال. L3.03 gives the same two readings.

<Block id="m3" title="Mistake 3: calling يُوَسْوِسُ Form II">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles

The pair و س repeats in يُوَسْوِسُ, and a repeat can look like doubling. Form II doubles the middle letter of a three-letter root, and the shadda shows it (L3.12). يُوَسْوِسُ has no shadda. Its root has four letters, so the Corpus gives it no form number, as L3.04 says of وَسْوَسَ.

</Block>

<Block id="m4" title="Mistake 4: calling the clause after مَا a description of شَرّ">

In مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ the clause خَلَقَ finishes مَا. It does not describe شَرّ. The word that completes the idafah is مَا, and the clause belongs to it (L3.11).

Say the pieces in order: the preposition, the first word, the second word, then the ṣila.

- Surah 113 has **5 verses** and **23 words**. Surah 114 has **6 verses** and **20 words**.
- Both start with قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ. In al-Jadwal, قُلْ is an opening sentence, أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ … is what is said, and the later phrases hang on أَعُوذُ.
- **Surah 113:** four مِن شَرِّ phrases. مَا is a joining noun with خَلَقَ as its ṣila. إِذَا is a time word tied to شَرّ. غَاسِقٍ and حَاسِدٍ are doer words, and ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ is a plural intensive form.
- **Surah 114:** a chain of three words (رَبِّ, مَلِكِ, إِلَٰهِ) before ٱلنَّاسِ. ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ has a four-letter root, and ٱلْخَنَّاسِ describes it. ٱلَّذِى and its ṣila finish verse 5. The last phrase is tied to a ḥāl.
- Al-Jadwal and al-Durr al-Maṣūn read six words in different ways. This lesson follows al-Jadwal and names the other reading.
- Al-Jadwal names no figure of speech for these surahs. Its one style note is about the repeated letter س in Surah 114.
- The side-by-side counts and the comparison of the two surahs are teaching summaries.
