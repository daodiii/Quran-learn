Read each verse from right to left. The meaning of each verse is given in its own block. It is our own plain English, not a translation.

The surah has **7 verses** and **29 written words**, as the Corpus counts them. Four of the words are verbs: نَعْبُدُ, نَسْتَعِينُ, ٱهْدِنَا (with ٱهْدِ inside it) and أَنْعَمْتَ. All the others are nouns, names, pronouns or little words.

<Note kind="know">At the end of its note on verse 2, al-Jadwal lists names of the surah, for example Fātiḥat al-Kitāb, Umm al-Qurʾān and al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī. It also reports a saying in Muslim, from Abū Hurayra, in which Allah says that the prayer is divided between Him and His servant. This lesson teaches grammar and style. It does not weigh reports.</Note>

The verse has four words and no verb. Meaning, in al-Jadwal’s reading: “My beginning ⟨is⟩ in the name of Allah, the Most Kind, the Most Merciful.”

A phrase has to belong to a word (L2.07). Nothing is written to tie بِسْمِ to, so al-Jadwal reads a noun as left out: the mubtadaʾ “my beginning”. The phrase is then the khabar.

Every word ends in a kasra, for three different reasons: the preposition, the idafah, and an adjective copying its noun (L1.10). L1.03 and L1.10 read the same verse. This block adds the sentence and the places.

L1.10 promised a note on the left-out word, and L5.01 taught the idea. Here are the two views on this verse.

- **The noun view.** Al-Jadwal, and in *al-Durr al-Maṣūn* the people of Basra. A noun is left out. The phrase is the khabar and the sentence is nominal.
- **The verb view.** In *al-Durr al-Maṣūn*, the people of Kufa. A verb is left out, such as “I begin”. The phrase is tied to the verb and the sentence is verbal.

*Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* adds detail. Some who take a verb put it before the phrase and some after it. Al-Zamakhsharī puts it after, as a reply to people who began with the names of their gods, and so the phrase comes first. This lesson keeps to the two views above.

The endings are the same in both views. بِسْمِ is genitive after بِ, and ٱللَّهِ is genitive as the second word of the idafah. What changes is the left-out word and the kind of sentence. Take **one view** whole. This lesson takes al-Jadwal’s. L5.01 shows the slip of mixing the views: a nominal sentence with a left-out verb.

Al-Jadwal has a paragraph on style for verse 1. It makes two points.

**Takrīr.** The two names come again in verse 3. Al-Jadwal says mercy is giving to those in need. Verse 1 names the one who gives and not those who are given to. After the worlds are named in verse 2, the names come back, now with those who receive. L4.17 met this note in its Exercise 3.

**The order.** ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ comes before ٱلرَّحِيمِ. Al-Jadwal says ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ is the stronger name, and a name that belongs to Allah alone, like ٱللَّهِ. It takes in the great favours and their roots. ٱلرَّحِيمِ follows to cover the finer ones. What is great has the better claim to come first.

This lesson adds nothing to these two points. They are al-Jadwal’s reading of the meaning.

<Exercise n={1} q="Give the role, the case or mood, the mark and the reason for each word. (a) ٱلْحَمْدُ (Al-Fatiha 1:2, start) (b) نَعْبُدُ (Al-Fatiha 1:5, part) (c) ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ (Al-Fatiha 1:6, end) (d) أَنْعَمْتَ (Al-Fatiha 1:7, part)">

<Exercise n={3} q="Put إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ (Al-Fatiha 1:5, part) in the usual order, and say what moved. Then give al-Jadwal’s reason for the move, and for saying إِيَّاكَ twice.">

The usual order is **نَعْبُدُكَ** (made-up): the verb, with the object pronoun joined to it. In the verse the object moved in front of the verb. The joined pronoun cannot stand alone in front, so the verse uses the separate pronoun إِيَّاكَ (L3.01).

Al-Jadwal says the move cuts the sharing between the two verbs and limits the worship to Allah. Said twice, each verb keeps its own limited object. Said once, the gain of putting it first would be lost. L5.14 looks at word order in more verses.

</Exercise>

- Surah Al-Fatiha has **seven verses**, **29 words** and **four verbs**, all in verses 5 to 7.
- **Verse 1** has no verb. Al-Jadwal ties بِسْمِ to a left-out khabar and reads a left-out mubtadaʾ, “my beginning”. *Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* gives the verb view too.
- **Verses 2 to 4** are one sentence. ٱلْحَمْدُ is the mubtadaʾ and لِلَّهِ the khabar. رَبِّ, ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ, ٱلرَّحِيمِ and مَٰلِكِ describe ٱللَّهِ, with a badal allowed for each.
- **Verse 5** has two sentences with the object put first. Al-Jadwal names the change from “Allah” to “You” iltifāt.
- **Verse 6** is a command with two objects. **Verse 7** explains the path with a badal, a ṣila and غَيْرِ in three readings.
- **Style.** Al-Jadwal names takrīr, ikhtiṣāṣ, iltifāt, an open metaphor, explaining after a vague mention, tasjīʿ and istihlāl. The endings in -īm and -īn, and the count of cases, are our own look.
