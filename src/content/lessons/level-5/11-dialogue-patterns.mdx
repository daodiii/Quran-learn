The first word, **قَالَ**, is the verb “he said”. Its doer is a hidden “he”. Al-Jadwal says it is Yaʿqub. Everything after قَالَ is what he says.

That makes one speech: a verb of saying, its doer, and the words that are said. The Quran builds speech this way again and again. This lesson reads the words that open a speech (قَالَ, قُلْ, يَقُولُ), the call with يَا, and the way a new speaker shows in the grammar.

The sentence قَالَ has no place of its own. The words after it do. Al-Jadwal puts them in the place of accusative and calls them مَقُول الْقَوْل, “what is said”. L1.09 met this name with قُلْ in 109:1.

The Quranic Arabic Corpus has **1,618** verb words from the root ق و ل. They sit in **1,321** of its 6,236 verses, which is 21%. The old lesson said that over 40% of the verses contain dialogue. This count does not reach that. Other verbs open speech too, such as نَادَىٰ in 19:24, so the real share of speech is higher than 21%. No count in this lesson says by how much.

| Form | Meaning | Words |
|---|---|---|
| قَالَ (also وَقَالَ, فَقَالَ) | he said | 524 |
| قَالُوا۟ (two spellings) | they said | 328 |
| قَالَتْ, قَالَتِ | she said | 41 |
| يَقُولُ | he says, he will say | 58 |
| يَقُولُونَ | they say, they will say | 80 |
| قُلْ, قُل, قُلِ | say! (to one man) | 332 |

The other 255 words are other persons and forms. 52 of them are passive, like قِيلَ, “it was said” (49). L4.12 teaches all these shapes.

The Corpus tags have errors, and two matter in this lesson: it gives its “call” tag to the هَا of هَٰٓأَنتُمْ and هَٰهُنَا, and it gives the same tag to the يَا of يَٰلَيْتَنِى, which al-Jadwal reads as a word of attention. Mistake 3 comes back to this.</Note>

The old lesson sorted speech into four types: narrative, divine command, prophetic address and Last Day. This lesson keeps a sorting as a way to find your place, and it sorts by the first word only. This is a **teaching summary** by Quran Learn. Quran Learn found no approved source that calls these “types” or gives each one a fixed job. The old lesson also gave each type a speaker, an audience and a tense. Here those are read from each verse.

| Opening | Step | Who speaks | What the grammar marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| قَالَ, قَالُوا۟, قَالَتْ | 2 | the doer of the verb | the doer, in the verb or after it |

The doer of a verb of saying can sit in four places. In 7:60 it is a noun after the verb: ٱلْمَلَأُ, “the chiefs”, in the nominative (L2.03). In 11:32 it is the ending -ū of قَالُوا۟, “they”. In 19:20 the ending -at of قَالَتْ says “she”, and no noun is written. In 12:5, in block 1, it is a hidden “he”.

In the Corpus, قَالَ (524 words) is followed right away by a nominative noun 142 times, and by ٱلَّذِينَ or a noun like it 57 times, as in قَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟. In 47 the next word is a call. In the other 278 the next word is something else: the quote starts, or another word comes before the doer. So in 325 of the 524 (62%) no doer is written next to the verb. This is a count of tags. The Corpus does not mark doers.

In 7:61 every word after قَالَ is what is said. It starts with a call, يَٰقَوْمِ. Al-Jadwal puts the call sentence in the place of accusative, and reads the rest as the answer to the call. The sentence قَالَ itself has no place. Al-Jadwal calls it an explaining start: it answers the question “and what did he say?”.

What is said can be any kind of sentence. It takes the same place each time.

| Verse | What is said starts with | Kind of sentence |
|---|---|---|
| 12:5 | يَٰبُنَىَّ | a call, then a ban |
| 7:60 | إِنَّا | an إِنَّ sentence (L2.10) |
| 19:20 | أَنَّىٰ | a question |
| 7:61 | يَٰقَوْمِ | a call, then a statement |

A sentence has no case ending, so you will not see an accusative on a quote. The place belongs to the whole sentence. L1.09 and L4.12 use the same name.

19:21 has two قَالَ. The first has a hidden “he”. Look back at 19:19: قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ رَسُولُ رَبِّكِ, “he said: I am only the messenger of your Lord”. Then Maryam speaks in 19:20, and 19:21 answers her. So the hidden “he” is the one who spoke in 19:19. The verses show this. The grammar only says “he”.

The second قَالَ has a written doer, رَبُّكِ, “your Lord”. It comes after كَذَٰلِكِ, in the same breath. The speaker passes on what the Lord said. Al-Jadwal puts كَذَٰلِكِ and هُوَ عَلَىَّ هَيِّنٌ each in the place of accusative, as what is said by their verbs. The sentence of the second قَالَ has no place of its own.

Each verse here has قَالَ, with no “and” or “then” in front of the second and the third. Each new قَالَ opens a new speech, and the doer tells you who speaks. Al-Jadwal reads each as an explaining start: it answers “and what did they say?”.

| Verse | Who speaks | Who is spoken to |
|---|---|---|
| 7:59 | Nuh, a hidden “he” (the same verse names him: نُوحًا) | his people, called with يَٰقَوْمِ |
| 7:60 | the chiefs, written: ٱلْمَلَأُ | Nuh, the كَ of لَنَرَىٰكَ, “you” |
| 7:61 | Nuh, a hidden “he” (the one called “you” in 7:60) | his people, called with يَٰقَوْمِ |

Two things show who is spoken to. A call inside what is said names the hearers. A pronoun “you” inside what is said points to the person the speaker faces. When a doer is hidden, the verses before it say who he is. The grammar of قَالَ cannot say it alone.

**قُلْ** is a command: “say!”. Its doer is a hidden “you”, one man. What follows it is what is said, and here it is an إِنَّ sentence. Al-Jadwal puts the sentence إِنَّ صَلَاتِى … لِلَّهِ in the place of accusative. The sentence قُلْ has no place: it starts a new speech.

L3.08 counted the commands of this verb: 349 words, and 332 of them are for one man (قُلْ, قُل and قُلِ). L3.08 also explains the three spellings. The new counts here: those 332 words stand in 306 verses of 57 surahs. In 187 of them the word is the first word of the verse. Al-Anʿam (6) has the most, with 44.

<Note kind="know">Who is the “you” of قُلْ? The grammar says only “you, one man”: the Corpus tags it that way, and al-Jadwal says the doer is “you”. Who is meant comes from the surah around the verse. The old lesson said that قُلْ is always Allah speaking to the Prophet. No count can test that, and al-Jadwal does not state it, so this lesson does not say “always”. L1.11 and L5.04 read the قُلْ of 112:1 as a command to the Prophet.</Note>

Some verses report what people say on the Last Day. The grammar is the same as in the rest of this lesson. 33:66 has يَقُولُونَ, a present verb. 33:67 has وَقَالُوا۟, a past verb, joined to it with وَ. What puts the speech on the Last Day is a time word and what it says: يَوْمَ تُقَلَّبُ وُجُوهُهُمْ فِى ٱلنَّارِ, “on the day when their faces are turned in the Fire”, in 33:66, and يَوْمَئِذٍ, “on that day”, in 75:10. It is not the tense of the verb.

The old lesson said that the Quran uses the past tense for the Last Day to show certainty, and gave it a name: الْمَاضِي بِمَعْنَى ٱلْمُسْتَقْبَلِ, “the past with the meaning of the future”. <Step id="practice" label="Mistakes, practice" title="Five mistakes, and practice">
<Block id="mistake-object" title="Mistake 1: looking for an ending on what is said">
  <Stage label="Two things after قَالَ">
    <Tiles
      label="A doer has an ending. A quote has a place."
      items={[
        { ar: 'قَالَ ٱلْمَلَأُ', en: 'the doer: nominative (7:60)', type: 'n' },
        { ar: 'إِنَّا لَنَرَىٰكَ', en: 'what is said: a place, no ending', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

After قَالَ you can find a doer and what is said. The doer has a nominative ending, or an ending of the verb. What is said can be a whole sentence, and a sentence has no ending. Al-Jadwal gives it a place, in the accusative, and a name, “what is said”. Do not look for a fatḥa on a quote.

The sentence of the verb has no place either. قَالَ ٱلْمَلَأُ is a new start. The quote after it is the part that has a place.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-doer" title="Mistake 2: a doer that is always written">
  <Stage label="One shape, two verses">
    <Tiles
      label="Two verses that look alike"
      items={[
        { ar: 'قَالَ فِرْعَوْنُ', en: 'doer written (7:123)', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'قَالَ ءَامَنتُمْ', en: 'no doer written (26:49)', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The old lesson quoted قَالَ فِرْعَوْنُ ءَامَنتُم بِهِۦ as 26:49 and used it to show a written doer. Those words are in 7:123. 26:49 reads قَالَ ءَامَنتُمْ لَهُۥ, and no doer is written. A reader finds him from the verses before.

In the Corpus, 325 of the 524 words of قَالَ have no doer written right next to them. Test the verse. Do not assume the doer is a noun.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-ya" title="Mistake 3: every يَا is a call">
  <Stage label="Two words that start with يَا">
    <Tiles
      label="The same letters, two jobs"
      items={[
        { ar: 'يَٰنُوحُ', en: 'a call: a noun is called (11:32)', type: 'n' },
        { ar: 'يَٰلَيْتَنِى', en: 'attention: no one is called (78:40)', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

In 78:40 the disbeliever says يَٰلَيْتَنِى كُنتُ تُرَٰبًۢا, “if only I were dust”. Al-Jadwal calls this يَا a word of attention (حَرْف تَنْبِيه). The word لَيْتَ is a word of wishing that works like إِنَّ (L2.10). No noun is called, and nothing answers a call.

The Corpus gives its “call” tag to 376 words. That includes this يَا, and it includes 8 words that start with هَا. Ask two questions. Is a noun called? Does a sentence follow that finishes the call?
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-types" title="Mistake 4: four types with fixed jobs">
  <Stage label="A verse that fits none of the four">
    <Tiles
      label="Speech opened by another verb"
      items={[
        { ar: 'فَنَادَىٰهَا', en: 'so he called her (19:24)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'أَلَّا تَحْزَنِى', en: 'that: do not grieve', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The four openings of step 1 are a way to find your place. They leave things out. 19:24 opens a speech with نَادَىٰ, “he called”, and أَنْ, “that”. Al-Jadwal calls this أَنْ a word that explains (حَرْف تَفْسِير), and the sentence after it has no place of its own. A verb other than قَالَ can open speech.

The old lesson also gave each type a speaker and a purpose. يَٰٓأَيُّهَا had “the Prophet” as speaker, and قُلْ verses were “always” Allah to the Prophet. The sentence does not give those. Give a verse a speaker only when the verb, a call or the verses before it do.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-day" title="Mistake 5: every يَقُولُ is the Last Day">
  <Stage label="Two verses the old lesson misread">
    <Tiles
      label="Look for a time word"
      items={[
        { ar: 'فَيَقُولُ رَبِّىٓ أَكْرَمَنِ', en: 'a man says it (89:15)', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'وَقَالَ ٱلرَّسُولُ', en: 'the Tanzil text (25:30)', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The old lesson put 89:15 and 25:30 under the Last Day. 89:15 is about a man whom his Lord tests and honours: فَأَمَّا ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ إِذَا مَا ٱبْتَلَىٰهُ رَبُّهُۥ فَأَكْرَمَهُۥ وَنَعَّمَهُۥ فَيَقُولُ رَبِّىٓ أَكْرَمَنِ. The speaker is the man, and the old lesson called it “Allah’s response”. It is the man’s own saying, and no time word puts it on the Last Day.

25:30 reads وَقَالَ ٱلرَّسُولُ يَٰرَبِّ in the Tanzil text, with the past verb. The old lesson wrote وَيَقُولُ. The verse does not name a day. Read the time from a time word, as in 33:66 and 75:10, and not from the verb.
</Block>

- (b) The first speech is رَبِّ ٱجْعَل لِّىٓ ءَايَةً: a call, then a request. The second is ءَايَتُكَ … سَوِيًّا: a nominal sentence. - (c) No. The verse has a hidden “he” twice. The verses before it name the first speaker: 19:2 names Zakariyya, and 19:3 says he called his Lord. The old lesson said that the second speaker is Allah. This verse does not say it, and al-Jadwal’s page for 19:10 gives no name.

- (b) The opening is a call, with no verb of saying. The one called is أَيُّ, and ٱلنَّاسُ is its badal. The answer to the call is ٱتَّقُوا۟ رَبَّكُمُ.

The sorting by opening is a teaching summary. Do not treat it as four types with set jobs.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Who speaks, who is spoken to, and what shows it? قَالُوا۟ يَٰصَٰلِحُ قَدْ كُنتَ فِينَا مَرْجُوًّا قَبْلَ هَٰذَآ (Hud 11:62)">

</Exercise>

- (c) **أَيُّ**: a noun with no ٱل, built on the ḍamma, in the place of accusative. هَا is a word of attention. ٱلنَّاسُ is a badal of أَيُّ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="For each verse give the verb and its tense, the doer, what is said, and what puts the speech on the Last Day. Does the tense change the grammar? (a) يَقُولُ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ يَوْمَئِذٍ أَيْنَ ٱلْمَفَرُّ (Al-Qiyamah 75:10) (b) وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ رَبَّنَآ أَرِنَا ٱلَّذَيْنِ أَضَلَّانَا مِنَ ٱلْجِنِّ وَٱلْإِنسِ (Fussilat 41:29)">

- (a) The verb is **يَقُولُ**, present. The doer is ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ, after the verb. What is said is أَيْنَ ٱلْمَفَرُّ. The day comes from يَوْمَئِذٍ and from the verses before, 75:7 to 75:9.
- (b) The verb is **قَالَ**, past. The doer is ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟, written after the verb. What is said is the call رَبَّنَآ, with the call word left out, and the request that follows. The part quoted has no time word, so the day comes from the verses around it.

The tense does not change the grammar. In both verses al-Jadwal reads the verb like any speech verb, and the quote stands in the place of accusative.

</Exercise>

(b) The Corpus has 332 command words from قَالَ for one man. (c) The Corpus shows that every قُلْ is a command to the Prophet. (d) The Corpus has 143 words of يَٰٓأَيُّهَا.">

<Exercise n={11} q="Build this made-up sentence in Arabic: “He said: O my son, sit.” Then write it again with the doer written: “The father said: O my son, sit.”">

This is a made-up example, not a verse.

- With a hidden doer: قَالَ يَٰبُنَىَّ ٱجْلِسْ. The call is يَٰبُنَىَّ, and ٱجْلِسْ is the answer to the call. Both are what is said.
- With the doer written: قَالَ ٱلْأَبُ يَٰبُنَىَّ ٱجْلِسْ. ٱلْأَبُ is the doer, nominative, after the verb, and the quote comes after it.

The quote stays in the same place in both. Only the doer moves from the verb into a noun.

    <Formula

      items={[

        { kind: 'k', text: 'its doer', small: 'in the verb or after it' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'what is said', small: 'in the place of accusative' },

- **قَالَ** opens a speech. The doer is the verb’s ending, a noun after it, or a hidden pronoun. What is said is in the place of accusative. The sentence of the verb has no place.
- **قُلْ** is a command with a hidden “you”. What follows it is what is said, as after قَالَ. Who is meant comes from the surah around the verse.
- A **call** names who hears. It can be a single name, a noun with “my”, or a noun with the call word left out. يَٰٓأَيُّهَا is a call with no verb of saying.
- A **new speaker** shows in a new verb and a new doer. A call or a pronoun “you” inside the speech shows who is spoken to.
- On the **Last Day**, the grammar is the same. A time word puts the speech there, and the tense does not.
- The four openings are a **teaching summary**. The counts say what is there: 1,321 verses with قَالَ, 332 words of قُلْ, 143 words of يَٰٓأَيُّهَا.
