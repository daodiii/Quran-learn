L5.01 gave you five moves for taking a verse apart ([Full I‘rab analysis, L5.01](/learn/level-5/01-full-irab-analysis/)). This lesson does not teach them again. It gives you the grammar behind them in four maps, so you can look a point up and see which lesson taught it.

| Map | Moves it feeds | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Sentences: kinds, parts, places | 2, 3 and 5 | Step 1 |
| Cases and moods | 4 | Step 2 |
| Little words: inna, kāna, conditions, exception, negation, oath, pointing and “who” nouns | 2, 3 and 4 | Step 3 |
| Objects, the ḥāl and the tamyīz | 3 and 4 | Step 4 |

Step 5 uses all four on whole verses. Each row of a map names the lesson that teaches it. Where that lesson calls a point a “usual pattern”, a “tendency” or a “teaching summary”, the row says so too.

<Note kind="know">The maps are a teaching summary. They collect what Levels 1 to 4 teach. No approved source states them as one table, and the five moves are also a teaching summary (L5.01). The Arabic names are the classical terms the finished lessons use.</Note>

Both sentences start with a noun, so both are **nominal** (جُمْلَة اِسْمِيَّة). The first word decides. In the second sentence the pronoun هُوَ comes first, and a phrase and then the khabar follow. Al-Jadwal reads the two sentences like this: the first starts a new passage, and the second is joined to it.

| Claim | Taught in | How sure |
|---|---|---|
| The first word decides: a noun first makes the sentence nominal, a verb first makes it verbal | [L1.09](/learn/level-1/09-simple-sentences/) | Rule |
| A verb later in the sentence does not make it verbal | [L1.09](/learn/level-1/09-simple-sentences/) | Rule |
| A little word at the front is skipped when you sort. Inna and kāna change what follows | [L1.09](/learn/level-1/09-simple-sentences/), [L2.10](/learn/level-2/10-inna-sisters/), [L2.11](/learn/level-2/11-kaana-sisters/) | First guide in L1.09 |
| Arabic has no word for “is” in the present. Do not add a verb | [L1.09](/learn/level-1/09-simple-sentences/), [L2.01](/learn/level-2/01-nominal-sentence/) | Rule |
| Mubtadaʾ and khabar are both nominative | [L2.01](/learn/level-2/01-nominal-sentence/), [L2.04](/learn/level-2/04-nominative-case/) | Rule |
| A definite word with an indefinite one usually makes a sentence. Two definite words usually make a phrase | [L2.01](/learn/level-2/01-nominal-sentence/) | Usual pattern: L2.01 calls it a first guide, not a law |

The khabar can be a word, a phrase, a place or time word, a verbal sentence or a nominal sentence. In 13:11 the phrase لَهُۥ is the khabar, and the mubtadaʾ مُعَقِّبَٰتٌ is indefinite, so the khabar comes first.

| Claim | Taught in | How sure |
|---|---|---|
| The khabar can be a single word (وَكِيلٌ, 39:62), a phrase, a place or time word, a verbal sentence or a nominal sentence | [L2.01](/learn/level-2/01-nominal-sentence/) | Rule |
| A khabar that is a sentence needs a link back to the mubtadaʾ: a pronoun, or the same noun said again | [L2.02](/learn/level-2/02-subject-predicate/) | Rule |
| A phrase as a khabar is tied to a left-out word like “is found” (al-Jadwal). This course calls the phrase the khabar | [L2.01](/learn/level-2/01-nominal-sentence/), [L2.07](/learn/level-2/07-prepositions-genitive/) | Al-Jadwal’s wording |
| With an indefinite mubtadaʾ and a phrase as khabar, the khabar comes first | [L2.01](/learn/level-2/01-nominal-sentence/), [L2.02](/learn/level-2/02-subject-predicate/) | Rule |
| An indefinite mubtadaʾ needs a reason: the khabar first, an adjective, a question before it, or a wish or prayer | [L2.02](/learn/level-2/02-subject-predicate/) | Rule |
| The khabar matches the mubtadaʾ in gender and number. A plural of things takes a feminine singular khabar | [L2.02](/learn/level-2/02-subject-predicate/) | Usual pattern: “usually” in L2.02 |
| One mubtadaʾ can have more than one khabar: al-Jadwal calls the next one the second khabar | [L2.01](/learn/level-2/01-nominal-sentence/), [L2.02](/learn/level-2/02-subject-predicate/) | Rule |

<Note kind="know">Not every word after a mubtadaʾ is its khabar. L2.09 shows that a word after a pointing noun can be a badal, and L2.12 shows ٱلْأَبْتَرُ as a khabar and not an adjective.</Note>

After the little word comes a verb, so this sentence is **verbal** (جُمْلَة فِعْلِيَّة). The usual order is verb, doer, object. The doer is the ending -ū. The object أَوْلَٰدَكُمْ ends in -a, with the attached -kum. Al-Jadwal reads the lā as a ban that brings jazm, and the verb has lost its nūn.

| Claim | Taught in | How sure |
|---|---|---|
| The usual order is verb, doer, object. The object may come before the doer, and the endings show which is which | [L2.03](/learn/level-2/03-verbal-sentence/) | Rule |
| The doer is nominative. The object is accusative. The verb has no case | [L2.03](/learn/level-2/03-verbal-sentence/), [L2.04](/learn/level-2/04-nominative-case/), [L2.05](/learn/level-2/05-accusative-case/) | Rule |
| The doer can be a written noun, an attached ending (-tu, -nā, -ū) or hidden inside the verb | [L2.03](/learn/level-2/03-verbal-sentence/), [L3.02](/learn/level-3/02-attached-pronouns/) | Rule |
| A verb before a written doer stays singular and matches the doer in gender | [L2.03](/learn/level-2/03-verbal-sentence/) | Rule |
| Some verbs take no object, some one, some two | [L2.03](/learn/level-2/03-verbal-sentence/) | Rule |
| In the passive the doer is not named. The stand-in (nāʾib fāʿil) is nominative. With two objects the first becomes the stand-in and the second stays accusative | [L3.09](/learn/level-3/09-passive-voice/) | Rule |
| The attached -ka after a verb is the object. After a noun it means “your” | [L2.12](/learn/level-2/12-checkpoint-al-kawthar/), [L3.02](/learn/level-3/02-attached-pronouns/) | Rule |

Take the cases one at a time. **هُوَ** is a pronoun. It shows no ending, and it stands in the place of the nominative (L2.04). **رَسُولَهُۥ** is the object of أَرْسَلَ, so it is accusative with -a (L2.05). **بِٱلْهُدَىٰ** is genitive after the preposition بِ, but the word ends in an alif, so the kasra is hidden (L2.06). **وَدِينِ ٱلْحَقِّ** is genitive too. The first word follows ٱلْهُدَىٰ, and ٱلْحَقِّ is the second word of an idafah (L2.08).

Al-Jadwal ties بِٱلْهُدَىٰ to a ḥāl: a state of the doer or of the object. The next three blocks go case by case.

The nominative (رَفْع) is the case of the mubtadaʾ, the khabar and the doer. It is also the case of the stand-in for the doer, the ism of kāna and the khabar of inna, and of words that follow a nominative word (L2.04). In 65:3, ٱللَّهُ is the doer of جَعَلَ, and بَٰلِغُ is the khabar of inna: both end in -u.

The accusative (نَصْب) has more jobs than the other two cases. Each job answers its own question. The questions are in Step 4.

| Job | What it says | Taught in |
|---|---|---|
| Object (mafʿūl bihi) | Whom or what the verb acts on | [L2.03](/learn/level-2/03-verbal-sentence/), [L2.05](/learn/level-2/05-accusative-case/) |

| Khabar of kāna | The second part after kāna | [L2.11](/learn/level-2/11-kaana-sisters/) |
| Time and place words | When or where | [L2.05](/learn/level-2/05-accusative-case/) |
| Ḥāl | In what state | [L4.01](/learn/level-4/01-hal-clauses/) |
| Tamyīz | Of what: it makes something vague clear | [L4.02](/learn/level-4/02-tamyiz/) |
| Mafʿūl muṭlaq | How, or how many times | [L4.07](/learn/level-4/07-maf-ul-mutlaq/) |
| Mafʿūl li-ajlih | Why | [L4.08](/learn/level-4/08-maf-ul-li-ajlih/) |

A -a on a verb is not a case. A kasra, -īna or -ayni can also be genitive, so check the job.

A kasra does not always mean the genitive. The sound feminine plural shows the accusative with a kasra (L2.05). A word that ends in a long ā hides every vowel (L2.04 to L2.06). <Exercise n={1} q="Sort each sentence as nominal, verbal or a question, and name its parts. (a) ٱللَّهُ نُورُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ (An-Nur 24:35, part) (b) خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ بِٱلْحَقِّ (An-Nahl 16:3, part) (c) إِنَّ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ كَانَتْ عَلَى ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ كِتَٰبًا مَّوْقُوتًا (An-Nisa 4:103, part) (d) أَلَمْ تَرَ كَيْفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِأَصْحَٰبِ ٱلْفِيلِ (Al-Fil 105:1)">

**(a)** Nominal. ٱللَّهُ is the mubtadaʾ. نُورُ is the khabar and the first word of an idafah, so both are nominative. ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ is the second word, genitive with a kasra. وَٱلْأَرْضِ is joined to it and is genitive too.

**(b)** Verbal. خَلَقَ is a past verb with a hidden doer, “He”. ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ is the object. It is accusative, and a sound feminine plural shows that with a kasra (L2.05). وَٱلْأَرْضَ is joined to it, accusative -a. Al-Jadwal ties بِٱلْحَقِّ to a ḥāl of the doer.

**(c)** Skip إِنَّ and sort by the next word: a noun, so nominal. ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ is the ism of inna, accusative -a. The khabar of inna is the sentence كَانَتْ ... كِتَٰبًا, in the place of the nominative. Inside it, كَانَتْ has a hidden ism, “she”, and كِتَٰبًا is the khabar of kāna, accusative -an. Al-Jadwal ties عَلَى ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ to كِتَٰبًا, because it is a verbal noun. مَّوْقُوتًا is the naʿt of كِتَٰبًا, a done-to word, accusative.

**(d)** A question. أَ is the question letter, which asks you to agree. لَمْ is a negative that brings jazm. تَرَ is a present verb in jazm. Its weak last letter has dropped, and the doer is a hidden “you” (L3.07, L4.13). كَيْفَ is a question noun. Al-Jadwal puts it in the place of the accusative, and names a second reading, a ḥāl. فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ is a verbal sentence with the doer رَبُّكَ in -u. Al-Jadwal puts the whole sentence in the place of the accusative. بِأَصْحَٰبِ ٱلْفِيلِ is tied to فَعَلَ: أَصْحَٰبِ is genitive after بِ and the first word of an idafah, and ٱلْفِيلِ is its second word.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Show what each little word does. (a) How does إِنَّ change ٱللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ in فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ (Al-Baqarah 2:227, part)? (b) How does كَانَ change ٱللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ in وَكَانَ ٱللَّهُ غَفُورًا رَّحِيمًا (An-Nisa 4:96, part)? (c) Take apart وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ (Al-Ikhlas 112:4).">

**(a)** Without inna both parts are nominative: ٱللَّهُ is the mubtadaʾ, and سَمِيعٌ and عَلِيمٌ are its khabars. With inna, ٱللَّهَ turns accusative as the ism. The two khabars stay nominative (L2.10).

**(b)** Without kāna, غَفُورٌ and رَّحِيمٌ are nominative khabars. With kāna, ٱللَّهُ stays nominative as the ism, and the khabars turn accusative: غَفُورًا, and رَّحِيمًا as a second khabar (L2.11). This course writes “has always been”.

**(c)** لَمْ is a negative that brings jazm. يَكُنْ is the present verb of kāna in jazm. Its middle letter و has dropped (L4.12), not a nūn as the old lesson said. كُفُوًا is the khabar of kāna, accusative -an, and لَهُۥ is a phrase tied to it (al-Jadwal). أَحَدٌ is the ism of kāna, nominative, and it comes after its khabar (L2.11).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Find the condition word, the condition and the answer. Say the mood of each verb and why. إِن تَنصُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ يَنصُرْكُمْ وَيُثَبِّتْ أَقْدَامَكُمْ (Muhammad 47:7, part)">

The condition word is إِنْ, a little word with no job in the sentence (L4.04). The condition is تَنصُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ. Its verb is in jazm: the five verbs show jazm by losing the nūn, and the -ū is the doer. ٱللَّهَ is the object, accusative -a.

وَيُثَبِّتْ is joined to the answer with “and” and is in jazm too. It is a Form II verb with a sukūn (L3.12). أَقْدَامَكُمْ is its object: accusative -a, with the -kum as the second word of an idafah.

<Exercise n={12} q="Before you open the answer, take this verse apart from scratch with the five moves. وَلَمَّا رَجَعَ مُوسَىٰٓ إِلَىٰ قَوْمِهِۦ غَضْبَٰنَ أَسِفًا (Al-Aʿraf 7:150, part)">

1. **Cut.** وَ, لَمَّا, رَجَعَ, مُوسَىٰٓ, إِلَىٰ, قَوْمِ, هِ, غَضْبَٰنَ, أَسِفًا.
2. **Kind.** After the little words the first word is a verb, so the sentence is verbal.
3. **Jobs.** رَجَعَ is a past verb. مُوسَىٰٓ is its doer. إِلَىٰ قَوْمِهِۦ is a phrase tied to the verb. غَضْبَٰنَ is a ḥāl, and أَسِفًا a second ḥāl (al-Jadwal).
4. **Endings.** مُوسَىٰٓ is nominative with a hidden ḍamma, and the name takes no tanwīn. قَوْمِ is genitive after إِلَىٰ, and the -hi is genitive in place. غَضْبَٰنَ is accusative with -a and no tanwīn: al-Jadwal says it is kept from tanwīn, the kind of word L2.06 calls a diptote. أَسِفًا is accusative, -an.
5. **Place.** لَمَّا here means “when” and leaves the verb alone, as in the second family of L4.04. Al-Jadwal says only that it parses this لَمَّا like the one before it, so this lesson names no place for the clause.

The two ḥāls say in what state Musa came back. Both can be taken out, and the sentence still stands.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={13} q="This verse has a verb with two objects and a word of meaning that follows one of them. Take it apart. وَعَلَّمَ ءَادَمَ ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ كُلَّهَا (Al-Baqarah 2:31, part)">

| Piece | Job | Case and reason |
|---|---|---|
| وَ | Starts a new sentence (al-Jadwal) | A little word |
| عَلَّمَ | A past verb, Form II, doer a hidden “He” | Built on a fatḥa (L3.12). The doubled middle letter is Form II |
| ءَادَمَ | The first object | Accusative, -a |
| ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ | The second object | Accusative, -a. A verb of Form II can take two objects (L2.03, L3.12) |
| كُلَّ | Tawkid of ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ (al-Jadwal) | Accusative: it copies the case of the word it follows (L4.06). It is the first word of an idafah |
| هَا | The second word of the idafah: “all of them” | Genitive, in place |

Take كُلَّهَا away and the sentence stays whole, as L4.06 says it must for a word of meaning. Take ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ away and it does not.
