Read each part from right to left. The English under each part is our own plain meaning, not a translation. A marked word is one that English needs and the Arabic leaves out.

The verse has **50 written words**, as the Quranic Arabic Corpus counts them. This lesson cuts it into ten parts, at the places where al-Jadwal’s sentences begin and end. The parts have 5, 2, 5, 7, 7, 6, 8, 4, 3 and 3 words.

<Note kind="know">People call this verse Ayat al-Kursi, “the verse of the Kursi”, after the word كُرْسِيُّهُ in part 8. This lesson teaches the grammar of the verse. It does not say anything about the reward of reciting it or about its rank among the verses, because we have not checked those points against a source.</Note>

| 2 | ٱلْحَىُّ ٱلْقَيُّومُ | News 2 and news 3. |

Part 1 holds the subject and its first piece of news. **ٱللَّهُ** is the mubtadaʾ. The rest, لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ, is a whole sentence that serves as its khabar. L1.01 explained how the word ٱللَّهُ is written.

L4.05 took apart the same words in 47:19, so the reading is not new here.

- **لَآ** denies a whole kind.
- **إِلَٰهَ** names the kind, “any god”. Its fatḥa has no tanwīn, and the books say the word counts as accusative.
- The sentence needs a khabar for لَا. Al-Jadwal supplies **مَوْجُود**, “existing”, though it is not written.
- **إِلَّا** sets one thing apart from the kind.
- **هُوَ** is that thing. It is a substitute for the hidden pronoun inside the left-out khabar, so it stands in the place of the nominative.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal gives a second reading in a footnote: هُوَ replaces the place of لَا together with its noun, and that place is nominative as well. Both readings agree on the case. L4.05 said the same of 47:19, and the same words open 3:2.</Note>

Part 6 is a plain verbal sentence. The verb has a hidden “He” as its doer, and the object is the joining noun **مَا**, with its ṣila. The ṣila is a place word, **بَيْنَ**, with the idafah **أَيْدِيهِمْ**. The second half repeats it with **خَلْفَهُمْ**.

Literally, بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ is “between their hands”, and English says “in front of them”. Which things and which times the two phrases cover is a question for the books of tafsir. Al-Jadwal gives no gloss, and this lesson does not decide.

<Note kind="know">The books differ on who “them” is. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn lists four views. First: all that the مَا of part 4 covers, in the form used for those who have reason. Second: those who have reason among them. Third: those meant by مَن ذَا in part 5, the angels and the prophets. Fourth: the angels only. The grammar is the same in all four, and this lesson does not choose.</Note>

The three words look the same and do two jobs. A complete negative sentence names a group and sets one thing apart: parts 1 and 7. An incomplete sentence names no group, and إِلَّا means “only”: part 5. L4.05 taught both.

<Note kind="know">The Corpus tags the first and second إِلَّا as exception and the third as restriction. Al-Jadwal calls the second a word of restriction and the third a word of exception. This lesson follows al-Jadwal.</Note>

<Block id="style" title="What al-Jadwal says about the style">
  <Stage label="Two names">
    <Tiles
      label="the two figures al-Jadwal names for the verse"
      items={[
        { ar: 'إِيجَاز', en: 'brevity', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'فَصْل', en: 'leaving out the joining word', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />

Al-Jadwal prints a paragraph on the style of this verse (balagha). It names these points.

- **Ījāz**, brevity. Al-Jadwal says the verse describes the one true King, whom nobody can dispute, with a brevity that cannot be copied. L4.18 met ījāz as al-Jadwal’s reason for leaving out an object.
- **Faṣl**, leaving out the joining word between sentences. It belongs to the first branch of balagha, the choices inside a sentence (L4.17). Al-Jadwal says it shows that each of the King’s qualities stands on its own.
- A second kind of brevity, brevity of brevity (**ījāz al-ījāz**). Al-Jadwal says the verse has 17 places where the name of Allah is meant, written in some and hidden in others. Quran Learn has not checked this number against a book. Exercise 10 tries one count.

You can see faṣl in the verse. Of the ten parts, seven begin with no joining word: parts 1 to 6 and part 8. Three begin with وَ: parts 7, 9 and 10. This count is ours, from the Corpus. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn quotes al-Zamakhsharī: each sentence explains the one before it, and an explanation needs no joining word. Al-Durr adds that three sentences do have one: وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ, وَلَا يَـُٔودُهُۥ and وَهُوَ ٱلْعَلِىُّ ٱلْعَظِيمُ.

These are the only figures al-Jadwal names here. It names no gradation, no figure for the fronting in part 4, and none for the question in part 5. This lesson therefore teaches none of those. This project has no approved source for L4.17 and L4.18, so take the names as names, and check them against the books.

<Block id="m1" title="Mistake 1: reading the verse as one long sentence">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles

A common reading takes ٱللَّهُ as the mubtadaʾ of the whole verse, with everything after it as news. Al-Jadwal does not read it so. Only parts 1 to 4 are news about ٱللَّهُ. Part 5 begins a new sentence with its own mubtadaʾ, مَن, and each later part has its own words. Of the six new sentences, two begin with a mubtadaʾ (مَن and هُوَ) and four begin with a verb.

Al-Jadwal does allow a second place for part 6, as another khabar. It is a footnote and not the main reading.
</Block>

<Block id="m2" title="Mistake 2: reading every لَا as the same لَا">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="the five negative words, by kind"
      items={[
        { ar: 'لَآ إِلَٰهَ', en: 'denies a kind', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'لَا تَأْخُذُهُۥ', en: 'plain', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'وَلَا نَوْمٌ', en: 'extra', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ', en: 'plain', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'وَلَا يَـُٔودُهُۥ', en: 'plain', type: 'p' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The verse has five negative words, and they are not all the same. **لَآ** in part 1 denies a whole kind. Its noun, إِلَٰهَ, has a fatḥa and no tanwīn. The other four are plain negatives, except the one before نَوْمٌ, which is extra. A plain negative before a present verb changes nothing (L4.10).

When you meet a لَا, look at the word after it. A noun with a fatḥa and no tanwīn marks the kind. A noun with -un is a plain negative. A present verb with its plain ending is a plain negative too.
</Block>

<Block id="m3" title="Mistake 3: the same kasra means the same case">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ in two parts"
      items={[
        { ar: 'فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ', en: 'part 4: genitive', type: 'n' },
        { ar: 'ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ', en: 'part 8: accusative', type: 'n' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ ends in a kasra in both places. In part 4 the preposition فِى pulls it into the genitive, and the kasra is the normal genitive mark. In part 8 the word is the object. A sound feminine plural takes a kasra in the accusative (L2.05), so the kasra is the accusative mark.

The Corpus tags the first as genitive and the second as accusative. To tell them apart, ask what the word does in its sentence.
</Block>

<Block id="m4" title="Mistake 4: the تَـ of تَأْخُذُ is not “you”">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="تَأْخُذُ in the verse and in a made-up sentence"
      items={[
        { ar: 'تَأْخُذُهُۥ سِنَةٌ', en: 'she takes Him: drowsiness (2:255)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'تَأْخُذُ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ', en: 'you take the book (made-up)', type: 'v' },

A present verb with the prefix تَـ can mean “she” or “you” (L3.06). Which one it means depends on the doer. In part 3 the doer is the noun سِنَةٌ, which is feminine, so the prefix is “she”. The Corpus tags it 3FS. In the made-up sentence there is no noun doer, so the تَـ would be “you”.

The verb in the verse is not a command to the reader, and no verb in the verse is in the second person.
</Block>

- Al-Baqarah 2:255 has **50 written words**, 7 verbs, 5 negative words and 3 إِلَّا. The lesson cuts it into ten parts, following al-Jadwal’s sentences.
- **Parts 1 to 4** are one nominal sentence with five khabars. The first is the sentence لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ, with the lā of a kind, a left-out khabar and a substitute هُوَ (L4.05).
- **Parts 5 to 10** are six sentences. Part 5 is a question in form, with an incomplete exception. Part 7 is a complete exception. Part 8 has an assimilated verb. Part 9 has a hamzated and hollow verb. Part 10 is a nominal sentence with two khabars.
- Al-Jadwal names two figures of speech for the verse, brevity (ījāz) and leaving out the joining word (faṣl). This lesson names no other.
- Where the books differ, you met the readings of ٱلْحَىُّ, ٱلَّذِى, the “them” of part 6 and the object of شَآءَ.
