Read each verse from right to left. The English under each verse is our own plain meaning, not a translation.

The surah has **4 verses** and **15 written words**, as the Quranic Arabic Corpus counts them. Four of the words are verbs: قُلْ, يَلِدْ, يُولَدْ and يَكُن. All the others are nouns or little words.

<Note kind="know">At the end of its page on this surah, al-Jadwal reports sayings of the Prophet (peace be upon him) about its merit, from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī and from Abū al-Dardāʾ. The first says that it equals a third of the Quran. This lesson teaches grammar and style. It does not weigh reports.</Note>

Al-Jadwal parses the four verses together, on one page. It reads them as one run of sentences. This is the map for the rest of the lesson.

| Verse | Sentence | Job, as al-Jadwal reads it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | قُلْ … | An opening sentence. No place. |
| 1 | هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ | What is said. In the place of the accusative. |
| 1 | ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ | The khabar of هُوَ. In the place of the nominative. |
| 2 | ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ | A second khabar of هُوَ. In the place of the nominative. |
| 3 | لَمْ يَلِدْ | A third khabar of هُوَ. In the place of the nominative. |
| 3 | وَلَمْ يُولَدْ | Joined to لَمْ يَلِدْ. In the place of the nominative. |
| 4 | وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ | Joined to لَمْ يَلِدْ. In the place of the nominative. |

So in al-Jadwal’s main reading, everything from ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ to the end of verse 4 is news about هُوَ. A note of al-Jadwal also allows verses 2 and 3 to be new sentences inside what is said, with no place. The words you read do not change either way.

Verse 1 has two layers (L1.11). **قُلْ** is a command, and everything after it is what is said.

هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ starts with a pronoun, so it is a nominal sentence (L2.01). Its mubtadaʾ is هُوَ. Its khabar is a whole sentence, ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ, which has a mubtadaʾ and a khabar of its own. A sentence inside a khabar is the pattern L2.01 showed.

Al-Jadwal gives only the place of the sentence قُلْ …, so the word-level line for قُلْ is the standard one for a command.

L1.11 gave two ways to read هُوَ. Here they are next to each other.

**First reading.** The pronoun of the matter. هُوَ is a **ḍamīr al-shaʾn** (ضَمِير الشَّأْن). It means “the matter is this”, and the sentence after it explains it. That sentence, ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ, is the khabar of هُوَ. Inside it, ٱللَّهُ is the mubtadaʾ and أَحَدٌ is its khabar. This is the shape of al-Jadwal’s main parse, and its note says it holds if هُوَ is the pronoun of the matter. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn gives this reading too. It says it suits a place of great honour.

**Second reading.** A pointer. هُوَ points back to the One the listeners had asked about. Al-Durr lists four parses of the words after it. One has the same shape as the first reading. Al-Jadwal’s note gives another: ٱللَّهُ is the khabar of هُوَ, and أَحَدٌ is a second khabar.

Now compare. The roles change from one reading to the other. The endings do not. In both, ٱللَّهُ ends in -u and أَحَدٌ in -un. That is why this lesson follows al-Jadwal’s main reading and names the other.

Verse 2 has two words. Both are definite, and both are nominative. The grammar is the same as the sentence inside the khabar in verse 1: a mubtadaʾ and a khabar (L2.01).

**ٱلصَّمَدُ** is worth a closer look. Al-Jadwal calls it a **ṣifa mushabbaha**: a describing word that looks like a participle, on the pattern faʿal (L3.19). It gives the meaning too. The word works like a passive participle: the one who is aimed at in needs. Al-Durr gives the same pattern and meaning, and adds one more view: some say it means the one with no hollow inside. L1.11 gave the main meaning, “the one everyone turns to”.

Al-Durr also notes that the best reading is a sentence that stands on its own and gives its own news.

L1.11 promised that Level 5 would return to this surah for its style. Al-Jadwal has a paragraph on style for these verses, headed *balāgha* (بَلَاغَة). It names two points. Here is the first.

For verses 1 and 2 together, al-Jadwal names **ījāz** (إِيجَاز), brevity. Its point: the two verses carry two names of Allah, أَحَدٌ and ٱلصَّمَدُ, and between them these names hold every quality of perfection. أَحَدٌ points to a being that no one shares. ٱلصَّمَدُ points to the One that lordship ends with and that requests go to.

This is al-Jadwal’s reading of what the two names hold. It is a statement about meaning, and this lesson gives it as al-Jadwal’s. L4.18 met the same name, ījāz, as al-Jadwal’s reason for leaving an object out in 2:216.

Books also compare أَحَدٌ with وَاحِدٌ. L1.11 gave what al-Durr reports on that, and this lesson adds no claim of its own.

      label="what L4.10 says لَمْ does"
      items={[
        { ar: 'لَمْ يَلِدْ', en: 'jazm, past meaning: did not father', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'لَنْ يَلِدَ', en: 'naṣb, future: will not father (made-up)', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />

The English “has never fathered” may tempt you to say that لَمْ means “at no time at all”. L4.10 teaches what the word does: it puts the present verb in jazm and gives it a past meaning, “did not”. A claim that the verse reaches beyond the past needs a source. Al-Jadwal’s page on this surah has no note on it, so this lesson makes no such claim.

<Block id="m3" title="Mistake 3: trusting the order in verse 4">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Ikhlas 112:4 (end)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'tied', label: 'not the ism', hint: 'a phrase, first after the verb', words: [{ ar: 'لَّهُۥ', en: 'to Him', say: 'lahū', type: 'p' }] },
        { role: 'khabarKana', label: 'khabar: -an', words: [{ ar: 'كُفُوًا', en: 'equal', say: 'kufuwan' }] },
        { role: 'ismKana', label: 'ism: -un, last', words: [{ ar: 'أَحَدٌۢ', en: 'anyone', say: 'aḥadun' }] },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

After kāna the usual order is the ism first and then the khabar. In verse 4 the order is a phrase, then the khabar, then the ism. A learner who takes the first noun after the verb for the ism gets this verse wrong.

Decide by the endings (L2.11). The ism is the nominative one, أَحَدٌۢ. The accusative one, كُفُوًا, is the khabar.

</Block>

<Block id="m4" title="Mistake 4: saying what the grammar “proves”">
  <Stage label="Two ways to say it">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Say what the words do:</b> “The phrase is put before كُفُوًا.”</li>
      <li><b>Say what a book says:</b> “Al-Jadwal says it is put first because the meaning rests on it.”</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

It is tempting to write “the order mirrors the message” or “the pronoun proves oneness”. Those sentences sound like analysis. They are claims about meaning, and they need a source.

In this lesson, each reason for a style point is al-Jadwal’s or al-Durr’s, and each is named as theirs. The grammar gives roles and endings. L4.18 says the same in its Mistake 4: where no book gives a reason, show the figure and stay silent about why.

<Check n={19} q="Which sentence does this lesson allow?" options={['Al-Jadwal says the phrase is put first because the meaning rests on it', 'The order of the words proves the doctrine', 'The matching endings make the verse more powerful']} answer={0} why="The first names its source. The other two say what the grammar achieves, with no source." />
</Block>

<Block id="m5" title="Mistake 5: calling كُفُوًا an object or a tamyīz">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="an accusative word, and its job in the two readings"
      items={[
        { ar: 'كُفُوًا', en: 'reading 1: the khabar of يَكُن' },
        { ar: 'كُفُوًا', en: 'reading 2: a ḥāl' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The accusative has many jobs (L2.05). A learner may name the first one that comes to mind, the object. Nothing is done to كُفُوًا (L1.11, Mistake 2).

It is not a tamyīz either. L4.02 teaches the tamyīz as an accusative word that makes something vague clear. Neither al-Jadwal nor al-Durr reads كُفُوًا that way. They give two readings: the khabar of يَكُن, or a ḥāl (L4.01). Choose between those two, and say which book you follow.

<Check n={20} q="What do al-Jadwal and the first view of al-Durr call كُفُوًا?" options={['The khabar of يَكُن', 'A tamyīz', 'An object']} answer={0} why="Both read it as the khabar of kāna. Al-Durr’s second view is a ḥāl." />
</Block>

<Exercise n={1} q="Take the two verbs of verse 3 apart: يَلِدْ and يُولَدْ (Al-Ikhlas 112:3). For each, give the root, the kind of weak verb, the voice, the mood and its mark. Say what happens to the و in each.">

| Point | يَلِدْ | يُولَدْ |
|---|---|---|
| Root | و ل د | و ل د |
| Form | I | I |
| Kind | Assimilated: the weak letter is first | Assimilated |
| Voice | Active: “He has not fathered” | Passive: “He was not born” |
| Mood | Jazm, after لَمْ | Jazm, after لَمْ |
| Mark | A sukūn on the د | A sukūn on the د |
| The و | Fell between the prefix and the kasra | Stays: no kasra follows it |
| Doer | A hidden “he” | The stand-in for the doer is a hidden “he” |

The two verbs say two different things. This lesson says no more than their meaning.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Look at the order of the words in وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ (Al-Ikhlas 112:4). (a) Name the ism and the khabar. (b) What is لَّهُۥ tied to? (c) What moved from the usual order? (d) Give al-Jadwal’s reason.">

- (a) The ism is **أَحَدٌۢ** (nominative, -un). The khabar is **كُفُوًا** (accusative, -an).
- (b) Al-Jadwal ties لَّهُۥ to the khabar, كُفُوًا.
- (c) Two things moved. The khabar comes before the ism (L2.11). And the phrase لَّهُۥ comes before the word it is tied to, which is taqdīm (L4.18). The usual order is the made-up لَمْ يَكُنْ أَحَدٌ كُفُوًا لَهُ.
- (d) The verse was spoken to deny a match to Allah, and that meaning rests on the phrase that holds “Him”. So it is the most important part and comes first. This is the reason of al-Jadwal and al-Durr.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Sort the nouns of the surah as definite or indefinite and say what shows it. Use هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ (Al-Ikhlas 112:1), ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ (Al-Ikhlas 112:2) and لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ (Al-Ikhlas 112:4).">

| Noun | Definite or indefinite | What shows it |
|---|---|---|
| هُوَ | Definite | A pronoun |
| ٱللَّهُ | Definite | A name |
| ٱلصَّمَدُ | Definite | ٱلْ |
| The هُ in لَّهُۥ | Definite | A pronoun |
| أَحَدٌ (verse 1) | Indefinite | Tanwīn, no ٱلْ |
| كُفُوًا | Indefinite | Tanwīn, no ٱلْ |
| أَحَدٌۢ (verse 4) | Indefinite | Tanwīn, no ٱلْ |

Books on style discuss what a definite or an indefinite word does in a sentence. This project has no approved source for that, so the lesson stops at what the ending shows (L1.06).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Take ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ (Al-Ikhlas 112:2) apart. Give each word’s role, case and mark. Then give the job of the whole sentence in al-Jadwal, and the form of ٱلصَّمَدُ.">

| Word | Role | Case and mark |
|---|---|---|
| ٱللَّهُ | Mubtadaʾ | Nominative, -u |
| ٱلصَّمَدُ | Khabar | Nominative, -u |

The sentence is a **second khabar of هُوَ**, in the place of the nominative. A note of al-Jadwal allows a new sentence inside what is said, with no place.

ٱلصَّمَدُ is a **ṣifa mushabbaha** on the pattern faʿal, with the meaning “the one aimed at in needs”. It is definite with ٱلْ, and the ص is a sun letter, so it carries the shadda.

</Exercise>

| Word | First reading: the pronoun of the matter | Second reading: a pointer (al-Jadwal’s note) |
|---|---|---|
| هُوَ | Mubtadaʾ | Mubtadaʾ |
| ٱللَّهُ | Mubtadaʾ of the sentence inside | First khabar of هُوَ |
| أَحَدٌ | Khabar of ٱللَّهُ | Second khabar of هُوَ |
| The sentence ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ | The khabar of هُوَ | Not a sentence of its own: two khabars |

What stays the same is every ending. ٱللَّهُ ends in -u and أَحَدٌ in -un in both readings. Al-Jadwal’s main parse has the shape of the first column. Its note gives the second column.

<Exercise n={6} q="Sort the four verbs of the surah: قُلْ (Al-Ikhlas 112:1), يَلِدْ (Al-Ikhlas 112:3), يُولَدْ (Al-Ikhlas 112:3) and يَكُن (Al-Ikhlas 112:4). Give the root, the kind of weak verb and why it looks short or whole.">

| Verb | Root | Kind | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| قُلْ | ق و ل | Hollow | The command ends in a sukūn, so the long ū is cut (L4.12) |
| يَلِدْ | و ل د | Assimilated | The و fell between the prefix and the kasra (L4.14) |
| يُولَدْ | و ل د | Assimilated | The و stays in the passive: no kasra after it (L4.14) |
| يَكُن | ك و ن | Hollow | The sukūn of jazm cuts the long ū (L4.12) |

No verb in the surah is defective.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Compare وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ شَرِيكٌ فِى ٱلْمُلْكِ (Al-Isra 17:111, part) with وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ (Al-Ikhlas 112:4). In each, find the ism of يَكُن and give its case. What does al-Jadwal tie لَّهُۥ to? What is different about the words after لَّهُۥ?">

In 17:111 the ism is **شَرِيكٌ**, nominative with -un. In 112:4 the ism is **أَحَدٌۢ**, nominative with -un.

Al-Jadwal ties لَّهُۥ to the khabar of يَكُن in both verses. In 112:4 that khabar is the written word كُفُوًا.

The difference: after لَّهُۥ, 17:111 has only the ism and a phrase tied to it, فِى ٱلْمُلْكِ, which al-Jadwal ties to شَرِيكٌ. 112:4 has an accusative word, كُفُوًا, and then the ism. In 17:111 no accusative word follows the verb.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Who says it? Sort each statement as al-Jadwal, al-Durr, or our own look. (a) The phrase is put first because the meaning rests on it. (b) The four verses end in -ad when you stop. (c) Verses 1 and 2 hold two names that between them hold every quality of perfection. (d) كُفُوًا may be a ḥāl. (e) ٱلصَّمَدُ may mean the one with no hollow inside.">

- (a) **Al-Jadwal**, in its paragraph on style. Al-Durr gives the same reasoning on this verse and credits it to al-Zamakhsharī.
- (b) **Our own look.** Al-Jadwal has no entry on the endings. It is a teaching summary (L4.18).
- (c) **Al-Jadwal**, which names it brevity (ījāz).
- (d) **Al-Durr**, as a second view from Makkī and Abū al-Baqāʾ. Abū Ḥayyān rejects it.
- (e) **Al-Durr**, as one of two views on the word. The main one is “the one aimed at in needs”.

</Exercise>

- Surah Al-Ikhlas has **four verses**, **fifteen written words** and **four verbs**: two hollow and two assimilated.
- **Verse 1** is a command and what is said. هُوَ has two readings: the pronoun of the matter, and a pointer. The endings are the same in both.
- **Verse 2** is a nominal sentence with two definite nouns. ٱلصَّمَدُ is a ṣifa mushabbaha.
- **Verse 3** has two sentences with لَمْ, and two verbs from one root: يَلِدْ is active, يُولَدْ is passive.
- **Verse 4** has kāna with its ism, أَحَدٌۢ, and its khabar, كُفُوًا. The khabar has a second reading, a ḥāl.
- **Style.** Al-Jadwal names brevity in verses 1 and 2 and taqdīm in verse 4, with its reason. The matching endings and the repeated word are our own look.
