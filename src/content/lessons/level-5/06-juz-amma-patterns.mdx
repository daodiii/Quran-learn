Read the verses from right to left. Each has **three words**. The first is a time word, إِذَا, “when”. The second is a noun. The third is a verb that ends in تْ.

That is one build, used three times in a row. The same build comes back in 15 more verses in Juz ʿAmma. A build that comes back like this is what this lesson calls a **pattern**. “Pattern” is a teaching word of Quran Learn. It is not a term from a grammar book.

Juz ʿAmma is the 30th part of the Quran. This lesson counts it as surahs 78 to 114.

The verbs in the table are not all passive. The Corpus tags **14** as passive and **4** as active in form: ٱنكَدَرَتْ (81:2), ٱنفَطَرَتْ (82:1), ٱنشَقَّتْ (84:1) and ٱنتَثَرَتْ (82:2). The old lesson said all of them were passive. That was wrong.

How do you tell? A past passive verb has a **ḍamma** on its first letter and a **kasra** on the next: كُوِّرَتْ, سُيِّرَتْ (L3.09). The four others start with ٱ. Form VII is ٱنْفَعَلَ (L3.18): after the ٱ comes the ن of the form, as in ٱنكَدَرَتْ. Form VIII is ٱفْتَعَلَ (L3.14): after the ٱ comes a root letter, then the ت of the form, as in ٱنتَثَرَتْ. Form VII often means “it happens to the doer”: the sky splits, and no one is named as the splitter.

<Note kind="warn">ٱنتَثَرَتْ starts with the same two letters as Form VII, but its ن is a root letter and the ت after it is the sign of the form. Check the root: ن ث ر.</Note>

<Note kind="know">Some passives do not show the plain look of L3.09. مُدَّتْ (84:3) has a doubled letter and بُعْثِرَتْ (82:4) has four root letters. The Corpus tags both as passive, and this lesson follows the tag.</Note>

All 18 verbs are marked “she” in the Corpus. The nouns before them are of two kinds. **Eight** are tagged feminine and single: ٱلشَّمْسُ, ٱلْمَوْءُۥدَةُ, ٱلسَّمَآءُ three times, ٱلْجَحِيمُ, ٱلْجَنَّةُ and ٱلْأَرْضُ. **Ten** are tagged plural: eight masculine and two feminine.

The ten plurals are broken plurals. Nine name things or animals, like ٱلنُّجُومُ, “the stars”, and ٱلْوُحُوشُ, “the wild animals”. The tenth is ٱلنُّفُوسُ, “the souls”. All ten take the same verb: feminine and singular.

Two earlier rules give this. A plural of things usually takes a feminine single word (L1.08, L2.02). A verb that stands with its doer agrees in gender and stays singular (L2.03). The ending تْ is the “she” ending of L3.05.

This is also why the verses end the same way. The noun comes first and the verb comes last, so the last word of each verse is a “she” verb.

L2.03 taught that the doer comes after its verb. Here the noun comes first, and ends in a ḍamma. What is it the doer of?

Al-Jadwal puts a verb in that is **not written**. For ٱلْجِبَالُ it gives a verb like “scattered”. The noun is the doer of that left-out verb, and the verb that is written explains it. A sentence that only explains has no place of its own. Al-Jadwal calls it an explaining sentence. The clause with the left-out verb is the part that إِذَا adds to, so it is in the place of the genitive.

Al-Jadwal reads the same way in 82:1 to 82:4 and 84:1. L4.04 noted this for 82:1.

<Note kind="know">Scholars differ here. Al-Jadwal adds a footnote: some read each noun as a stand-in for the doer (nāʾib fāʿil, L3.09) of a left-out passive verb. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn calls that the sounder view. It also gives another: the noun is a mubtadaʾ (the Kufans and al-Akhfash). This lesson follows al-Jadwal.</Note>

A time clause waits for its answer. The series in At-Takwir ends with 81:14, and the series in Al-Infitar ends with 82:5. Both answers have one build: **عَلِمَتْ نَفْسٌ مَّا** and a past verb. A search of the whole Quran finds these two verses and no others with the words عَلِمَتْ نَفْسٌ.

Al-Jadwal ties all the time words of the series to the answer. It says the answer is a sentence with no place, the answer of a time word that does not cut (L4.04).

Now compare 84:1 to 84:5. No answer is written. Al-Jadwal says it is left out, and gives something like “the souls will know their deeds”. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn lists five views for 84:1. One of them is the clause of 81:14 and 82:5, which it calls good.

Not every verse that starts with إِذَا has a noun next. In surahs 78 to 114, **30** verses start with إِذَا, alone or after وَ or فَ. In **18** the next word is a noun (the table above). In **12** the next word is a verb: 79:11, 79:34, 80:33, 83:3, 83:13, 83:30, 83:31, 83:32, 84:21, 94:7, 99:1 and 110:1.

In 80:33 the doer comes after its verb, as in L2.03. Nothing needs to be put in. The verb جَآءَتِ is “she” because ٱلصَّآخَّةُ is feminine. Al-Jadwal reads this verse at sentence level only: the clause جَآءَتِ ٱلصَّآخَّةُ is in the place of the genitive, and the answer is left out. It gives the sense “each person is busy with himself”.

So the shape of 81:1 is one choice of order. The usual order, verb first, is the other. When the noun is first, a verb has to be put in.

Al-Jadwal gives these parts in all eight verses that were read for this lesson.

- **أَدْرَىٰكَ** is the khabar of the first مَا. It is a whole sentence: a verb, a hidden “he” as doer (the Corpus tags the verb “he”), and **كَ** as the first object.

<Block id="mistake-ma" title="Mistake 4: reading the second مَا as the object">
  <Stage label="Two question nouns">
    <Tiles
      label="وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلطَّارِقُ (86:2)"
      items={[
        { ar: 'وَمَآ', en: 'question noun', type: 'n' },
        { ar: 'أَدْرَىٰكَ', en: 'verb + “you”', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'مَا', en: 'question noun', type: 'n' },
        { ar: 'ٱلطَّارِقُ', en: 'its khabar', type: 'n' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

It is easy to take the second مَا for the object of the verb. It is a question noun with its own khabar, ٱلطَّارِقُ. The whole clause مَا ٱلطَّارِقُ is the second object.
</Block>
</Step>

<Exercise n={1} q="For each verse give the noun, the verb and the kind of verb (passive, Form VII or Form VIII). (a) وَإِذَا ٱلْعِشَارُ عُطِّلَتْ (At-Takwir 81:4) (b) وَإِذَا ٱلْوُحُوشُ حُشِرَتْ (At-Takwir 81:5) (c) وَإِذَا ٱلْكَوَاكِبُ ٱنتَثَرَتْ (Al-Infitar 82:2)">

- (a) The noun is **ٱلْعِشَارُ**. The verb is **عُطِّلَتْ**: a ḍamma and a kasra, so it is passive.
- (b) The noun is **ٱلْوُحُوشُ**, a plural of animals. The verb is **حُشِرَتْ**: also passive, with the “she” ending.
- (c) The noun is **ٱلْكَوَاكِبُ**, a plural of things. The verb is **ٱنتَثَرَتْ**: it starts with ٱ, then the root letter ن, then the ت of Form VIII (root ن ث ر). It is not passive.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Sort these verbs into passive and not passive, and say which sign told you: (a) كُوِّرَتْ (At-Takwir 81:1) (b) ٱنكَدَرَتْ (At-Takwir 81:2) (c) سُيِّرَتْ (At-Takwir 81:3) (d) ٱنفَطَرَتْ (Al-Infitar 82:1)">

- (a) **Passive**: a ḍamma on the first letter, a kasra on the next.
- (b) **Not passive**: Form VII. It starts with ٱ and ن.
- (c) **Passive**: the same sign as (a).
- (d) **Not passive**: Form VII. Same sign as (b).

A count of the Corpus tags gives 14 passive and 4 not passive among the 18 verses.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Al-Jadwal reads the noun in وَإِذَا ٱلْمَوْءُۥدَةُ سُئِلَتْ (At-Takwir 81:8) in a certain way. What is ٱلْمَوْءُۥدَةُ, and what does the written verb do?">

Al-Jadwal reads **ٱلْمَوْءُۥدَةُ** as the **doer of a left-out verb**, and it supplies a verb that means something like “she complained of wrong”. The written verb **سُئِلَتْ** only explains it, so it is an explaining sentence with no place. The noun ends in a ḍamma because it is a doer.

Other readings are named in block `hidden`: a stand-in for the doer, or a mubtadaʾ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Which of these verses write out the answer of the time clauses, and which leave it out? (a) 81:14 (b) 82:5 (c) 84:1 (d) 80:33">

- (a) **Written**: عَلِمَتْ نَفْسٌ مَّآ أَحْضَرَتْ.
- (b) **Written**: عَلِمَتْ نَفْسٌ مَّا قَدَّمَتْ وَأَخَّرَتْ.
- (c) **Left out.** Al-Jadwal supplies something like “the souls will know their deeds”.
- (d) **Left out.** Al-Jadwal supplies something like “each person is busy with himself”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Compare two verses: وَإِذَا ٱلْجِبَالُ سُيِّرَتْ (At-Takwir 81:3) and فَإِذَا جَآءَتِ ٱلصَّآخَّةُ (ʿAbasa 80:33). Which has the noun first? Where is the doer in each? Why do both verbs end in تْ?">

In 81:3 the noun **ٱلْجِبَالُ** comes first. Al-Jadwal reads it as the doer of a left-out verb, and the written verb explains it. In 80:33 the verb **جَآءَتِ** comes first and the doer **ٱلصَّآخَّةُ** follows it, so nothing is put in.

Both verbs end in تْ because they stand with a feminine doer, or with a plural of things, which takes “she” (L2.03, L2.02).

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Exercise n={6} q="Parse the shape of وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلطَّارِقُ (At-Tariq 86:2). Name the first مَا, what the sentence أَدْرَىٰكَ is, the second مَا, ٱلطَّارِقُ, and the clause مَا ٱلطَّارِقُ.">

- The first **مَا** is a question noun and a mubtadaʾ.
- **أَدْرَىٰكَ** is a whole sentence and the khabar of the first مَا. The **كَ** is the first object.
- The second **مَا** is a question noun and a second mubtadaʾ.
- **ٱلطَّارِقُ** is the khabar of the second مَا, in the nominative.
- **مَا ٱلطَّارِقُ** is the second object of the verb, in the place of accusative.

Al-Jadwal reads the next verse as an explaining clause: “it is the star”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Which of these surahs have a verse with وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا: 90, 95, 97, 101, 105, 104? Count the verses in the ones that do.">

Surahs **90, 97, 101 and 104** have it. Surahs 95 and 105 do not.

The verses: 90:12, 97:2, 101:3, 101:10 and 104:5. That is **five verses** in these four surahs. Surah 101 has two.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Write the last word of each verse and say what they share: (a) ٱنشَقَّتْ (Al-Inshiqaq 84:1) (b) وَحُقَّتْ (Al-Inshiqaq 84:2) (c) مُدَّتْ (Al-Inshiqaq 84:3) (d) وَتَخَلَّتْ (Al-Inshiqaq 84:4)">

All four end in **تْ**, the “she” ending of a past verb. In 84:1 and 84:3 the verb is last because the noun came first. In 84:2 and 84:4 a verb joined by وَ closes the verse. The shared sound comes from the same kind of word at the end of each verse. The lesson says no more than that.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Find a shape of your own. Look at كَلَّا سَيَعْلَمُونَ (An-Naba 78:4) and كَلَّا سَوْفَ تَعْلَمُونَ (At-Takathur 102:3). Use the four questions.">

A model answer.

- **Identify:** كَلَّا, then a future word (سَ or سَوْفَ), then the verb “know”.
- **Catalog:** two searches of the Corpus text (كلا سيعلمون and كلا سوف) find four verses in all: 78:4, 78:5, 102:3 and 102:4. They are in two surahs.
- **Analyse:** the other pages of al-Jadwal show how the parts are read: كَلَّا is a word of refusal and warning, and سَوْفَ is a word for the future. The two verses were not parsed for this answer.
- **Compare:** each surah says the phrase twice, and the second time it starts with ثُمَّ: 78:5 and 102:4. The first uses سَ with a verb for “they”, and the second uses سَوْفَ with “you”.

The count is only four verses, so call it a small pattern.

</Exercise>

(b) Only Juz ʿAmma has a time word, a noun and a “she” verb. (c) Every verb in 81:1 to 81:13 is passive. (d) The 18 verses of block catalog all have three words.">

      items={[

        { kind: 'k', text: 'counted and parsed', small: 'the Corpus and al-Jadwal' },

      ]}

- A **pattern** here is a shape that comes back in several verses. The four questions are identify, catalog, analyse and compare. They are Quran Learn's teaching order.
- **Pattern 1:** (وَ)إِذَا, a noun, and a “she” verb. The Corpus finds 18 verses in surahs 81, 82 and 84, and 4 more in 77. Al-Jadwal reads the noun as the doer of a left-out verb. 14 of the 18 verbs are passive and 4 are Form VII or VIII.
- The clauses wait for an answer. It is **عَلِمَتْ نَفْسٌ مَّا** in 81:14 and 82:5, and it is left out in 84:1 and 80:33.
- **Pattern 2:** **وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا** and a noun. Two question nouns, a verb with “you”, and a clause that is the second object. 13 verses in the Quran, 10 in Juz ʿAmma.
- **Pattern 3:** the last words of verses. The counts say what is there. They do not say why.
- A count covers only what the test looks for. The shape of 81:1 is also in 77:8 to 77:11.
