L2.01 showed this verse and made a promise: L5.14 looks at the order of its words. Here is the look.

Each half has the same shape. A phrase comes first (“for you”, “for me”). The word it is about comes second (“your religion”, “my religion”). L2.01 called the first part the khabar put first and the second the mubtadaʾ put later.

The second line is made up. It shows the usual order, and it is correct Arabic too. دِينُكُمْ is a definite word, because it is joined to “your” (L2.02). So the verse could have used either order, and it chose this one. This lesson asks what a book can tell us about such a choice, and where it can tell us nothing.

You know two usual orders. A nominal sentence puts the mubtadaʾ first and the khabar second (L2.01). A verbal sentence puts the verb first, then the doer, then the object (L2.03).

A verse can move a word out of its usual place. This lesson follows three moves.

- The khabar goes before the mubtadaʾ: 109:6, 2:142 and 2:284.
- The object goes before the doer: 35:28.
- A phrase goes before the verb it belongs to: 3:122.

After a move, the endings still tell you the role of each word. L2.03 showed this with an object put first: -u marks the doer and -a marks the object, in either order.

For a move that was a choice, this lesson gives a reason only when a named book gives it. The books here are al-Jadwal and, on a few points, al-Durr al-Maṣūn. Where neither gives a reason, the lesson says so. A thought of our own is labelled “our own look”.

<Step id="khabar" label="The khabar first" title="The khabar put first: forced and chosen">
<Block id="forced" title="When the order is not a choice">
  <Stage label="From the old lesson on this topic">
    <blockquote class="lx-quote" lang="ar" dir="rtl">
      <p>فِى قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ</p>
    </blockquote>
    <p class="lx-quote-note">Al-Baqarah 2:10 (start). Meaning: “In their hearts is a disease.”</p>
  </Stage>

The khabar is the phrase فِى قُلُوبِهِم. The mubtadaʾ is مَرَضٌ, and it ends in -un, so it is **indefinite**. L2.01 and L2.02 taught the rule: an indefinite mubtadaʾ with a phrase as its khabar needs the khabar to come first. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn calls the phrase here a khabar that must come first (wājib al-taqdīm). Al-Jadwal calls it a khabar put first.

So this verse had no other order to choose from. The usual order, مَرَضٌ فِى قُلُوبِهِم, would start the sentence with an indefinite word and no reason for it (a made-up order). A reading such as “the verse puts the heart first so that we notice where the disease is” has nothing to stand on. The order came from the grammar.

Al-Jadwal has a balagha note on 2:10, and it says something else. It names a borrowed word, istiʿāra (L4.17): “disease” stands for ignorance and bad belief. It also says the indefinite form points to a disease of an unusual kind, not one of the diseases people know. The note says nothing about the order.

Both halves have one shape, and al-Jadwal parses them in one way. It names the khabar as put first, and it gives no reason for that order.

L2.01 said that moving a word to the front often gives it weight. That is a teaching summary. Neither al-Jadwal nor al-Durr al-Maṣūn says it about this verse. What you can see is that both halves name the person first: for you, for me. That is our own look, not a reading from a book.

The surah says the refusal four times. Al-Jadwal names this takrīr, “repeating”. Its balagha note gives the job: emphasis (taʾkīd). It adds that the Quran came down in the language of the Arabs, and that they repeat speech to emphasize it and to make it understood. A person who agrees says “yes, yes”, and a person who refuses says “no, no”. L4.17 met takrīr, and L5.12 reads more repeats.

Now look at the first word after لَا in each verse. Verse 2 has a verb: أَعْبُدُ, “I worship”. Verses 3 and 5 have a pronoun, أَنتُمْ, and then a doer word, عَٰبِدُونَ. Verse 4 has أَنَا۠ and عَابِدٌ. So verse 2 is built differently from the verses after it. Al-Jadwal says nothing about this difference, so the lesson only shows it. It is our own look.

<Block id="two-books" title="Two books, two different questions about 109:6">
  <Stage label="What each book says">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Al-Jadwal:</b> the khabar is put first, twice. No reason is given for that order.</li>
      <li><b>Al-Durr al-Maṣūn:</b> a reason for a different order: why verse 6 comes after the refusals.</li>

Al-Durr al-Maṣūn (al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī) speaks about the surah's order, not the order inside لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ. Its reading is this. The most important thing was that the Prophet clears himself of their religion. So the verses before verse 6 begin with the denial about himself. When that was settled, the verse turns to them with two affirmative sentences: لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ and وَلِىَ دِينِ.

So the two books answer two different questions. For the order inside each half, neither gives a reason. For the place of the two sentences after the refusals, al-Durr gives one. This lesson keeps the two apart.

In 2:142 the verse answers a question about the direction of prayer. The answer starts as 109:6 does: a phrase with li- first, then the mubtadaʾ, ٱلْمَشْرِقُ وَٱلْمَغْرِبُ. The mubtadaʾ is definite, so the usual order was open too. Al-Jadwal ties لِّلَّهِ to a khabar that is put first. It gives no reason for the order, and al-Durr al-Maṣūn says nothing about it either.

The same is true of وَلِلَّهِ ٱلْمَشْرِقُ وَٱلْمَغْرِبُ in 2:115 (exercise 5). Both verses have the same words, and al-Jadwal reads both in the same way.

Verse 2:284 has the same shape as 2:142 and 109:6: the phrase with li- first, then the mubtadaʾ. Al-Jadwal names the order and gives no reason.

The mubtadaʾ here is a joining noun, so you cannot find it by its ending. Ask which word the news is about. “To Allah belongs what is in the heavens” makes مَا the thing that belongs.

Near the end of the same verse, وَٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ قَدِيرٌ keeps the usual order: ٱللَّهُ is the mubtadaʾ and comes first (L2.01). One verse can hold both.

To show a move you must write the usual order of the same sentence. For 35:28 it is verb, doer, object: يَخْشَى ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ ٱللَّهَ.

When a verse starts with a phrase, the first word is the khabar. It is not the mubtadaʾ. In 109:6 the mubtadaʾ is the -u word دِينُكُمْ. L2.01 gave the tip: the noun right after li- ends in -i, so look further for the -u.

In 2:284 the tip fails, because the mubtadaʾ is a joining noun with no ending to see. Then ask which word the news is about.

<Check n={18} q="In لِّلَّهِ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ, why can you not find the mubtadaʾ by its -u?" options={['It is a joining noun and shows no ending', 'It is a verb', 'It is genitive']} answer={0} why="مَا is a joining noun. It never changes, and its role is the mubtadaʾ." />
</Block>

<Block id="m6" title="Mistake 6: a reason for every word, even one that did not move">
  <Stage label="Where nothing moved">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>1:2:</b> ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ, mubtadaʾ first, usual order</li>
      <li><b>112:1:</b> هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ, mubtadaʾ first, usual order</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

Some lessons give a reason for the usual order. They say 1:2 puts “praise” first to set the idea before directing it, or that 112:1 puts هُوَ first to give a definition. We found no source for that wording. For 1:2 al-Jadwal does say something: the praise comes before the name because the situation calls for special care for it. The order itself is the usual one for a nominal sentence. For 112:1 we found no reason in al-Jadwal.

When nothing moved, there is no order to explain. Some people hold that every order in the Quran has a reason in the speaker's mind. This lesson does not argue with that. It gives a reason only when a named book states it.

<Check n={19} q="1:2 starts with the mubtadaʾ ٱلْحَمْدُ. What did the verse put first?" options={['Nothing: this is the usual order', 'The khabar', 'The object']} answer={0} why="The mubtadaʾ comes first. That is the usual order of a nominal sentence (L2.01)." />
</Block>
</Step>

<Exercise n={1} q="Put the words back in the usual order and say what moved. (a) إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ (Al-Fatiha 1:5, part) (b) لِّلَّهِ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ (Al-Baqarah 2:284, start) (c) وَعَلَى ٱللَّهِ فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ (Al-Imran 3:122, end) (d) قُل لِّلَّهِ ٱلْمَشْرِقُ وَٱلْمَغْرِبُ (Al-Baqarah 2:142, part)">

- (a) The usual order is نَعْبُدُكَ (made-up): the verb with the object pronoun attached. The object moved before the verb. With the object first, the verse uses the detached pronoun إِيَّاكَ (L3.01). Al-Jadwal reads the order as a restriction (ḥaṣr). L5.02 reads Al-Fatiha.
- (b) The usual order is مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ لِلَّهِ (made-up). The khabar moved before the mubtadaʾ مَا. Al-Jadwal gives no reason.
- (c) The usual order is فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ (made-up). The phrase moved before the verb. Al-Jadwal says importance. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn says restriction and the verse endings.
- (d) The usual order is ٱلْمَشْرِقُ وَٱلْمَغْرِبُ لِلَّهِ (made-up). The khabar moved before the mubtadaʾ. Al-Jadwal gives no reason.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Say what is put first and what is put later, in al-Jadwal's words. (a) إِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ (Al-Fatiha 1:5, part) (b) فِى قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ (Al-Baqarah 2:10, start) (c) قُل لِّلَّهِ ٱلْمَشْرِقُ وَٱلْمَغْرِبُ (Al-Baqarah 2:142, part) (d) إِنَّمَا يَخْشَى ٱللَّهَ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ (Fatir 35:28, part)">

- (a) The **object put first** (mafʿūl bihi muqaddam): إِيَّاكَ, before the verb.
- (b) The **khabar put first** (khabar muqaddam): فِى قُلُوبِهِم. The **mubtadaʾ put later** (mubtadaʾ muʾakhkhar): مَرَضٌ.
- (c) The **khabar put first**: لِّلَّهِ. The **mubtadaʾ put later**: ٱلْمَشْرِقُ.
- (d) The **object put first**: ٱللَّهَ. The **doer put later** (fāʿil muʾakhkhar): ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟.

Cases (b) and (c) have the same name, because both are a khabar put first. They differ in the second question: (b) is forced and (c) is chosen.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Forced or chosen? Give the reason. (a) فِى قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ (Al-Baqarah 2:10, start) (b) لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ (Al-Kafirun 109:6, start) (c) لَهُمْ فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا خِزْىٌ (Al-Baqarah 2:114, end) (d) وَلِلَّهِ ٱلْمَشْرِقُ وَٱلْمَغْرِبُ (Al-Baqarah 2:115, start)">

- (a) **Forced.** مَرَضٌ is indefinite and the khabar is a phrase.
- (b) **Chosen.** دِينُكُمْ is definite, because it is joined to “your”. The usual order was open too.
- (c) **Forced.** خِزْىٌ is indefinite and the khabar is a phrase. L2.02 uses this verse for the same rule.
- (d) **Chosen.** ٱلْمَشْرِقُ is definite, so both orders were open.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Read فِى قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ فَزَادَهُمُ ٱللَّهُ مَرَضًا (Al-Baqarah 2:10, start). (1) Which part of the first clause is the khabar, and which is the mubtadaʾ? (2) Is its order forced or chosen? (3) Does al-Jadwal give a reason for the order? What does its balagha note say? (4) In the second clause, name the verb, the two objects and the doer, in the order they stand.">

1. The khabar is the phrase فِى قُلُوبِهِم, put first. The mubtadaʾ is مَرَضٌ, indefinite and put later.
2. **Forced**: the mubtadaʾ is indefinite and the khabar is a phrase (L2.01).
3. No reason for the order. Its balagha note names a borrowed word (istiʿāra): disease for ignorance and bad belief. It says the indefinite form points to a disease of an unusual kind.
4. Verb فَزَادَ; first object -hum (attached to the verb); doer ٱللَّهُ; second object مَرَضًا. Nothing is put first here: the attached pronoun stays on the verb.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Ask the three questions of both verses. (a) وَلِلَّهِ ٱلْمَشْرِقُ وَٱلْمَغْرِبُ (Al-Baqarah 2:115, start) (b) وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَتَّخِذُ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ أَندَادًا (Al-Baqarah 2:165, start)">

- (a) The khabar لِلَّهِ is put first. The mubtadaʾ ٱلْمَشْرِقُ is definite, so the order was chosen. Al-Jadwal names the order and gives no reason. It is the same shape as 2:142.
- (b) The phrase وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ is the khabar, put first. The mubtadaʾ is مَن, a joining noun: “some who”. In al-Jadwal's main reading مَن is definite, so the order was open. Al-Jadwal also allows مَن as an indefinite word described by the clause. In that reading the order is forced. It gives no reason for the order in either reading.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Who says it? Sort each statement as al-Jadwal, al-Durr al-Maṣūn, or no named source. (a) On 3:122, the phrase is put first for importance. (b) On 3:122, the phrase is put first for restriction and to fit the endings of the verses. (c) On 35:28, the fear is limited to the knowers. (d) Putting لَكُمْ first gives the religion more weight. (e) A word put first always means “only”.">

- (a) **Al-Jadwal**, in a note on 3:122. It says the same on 5:11.
- (b) **Al-Durr al-Maṣūn**, on 3:122.
- (c) **Al-Jadwal**, in its paragraph on 35:27 and 35:28.
- (d) **No named source.** L2.01 says moving a word often gives weight, as a teaching summary. Neither book says it of 109:6.
- (e) **No named source.** Al-Jadwal reads importance in 3:122 and gives no reason in 2:142.

<Exercise n={7} q="Read the end of 5:11: وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَعَلَى ٱللَّهِ فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ (Al-Maidah 5:11, end). (1) What is put first? (2) What does al-Jadwal say about it, and where else does it say the same? (3) What does al-Jadwal say fa- joins the command to? (4) How many verses end with the same phrase?">

1. The phrase عَلَى ٱللَّهِ, tied to the verb فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ.
2. It is put first for importance (ihtimām). Al-Jadwal says the same on 3:122, and it sends the reader to 3:122 and 3:160 for the parse.
3. To a condition that is left out. For 5:11 al-Jadwal gives it as: if people lean on something other than Allah, let the believers rely on Allah.
4. Seven (3:122, 3:160, 5:11, 9:51, 14:11, 58:10, 64:13), counted in the Corpus text.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Made-up example. إِنَّمَا يَخْشَى ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ ٱللَّهَ. Following al-Jadwal's note on 35:28, say what this sentence would mean. Then say how the verse, إِنَّمَا يَخْشَى ٱللَّهَ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ (Fatir 35:28, part), differs.">

The made-up sentence has the doer before the object. Al-Jadwal says that order turns the meaning round: the knowers fear only Allah. They fear nothing else.

The verse has the object before the doer. Al-Jadwal reads it as a restriction of the fear to the knowers: among His servants, the knowers are the ones who fear Allah. The words are almost the same, and the meaning differs.

The made-up line is ours. It is built to follow al-Jadwal's remark, and it does not occur in the Quran.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="In لِّلَّهِ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ (Al-Baqarah 2:284, start), which word is the mubtadaʾ? Why does the -u test of L2.01 not find it? What is the job of فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ?">

The mubtadaʾ is **مَا**, a joining noun. It never changes, so there is no -u to see. Al-Jadwal says it is built, in the place of the nominative.

The phrase فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ is the relative clause of مَا: it says which things. Al-Jadwal ties it to a left-out word, like “is found”.

لِّلَّهِ is the khabar, put first.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Write a reading of three lines for وَلِىَ دِينِ (Al-Kafirun 109:6, end): the move, forced or chosen, and what you may write about the reason.">

1. **The move.** The khabar لِىَ is put before the mubtadaʾ دِينِ. The usual order is دِينِى لِى (made-up).
2. **Forced or chosen.** Chosen. دِينِ is definite, because it is joined to “my”. The usual order was open.
3. **The reason.** Al-Jadwal gives none for this order. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn speaks about another order, the place of the two sentences after the refusals. So write only the move and that no source in this lesson gives a reason. A remark about weight is our own look, and you must label it so.

</Exercise>

  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="the usual order plus a move plus a source equals a reading"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'the usual order', small: 'where the word would stand' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'the move', small: 'what stands first instead' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'a reading', small: 'only if a book says why' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A verse can put the khabar before the mubtadaʾ, the object before the doer, or a phrase before its verb. The endings keep the roles clear.
- Some orders are forced. An indefinite mubtadaʾ with a phrase as khabar needs the khabar first (2:10). A forced order has no reason to look for.
- Some orders are a choice (109:6, 2:142, 2:284). For these three, al-Jadwal names the order and gives no reason.
- Where al-Jadwal gives a reason, the lesson gives it with the book: restriction in 35:28, importance in 3:122 and 5:11. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn gives restriction and the verse endings for 3:122.
- A reading belongs to one verse. A word put first does not always mean “only”.
- “Weight” and the other remarks of ours are labelled “our own look”.
