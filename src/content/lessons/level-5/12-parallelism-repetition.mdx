Read the verse from right to left. It is a question, and it has four words.

We counted it with a script over the Corpus text. The same four words, with the same marks, make up **31 verses**, and all 31 are in Surah Ar-Rahman. The surah has 78 verses. The first two are 55:13 and 55:16. The last is 55:77.

L4.06 met this as a whole sentence said again (tawkīd lafẓī). L4.17 gave al-Jadwal’s name for it, takrīr, and its reason. This lesson looks at the grammar of the sentence and at the verses around it.

Al-Jadwal names takrīr for 55:13. For the other verses in this lesson it names no figure of repeating and no figure of clauses built alike. So the lesson reads their grammar with al-Jadwal, and then gives counts and looks of our own. Each one is labelled as ours.

L1.09 read 55:1 and 55:2 together: ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ is the mubtadaʾ, and the verbal sentence عَلَّمَ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ is its khabar. Here the idea goes on to 55:3 and 55:4.

Al-Jadwal says ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ is the mubtadaʾ. The sentence عَلَّمَ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ is its khabar, in the place of the nominative. خَلَقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ is a second khabar, and عَلَّمَهُ ٱلْبَيَانَ is a third. The doer of each verb is a hidden “He” that goes back to ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ. So one mubtadaʾ has three news, and each one is a sentence (L2.01, L2.02).

For ٱلْقُرْءَانَ al-Jadwal says second object. The first object is left out. Al-Jadwal lists a few readings of who it is, and this lesson does not choose one.

Each of 55:2, 55:3 and 55:4 is a past verb, a hidden “He” and an object that ends in -a. That is one build, three times. L4.18 calls clauses built the same way **tawāzī**, a teaching word. Al-Jadwal names no figure on these verses, so this is only our description of what you can see.

| Verse | Verb | Form (Corpus) | Root | Object |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 55:2 | عَلَّمَ | II | ع ل م | ٱلْقُرْءَانَ |
| 55:3 | خَلَقَ | I | خ ل ق | ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ |
| 55:4 | عَلَّمَهُ | II | ع ل م | ٱلْبَيَانَ, and -hu |

The build is the same in all three places. The verb is not: Form II, Form I, Form II. The first and the third have the same root and are the same verb, عَلَّمَ.

The old lesson said this list “builds anticipation” and makes the actions “equally important”. No source we can name says that, so the lesson leaves it out.

**ٱلنَّجْمُ** is the word to watch. Next to the sun and the moon you may think of stars. Al-Jadwal says it names a plant with no stem here. The next word, ٱلشَّجَرُ, “the tree”, fits that reading. This is why the meaning line says “stemless plants”. The Corpus gives the word the root ن ج م and does not say which meaning it has. The old lesson said “stars”.

Al-Jadwal also names two figures in 55:6. The first is an **istiʿāra** (L4.18): يَسْجُدَانِ, “bow down”, is a word borrowed for the way plants and trees follow what their nature does, as a person bows to the One who made him. Its full name in the book is al-istiʿāra al-taṣrīḥiyya al-tabaʿiyya. This lesson teaches only the main word. The second figure is called **tawhīm** (تَوْهِيم): a word that the rest of the speech makes you read in a wrong way, while another meaning is meant. Here the sun and the moon make you think of a star, and the plant is meant. This is al-Jadwal’s example, and Quran Learn teaches the name only through it.

The verb يَسْجُدَانِ ends in -āni. It is dual, and its doers are ٱلنَّجْمُ and ٱلشَّجَرُ. L1.08 used this verse for the dual verb.

<Exercise n={7} q="Parse وَيْلٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ لِّلْمُكَذِّبِينَ (Al-Mursalat 77:15). Name the mubtadaʾ, say why it may be indefinite, and say what the phrase لِّلْمُكَذِّبِينَ is tied to.">

The mubtadaʾ is **وَيْلٌ**, nominative with -un. It is indefinite. Al-Jadwal says it may start the sentence because the wording is a prayer (a duʿāʾ). L2.01 gave the usual case, a definite mubtadaʾ.

يَوْمَئِذٍ is a time word, “that day”. The phrase **لِّلْمُكَذِّبِينَ** is tied to the khabar, which is left out. The preposition لِ makes ٱلْمُكَذِّبِينَ genitive with -īna, the ending of a sound masculine plural. So the sense is “woe that day for the deniers”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Fill the table, place by place. Compare فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ لِلْيُسْرَىٰ (Al-Layl 92:7) with فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ لِلْعُسْرَىٰ (Al-Layl 92:10). Which places are the same? Which differ? What does al-Jadwal say about the last word?">

| Place | 92:7 | 92:10 |
|---|---|---|
| Start | فَ | فَ |
| Verb | سَنُيَسِّرُ | سَنُيَسِّرُ |
| Object | -hu | -hu |
| Phrase | لِلْيُسْرَىٰ | لِلْعُسْرَىٰ |

Three places are the same: the فَ, the verb with سَ and the object -hu. Only the last word differs.

Al-Jadwal’s ṣarf note calls ٱلْعُسْرَىٰ the opposite of ٱلْيُسْرَىٰ. It names no figure for the pair on its page.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Find the dual words and say what kind of word each is, and how it ends. (a) وَٱلنَّجْمُ وَٱلشَّجَرُ يَسْجُدَانِ (Ar-Rahman 55:6) (b) فَبِأَىِّ ءَالَآءِ رَبِّكُمَا تُكَذِّبَانِ (Ar-Rahman 55:13) (c) أَلَمْ نَجْعَل لَّهُۥ عَيْنَيْنِ (Al-Balad 90:8) (d) وَهَدَيْنَٰهُ ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ (Al-Balad 90:10)">

- (a) **يَسْجُدَانِ:** a dual verb, ending -āni. Its doers are ٱلنَّجْمُ and ٱلشَّجَرُ.
- (b) **رَبِّكُمَا:** a dual attached pronoun, -kumā, “you two”. **تُكَذِّبَانِ:** a dual verb, ending -āni.
- (c) **عَيْنَيْنِ:** a dual noun, ending -ayni.
- (d) **ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ:** a dual noun, ending -ayni.

A dual verb and a dual pronoun end in -āni and -kumā. A dual noun ends in -āni in the nominative and in -ayni in the other two cases (L1.08).
