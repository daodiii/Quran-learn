Read each verse from right to left. The English under each verse is our own plain meaning, not a translation.

Both verses start with a past verb of saying: **قَالَ**, “he said”, and **قَالُوا۟**, “they said”. The words after the verb are what was said. In 26:70 it is a question. In 26:71 it is the answer. The two verbs differ by one letter, the wāw of “they”. That wāw is the doer (L3.05).

A new قَالَ opens a new turn. Ibrahim speaks in 26:70 and his people answer in 26:71. The verb shows only “he” or “they”. The names come from the verses around it: 26:69 speaks of the news of Ibrahim, and 26:70 says whom he spoke to.

After قَالَ the sentence can be of any kind. This table shows four kinds from the passages. Al-Jadwal gives the same place to each of them: the place of an object (naṣb), because it is what was said.

| What is said | Verse | Kind of sentence |
|---|---|---|
| مَا تَعْبُدُونَ | 26:70 | A question |
| نَعْبُدُ أَصْنَامًا | 26:71 | A statement |
| ٱبْنُوا۟ لَهُۥ بُنْيَٰنًا | 37:97 | A command |
| يَٰبُنَىَّ إِنِّىٓ أَرَىٰ | 37:102 | A call, and what comes after it |

The verb of saying stays the same. The sentence after it changes. A sentence in this place has no case ending of its own. That is why al-Jadwal gives it a place, “in the place of naṣb”, and not a case.

This lesson does not sort the kinds of speech in a story. The Level 5 lesson on dialogue patterns, [L5.11](/learn/level-5/11-dialogue-patterns/), looks at that.

These are counts of verb words, made by script over the Corpus file. The 13 verbs of saying are 10 قَالَ (he said) and 3 قَالُوا۟ (they said).

In Ash-Shuʿarā there are more present verbs than past ones, because nearly all of the passage is speech. In As-Saffāt past verbs outnumber present ones, 28 to 16, and the story moves by acts: looking, turning away, striking, coming on. Present verbs are mostly inside speech: all 17 in Ash-Shuʿarā, and 14 of the 16 in As-Saffāt. The two others are يَزِفُّونَ in 37:94, a ḥāl, and نَجْزِى in 37:110, a comment on the story. Steps 3 and 4 come back to both.

The time words are few. إِذْ comes 4 times, إِذَا once and لَمَّا twice, and each has its own block.

Verse 69 opens the passage with a command (L3.08). **وَٱتْلُ** is a command of the root ت ل و, and its doer is a hidden “you”. **نَبَأَ** is the object, so it ends in -a, and it is the first word of an idafah (L2.08). **إِبْرَٰهِيمَ** is the second word, so it is genitive. It ends in -a because it is a diptote (L2.06).

Al-Jadwal reads the wāw as a new start. It adds that other books read it as “and”, joined to a sentence in verse 10 of the same surah, and it calls that reading a stretch. The sentence وَٱتْلُ … has no place of its own.

The next verse begins with إِذْ. The story is told as the news of a moment, and the moment is given in verse 70.

**إِذْ** means “when”. Al-Jadwal calls it a time word for the past, and a badal of نَبَأَ in verse 69. So the news of Ibrahim is given as a moment: “when he said to his father and his people”. The clause قَالَ … is added to إِذْ, as the first word of an idafah is added to the second (L2.08).

**قَالَ** is a hollow verb (L4.12). Its doer is a hidden “he”. The names of the two groups he spoke to come after it, tied to the verb: لِأَبِيهِ, “to his father”, and وَقَوْمِهِۦ, “and his people”.

What he said starts at **مَا**. Al-Jadwal reads it as a question noun: the object of تَعْبُدُونَ, put in front of its verb (L2.03). The whole question is the thing said, in the place of naṣb (L1.09).

It says the question asked only what they worship, so “idols” would have been enough, and the people added the second clause. It says they said “we stay” because they worshipped the idols by day and not by night. L4.17 and L4.18 do not teach iṭnāb, so take the name as al-Jadwal’s.</Note>

Verse 76 goes on with what Ibrahim said. Al-Jadwal reads **أَنتُمْ** as a repeat of the doer pronoun in تَعْبُدُونَ, a strengthening repeat (L4.06). **وَءَابَآؤُكُمُ** is joined to that pronoun and is nominative, -u. So the sentence says “what you worshipped, you and your fathers”.

From 37:88 to 37:94 the story runs on a chain of **seven past verbs**, each with فَ: فَنَظَرَ, فَقَالَ, فَتَوَلَّوْا۟, فَرَاغَ, فَقَالَ, فَرَاغَ and فَأَقْبَلُوٓا۟. Al-Jadwal reads each فَ as a joining فَ and each clause as having no place. The first is joined to a left-out clause, which it words as “his people said: come out with us”.

**فَنَظَرَ نَظْرَةً.** The noun نَظْرَةً means one look. Al-Jadwal calls it a **mafʿūl muṭlaq** (L4.07, the block on number), and it names its pattern: a noun for a single time. The phrase فِى ٱلنُّجُومِ is tied to the verb, with the sense “thought about”.

**فَقَالَ إِنِّى سَقِيمٌ.** The sentence إِنِّى سَقِيمٌ is what was said, in the place of naṣb (L1.09). Al-Jadwal names the figure of this speech: it is a hint (rumz, īmāʾ, taʿrīḍ), and it gives the account of the commentators, which this lesson does not repeat. L4.17 and L4.18 do not define these names.

**Turning away.** In فَتَوَلَّوْا۟ عَنْهُ مُدْبِرِينَ the verb is defective (L4.13): the wāw is the doer, and the ḍamma before it is hidden. Al-Jadwal calls مُدْبِرِينَ a **ḥāl muʾakkida**, a confirming ḥāl, as L4.01 does for مُصَدِّقًا in 2:91.

The call of verse 104 gives its words in verse 105. The first sentence, قَدْ صَدَّقْتَ ٱلرُّءْيَآ, is the answer of the call, and قَدْ puts certainty on a past verb (L4.06). Al-Jadwal reads the second sentence, إِنَّا كَذَٰلِكَ نَجْزِى ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ, as a new start that is still inside the call.

This matters for the tense. The verb **نَجْزِى** is present, and it is in the call, so it is in speech. Of the 16 present verbs in 37:83 to 37:113, 14 are inside speech. The two that are not are يَزِفُّونَ in 37:94, a ḥāl, and نَجْزِى in 37:110.

Verses 107 to 111 close the story, and al-Jadwal treats them together. It reads فَدَيْنَٰهُ as joined to the answer of لَمَّا, “written or left out”, and تَرَكْنَا in verse 108 as joined to فَدَيْنَٰهُ.

For the rest it sends the reader to its page on 37:79 to 37:81, where the same words come after the story of Nūḥ. There it reads **سَلَٰمٌ** as a mubtadaʾ that may be indefinite because it is a prayer (L2.02), with the phrase tied to its khabar. It reads the sentence as one put in between, a prayer, and gives two other readings. It reads the sentence with **إِنَّا … نَجْزِى** as a reason for what came before. Verse 110 has no إِنَّا, and this lesson reads its words the same way.

So verse 110 has a present verb that is outside the call of verses 104 and 105. It comments on the story. It is not one of its steps.

<Step id="finish" label="Mistakes and practice" title="Mistakes, practice and summary">
<Block id="m1" title="Mistake 1: reading the speaker from the verb">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="the same verb, two speakers (37:102)"
      items={[
        { ar: 'قَالَ يَٰبُنَىَّ', en: 'the father', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'قَالَ يَٰٓأَبَتِ', en: 'the son', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

It is tempting to learn “singular, plural, singular” and to read the number of قَالَ as the speaker. In 26:70 to 26:75 the verbs do go he, they, he, they, he. In 37:102 two verbs in a row are both قَالَ, and the speakers differ.

The verb gives the number of the speaker and nothing more. The speaker comes from the verses around: the people he is talking to, the god he turns to, the call يَٰٓأَبَتِ. The rule of thumb “a new قَالَ means a new turn” fits these passages. It is a Quran Learn teaching summary, not a rule from a grammar book.

</Block>

<Block id="m2" title="Mistake 2: reading the time from the form of the verb">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="the form and the time do not always agree"
      items={[
        { ar: 'يَسْمَعُونَكُمْ', en: '26:72: present form, past meaning', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'مَرِضْتُ', en: '26:80: past form, after إِذَا, looks ahead', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A present verb does not always mean the present, and a past verb does not always mean the past. In 26:72 al-Jadwal says the four present verbs are past in meaning. In 26:80 the past verb مَرِضْتُ comes after إِذَا and looks ahead, “when I am ill” (L4.04).

Read the time from the whole sentence. The form of the verb is a first clue, and the sentence has the last word.

</Block>

<Block id="m3" title="Mistake 3: calling every -īna word a ḥāl">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="two words that end in -īna after a verb"
      items={[
        { ar: 'عَٰكِفِينَ', en: '26:71: the khabar of ظَلَّ' },
        { ar: 'مُدْبِرِينَ', en: '37:90: a confirming ḥāl' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Both words are accusative, with -īna, and both follow a verb. They are not the same job. Use the test of L4.01: take the word out.

“We stay” without عَٰكِفِينَ is unfinished. The word is needed, so it is the khabar of a sister of kāna (L2.11). “They turned away from him” without مُدْبِرِينَ is a full sentence, so the word is a ḥāl. Al-Jadwal calls it a confirming ḥāl, because مُدْبِرِينَ says again what فَتَوَلَّوْا۟ says.

</Block>

<Block id="m4" title="Mistake 4: taking the next verb as the answer of لَمَّا">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="two when-clauses side by side"
      items={[
        { ar: 'فَلَمَّا بَلَغَ … قَالَ', en: '37:102: the answer is written', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'فَلَمَّآ أَسْلَمَا وَتَلَّهُۥ …', en: '37:103: the answer is left out', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

After لَمَّا the answer is often the next past verb, as قَالَ is in 37:102. In 37:103 that reading fails. The condition already has two verbs, أَسْلَمَا and تَلَّهُۥ, joined by وَ. Then نَٰدَيْنَٰهُ is joined to the condition too. Al-Jadwal says the answer is left out, and gives two ways to fill it.

Count the verbs joined by وَ before you pick the answer. In 37:103 they all belong to the condition.

<Block id="m5" title="Mistake 5: reading every فَ as “so”">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="one letter, four jobs in these passages"
      items={[
        { ar: 'فَنَظَرَ', en: 'joins (37:88)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'فَإِنَّهُمْ', en: 'a new start (26:77)', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'فَهُوَ', en: 'ties the answer (26:80)' },
        { ar: 'فَٱنظُرْ', en: 'joins a result to its cause (37:102)', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

In the Ibrahim passages the letter فَ is not one word with one meaning. Al-Jadwal reads it in these ways.

| The فَ | Verse | Al-Jadwal’s reading |
|---|---|---|
| فَنَظَلُّ | 26:71 | Joins a second clause to the first |
| فَإِنَّهُمْ | 26:77 | Starts a new sentence inside the speech |
| فَهُوَ | 26:80 | Ties the answer of a condition (L4.03) |
| فَنَظَرَ, فَقَالَ, فَرَاغَ | 37:88 to 37:94 | Joins one past verb to the one before |
| فَٱنظُرْ | 37:102 | Joins what follows to its cause |

Ask three questions. Is there a condition before it, with an answer that needs tying? Is it the start of a new sentence? Or does it join two clauses? The English “so” fits only some of them.

<Exercise n={3} q="Look at مَا تَعْبُدُونَ (Ash-Shuʿara 26:70, part). (a) Is it a verbal or a nominal sentence? (b) What is the mood of تَعْبُدُونَ, and how do you know? (c) Where does it stand in the verse, and what place does al-Jadwal give it? (d) How is it different from نَعْبُدُ أَصْنَامًا (Ash-Shuʿara 26:71, part)?">

- (a) **Verbal.** The verb تَعْبُدُونَ has its object مَا put in front of it (L2.03).
- (b) **Raised (rafʿ).** The nūn of -ūna stays, and nothing before the verb cuts it short (L3.06).
- (c) **After قَالَ.** It is what was said. Al-Jadwal gives the sentence the place of naṣb (L1.09).
- (d) **A question, then a statement.** 26:70 is a question with the object put first. 26:71 is a statement in the usual order: verb, then object. The verb is also “we” in 26:71 and “you all” in 26:70. Both sentences have the place of naṣb, because both are what was said.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Put إِذْ قَالَ لِأَبِيهِ وَقَوْمِهِۦ مَا تَعْبُدُونَ (Ash-Shuʿara 26:70) next to إِذْ قَالَ لِأَبِيهِ وَقَوْمِهِۦ مَاذَا تَعْبُدُونَ (As-Saffat 37:85). What is the same, what is different, and what does al-Jadwal say about each إِذْ?">

**The same.** The time word, the verb قَالَ with a hidden “he”, the two groups he spoke to, and the verb تَعْبُدُونَ. In both, the question is what was said, in the place of naṣb.

**Different.** The question word is مَا in 26:70 and مَاذَا in 37:85. The Corpus tags the first as a joining noun and the second as a question word, and al-Jadwal reads both as a question noun. A footnote on 37:85 also allows مَا as the question and ذَا as a joining noun.

**The time words.** In 26:70 al-Jadwal reads إِذْ as a badal of نَبَأَ in 26:69. In 37:85 it reads إِذْ as a badal of the first إِذْ, in verse 84. Verse 85 goes on with two more questions, and 26:70 is followed by the answer of the people.

- The passages are **Ash-Shuʿarā 26:69 to 82** (14 verses) and **As-Saffāt 37:83 to 37:113** (31 verses). They have 13 verbs of saying: 10 قَالَ and 3 قَالُوا۟.
- After a verb of saying, the sentence has the place of **naṣb**, what is said (L1.09). It can be a question, a statement, a command or a call.
- Of the present verbs, all 17 in Ash-Shuʿarā and 14 of the 16 in As-Saffāt are inside speech. A present verb can have a past meaning, as in 26:72.
- **إِذْ** names the moment, **إِذَا** opens a condition, and **لَمَّا** opens a when-clause. In 37:102 the answer is written. In 37:103 it is left out.
- The ḥāl appears as one word, a sentence with a verb, a sentence with wāw, and a phrase.
- Hollow verbs: قَالَ, رَاغَ. Defective: يَشْفِينِ, نَجْزِى, نَادَيْنَا. Assimilated: وَجَدْنَا, which takes two objects (26:74).
