<Step id="no-is" label="No “is”" title="Arabic leaves out “is”">
  <Stage label="The missing word">
    <Tiles
      label="هُوَ, then nothing, then ٱلْأَوَّلُ: He is the First"
      items={[
        { ar: 'هُوَ', en: 'he' },
        { ghost: 'is', note: 'English only' },
        { ar: 'ٱلْأَوَّلُ', en: 'the First' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

English needs a small word like *is*, *am* or *are*: “The sky <u>is</u> blue.” Arabic does not. It puts two words side by side.

A sentence like this is called a **nominal sentence**, جُمْلَة اِسْمِيَّة. *Ism* means noun, so it is a sentence that starts with a noun. No verb is needed.

  <Note kind="remember">You add “is” when you say it in English. There is no Arabic word for it.</Note>
</Step>

<Step id="parts" label="Two parts" title="Every nominal sentence has two parts">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Hadid 57:3 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'هُوَ', en: 'he', say: 'huwa' }] },
        { role: 'khabar', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلْأَوَّلُ', en: 'the First', say: 'al-awwalu' }] },
      ]}
      en={['He ', { gap: 'is' }, ' the First.']}
    />
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Baqarah 2:2 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'ذَٰلِكَ', en: 'that', say: 'dhalika' }] },
        { role: 'khabar', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلْكِتَٰبُ', en: 'the Book', say: 'al-kitabu' }] },
      ]}
      en={['That ', { gap: 'is' }, ' the Book.']}
    />
  </Stage>

- **Mubtadaʾ** مُبْتَدَأ: the starting point. What the sentence is about.
- **Khabar** خَبَر: the news. What the sentence says about it.

In the second sentence, *that* is the topic and *the Book* is the news about it.

  <Note kind="know">Many grammar books say **subject** and **predicate**. They mean roughly the same as mubtadaʾ and khabar.</Note>
</Step>

<Step id="rule" label="The rule" title="The rule: both end in -u">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Baqarah 2:218 (end)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'وَٱللَّهُ', en: 'and Allah', say: 'wa-llahu' }] },
        { role: 'khabar', words: [{ ar: 'غَفُورٌ', en: 'Forgiving', say: 'ghafurun' }, { ar: 'رَّحِيمٌ', en: 'Merciful', say: 'rahimun' }] },
      ]}
      en={['And Allah ', { gap: 'is' }, ' Forgiving, Merciful.']}
    />
  </Stage>

The mubtadaʾ and the khabar both take the **nominative** case, رَفْع. That means they end in **-u** (like بُ) or **-un** (like بٌ).

وَٱللَّهُ ends in **-u**. غَفُورٌ and رَّحِيمٌ end in **-un**. The khabar can be more than one word. Both words say something about Allah.

  <Note kind="know">Words like *huwa* (he) and *dhalika* (that) never show an ending. They are still the mubtadaʾ.</Note>

  <Note kind="warn">The mubtadaʾ is not the doer of a verb (the *fāʿil*). Both end in -u, but they belong to different sentence types. You will meet the fāʿil in L2.03.</Note>
</Step>

<Step id="more" label="A longer one" title="A longer one">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="An-Nur 24:35 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'ٱللَّهُ', en: 'Allah', say: 'allahu' }] },
        { role: 'khabar', words: [{ ar: 'نُورُ', en: 'light of', say: 'nuru' }, { ar: 'ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ', en: 'the heavens', say: 'as-samawati' }, { ar: 'وَٱلْأَرْضِ', en: 'and the earth', say: 'wa-l-ardi' }] },
      ]}
      en={['Allah ', { gap: 'is' }, ' the light of the heavens and the earth.']}
    />
  </Stage>

The mubtadaʾ is a single word here. The khabar is a group of words that belong together.

Look for the -u ending to find where each part starts. ٱللَّهُ is the topic. نُورُ begins the news, and the words after it finish the idea.

  <Note kind="tip">To find the end of the khabar, say the whole phrase out loud. Stop when the idea is finished.</Note>
</Step>

<Step id="first" label="Reversed" title="Sometimes the news comes first">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Kafirun 109:6"
      groups={[
        { role: 'khabar', words: [{ ar: 'لَكُمْ', en: 'for you', say: 'lakum', type: 'p' }] },
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'دِينُكُمْ', en: 'your religion', say: 'dinukum' }] },
      ]}
      en={['For you ', { gap: 'is' }, ' your religion.']}
    />
  </Stage>

The khabar can be a little phrase such as “for you”. Then it often comes **before** the mubtadaʾ.

Word for word: “For you <u>is</u> your religion.” The mubtadaʾ is still the word that ends in **-u**.

  <Note kind="tip">A phrase that starts with a little word like *li-* (for) is not the topic. The topic is the next word that ends in -u.</Note>
</Step>

- A nominal sentence starts with a noun and needs no verb.
- It has two parts: the **mubtadaʾ** (topic) and the **khabar** (news).
- Both end in **-u** or **-un**. Add “is” in English only.
