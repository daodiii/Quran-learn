Read it from right to left. The first word is خَلَقَ, “created”. It is a verb. After it comes the one who did it, ٱللَّهُ. After that comes what he made: the heavens and the earth.

Verb, then doer, then object. English says “Allah created the heavens”, with the doer first. Arabic says “Created Allah the heavens”.

<Note kind="warn">ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ ends in -i, but it is not genitive here. It is the object, so it is accusative. A plural like this one shows the accusative with a kasra. L2.05 explains.</Note>

<Block id="name" title="Why it is called a verbal sentence" level={2}>
  <Stage label="The first word decides">

      label="خَلَقَ, then ٱللَّهُ: a verb first"
      items={[
        { ar: 'خَلَقَ', en: 'created', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ٱللَّهُ', en: 'Allah' },
      ]}

A sentence that starts with a verb is a **verbal sentence**, جُمْلَة فِعْلِيَّة (*jumla fiʿliyya*). *Fiʿl* means verb. In L1.09 you learned to sort sentences by their first word. In L2.01 you met the other kind, the nominal sentence, which starts with a noun.

The test is simple. Look at the first word. A verb means a verbal sentence. A noun means a nominal sentence.

<Note kind="know">A verbal sentence is often about something that happens: “created”, “came”, “said”. That is only a way to remember it. The first word is what decides the grammar.</Note>
</Block>

        { role: 'tied', hint: 'goes with “disbelief”', words: [{ ar: 'مِنْهُمُ', en: 'from them', say: 'minhumu', type: 'p' }] },
        { role: 'maful', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلْكُفْرَ', en: 'the disbelief', say: 'al-kufra' }] },
      ]}
      en={['Jesus sensed disbelief from them.']}
    />
  </Stage>

A verbal sentence has up to three parts.

- **Fiʿl**, فِعْل: the verb. Here أَحَسَّ, “sensed”.
- **Fāʿil**, فَاعِل: “the doer”. It is the one who does the verb. Here عِيسَىٰ.
- **Mafʿūl bihi**, مَفْعُول بِهِ: “the one the verb is done to”. We say **object**. Here ٱلْكُفْرَ, “the disbelief”.

The little phrase مِنْهُمُ, “from them”, belongs to the sentence but is not one of the three parts.

The doer عِيسَىٰ ends in a long ā (ى), so its -u is hidden. You met this in L2.01 with مَوْلَىٰ. The word is still nominative.

The cases are the ones you know from L1.10.

- The **fāʿil** is **nominative** (*rafʿ*): -u or -un. Here أَنفُسُ ends in -u.
- The **mafʿūl bihi** is **accusative** (*naṣb*): -a or -an. Here أَمْرًا ends in -an.
- The **verb** has no case. Case belongs to nouns.

Notice that the doer is nominative, just like the mubtadaʾ in L2.01. The ending says “this is a main part of the sentence”. It does not say which kind of sentence it is. The word order does.

<Note kind="know">A present-tense verb does have an ending that changes, but the grammar books call it a **mood**, not a case. L3.06 and L3.07 teach the moods.</Note>

</Block>

  <Stage label="Arabic against English">

English puts the doer first: “Allah created the heavens”. In the usual Arabic order the verb comes first, then the doer, then the object. The grammar books give the order as verb, doer, object (V-S-O in English books).

English can do this too, for effect: “Came the dawn.” In Arabic it is the normal way to tell what happened.

Two things follow.

- The doer comes **after** its verb. If a noun comes before the verb, the sentence starts with a noun and is nominal (see Mistake 1 below).
- The object comes after the doer. Sometimes it jumps to the front for a reason. You will see this in the next step.

<Note kind="tip">Read a verbal sentence in this order: verb, then who did it, then what was done to. Find the doer by its -u. Find the object by its -a.</Note>
</Block>

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah*, the short grammar book you met in L2.01, defines the two new parts in a line each.

- “The fāʿil is the nominative noun whose verb is mentioned before it.” So the verb comes first, and the doer is a nominative noun.
- “It is of two kinds: written (ẓāhir) and pronoun (muḍmar).” The next step shows both, and splits the pronoun into two types.
- “The mafʿūl bihi is the accusative noun that the verb falls on.”

The book’s own examples are قَامَ زَيْدٌ, “Zayd stood up”, and ضَرَبْتُ زَيْدًا, “I hit Zayd”.
</Block>

</Step>

The simplest doer is a noun written after the verb. Grammar books call it **ẓāhir**, ظَاهِر, “visible”. Here the doer is ٱلْحَوَارِيُّونَ, “the disciples”.

It is nominative, but it does not end in -u. It ends in -ūna, with the letter و. A plural like this one shows the nominative with a letter instead of a ḍamma. Al-Jadwal says the mark is the wāw. L2.04 teaches this plural.

This sentence has no object. The words the disciples said come next in the verse. A verb that does not need an object is fine.
</Block>

  <Stage label="Look at the verb">

The disciples are many, but the verb is قَالَ, “he said”. It is singular. In Arabic a verb that comes **before** its written doer stays singular, even when the doer is plural. The Arabic says “said the disciples”.

The plural verb قَالُوا, “they said”, has the doer inside it as an ending. You do not add a written doer to it. The next blocks show this.

<Note kind="know">This is the usual pattern, and 3:52 shows it. Al-Jadwal analyses this verse word by word but does not state the rule. The verb endings are taught in L3.05.</Note>

سَيَّارَة, “a caravan”, and ٱمْرَأَة, “a woman”, are feminine words. Their verbs end in **-t**: جَآءَتْ and قَالَتِ. Al-Jadwal names this ending in 3:35: “the tāʾ is for the feminine”. It says the same for سَوَّلَتْ in 12:18, which the entry for 12:19 points to.

So a verb before its doer usually matches the doer in gender, and it stays singular. A feminine doer gives a verb with -t. A masculine doer gives a verb without it, as in خَلَقَ ٱللَّهُ.

The doer ٱمْرَأَتُ is the first word of an idafah (L2.08). The second word, عِمْرَٰنَ, is genitive, but it ends in a fatḥa, because the name Imran is a word that does not take -in. Al-Jadwal marks it this way.

<Note kind="know">The -t is a past-tense ending. L3.05 teaches how the past tense changes for every doer.</Note>

Often the doer is a short pronoun joined to the end of the verb. It is called an **attached pronoun**, ضَمِير مُتَّصِل (*ḍamīr muttaṣil*). The attached pronoun **is** the doer, so the sentence needs no other doer.

- In نَذَرْتُ, the ending **-tu** means “I”. Al-Jadwal: “the tāʾ is the doer”.
- In أَقِيمُوا۟, the ending **-ū** (و) means “you all”. Al-Jadwal: “the wāw is the doer”.
- In ءَامَنَّا (3:52), the ending **-nā** means “we”. Al-Jadwal: “nā is the doer”.

So one word can be a whole verbal sentence: verb plus doer. Add an object, and you have all three parts.

<Note kind="know">The endings that change the verb for “I”, “we” and “you all” are taught in L3.05 (past tense) and L3.01 (subject pronouns). The command أَقِيمُوا۟ is L3.08. For now, spot the ending and name it as the doer.</Note>

Compare this verse with 29:44 at the start of the lesson. The words are almost the same. In 29:44 the doer ٱللَّهُ is written. Here it is not.

The doer is still there. It is a hidden “he” inside the verb. Grammar books call it a **hidden pronoun**, ضَمِير مُسْتَتِر (*ḍamīr mustatir*). Al-Jadwal: “the doer is a hidden ‘he’”. The listener knows who “he” is from the verses before, which speak about Allah.

A hidden doer shows no ending and no mark. It is the doer of a verb like خَلَقَ, “he created”. In a command such as تَقَبَّلْ, “accept”, the hidden doer is “you” (3:35, al-Jadwal).

  <Stage label="Doers in this lesson">
    <Formula

| Kind of doer | Where it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Written, ظَاهِر | A noun after the verb | خَلَقَ **ٱللَّهُ** (29:44) |
| Attached, ضَمِير مُتَّصِل | An ending joined to the verb | نَذَرْ**تُ** (3:35), أَقِيمُ**وا** (2:43) |
| Hidden, ضَمِير مُسْتَتِر | Inside the verb, with no letter | **خَلَقَ** ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ (16:3) |

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* splits the doers into two kinds, written and pronoun. The pronoun kind is attached or hidden.

One verb has **one** doer. When a written doer follows the verb, there is no second, hidden “he” as well. Al-Jadwal parses خَلَقَ ٱللَّهُ with ٱللَّهُ alone as the doer, and خَلَقَ with no noun after it with a hidden “he”.

<Note kind="remember">Every verb has a doer. It is written, attached or hidden. Never look for it before the verb.</Note>
</Block>

        { role: 'fiil', label: 'fiʿl + fāʿil', hint: 'the ending -ū is the doer: “they”', words: [{ ar: 'فَأَرْسَلُوا۟', en: 'so they sent', say: 'fa-arsalu', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'maful', hint: 'the object: -a', words: [{ ar: 'وَارِدَهُمْ', en: 'their water-drawer', say: 'waridahum' }] },

      en={['So they sent their water-drawer.']}

  </Stage>

The **mafʿūl bihi** is the thing or person the verb is done to. Here the caravan sent someone: وَارِدَ, “the water-drawer”. It ends in a fatḥa, as an accusative should.

The ending هُمْ, “their”, belongs to وَارِدَ. Al-Jadwal says it is the second word of an idafah, a genitive pronoun. The object is the whole word وَارِدَهُمْ.

<Block id="no-object" title="Some verbs have no object">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Yusuf 12:19 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'وَجَآءَتْ', en: 'and came', say: 'wa-jaat', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'fail', words: [{ ar: 'سَيَّارَةٌ', en: 'a caravan', say: 'sayyaratun' }] },
      ]}
      en={['And a caravan came.']}
    />
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Yusuf 12:19 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', label: 'fiʿl + fāʿil', hint: 'hidden doer: “he”', words: [{ ar: 'فَأَدْلَىٰ', en: 'and he let down', say: 'fa-adla', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'maful', words: [{ ar: 'دَلْوَهُۥ', en: 'his bucket', say: 'dalwahu' }] },
      ]}
      en={['And he let down his bucket.']}
    />
  </Stage>

Compare two verbs from the same verse.

- جَآءَتْ, “came”, has a doer and **no object**. Nothing is “came” to.
- أَدْلَىٰ, “let down”, has a hidden doer and an object: the bucket.

A verb that needs an object is called **transitive**, مُتَعَدٍّ (*mutaʿaddī*). A verb that does not is **intransitive**, لَازِم (*lāzim*). Every verbal sentence has a verb and a doer. An object appears only when the verb needs one.

<Note kind="tip">In English you often add a word: “came **to** him”. In Arabic a verb like جَآءَ may take its object straight after it, as in جَآءُوٓ أَبَاهُمْ in the practice below.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="two-objects" title="Some verbs take two objects">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Hijr 15:87 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', label: 'fiʿl + fāʿil + object', hint: '-nā: “we” is the doer, -ka: “you” is the first object', words: [{ ar: 'ءَاتَيْنَٰكَ', en: 'we gave you', say: 'ataynaka', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'maful', label: 'mafʿūl bihi thānin', hint: 'the second object: -an', words: [{ ar: 'سَبْعًا', en: 'seven', say: 'sab-an' }] },
      ]}
      en={['We gave you seven …']}
    />
  </Stage>

The verb “give” has two things in it: the one who receives, and the thing given. Arabic gives both the accusative.

In ءَاتَيْنَٰكَ there are three parts in one word.

- **-nā**, “we”: the doer. Al-Jadwal: “nā is the doer”.
- **-ka**, “you”: the first object.
- **سَبْعًا**, “seven”: the second object (*mafʿūl bihi thānin*). It ends in -an.

<Note kind="warn">The attached **-ka** is the object, not the doer. A pronoun on the end of a verb can be the doer (-tu, -ū, -nā) or the object (-ka, -hum, -hu). L3.02 teaches the object endings.</Note>

</Block>

<Block id="object-first" title="Sometimes the object comes first">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Baqarah 2:124 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'ٱبْتَلَىٰٓ', en: 'tested', say: 'ibtala', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'maful', hint: 'the object, put first', label: 'mafʿūl bihi muqaddam', words: [{ ar: 'إِبْرَٰهِۦمَ', en: 'Ibrahim', say: 'ibrahima' }] },
        { role: 'fail', hint: 'the doer: -u, with “his” pointing back', words: [{ ar: 'رَبُّهُۥ', en: 'his Lord', say: 'rabbuhu' }] },
        { role: 'tied', hint: 'goes with the verb', words: [{ ar: 'بِكَلِمَٰتٍ', en: 'with words', say: 'bi-kalimatin', type: 'p' }] },
      ]}
      en={['His Lord tested Ibrahim with words.']}
    />
  </Stage>

The usual order is verb, doer, object. Here the order is verb, **object**, doer. Who tested whom? The endings tell you.

- رَبُّ ends in -u: it is the doer.
- إِبْرَٰهِۦمَ ends in -a: it is the object.

So “his Lord tested Ibrahim”, not “Ibrahim tested his Lord”. Look at the ending هُ in رَبُّهُ, “his”. It points back to Ibrahim, the word that came first.

Al-Jadwal notes this verse on purpose. Its note says: **the doer cannot move in front of its verb. The object can, in some cases.** If the doer did move in front, it would no longer be a doer. It would become a mubtadaʾ, and a hidden “he” would be the doer (see Mistake 1).

</Block>

</Step>

<Step id="mistakes" label="Mistakes" title="Verbal or nominal, and six mistakes">
<Block id="compare" title="Same words, two kinds of sentence">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Yunus 10:34 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'ٱللَّهُ', en: 'Allah', say: 'allahu' }] },
        { role: 'khabar', hint: 'a verb with its hidden doer', words: [{ ar: 'يَبْدَؤُا۟', en: 'he begins', say: 'yabdau', type: 'v' }, { ar: 'ٱلْخَلْقَ', en: 'the creation', say: 'al-khalqa' }] },
      ]}
      en={['Allah starts the creation.']}
    />
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Ankabut 29:19 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'يُبْدِئُ', en: 'begins', say: 'yubdiu', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'fail', words: [{ ar: 'ٱللَّهُ', en: 'Allah', say: 'allahu' }] },
        { role: 'maful', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلْخَلْقَ', en: 'the creation', say: 'al-khalqa' }] },
      ]}
      en={['Allah starts the creation.']}
    />
  </Stage>

Both verses say that Allah starts the creation. The verbs are two forms of the same idea, يَبْدَأُ and يُبْدِئُ.

- **10:34** starts with ٱللَّهُ, a noun. It is a **nominal sentence**. ٱللَّهُ is the mubtadaʾ. The khabar is a whole verbal sentence: يَبْدَؤُا۟ ٱلْخَلْقَ, with a hidden “he” as its doer. You saw this kind of khabar in L2.01.
- **29:19** starts with the verb يُبْدِئُ. It is a **verbal sentence**. ٱللَّهُ is the doer.

In both, ٱللَّهُ ends in -u and ٱلْخَلْقَ ends in -a. The ending does not show the kind of sentence. The first word does.

| What to look at | Nominal sentence | Verbal sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Starts with | A noun or pronoun | A verb |
| Main parts | Mubtadaʾ and khabar | Verb, doer, and sometimes an object |
| The noun ٱللَّهُ is called | Mubtadaʾ | Fāʿil |
| Time | None of its own: the verses around it tell you | Shown by the verb: past, present or command |

<Note kind="know">The present-tense verbs here end in -u. L3.06 teaches this. Grammar books and Quran teachers say that the word order can change what a sentence stresses. L4.17 introduces how.</Note>

<Check n={11} q="Is ٱللَّهُ يَبْدَؤُا۟ ٱلْخَلْقَ nominal or verbal?" options={['Nominal: it starts with a noun', 'Verbal: it contains a verb']} answer={0} why="The first word is a noun, ٱللَّهُ. The verb is inside the khabar. Only the first word decides." />
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-first" title="Mistake 1: calling a sentence verbal because it has a verb">
  <Stage label="Made-up examples">
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Made-up example"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'كَتَبَ', en: 'wrote', say: 'kataba', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'fail', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلطَّالِبُ', en: 'the student', say: 'at-talibu' }] },
        { role: 'maful', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلدَّرْسَ', en: 'the lesson', say: 'ad-darsa' }] },
      ]}
      en={['The student wrote the lesson.']}
    />
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Made-up example"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلطَّالِبُ', en: 'the student', say: 'at-talibu' }] },
        { role: 'khabar', hint: 'a verb with its hidden doer', words: [{ ar: 'كَتَبَ', en: 'he wrote', say: 'kataba', type: 'v' }, { ar: 'ٱلدَّرْسَ', en: 'the lesson', say: 'ad-darsa' }] },
      ]}
      en={['The student: he wrote the lesson.']}
    />
  </Stage>

The two lines have the same three words. Only the order differs.

- **First line:** starts with the verb. Verbal. ٱلطَّالِبُ is the **doer**.
- **Second line:** starts with a noun. Nominal. ٱلطَّالِبُ is the **mubtadaʾ**. The doer of كَتَبَ is now a hidden “he”.

The rule is the first word, not “is there a verb in it”. A sentence can contain many verbs and still be nominal.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-faail" title="Mistake 2: mixing up the doer and the mubtadaʾ">
  <Stage label="Same word, two jobs">
    <Tiles
      label="خَتَمَ ٱللَّهُ with the doer after the verb"
      items={[
        { ar: 'خَتَمَ', en: 'sealed', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ٱللَّهُ', en: 'Allah: the doer' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

You met this in L2.01 with the verses 2:15 and 2:7. In 2:7 ٱللَّهُ comes after خَتَمَ and is the doer. In 2:15 it comes before the verb and is the mubtadaʾ.

Both words are nominative, so the ending cannot tell them apart. The position does.

- A nominative noun **after** a verb is its doer.
- A nominative noun **at the start**, before the verb, is a mubtadaʾ.

Al-Jadwal says this in its note on 2:124: a doer cannot come before its verb. If a noun does come first, it is a mubtadaʾ, and the doer is a hidden pronoun.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-hidden" title="Mistake 3: forgetting the hidden doer">
  <Stage label="Nothing is missing">
    <Tiles
      label="قَالَ with a hidden doer"
      items={[
        { ar: 'قَالَ', en: 'said', type: 'v' },
        { ghost: 'he', note: 'inside the verb' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A learner sees خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ in 16:3 and asks: “Where is the doer? The sentence is not finished.” It is finished. The doer is the hidden “he”.

Many verbal sentences have no written doer. When you cannot find one after the verb, look for an attached ending. If there is none, the doer is hidden.

Do not add an English subject to your analysis. The hidden “he” belongs to the verb. The verse before tells you who it is.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-english" title="Mistake 4: reading in English order">
  <Stage label="Word by word, then in English">
    <Tiles
      label="سَوَّلَتْ لَكُمْ أَنفُسُكُمْ أَمْرًا"
      items={[
        { ar: 'سَوَّلَتْ', en: 'made look good', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'لَكُمْ', en: 'to you', type: 'p' },
        { ar: 'أَنفُسُكُمْ', en: 'your selves' },
        { ar: 'أَمْرًا', en: 'a matter' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

An English reader looks for the doer before the verb. In 12:18 there is nothing before سَوَّلَتْ that could be a doer. The doer comes after the verb, and a little phrase, لَكُمْ, even stands between them.

Read the verb first, then look to the right for a nominative noun. Do not stop at the little phrase. The phrase belongs to the verb. It is not the doer.

Say the Arabic order first, then turn it into good English at the end: “Your own selves made a matter look good to you.”
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-plural" title="Mistake 5: making the verb plural for a plural doer">
  <Stage label="Written doer: singular verb">
    <Tiles
      label="قَالَ ٱلْحَوَارِيُّونَ"
      items={[
        { ar: 'قَالَ', en: 'said', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ٱلْحَوَارِيُّونَ', en: 'the disciples' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

English learners often feel that a plural doer needs a plural verb. In Arabic, when the verb comes first and the doer is written, the verb stays singular: قَالَ ٱلْحَوَارِيُّونَ, not قَالُوا ٱلْحَوَارِيُّونَ.

The plural verb is for a doer that is **inside** the verb: قَالُوا, “they said”, with no written doer.

</Block>

<Block id="mistake-participle" title="Mistake 6: taking a word from a verb for a verb">
  <Stage label="Made-up examples">
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Made-up example"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'خَلَقَ', en: 'created', say: 'khalaqa', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'fail', words: [{ ar: 'ٱللَّهُ', en: 'Allah', say: 'allahu' }] },
      ]}
      en={['Allah created.']}
    />
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Made-up example"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'ٱللَّهُ', en: 'Allah', say: 'allahu' }] },

خَلَقَ and خَالِق both come from the root خ ل ق. But خَلَقَ is a verb, and خَالِق is a noun, “creator”. A noun like this is called **ism fāʿil**, اِسْم فَاعِل, “name of the doer”. L3.19 teaches it.

- خَلَقَ ٱللَّهُ is **verbal**. Allah is the doer.
- ٱللَّهُ خَالِقٌ is **nominal**. Allah is the mubtadaʾ and خَالِقٌ is the khabar, ending in -un.

Do not mix up the two names. A **fāʿil** is a role in a verbal sentence. An **ism fāʿil** is a kind of noun. Only a real verb makes a sentence verbal.

The verb is first, but the object comes before the doer. The cards show how to keep them apart: by the ending, not the position. Al-Jadwal marks the object as “put first”.

<Exercise n={1} q="Verbal or nominal? Name the first word each time. (a) خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ (An-Nahl 16:3) (b) ٱللَّهُ يَبْدَؤُا۟ ٱلْخَلْقَ (Yunus 10:34) (c) يُبْدِئُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْخَلْقَ (Al-Ankabut 29:19). (d) ٱللَّهُ خَالِقٌ is a made-up example.">

- (a) **Verbal.** The first word is the verb خَلَقَ. Its doer is a hidden “he”, and ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ is the object.
- (b) **Nominal.** The first word is the noun ٱللَّهُ, the mubtadaʾ. The khabar is the verbal sentence يَبْدَؤُا۟ ٱلْخَلْقَ.
- (c) **Verbal.** The first word is the verb يُبْدِئُ. ٱللَّهُ is the doer and ٱلْخَلْقَ is the object.
- (d) **Nominal.** The first word is a noun, and خَالِقٌ is a noun too (an ism fāʿil), not a verb.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Find the verb, the doer and the object: وَجَآءُوٓ أَبَاهُمْ عِشَآءً يَبْكُونَ (Yusuf 12:16). Meaning: “And they came to their father in the evening, weeping.”">

- جَآءُوٓ is the **verb**, “they came”. The ending -ū (و) is the **doer**, an attached pronoun. Al-Jadwal says the verb “has its doer”.
- أَبَاهُمْ, “their father”, is the **object**: accusative. The pronoun هُمْ, “their”, belongs to it. The Arabic puts the object straight after the verb, where English says “to”.
- عِشَآءً, “in the evening”, is a time word that belongs to the verb.
- يَبْكُونَ, “weeping”, is a describing sentence. L4.01 teaches this kind of phrase (ḥāl).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Is the doer written, attached or hidden? (a) نَذَرْتُ لَكَ (Al-Imran 3:35) (b) فَأَرْسَلُوا۟ وَارِدَهُمْ (Yusuf 12:19) (c) خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ (An-Nahl 16:3) (d) وَجَآءَتْ سَيَّارَةٌ (Yusuf 12:19)">

- (a) **Attached.** The ending -tu is the doer: “I”.
- (b) **Attached.** The ending -ū (و) is the doer: “they”.
- (c) **Hidden.** No noun and no ending: a hidden “he”.
- (d) **Written.** سَيَّارَةٌ is a noun after the verb, ending in -un.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Who did the testing, and how do you know? ٱبْتَلَىٰٓ إِبْرَٰهِۦمَ رَبُّهُۥ (Al-Baqarah 2:124)">

His Lord tested Ibrahim.

The verb is ٱبْتَلَىٰٓ. رَبُّ ends in -u, so it is the **doer**. إِبْرَٰهِۦمَ ends in -a, so it is the **object**, put in front of the doer. The ending هُ in رَبُّهُ, “his”, points back to Ibrahim.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Build a verbal sentence from these three words, in the usual order, and say the case of each: كَتَبَ (wrote), ٱلطَّالِبُ (the student), ٱلدَّرْسَ (the lesson). Then make the doer feminine with ٱلطَّالِبَةُ (the female student). These are made-up examples.">

كَتَبَ ٱلطَّالِبُ ٱلدَّرْسَ, “The student wrote the lesson.”

- كَتَبَ: the **verb**. No case.
- ٱلطَّالِبُ: the **doer**, nominative, -u.
- ٱلدَّرْسَ: the **object**, accusative, -a.

With a feminine doer the verb takes -t: كَتَبَتِ ٱلطَّالِبَةُ ٱلدَّرْسَ, “The female student wrote the lesson.” The verb stays singular and agrees in gender. L3.05 teaches the past endings.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Why is the verb in قَالَ ٱلْحَوَارِيُّونَ (Al-Imran 3:52) singular, and why does جَآءَتْ (Yusuf 12:19) end in -t?">

قَالَ is singular because it comes before its written doer. A verb in that place stays singular, even with a plural doer.

جَآءَتْ ends in -t because its doer, سَيَّارَةٌ, “a caravan”, is feminine. The verb matches the doer in gender.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Do the full analysis of two made-up sentences: (a) نَصَرَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ (Allah helped the believers) and (b) أَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْقُرْآنَ (Allah sent down the Quran).">

- (a) **نَصَرَ**: the verb. **ٱللَّهُ**: the doer, nominative, -u. **ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ**: the object, accusative. A sound plural like this shows the accusative with the letter ي, in -īna. L2.05 teaches this.
- (b) **أَنزَلَ**: the verb. **ٱللَّهُ**: the doer, nominative, -u. **ٱلْقُرْآنَ**: the object, accusative, -a.

In both, the doer is written, so the verb has no hidden “he”. Each starts with the verb, so each is verbal.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="These two made-up sentences both mean “Allah knows”: (a) ٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ and (b) يَعْلَمُ ٱللَّهُ. Which is nominal and which is verbal? What is ٱللَّهُ in each?">

- (a) **Nominal.** It starts with the noun ٱللَّهُ, the mubtadaʾ. The khabar is the verbal sentence يَعْلَمُ, with a hidden “he” as its doer.
- (b) **Verbal.** It starts with the verb يَعْلَمُ. ٱللَّهُ is the doer.

The grammar is different, and the English can be the same. Do not say that one is about a permanent state and the other about an event. That is not a rule. The difference is what comes first: L4.17 looks at how this changes the stress.

</Exercise>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="verb plus doer plus object equals a verbal sentence"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'fiʿl', small: 'the verb' },
        { kind: 'm', text: 'fāʿil', small: 'the doer' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'mafʿūl bihi', small: 'the object' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'verbal sentence', small: 'جُمْلَة فِعْلِيَّة' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A verbal sentence starts with a verb. The first word decides: a noun first makes it nominal, even if a verb follows.
- The usual order is **verb, doer, object**. The object may come before the doer; the endings show which is which.
- The **doer** (fāʿil) is nominative. The **object** (mafʿūl bihi) is accusative. The verb has no case.
- The doer is **written** (a noun after the verb), **attached** (an ending such as -tu, -nā, -ū) or **hidden** (a “he” inside the verb). A verb has one doer.
- A verb before a written doer stays singular, and matches the doer in gender.
- Some verbs have no object, some take one, and some take two.

**Next:** L2.04 teaches the nominative case, which the doer takes. L2.05 teaches the accusative case, which the object takes. The verbs themselves are taught in Level 3: past tense (L3.05), present tense (L3.06), commands (L3.08) and the passive voice (L3.09), where the doer is left out. Before you go on, review L2.01 if the difference between the mubtadaʾ and the doer felt new.
