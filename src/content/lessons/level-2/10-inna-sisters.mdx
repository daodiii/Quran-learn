Two verses, almost the same words. The first is a plain nominal sentence from L2.01: ٱللَّهُ ends in **-u**, and غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ end in **-un**.

In the second, a little word, إِنَّ (“indeed”), stands in front. Now ٱللَّهَ ends in **-a**. The other two words did not change.

<Block id="name" title="What inna does, and how the first grammar book says it" level={2}>
  <Stage label="From al-Ajurrumiyyah">
    <blockquote class="lx-quote lx-quote-list" lang="ar" dir="rtl">
      <p>وَأَمَّا إِنَّ وَأَخَوَاتُهَا فَإِنَّهَا تَنْصِبُ الِاسْمَ وَتَرْفَعُ الْخَبَرَ</p>
      <p>وَهِيَ إِنَّ وَأَنَّ وَلَكِنَّ وَكَأَنَّ وَلَيْتَ وَلَعَلَّ</p>
      <p>تَقُولُ: إِنَّ زَيْدًا قَائِمٌ</p>
    </blockquote>
    <p class="lx-quote-note">Vowel marks added by Quran Learn.</p>
  </Stage>

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* says: “As for inna and her sisters, they put the noun in the accusative (*naṣb*) and the khabar in the nominative (*rafʿ*).” Then it lists the six, and gives an example: “Indeed Zayd is standing.”

So the words after inna get new names:

- **Ism inna**, اِسْم إِنَّ: the first part. It was the mubtadaʾ. It now takes **-a**.
- **Khabar inna**, خَبَر إِنَّ: the news. It was the khabar. It **stays -u**.

Do not say “mubtadaʾ” after inna. The old name goes, and the new one comes.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal calls these words *ḥarf mushabbah bi-l-fiʿl*, “a little word that looks like a verb”, and *nāsikh*, “one that overrides”. They override the usual -u of the mubtadaʾ. In L2.01 you saw that the khabar of a plain nominal sentence does not copy the mubtadaʾ. Here the khabar again keeps its own -u.</Note>

</Block>

<Block id="sisters" title="The six words and what they mean" level={2}>

All six do the same job to the endings: the ism takes -a, the khabar keeps -u. They differ in meaning. *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* names each meaning:

| Word | Its meaning in the book | In plain English | You will see it in |
|---|---|---|---|
| إِنَّ | تَوْكِيد (*tawkīd*) | Emphasis: “indeed” | 2:173 |
| أَنَّ | تَوْكِيد | Emphasis, joined to what came before: “that” | 2:235 |
| لَٰكِنَّ | اِسْتِدْرَاك (*istidrāk*) | Correcting what came before: “but” | 2:243 |
| كَأَنَّ | تَشْبِيه (*tashbīh*) | A likeness: “as if” | 2:101 |
| لَيْتَ | تَمَنٍّ (*tamannī*) | A wish: “would that” | 6:27 |
| لَعَلَّ | تَرَجٍّ وَتَوَقُّع (*tarajjī*, *tawaqquʿ*) | Hope and expectation: “perhaps” | 3:200 |

The book spells the fourth word with a plain kasra, لَكِنَّ. The Quran writes it لَٰكِنَّ, with a small alif above.

</Block>

The ism is accusative, so it takes the marks from L2.05: -a or -an on a single word, -īna on a masculine sound plural, -ayni on a dual, a kasra on a feminine sound plural.

| Before inna (-u) | After inna | What shows the case |
|---|---|---|
| ٱلْكِتَابُ | ٱلْكِتَابَ | -a |
| كِتَابٌ | كِتَابًا | -an |
| ٱلْمُسْلِمُونَ | ٱلْمُسْلِمِينَ | -īna |
| ٱلْمُسْلِمَانِ | ٱلْمُسْلِمَيْنِ | -ayni |
| ٱلْمُسْلِمَاتُ | ٱلْمُسْلِمَاتِ | a kasra |

These are made-up examples: the words are real, but they are not from a verse.

Some words show nothing. ٱلَّذِينَ (“those who”) never changes its shape. Al-Jadwal says it is the ism of inna and “in the place of the accusative”, in the same way that هُوَ was “in the place of the nominative” in L2.01.

أَنَّ is inna's twin. The ism takes -a (ٱللَّهَ) and the khabar keeps its own ending. The khabar here is a whole verbal sentence: يَعْلَمُ مَا فِىٓ أَنفُسِكُمْ.

The difference is use. **Inna** usually starts a new sentence: “Indeed, …”. **Anna** joins a sentence to what came before: “know **that** …”.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal says anna with its ism and its khabar together stand in the place of the two things that “know” needs. In plain words: the whole group “that Allah knows what is in yourselves” is what you are told to know.</Note>

</Block>

  <Stage>

        { role: 'khabarInna', label: 'khabar lākinna', hint: 'a verb with its doer', words: [{ ar: 'لَا', en: 'not', say: 'la', type: 'p' }, { ar: 'يَشْكُرُونَ', en: 'give thanks', say: 'yashkurun', type: 'v' }] },
      ]}
      en={['But most people do not give thanks.']}
    />

The verse first says that Allah is full of favour to people. Then لَٰكِنَّ adds a correction: “**but** most people do not give thanks.” This is *istidrāk*: it fixes the picture the first half gave.

The ism is أَكْثَرَ (“most of”), with **-a**. It is the first word of an idafah (L2.08), so ٱلنَّاسِ after it is genitive. The khabar is a verbal sentence, لَا يَشْكُرُونَ.

<Note kind="warn">There is a second, light word, لَٰكِنْ, with a sukūn on the last letter. It does nothing to the endings. Step 4 shows it.</Note>

</Block>

    <Sentence

        { role: 'khabarInna', label: 'khabar kaʾanna', hint: 'a verb with its doer', words: [{ ar: 'لَا', en: 'not', say: 'la', type: 'p' }, { ar: 'يَعْلَمُونَ', en: 'know', say: 'yalamun', type: 'v' }] },
      ]}
      en={['… as if they did not know.']}
    />
  </Stage>

كَأَنَّ makes a likeness (*tashbīh*): “as if”. The verse says some people threw away the Book “behind their backs”, **as if they did not know**.

Here the ism is not a separate word. It is the short pronoun **-hum** joined to the end of كَأَنَّ. It counts as accusative, even though a pronoun cannot show it. The next step comes back to this.

The khabar is the verbal sentence لَا يَعْلَمُونَ.

</Block>

  <Stage>
    <Sentence

        { role: 'khabarInna', label: 'khabar layta', hint: 'a verbal sentence', words: [{ ar: 'نُرَدُّ', en: 'were sent back', say: 'nuraddu', type: 'v' }] },
      ]}
      en={['Oh, would that we were sent back!']}

لَيْتَ is the word of wishing (*tamannī*), often for something that cannot happen. People who see the Fire say: “Oh, would that we were sent back!”

The little word يَا in front is not “O …” calling someone. Al-Jadwal calls it *ḥarf tanbīh*, “a word that makes you notice”: “Oh!”. Then comes لَيْتَ with the pronoun **-nā** (“we”) as its ism. The khabar is the verbal sentence نُرَدُّ.

<Note kind="know">When layta meets “me” you get لَيْتَنِي, with an extra nūn. Al-Jadwal says on 19:23 that this nūn is there “to protect” the word (*nūn al-wiqāya*). The ī is the ism.</Note>
</Block>

        { role: 'khabarInna', label: 'khabar laʿalla', hint: 'a verbal sentence', words: [{ ar: 'تُفْلِحُونَ', en: 'will succeed', say: 'tuflihun', type: 'v' }] },

لَعَلَّ is hope and expectation (*tarajjī*, *tawaqquʿ*): “perhaps”. In the Quran it often follows an order, and then English says “so that you may …”: be patient, keep guard, and fear Allah, **so that you may succeed**.

Again the ism is a pronoun joined to the end: **-kum**. The khabar is the verbal sentence تُفْلِحُونَ.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal reads the clause “laʿallakum …” as giving the reason for what came before. You will see the same on 12:2 in the word-by-word step.</Note>

Very often the ism is not a separate word. It is a short pronoun joined to the end of the sister:

| Pronoun | With inna | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| -nā | إِنَّا | indeed we |
| -ka | إِنَّكَ | indeed you (one man) |
| -kum | إِنَّكُمْ | indeed you (many) |
| -hu | إِنَّهُ | indeed he |
| -hum | إِنَّهُمْ | indeed they |
| -ī | إِنِّي | indeed I |

A joined pronoun never changes its shape. It is still the ism, and the books say it is **in the place of the accusative** (*fī maḥall naṣb*).

In 2:32 the stressing word أَنتَ stands between the ism and the khabar. Al-Jadwal calls it a *separating pronoun* (*ḍamīr faṣl*) and gives it no role of its own. The khabar is ٱلْعَلِيمُ, and ٱلْحَكِيمُ is a second khabar, as in L2.01.

The khabar of inna can be any kind of khabar from L2.01. Nothing new to learn: only the ism changed.

| Kind of khabar | Verse | Khabar inna |
|---|---|---|
| Single | 2:173 | غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ |
| A phrase | 26:8 | فِى ذَٰلِكَ |
| A place or time word | 73:12 | لَدَيْنَآ |
| A verbal sentence | 12:2 | أَنزَلْنَٰهُ |
| A nominal sentence | 31:34 | عِندَهُۥ عِلْمُ ٱلسَّاعَةِ |

In 31:34 the khabar is a sentence inside the sentence, as in 3:14 in L2.01. Its own parts are عِندَهُۥ (khabar, first) and عِلْمُ (mubtadaʾ, later). The inner mubtadaʾ keeps its **-u**. Inna only changes the ism of the outer sentence.

<Note kind="remember">When the khabar is a sentence, it has no ending. Al-Jadwal says it is “in the place of the nominative”.</Note>

Look at the khabar: لَكَنُودٌ. It starts with an extra **لَ** (*la-*). This is the **lām of emphasis**. It says: “really, truly”. Al-Jadwal calls it *al-lām al-muzaḥlaqa*, “the lām that slid away” from inna to the khabar, and says it adds emphasis.

It does not change anything else. كَنُودٌ still ends in **-un**, as a khabar should. You can leave the la- out when you look for the case.

Be careful with the phrase in the middle. لِرَبِّهِۦ starts with *li-* (“to”), which is a preposition, not the lām of emphasis. It belongs to كَنُودٌ. Mistake 5 comes back to this.

Three words look alike:

  <Stage>

<Block id="practice-1" title="Practice: find the parts">
  <Stage label="For each sentence, say">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">

      <li><b>The ism:</b> which word or joined pronoun, and its mark.</li>
      <li><b>The khabar:</b> which kind, and its mark.</li>

Work out each answer first, on paper or in your head. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Find the ism and the khabar: فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ (Al-Baqarah 2:227, end). Meaning: “Then indeed Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing.”">

The فَ is a joining word (“then”). إِنَّ is the sister.

ٱللَّهَ is the **ism inna** (-a). سَمِيعٌ is the **khabar inna** (-un). عَلِيمٌ is a **second khabar** (-un).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Which sister is used, and what does it add? (a) يَٰلَيْتَنِى كُنتُ مَعَهُمْ (An-Nisa 4:73, part) (b) كَأَنَّهُمْ حُمُرٌ مُّسْتَنفِرَةٌ (Al-Muddaththir 74:50) (c) إِنَّكَ لَا تَهْدِى مَنْ أَحْبَبْتَ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَهْدِى مَن يَشَآءُ (Al-Qasas 28:56, part)">

- (a) **لَيْتَ**: a wish, “Oh, would that I …”. The ism is the -ī (with the protecting nūn). The khabar is the sentence كُنتُ مَعَهُمْ.
- (b) **كَأَنَّ**: a likeness, “as if they were …”. The ism is -hum. The khabar is حُمُرٌ, “donkeys”, in **-un**, and مُّسْتَنفِرَةٌ describes it.
- (c) Two sisters. **إِنَّ**: ism -ka, khabar the sentence لَا تَهْدِى …. **لَٰكِنَّ**: “but”, ism ٱللَّهَ (-a), khabar the sentence يَهْدِى مَن يَشَآءُ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Give the role and case of every word: إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ قَدِيرٌ (Al-Baqarah 2:20, end). Meaning: “Indeed, Allah has power over everything.”">

- إِنَّ: the sister, “indeed”. No case.
- ٱللَّهَ: **ism inna**, accusative, **-a**.
- عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ: a phrase, “over every thing”. كُلِّ is genitive after عَلَىٰ, and شَىْءٍ is genitive as the second word of the idafah. The phrase belongs to قَدِيرٌ. It is **not** the khabar.
- قَدِيرٌ: **khabar inna**, nominative, **-un**.

This is the “stopping at the phrase” mistake again (L2.01). Read to the end.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Add إِنَّ to each made-up sentence. Show what changes. (a) ٱلْكِتَابُ جَدِيدٌ (b) ٱلْمُسْلِمُونَ صَادِقُونَ (c) ٱلْمُسْلِمَاتُ صَادِقَاتٌ. These are made-up examples.">

- (a) إِنَّ ٱلْكِتَابَ جَدِيدٌ: ٱلْكِتَابَ (**-a**, ism), جَدِيدٌ stays **-un**.
- (b) إِنَّ ٱلْمُسْلِمِينَ صَادِقُونَ: the ism changes from -ūna to **-īna**. The khabar stays **-ūna**.
- (c) إِنَّ ٱلْمُسْلِمَاتِ صَادِقَاتٌ: the ism takes a **kasra**. The khabar stays **-un**.

Look at (b): the ism and the khabar are the same plural. Only the ending shows which is which.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Exercise n={5} q="Put each of the six in front of ٱلْكِتَابُ جَدِيدٌ, and say what the new sentence means. This is a made-up example.">

- إِنَّ ٱلْكِتَابَ جَدِيدٌ: “Indeed, the book is new.”
- عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ ٱلْكِتَابَ جَدِيدٌ: “I knew that the book is new.” (Anna needs something in front to join to.)
- لَٰكِنَّ ٱلْكِتَابَ جَدِيدٌ: “But the book is new.”
- كَأَنَّ ٱلْكِتَابَ جَدِيدٌ: “As if the book is new.”
- لَيْتَ ٱلْكِتَابَ جَدِيدٌ: “Would that the book were new.”
- لَعَلَّ ٱلْكِتَابَ جَدِيدٌ: “Perhaps the book is new.”

In all six, ٱلْكِتَابَ has **-a** and جَدِيدٌ keeps **-un**.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Find the ism and the khabar, and say which one comes first: إِنَّ لَكَ فِى ٱلنَّهَارِ سَبْحًا طَوِيلًا (Al-Muzzammil 73:7). Meaning: “Indeed, you have a long busy time in the day.”">

The ism is سَبْحًا (**-an**), and طَوِيلًا (-an) describes it. The khabar is the phrase لَكَ فِى ٱلنَّهَارِ, and it is **put first**, because سَبْحًا is indefinite. Al-Jadwal says سَبْحًا is the ism of inna.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Write inna with these pronouns: “we”, “you” (one man), “he”, “I”. Then say which word is the ism in إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْعَلِيمُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ (Al-Baqarah 2:32, end).">

- “we”: إِنَّا. “you”: إِنَّكَ. “he”: إِنَّهُ. “I”: إِنِّي.
- In 2:32 the ism is the **-ka** of إِنَّكَ, in the place of the accusative. أَنتَ is a separating pronoun with no role. ٱلْعَلِيمُ is the khabar and ٱلْحَكِيمُ a second khabar.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Why does ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ end in -ūna (nominative) here? إِنَّمَا ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ إِخْوَةٌ (Al-Hujurat 49:10, start). Meaning: “The believers are only brothers.”">

The word is **إِنَّمَا**, not إِنَّ. The mā holds inna back (*kāffa*), so inna does no work. ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ is an ordinary mubtadaʾ in **-ūna**, and إِخْوَةٌ is its khabar in -un.

If it were the made-up sentence إِنَّ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ إِخْوَةٌ, with only inna, the first part would change to -īna.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Heavy lākinna, or light lākin? What happens to the word after it? (a) وَلَٰكِن لَّا تُوَاعِدُوهُنَّ (Al-Baqarah 2:235, part) (b) وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ ٱلنَّاسِ لَا يَشْكُرُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:243, end)">

- (a) **Light** لَٰكِن: no shadda. It does nothing. A verb follows, لَّا تُوَاعِدُوهُنَّ.
- (b) **Heavy** لَٰكِنَّ: with a shadda. The next word أَكْثَرَ is its ism, in **-a**.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="inna plus the ism in -a plus the khabar in -u"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'ism inna', small: 'first part: now -a' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'khabar inna', small: 'second part: still -u' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'sentence after inna', small: 'إِنَّ وَأَخَوَاتُهَا' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- Six words come in front of a nominal sentence: إِنَّ، أَنَّ، لَٰكِنَّ، كَأَنَّ، لَيْتَ، لَعَلَّ. They mean “indeed”, “that”, “but”, “as if”, “would that”, “perhaps”.
- The first part is now the **ism**, and it takes **-a**. The khabar is the **khabar** and **stays -u**.
- The ism can be a joined pronoun (إِنَّا، لَعَلَّكُمْ). It stays in the place of the accusative.
- The khabar can be single, a phrase, a place word, a verbal sentence or a nominal sentence.
- With an indefinite ism and a phrase as khabar, the khabar comes first (إِنَّ لَهُۥٓ أَبًا).
- The lām of emphasis sits on the khabar, or on the ism when the khabar is first.
- إِنَّمَا and the light لَٰكِنْ do not work. Check the shadda.

**Next:** L2.11 teaches *kāna* and her sisters, which do the opposite: the first part keeps -u and the khabar takes -an. Review L2.04 and L2.05 if the cases felt new.
