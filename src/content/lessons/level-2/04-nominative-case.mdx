Three words in this verse are nominative: ٱلْمَالُ, ٱلْبَنُونَ and زِينَةُ.

- ٱلْمَالُ is the mubtadaʾ (L2.01). It ends in **-u**.
- زِينَةُ is the khabar (L2.01). It ends in **-u** too.
- ٱلْبَنُونَ, “the sons”, is joined to ٱلْمَالُ by وَ. It is nominative as well. But it does **not** end in -u. It ends in **-ūna**.

So the same case can look different. This lesson shows you which words are nominative, and how to see it in each form.

</Block>

<Block id="raf" title="What “nominative” means" level={2}>
  <Stage label="The name">
    <Tiles
      label="rafʿ means raising"
      items={[
        { ar: 'رَفْع', en: 'raising' },
        { ar: 'ـُ', en: 'the ḍamma, -u' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The Arabic name is رَفْع (*rafʿ*), “raising”. It is the case of the words with the main jobs in a sentence: the starting point, the news and the doer.

You met the case in L1.10 and used it in L2.01. Now you will learn **which jobs** give it, and **every way** it can show.

<Note kind="know">English keeps a little of this in pronouns. We say “**he** ran”, not “**him** ran”. Arabic does it for every noun, with endings.</Note>

<Note kind="warn">The ḍamma does not mean “the doer” only. A mubtadaʾ and a khabar are nominative too, and they do nothing. The case tells you the word has one of the jobs in the next step. It does not tell you which one.</Note>
</Block>

        { ar: 'فَتَيَانِ', en: 'alif (-āni)' },
        { ar: 'ذُو', en: 'wāw' },
        { ar: 'ٱلْهُدَىٰ', en: 'hidden ḍamma' },
      ]}
    />

The mark depends on what kind of word it is. *Al-Ajurrumiyyah*, the first grammar book many students learn, lists four marks of the nominative: the **ḍamma**, the **wāw**, the **alif** and the **nūn**.

| Word | Mark of the nominative | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| A single noun, a broken plural, a sound feminine plural | ḍamma: -u, or -un when indefinite | 18:46 |
| A sound masculine plural | wāw: **-ūna** | 9:67, 18:46 |
| A dual | alif: **-āni** | 12:36, 18:80 |
| The five nouns (ذُو, أَبُو and others) | wāw | 3:74 |
| A word that cannot show a vowel | a hidden ḍamma | 2:120, 18:17 |

The fourth mark, the nūn, belongs to some present-tense verbs, not to nouns. Level 3 teaches it.

Step 3 takes the marks one by one. Step 2 comes first: which words are nominative at all.
</Block>
</Step>

<Block id="mk" title="Mubtadaʾ and khabar">

You learned these two in L2.01. The mubtadaʾ is the starting point of a nominal sentence, and the khabar is the news about it. **Both are nominative.**

Here ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ is the mubtadaʾ (-u). خَيْرٌ is the khabar (-un). The word between them, ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ, is an adjective. The next blocks come back to it.

<Note kind="know">There is one small exception to “a mubtadaʾ always shows -u”. After the little word مِنْ added for emphasis, the mubtadaʾ looks genitive but counts as nominative. Al-Jadwal says this about مِن وَلِىٍّ in Al-Baqarah 2:120: it is “genitive in the word, nominative in its place”.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="doer" title="The doer of a verb (fāʿil)">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence

A verb has a doer: the one who does the action. The doer is the **fāʿil**, فَاعِل, and it is nominative. Here two men did the saying. L2.03 teaches the verbal sentence in full. For now, remember one thing: **the doer is nominative.**

رَجُلَانِ does not end in -u. It ends in **-āni**, because it means “two men”. You will see this mark in step 3.

<Note kind="remember">Mubtadaʾ, khabar and fāʿil are the three main jobs. All three are nominative.</Note>

Some words are nominative only because they follow a nominative word. They copy its case.

- **A word joined by وَ** (the maʿṭūf, مَعْطُوف). ٱلْبَنُونَ is joined to ٱلْمَالُ. Al-Jadwal: it is “joined to ٱلْمَالُ by wa, and its mark is the wāw”.
- **An adjective** (the naʿt, نَعْت). ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ describes ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ, so it is nominative like it. L2.09 teaches nouns and adjectives.

These are not new jobs. They only follow. If you know the case of the first word, you know the case of the second.

</Block>

In L2.01 you saw that *kāna* leaves its subject in -u and changes the khabar. *Inna* does the opposite.

- After **kāna**, the subject (ism kāna) stays **nominative**. Here أَبَوَاهُ is nominative. The khabar changes to accusative, and مُؤْمِنَيْنِ is accusative. Level 2 teaches this in L2.11.
- After **inna**, the subject (ism inna) turns **accusative**, and the khabar stays **nominative**. Here the “you” of فَإِنَّكُمْ is accusative, and غَٰلِبُونَ is nominative. L2.10 teaches this.

So the nominative list has two more entries: the **subject of kāna** and the **khabar of inna**. Al-Jadwal says both: أَبَوَاهُ is “the ism of kāna, nominative, and its mark is the alif”, and غَٰلِبُونَ is “the khabar of inna, nominative, and its mark is the wāw”.

</Block>

When a verb says what happened and not who did it, the word that was affected stands in the doer’s place. It is the **nāʾib fāʿil**, نَائِب فَاعِل, “the stand-in for the doer”. It is nominative too. Here ٱلْكِتَٰبُ has -u.

Do not worry about this yet. L3.07 teaches the passive. You only need to know that it is another nominative job.
</Block>

<Block id="damma" title="The usual mark: -u and -un">

        { role: 'khabar', words: [{ ar: 'خَيْرٌ', en: 'better', say: 'khayrun' }] },
      ]}

You know this mark from L1.10. A **single noun** takes **-u**. With no *al-*, it takes **-un**. Both are the ḍamma. The two ḍammas in -un are only the sign that the word is indefinite.

- ٱلْمَالُ and زِينَةُ: **-u**. The first has *al-*. The second is the first word of an idafah.
- خَيْرٌ: indefinite, **-un**.

A **broken plural**, a plural made by changing the word inside, shows the same marks. So does a **sound feminine plural**, which ends in -āt: ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ and ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ are both **-u**.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal says only “nominative” for ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ and ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ. It does not name the mark. The -u here is the rule from *al-Ajurrumiyyah*: the ḍamma is the mark of the single noun, the broken plural and the sound feminine plural.</Note>
</Block>

      ]}
      en={['Indeed the hypocrites, they ', { gap: 'are' }, ' the rebels.']}
    />

A **sound masculine plural** is made by adding an ending to the single word. In the nominative the ending is **-ūna**: ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ, ٱلْفَٰسِقُونَ. The mark is the letter **و**, a wāw. Al-Jadwal says this in the same words for both: “nominative, and its mark is the wāw”.

The second sentence shows why you must look at the ending. The same word “hypocrites” appears twice:

- ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ (-ūna) is the mubtadaʾ, so it is **nominative**.
- ٱلْمُنَٰفِقِينَ (-īna) comes after *inna*, so it is **accusative**. Al-Jadwal says its mark is the yāʾ.

In the first sentence the feminine plural ٱلْمُنَٰفِقَٰتُ is joined with وَ. It follows the nominative, with -u.

<Note kind="know">In this verse al-Jadwal reads هُمُ as a “separating pronoun” that sits between the two parts, and ٱلْفَٰسِقُونَ as the khabar of inna. It also allows another reading: هُمْ is a mubtadaʾ and ٱلْفَٰسِقُونَ is its khabar. Both readings leave ٱلْفَٰسِقُونَ nominative.</Note>

<Check n={5} q="Which one is nominative: ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ or ٱلْمُنَٰفِقِينَ?" options={['ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ, the -ūna', 'ٱلْمُنَٰفِقِينَ, the -īna']} answer={0} why="The -ūna is the nominative form of the sound masculine plural. The -īna is for the accusative and the genitive." />
</Block>

  <Stage>
    <Sentence

The **dual** is a noun for exactly two. In the nominative it ends in **-āni**: ـَانِ. The mark is the **alif**. Al-Jadwal says it for the doer فَتَيَانِ: “nominative, and its mark is the alif”.

You have seen two duals already: رَجُلَانِ (5:23) and أَبَوَاهُ (18:80, “his two parents”). The last one has a ـَاهُ ending because the pronoun “his” was added, and the nūn drops (see below). The mark is still the alif.

<Note kind="tip">The dual and the sound masculine plural both end in a nūn. Look at the letter before it: alif and nūn (-āni) for two, wāw and nūn (-ūna) for three or more.</Note>
</Block>

        { role: 'khabar', words: [{ ar: 'ذُو', en: 'the owner of', say: 'dhu' }, { ar: 'ٱلْفَضْلِ', en: 'the favour', say: 'al-fadli' }, { ar: 'ٱلْعَظِيمِ', en: 'the great', say: 'al-azimi' }] },
      ]}
      en={['And Allah ', { gap: 'is' }, ' the owner of great favour.']}
    />
  </Stage>

A small group of nouns are called **the five nouns**, الأَسْمَاء الخَمْسَة. They are أَبٌ (father), أَخٌ (brother), حَمٌ (a husband’s relative), فُو (mouth) and ذُو (owner of). They show the case with a letter, not a ḍamma. In the nominative the letter is the **wāw**: أَبُو, أَخُو, ذُو.

Al-Jadwal says it for ذُو here: “the khabar, nominative, and its mark is the wāw”.

They do this when another word follows them: ذُو ٱلْفَضْلِ, “the owner of favour”. ذُو is the first word of an idafah, and ٱلْفَضْلِ is the second, in the genitive (L2.08).

<Note kind="know">*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* lists these five, with “your father”, “your brother” and so on: أَبُوكَ، أَخُوكَ، حَمُوكَ، فُوكَ، ذُو مَالٍ. Some later books add a sixth, هَنٌ. This course uses the five.</Note>

</Block>

You saw in L2.01 that a word ending in a long **ā** (written ى or ا) cannot carry a vowel. The ḍamma is still there in the grammar. The books say it is **hidden**: *muqaddara*, “understood but not seen”.

- ٱلنَّصَٰرَىٰ is joined to the doer ٱلْيَهُودُ, so it is nominative. Its ḍamma is hidden on the last letter.
- ٱلْهُدَىٰ is the khabar of inna, so it is nominative. Its ḍamma is hidden too.

Look at هُدَى in the second sentence. It is the subject of *inna*, so it is **accusative**, and its fatḥa is hidden in the same way. Al-Jadwal says it: “the ism of inna, accusative, and its fatḥa is hidden”.

A ḍamma can also be hidden for another reason. Look at the third sentence above.

The full word is ٱلْمُهْتَدِى. Its last letter, the yāʾ, is dropped in writing. Al-Jadwal says the ḍamma is hidden “on the dropped yāʾ”, and that the yāʾ is dropped to make the word lighter. The word is the khabar, so it is nominative.

<Note kind="warn">A word with no visible ḍamma is not automatically not nominative. Ask first: what job does this word have? Use the job to find the case.</Note>

  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Baqarah 2:46 (part)"
      groups={[

The -ūna of the sound masculine plural and the -āni of the dual both end in a nūn. When the word is the **first word of an idafah**, the nūn drops. You see only the و or the ا.

Here the full plural is مُلَٰقُونَ. It is the first word of an idafah (“the meeters of their Lord”), so the nūn falls and the word becomes مُلَٰقُوا۟. Al-Jadwal says: “the khabar of anna, nominative, and its mark is the wāw; the nūn is dropped for the idafah”.

The wāw is still there, so the mark is still the wāw. Level 2 teaches the idafah in L2.08.

<Step id="mistakes" label="Mistakes, word by word" title="Four mistakes, and four verses word by word">
<Block id="mistake-next" title="Mistake 1: thinking the word after the verb is the doer">
  <Stage label="The doer comes last">
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Yusuf 12:36 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'وَدَخَلَ', en: 'and entered', type: 'v' }] },
        { words: [{ ar: 'مَعَهُ', en: 'with him' }] },
        { role: 'maful', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلسِّجْنَ', en: 'the prison' }] },
        { role: 'fail', words: [{ ar: 'فَتَيَانِ', en: 'two young men' }] },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

In English the doer comes right after the verb: “*Two young men* entered the prison.” In this verse the word right after the verb is ٱلسِّجْنَ, “the prison”. But the prison did not enter. It is the place that was entered.

The ending decides, not the order. ٱلسِّجْنَ ends in -a, so it is accusative, and it is not the doer. فَتَيَانِ ends in -āni, the nominative of a dual, so it is the doer. Al-Jadwal says the same: ٱلسِّجْنَ is the object (L2.05), and فَتَيَانِ is the doer.

</Block>

<Block id="mistake-verb" title="Mistake 2: reading -ūna on a verb as a case mark">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="At-Tawbah 9:67 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ', en: 'the hypocrites (men)', say: 'al-munafiquna' }] },
      ]}
    />
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="At-Tawbah 9:67 (part)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', words: [{ ar: 'يَأْمُرُونَ', en: 'they order', say: 'yamuruna', type: 'v' }] },
        { words: [{ ar: 'بِٱلْمُنكَرِ', en: 'what is wrong', say: 'bil-munkari', type: 'p' }] },
      ]}
      en={['… they order what is wrong.']}
    />
  </Stage>

Look at the two -ūna endings in this verse. One is a noun and one is a verb.

- In ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ, the -ūna is a **case mark**. The word is the mubtadaʾ.
- In يَأْمُرُونَ, the و is not a case mark. Al-Jadwal says the و is the **doer**, a pronoun meaning “they”, and the nūn is the ending of the verb. A verb does not take a case the way a noun does (L3.04).

Only nouns have cases. When you meet -ūna, first ask: is this a noun or a verb?
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-iina" title="Mistake 3: treating every plural as nominative">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="At-Tawbah 9:67 (end)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'ismInna', words: [{ ar: 'إِنَّ', en: 'indeed', type: 'p' }, { ar: 'ٱلْمُنَٰفِقِينَ', en: 'the hypocrites' }] },
        { words: [{ ar: 'هُمُ', en: 'they' }] },
        { role: 'khabarInna', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلْفَٰسِقُونَ', en: 'the rebels' }] },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The mistake: “This word comes first in the sentence, so it is the mubtadaʾ and nominative.” Here ٱلْمُنَٰفِقِينَ comes first after the little word *inna*, and it is **not** nominative. Its job is “subject of inna”, and that job is accusative (-īna). The khabar stays nominative (-ūna).

So do not decide the case from the place of the word alone. Look at the little word before it.

L2.10 teaches *inna* in full.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-damma" title="Mistake 4: looking only for a ḍamma">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="Words with no ḍamma that are still nominative"
      items={[
        { ar: 'فَتَيَانِ', en: 'alif' },
        { ar: 'ذُو', en: 'wāw' },
        { ar: 'ٱلنَّصَٰرَىٰ', en: 'hidden' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A student who learns “nominative means -u” often skips these words:

- فَتَيَانِ: the mark is the alif.
- ذُو: the mark is the wāw.
- ٱلنَّصَٰرَىٰ: the ḍamma is hidden.

So do not hunt for the ḍamma first. Find the job, and then look for the mark. If the job is mubtadaʾ, khabar or doer, the word is nominative, whatever it looks like.

</Block>

<Step id="practice" label="Practice" title="Practice">
<Block id="practice-1" title="Practice: find the nominative words">
  <Stage label="For each word, say">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Job:</b> mubtadaʾ, khabar, doer, or a word that follows?</li>
      <li><b>Case:</b> is the job one of the nominative jobs?</li>
      <li><b>Mark:</b> -u, -un, wāw, alif, or hidden?</li>
      <li><b>Reason:</b> why does it have this mark?</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

Work out each answer first, on paper or in your head. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Find the doer and say its mark: قَالَ رَجُلَانِ مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ يَخَافُونَ (Al-Maidah 5:23, start). Meaning: “Two men, from those who feared, said …”">

قَالَ is the verb. رَجُلَانِ, “two men”, is the **doer**. It is nominative. The mark is the **alif** of the dual, -āni.

مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ يَخَافُونَ, “from those who fear”, describes the two men. It is not the doer.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Which word is nominative, and which is not? إِنَّ ٱلْمُنَٰفِقِينَ هُمُ ٱلْفَٰسِقُونَ (At-Tawbah 9:67, end). Meaning: “Indeed the hypocrites, they are the rebels.”">

ٱلْفَٰسِقُونَ is nominative. It is the **khabar of inna**. The mark is the **wāw** of -ūna.

ٱلْمُنَٰفِقِينَ is **not** nominative. It is the subject of *inna*, so it is **accusative**. The -īna ending says so.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Say the case and mark of مُّلَٰقُوا۟, and say where its nūn went: أَنَّهُم مُّلَٰقُوا۟ رَبِّهِمْ (Al-Baqarah 2:46, part). Meaning: “that they will meet their Lord”.">

مُّلَٰقُوا۟ is the **khabar of anna**. It is nominative. The mark is the **wāw**.

The full plural is مُلَٰقُونَ. The nūn dropped because the word is the first word of an idafah, with رَبِّهِمْ as the second. رَبِّهِمْ is genitive (-i).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Find the ism kāna and the khabar of kāna, and say which is nominative: فَكَانَ أَبَوَاهُ مُؤْمِنَيْنِ (Al-Kahf 18:80, part). Meaning: “His two parents were believers.”">

أَبَوَاهُ, “his two parents”, is the **ism of kāna**. It is **nominative**. The mark is the **alif** of the dual.

مُؤْمِنَيْنِ, “two believers”, is the **khabar of kāna**. It is **accusative**. The ـَيْنِ ending is the dual in the accusative.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Say the job and the case of ٱلْمُهْتَدِ, and why you cannot see its ḍamma: فَهُوَ ٱلْمُهْتَدِ (Al-Kahf 18:17, part). Meaning: “then he is the guided one.”">

هُوَ is the **mubtadaʾ**, a pronoun that never changes. ٱلْمُهْتَدِ is the **khabar**, so it is **nominative**.

The ḍamma is hidden. The last letter of the word, a yāʾ, is dropped in writing, and the ḍamma is understood on that dropped yāʾ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Give the three jobs of the nominative words: ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ خَيْرٌ (Al-Kahf 18:46, part). Meaning: “The good things that last are better.”">

- ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ: the **mubtadaʾ**, nominative, -u.
- ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ: the **naʿt** (adjective) of ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ. It copies the nominative, -u.
- خَيْرٌ: the **khabar**, nominative, -un.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Exercise n={7} q="Write the nominative form of ٱلْمُسْلِمُ (the Muslim) for two, for three or more men, and for three or more women. These are made-up examples.">

- Two: ٱلْمُسْلِمَانِ (the alif of the dual).
- Three or more men: ٱلْمُسْلِمُونَ (the wāw of -ūna).
- Three or more women: ٱلْمُسْلِمَاتُ (-ātu with a ḍamma).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Say the mark of the nominative: (a) جَاءَ أَبُو زَيْدٍ (b) جَاءَ ٱلرَّجُلُ (c) جَاءَ ٱلرَّجُلَانِ (d) جَاءَ ٱلْمُعَلِّمُونَ. Meaning: “The father of Zayd came; the man came; the two men came; the teachers came.” These are made-up examples.">

- (a) أَبُو: the **wāw**. It is one of the five nouns, and it is the doer.
- (b) ٱلرَّجُلُ: **-u**. A single noun.
- (c) ٱلرَّجُلَانِ: the **alif**. A dual.
- (d) ٱلْمُعَلِّمُونَ: the **wāw** of -ūna. A sound masculine plural.

In all four, the word after جَاءَ is the doer, so it is nominative.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Find the mistake and correct it: جَاءَ ٱلْمُسْلِمِينَ. This is a made-up example.">

ٱلْمُسْلِمِينَ ends in -īna. That form is for the accusative or genitive. The word is the doer of جَاءَ, so it must be nominative: جَاءَ **ٱلْمُسْلِمُونَ**, “the Muslims came”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="What is the job of ٱلْكِتَٰبُ, and what is its case: وَوُضِعَ ٱلْكِتَٰبُ (Al-Kahf 18:49, start). Meaning: “And the book is put down.”">

وُضِعَ is a verb that says what happened, without naming who did it. ٱلْكِتَٰبُ, “the book”, stands in for the doer. It is the **nāʾib fāʿil**, and it is **nominative**, with -u. This is a preview of L3.07.

</Exercise>

- The nominative (rafʿ) is the case of the **mubtadaʾ**, the **khabar** and the **doer**. It is also the case of the stand-in for the doer, the subject of *kāna*, the khabar of *inna*, and words that follow a nominative word (joined by wa, or describing it).
- The main mark is the **ḍamma**: -u, or -un without *al-*.
- A **sound masculine plural** shows it with the wāw (-ūna). A **dual** shows it with the alif (-āni). The **five nouns** show it with the wāw. If a word ends in a long ā, the ḍamma is **hidden**.
- The nūn of -ūna and -āni drops when the word is the first word of an idafah.
- Find the **job** first. Then look for the mark. A word with no visible ḍamma can still be nominative.
- After *inna*, the subject is accusative and the khabar stays nominative. After *kāna*, the subject stays nominative and the khabar becomes accusative.
