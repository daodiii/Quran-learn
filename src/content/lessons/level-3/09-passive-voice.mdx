It is part of a verse about fasting. Read it from right to left.

The verb is كُتِبَ, “was written”. Who wrote it? The verb does not say. The sentence names the thing that was done, ٱلصِّيَامُ, “the fasting”, and leaves the doer out.

In L2.03 the verbs had doers: written, attached or hidden. Here the doer is gone, and another word has taken its place. That word is the **nāʾib fāʿil**, نَائِب فَاعِل, “the stand-in for the doer”.

</Block>

Compare the two lines. Three things change.

1. **The verb changes its vowels.** كَتَبَ (*kataba*) becomes كُتِبَ (*kutiba*). The letters ك ت ب stay the same.
2. **The doer goes.** ٱللَّهُ is not in the second line.
3. **The object moves up.** ٱلصِّيَامَ was the object, with **-a** (L2.05). Now it ends in **-u**. It stands in for the doer, so it is nominative (L2.04).

The first line is a made-up example. The second is the verse.

<Note kind="remember">In the passive, the verb changes its vowels, the doer is not named, and the object takes the doer’s place.</Note>

<Block id="names" title="Active and passive in Arabic" level={2}>
  <Stage label="Two names">
    <Tiles
      label="مَعْلُوم, the known, is active. مَجْهُول, the unknown, is passive."
      items={[
        { ar: 'مَعْلُوم', en: 'the known: active' },
        { ar: 'مَجْهُول', en: 'the unknown: passive' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The grammar books call an active verb **“built for the known”**, مَبْنِيّ لِلْمَعْلُوم. The doer is known: it is written, attached or hidden. They call a passive verb **“built for the unknown”**, مَبْنِيّ لِلْمَجْهُول. The doer is not named.

English makes the passive with a helper word and a second verb form: “was written”, “is done”. Arabic changes the vowels inside the verb. You will see how in the next step.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal writes <i>fiʿl māḍin mabnī lil-majhūl</i> for a past passive verb, and uses the name <i>nāʾib fāʿil</i> for the stand-in. Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus: 1,140 verb words are passive, 634 past and 506 present. Of the 1,140, 927 are in the third person (“he”, “she”, “they”). A script counted them in the Corpus file.</Note>
</Block>

You met this sentence in L2.04. The verse is about the Day of Judgement. The news is that the book is put down. Who puts it down is not the point.

A speaker can leave out the doer for several plain reasons. The listener may know the doer already. The doer may not matter. The doer may be unknown. Arabic grammar gives the **form** for all of these. You find the reason in the meaning of the verse, and in the verses around it.

<Note kind="know">This lesson teaches the grammar of the passive. Why a verse chooses it belongs to the study of style, <i>balāgha</i>. L4.17 gives a first look at it.</Note>

<Step id="shapes" label="The two shapes" title="The two shapes: fuʿila and yufʿalu">
<Block id="book" title="How the first grammar book says it">
  <Stage label="From al-Ajurrumiyyah">
    <blockquote class="lx-quote" lang="ar" dir="rtl">
      <p>وَهُوَ الِاسْمُ الْمَرْفُوعُ الَّذِي لَمْ يُذْكَرْ مَعَهُ فَاعِلُهُ</p>
      <p>فَإِنْ كَانَ الْفِعْلُ مَاضِيًا ضُمَّ أَوَّلُهُ وَكُسِرَ مَا قَبْلَ آخِرِهِ</p>
      <p>وَإِنْ كَانَ مُضَارِعًا ضُمَّ أَوَّلُهُ وَفُتِحَ مَا قَبْلَ آخِرِهِ</p>
    </blockquote>
    <p class="lx-quote-note">Vowel marks added by Quran Learn.</p>
  </Stage>

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* has a chapter called <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">بَابُ الْمَفْعُولِ الَّذِي لَمْ يُسَمَّ فَاعِلُهُ</span>, “the object whose doer is not named”. That is the book’s name for the stand-in. Here is what it says:

- “It is the nominative noun whose doer is not mentioned with it.”
- “If the verb is **past**, its first letter gets a **ḍamma**, and the letter before the last gets a **kasra**.”
- “If the verb is **present**, its first letter gets a **ḍamma**, and the letter before the last gets a **fatḥa**.”

The rest of this step shows the rule at work. The book’s own examples are ضُرِبَ زَيْدٌ and يُضْرَبُ زَيْدٌ, “Zayd was hit” and “Zayd is hit”.

<Note kind="know">“The letter before the last” is the middle letter in a verb of three root letters. In longer verbs it is still the letter before the last.</Note>

<Check n={4} q="In a past passive verb, which vowel goes on the first letter?" options={['Ḍamma (-u)', 'Kasra (-i)', 'Fatḥa (-a)']} answer={0} why="Past: ḍamma on the first letter, kasra on the letter before the last." />
</Block>

<Block id="past-shape" title="The past passive: fuʿila">
  <Stage label="Pattern">
    <Tiles
      label="فَعَلَ, faʿala, he did, becomes فُعِلَ, fuʿila, it was done"
      items={[
        { ar: 'فَعَلَ', en: 'faʿala: he did (active)' },
        { ar: 'فُعِلَ', en: 'fuʿila: it was done (passive)' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The letters f-ʿ-l stand for the three root letters of any verb. The past passive shape is **فُعِلَ**, *fuʿila*:

- **ḍamma** on the first root letter,
- **kasra** on the second root letter,
- the last root letter keeps its fatḥa.

Here are real verbs from the Quran:

| Active | Passive | Meaning of the passive | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| كَتَبَ | كُتِبَ | was written, was made a duty | 2:183 |
| قَتَلَ | قُتِلَ | was killed | 3:144 |
| خَلَقَ | خُلِقَ | was created | 4:28 |
| وَضَعَ | وُضِعَ | was put down | 18:49 |
| ذَبَحَ | ذُبِحَ | was slaughtered | 5:3 |

The middle vowel of the active verb can be -a (كَتَبَ) or -i (سَمِعَ, “he heard”). L3.04 teaches this. The passive ignores it: كُتِبَ and سُمِعَ both take the kasra.

<Check n={5} q="Which one is the passive of قَتَلَ, “he killed”?" options={['قَتَلَ', 'قُتِلَ', 'قِتُلَ']} answer={1} why="Ḍamma on the first letter and kasra on the second: قُتِلَ, “he was killed”." />
</Block>

The endings are the endings of the active past tense, which [L3.05](/learn/level-3/05-past-tense/) teaches. Only the first part of the verb changes. Every row starts with ضُرِ-: ḍamma, then kasra.

The book gives twelve of these forms with the same verb, from ضُرِبْتُ to ضُرِبْنَ. The table adds the feminine “they two”.

There is one change of meaning to watch. In the active, ضَرَبْتُ means “I hit”. In the passive, ضُرِبْتُ means “I was hit”. The ending -tu is now the person who receives the action.

<Block id="present-rule" title="The present passive: yufʿalu">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Anʿam 6:160 (end)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'وَهُمْ', en: 'and they', say: 'wa-hum' }] },

      en={['And they will not be wronged.']}
    />

The present passive shape is **يُفْعَلُ**, *yufʿalu*:

- **ḍamma** on the prefix (يُـ، تُـ، أُـ، نُـ),
- **fatḥa** on the second root letter,
- the ending is the same as in the active.

| Active | Passive | Meaning of the passive |
|---|---|---|
| يَكْتُبُ | يُكْتَبُ | it is written |
| يَفْتَحُ | يُفْتَحُ | it is opened |
| يَضْرِبُ | يُضْرَبُ | he is hit |
| يَظْلِمُ | يُظْلَمُ | he is wronged |

The active middle vowel can be -u, -a or -i. In a verb with three strong root letters, the passive puts **fatḥa** on the second letter. In يَفْتَحُ the fatḥa was already there, so only the prefix changes.

The verse above has يُظْلَمُونَ, “they are wronged”, from ظَلَمَ. With لَا it means “they are not wronged”.

The prefixes and endings are those of the active present, which [L3.06](/learn/level-3/06-present-tense/) teaches. The prefix vowel changes from fatḥa to ḍamma, and the second root letter takes a fatḥa.

The table has thirteen rows. Some forms look the same: تُظْلَمُ is both “she” and “you (a man)”, and تُظْلَمَانِ is both “they two (women)” and “you two”. The sentence tells you which one is meant.

The moods (subjunctive, jussive) change the ending of a passive verb in the same way as an active one (L3.07). In Al-Anʿam 6:160, يُظْلَمُونَ keeps its nūn, so it is nominative.

تُرْجَعُ comes from the root ر ج ع (return). The prefix has a ḍamma and the second root letter has a fatḥa: the present passive shape.

The word that stands in for the doer is ٱلْأُمُورُ, “the matters”. It ends in **-u**. Al-Jadwal reads the verse as one sentence: it says إِلَى ٱللَّهِ belongs to تُرْجَعُ, and it does not go through the words one by one.

The verb begins with تُـ, not يُـ. ٱلْأُمُورُ is a plural of things, and L1.08 taught that such a plural usually takes a feminine single word. The verb is feminine single.

The first kind of stand-in is a **written noun**, as in 2:183 and here. It was the object of the active verb. In the passive it is nominative, with -u: ٱلْمَيْتَةُ.

The verb **matches** its stand-in in gender, as a verb matches a doer (L2.03). ٱلْمَيْتَةُ is feminine, so the verb ends in -t: حُرِّمَتْ. In 2:183 ٱلصِّيَامُ is masculine, so كُتِبَ has no -t.

The verb حُرِّمَتْ is not Form I. It is Form II, with a shadda on the rā. L3.12 teaches Form II. The passive rule is the same, as the book says.

L2.03 showed verbs that take **two objects**. The active verb here is آتَى, “he gave”. It takes “the people” and “the Book”: *he gave the people the Book*.

Two of these have a weak letter in the root: وُضِعَ (starts with و) and قِيلَ (middle و). وُضِعَ follows the pattern. قِيلَ looks different, and the next block shows why.

<Block id="weak" title="A passive that changes shape: قِيلَ">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Ya-Sin 36:26 (start)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', hint: 'passive of قَالَ', words: [{ ar: 'قِيلَ', en: 'it was said', say: 'qila', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'naibFail', label: 'the words said', hint: 'a whole sentence stands in', words: [{ ar: 'ٱدْخُلِ', en: 'enter', say: 'udkhuli', type: 'v' }, { ar: 'ٱلْجَنَّةَ', en: 'the Garden', say: 'al-jannata' }] },
      ]}
      en={['It was said, “Enter the Garden.”']}
    />
  </Stage>

The active verb is قَالَ, “he said”, from the root ق و ل. The middle root letter is **و**. By the pattern the passive would be قُوِلَ. It is **قِيلَ** instead.

Al-Jadwal explains it in its note on 2:11. The verb is first قُوِلَ. The wāw is a weak letter and cannot carry the kasra. The kasra moves to the qāf, and the wāw turns into yāʾ. That gives قِيلَ.

So قِيلَ does not show the fuʿila shape you learned. The same happens to other verbs with a weak middle letter. L4.12 teaches these hollow verbs, and L4.11 gives the first look at weak verbs.

In the Corpus, the passive of قَالَ appears 52 times: 49 in the past and 3 in the present. This is Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus.

<Note kind="tip">If a passive verb looks wrong for the pattern, check the root. A weak letter in the root is the usual reason.</Note>

<Block id="mistake-object" title="Mistake 1: keeping the object’s -a">
  <Stage label="Made-up example">
    <Tiles
      label="ضُرِبَ ٱلرَّجُلَ is wrong. ضُرِبَ ٱلرَّجُلُ is right."
      items={[
        { ar: 'ضُرِبَ ٱلرَّجُلَ', en: 'wrong: the -a of an object', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'ضُرِبَ ٱلرَّجُلُ', en: 'right: the -u of a stand-in', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A learner may change the verb and forget to change the noun. ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ ٱلرَّجُلَ, “Zayd hit the man”, has ٱلرَّجُلَ as an object, with -a. Make the verb passive and the man becomes the stand-in: ضُرِبَ ٱلرَّجُلُ, “the man was hit”.

Compare ٱلصِّيَامُ in 2:183. Its -u is the sign that it is a stand-in and not an object.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-vowels" title="Mistake 2: reading the letters and not the vowels">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="وَضَعَ, he put, and وُضِعَ, it was put"
      items={[
        { ar: 'وَضَعَ', en: 'active: he put', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'وُضِعَ', en: 'passive: it was put', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The two verbs use the same three letters. Only the first two vowels differ. A learner who reads only the letters will turn وُضِعَ ٱلْكِتَٰبُ into “he put the book” and then wonder about the -u on ٱلْكِتَٰبُ.

Two checks help. Is the first vowel a ḍamma? Is there a noun with -u after the verb and no other doer? If both answers are yes, the verb is passive.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-doer" title="Mistake 3: looking for the doer">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Al-Anbiya 21:37 (start)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', hint: 'passive', words: [{ ar: 'خُلِقَ', en: 'was created', say: 'khuliqa', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'naibFail', hint: 'not the one who creates', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ', en: 'the human being', say: 'al-insanu' }] },

      ]}
      en={['The human being was created from haste.']}
    />
  </Stage>

In a sentence like this, a learner may look for a doer. The noun with -u, ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ, is not the one who creates. It is the one who was created. It is a stand-in.

A passive verb does not name its doer. If a noun after the verb names the doer, the verb is active, like خَلَقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ in 96:2.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-ending" title="Mistake 4: reading an ending as a doer">
  <Stage label="Pattern">
    <Tiles
      label="أَمَرْتُ, I ordered, and أُمِرْتُ, I was ordered"
      items={[
        { ar: 'أَمَرْتُ', en: 'active: I ordered', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'أُمِرْتُ', en: 'passive: I was ordered', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Endings such as -tu, -ū and -nā are the doer in an active verb (L2.03). In a passive verb they are the stand-in. أُمِرْتُ in 10:72 does not mean “I ordered”. It means “I was ordered”.

Check the first vowel before you decide who the ending is.
</Block>

<Exercise n={7} q="Find the mistake and correct it: (a) ضُرِبَ ٱلرَّجُلَ (b) وُضِعَتْ ٱلْكِتَٰبُ. These are made-up mistakes.">

- (a) ٱلرَّجُلَ has the -a of an object. It is the stand-in, so it must be nominative: ضُرِبَ **ٱلرَّجُلُ**, “the man was hit”.
- (b) ٱلْكِتَٰبُ is masculine, so the verb has no -t: **وُضِعَ** ٱلْكِتَٰبُ, as in 18:49.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Explain تُرْجَعُ: وَإِلَى ٱللَّهِ تُرْجَعُ ٱلْأُمُورُ (Al-Hadid 57:5, end). Say its tense, its stand-in, its case and why it starts with تُـ.">

- It is a **present passive** verb: ḍamma on the prefix, fatḥa on the second root letter ج.
- The stand-in is **ٱلْأُمُورُ**, “the matters”.
- It is **nominative**, with -u.
- The verb starts with تُـ because ٱلْأُمُورُ is a plural of things, and the verb is feminine single (L1.08).
- The phrase إِلَى ٱللَّهِ belongs to the verb.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Make each sentence passive. (a) كَتَبَ ٱلطَّالِبُ ٱلدَّرْسَ, “the student wrote the lesson” (b) فَتَحَ ٱلرَّجُلُ ٱلنَّافِذَةَ, “the man opened the window” (c) يَفْتَحُ ٱلرَّجُلُ ٱلْبَابَ, “the man opens the door”. These are made-up examples.">

- (a) **كُتِبَ ٱلدَّرْسُ**, “the lesson was written”. The doer goes. The object ٱلدَّرْسَ becomes ٱلدَّرْسُ.
- (b) **فُتِحَتِ ٱلنَّافِذَةُ**, “the window was opened”. ٱلنَّافِذَةُ is feminine, so the verb takes -t.
- (c) **يُفْتَحُ ٱلْبَابُ**, “the door is opened”. Present passive.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Look at قِيلَ ٱدْخُلِ ٱلْجَنَّةَ (Ya-Sin 36:26). (1) What is the active verb and its root? (2) What is the stand-in? (3) Why is the shape not قُوِلَ?">

1. The active verb is **قَالَ**, from the root **ق و ل**.
2. The stand-in is the words said: **ٱدْخُلِ ٱلْجَنَّةَ**. Al-Jadwal reads the whole sentence as the stand-in.
3. The middle letter is a weak و. The kasra moves to the ق, and the و turns into ي. L4.12 teaches hollow verbs.

</Exercise>

  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="a passive verb plus the stand-in for the doer makes a sentence that does not name its doer"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'passive verb', small: 'فُعِلَ، يُفْعَلُ' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'stand-in for the doer', small: 'نَائِب فَاعِل' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'a sentence with no named doer', small: 'مَبْنِيّ لِلْمَجْهُول' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A passive verb does not name its doer. Active: كَتَبَ, “he wrote”. Passive: كُتِبَ, “it was written”.
- **Past:** ḍamma on the first letter, kasra on the letter before the last: فُعِلَ. **Present:** ḍamma on the prefix, fatḥa on the letter before the last: يُفْعَلُ. The endings are the same as in the active.
- Another word takes the doer’s place: the **nāʾib fāʿil**. It is nominative.
- The stand-in can be a written noun (the old object, now with -u), an attached ending (-tu, -ū, -ūna), a hidden pronoun, or the words said after قِيلَ.
- The verb matches its stand-in in gender.
- With a verb that took two objects, the first becomes the stand-in and the second stays accusative: أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ.
- The other verb forms have passives with the same rule. Weak verbs change shape: قِيلَ, not قُوِلَ.
