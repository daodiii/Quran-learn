Allah speaks to Musa. Two verbs in this verse have the same look: **ٱ** at the front and a **ت** right after the first letter. ٱسْتَمِعْ starts with ٱ, then س, then ت. Take off the ٱ and the ت, and the root is left: س م ع, “hearing”. L3.03 taught you to find a root by taking off the added letters. In this form two letters are added, and they sit in the same two places.

ٱخْتَرْتُكَ has the same look: ٱ, then خ, then ت. Its root is خ ي ر. The ي does not show in this form. Step 3 explains why.

Al-Jadwal reads the verse like this: وَأَنَا is the starting point, and the sentence ٱخْتَرْتُكَ is its news. ٱسْتَمِعْ starts a new sentence.

The scale word of Form VIII is **ٱفْتَعَلَ** (*iftaʿala*). The letters ف ع ل stand for the three root letters (L3.03). The ت sits between the first and the second. Put the root خ ل ف into the scale word and you get ٱخْتَلَفَ (*ikhtalafa*), “he differed”.

Here are four roots that the Quran uses in both Form I and Form VIII.

| Root | Form I, “he” | Form VIII, “he” | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| ك س ب | كَسَبَ, “he earned” | ٱكْتَسَبَ, “he earned” | 2:286 |
| ج م ع | جَمَعَ, “he gathered” | ٱجْتَمَعَ, “it came together” | 104:2, 17:88 |
| س م ع | سَمِعَ, “he heard” | ٱسْتَمَعَ, “he listened” | 58:1, 20:13 |
| ق ت ل | قَتَلَ, “he killed” | ٱقْتَتَلَ, “they fought each other” | 2:72, 49:9 |

The table shows the “he” forms to compare the shapes. The verses use other persons: 2:286 has كَسَبَتْ and ٱكْتَسَبَتْ, “she” forms. Step 4 shows the meanings in these verses.

A regular verb takes the same vowels each time. In the past, a fatḥa sits on the ت and on the second root letter: ٱخْتَلَفَ. In the present, the second root letter gets a kasra: يَخْتَلِفُ. Step 2 shows all the persons. Roots with a weak letter or a doubled letter change a little, and Step 3 shows how.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal's ṣarf notes call a Form VIII verb *khumāsī*, “five-letter”, because the past “he” form has five letters, ٱ and ت included. He writes its pattern as افتعل. His note on 2:164 calls ٱخْتِلَٰف the verbal noun of ٱخْتَلَفَ.</Note>

The Quranic Arabic Corpus marks a verb that is not Form I with its form number in brackets (L3.04). For Form VIII the mark is **(VIII)**. For example, the Corpus tags ٱخْتَرْتُكَ as a verb in the past (PERF), root خ ي ر, “I” (1S), (VIII). It tags فَٱسْتَمِعْ as a command (IMPV), root س م ع, “you, one man” (2MS), (VIII).

The Corpus has **963** verb words tagged (VIII). That is about 5% of the Quran's 19,356 verb words. Of the 963, 379 are in the past, 424 in the present and 160 are commands. These are Quran Learn's counts from the Corpus tags.

Three verbs make 426 of the 963 words. These are the verbs you will meet most.

| Verb | Root | Words | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| ٱتَّقَىٰ | و ق ي | 166 | he was mindful (of Allah) |
| ٱتَّبَعَ | ت ب ع | 136 | he followed |
| ٱتَّخَذَ | أ خ ذ | 124 | he took, he made |
| ٱفْتَرَىٰ | ف ر ي | 50 | he invented (a lie) |
| ٱهْتَدَىٰ | ه د ي | 40 | he took the right way |
| ٱخْتَلَفَ | خ ل ف | 35 | he differed |

The first three make the 426. The other three come next. Each count is the number of Corpus words under that verb, counted by Quran Learn.

All three of the top verbs start with ٱتَّ, a doubled ت. It hides the root's first letter. Step 3 shows what is under it.

Three forms start with ٱ and a consonant, and they can look alike at the start.

- **Form VIII** has ٱ, a root letter, then ت.
- **Form X** has ٱسْتَ in front of the root: ٱسْتَغْفَرَ, “he asked forgiveness” ([L3.16](/learn/level-3/16-verb-form-x/) teaches it).
- **Form VII** has ٱنْ in front of the root: ٱنفَطَرَتْ (82:1) ([L3.18](/learn/level-3/18-verb-forms-vi-vii-ix/) teaches it).

The root decides. Take off ٱ and the ت, and see if three root letters are left. ٱسْتَمَعَ gives س م ع, so it is Form VIII, and the س is a root letter. ٱسْتَغْفَرَ gives سْغْفَرَ, which is not a root: take off ٱسْتَ instead, and غ ف ر is left, so it is Form X. In ٱنتَصَرَ the ن is the first root letter, and ن ص ر is a root, so it is Form VIII.

[L3.04](/learn/level-3/04-verb-form-i/) showed the same trap with نَسْتَبِقُ (12:17): the root is س ب ق, and the Corpus tags the verb (VIII). It is not Form X.

ٱخْتَلَفَ is a real Quran verb, from the root خ ل ف. The tables are teaching tables: they give all 13 forms, and the Quran uses only some of them.

The endings are the ones you learned in [L3.05](/learn/level-3/05-past-tense/). Only the stem is new. The stem ٱخْتَلَفَ stays the same in every row, and the ending is added to it. The last root letter takes a sukūn before the “you”, “I” and -na endings: ٱخْتَلَفْتَ.

The present has three moves, and they work for a regular Form VIII verb.

1. **Drop the ٱ.** The prefix takes its place.
2. **Add the prefix** يَ, تَ, أَ or نَ (L3.06). It sits in front of the first root letter, and that letter takes a sukūn: يَخْ.
3. **Put a kasra on the second root letter.** The past has a fatḥa there, ٱخْتَلَفَ, and the present has a kasra, يَخْتَلِفُ.

The ت stays in the same place as before. The prefix and endings follow the table of [L3.06](/learn/level-3/06-present-tense/) exactly.

The ٱ is the joining alif, هَمْزَة الْوَصْل, from [L3.08](/learn/level-3/08-imperative/). It has four fates.

- **At the start of a sentence** you say it with the sound **i**: *ikhtalafa*, *ittabaʿa*.
- **After a little word** such as وَ or فَ you skip it: *fa-khtalafa*, *wa-ttabaʿa*. The ٱ is still written.
- **In the present** there is no ٱ. The prefix takes its place: يَخْتَلِفُ.
- **After a question أَ** it is not written at all. In 37:153 the question أَ and the verb ٱصْطَفَىٰ join as أَصْطَفَى. Al-Jadwal says the joining hamza is dropped because the question hamza enters the verb.

<Note kind="know">In the passive the helper sounds **u**: ٱخْتُلِفَ (11:110) and ٱضْطُرَّ (2:173). L3.09 teaches the passive.</Note>

<Block id="merge" title="A doubled ت: ٱتَّ">
  <Stage label="Three verbs, one start">
    <Tiles

      items={[

The ت of Form VIII sits right after the first root letter. If that letter is ت, two ت meet: the root's ت and the added ت. If the first letter is و or أ, it can turn into ت and then meet the added ت. Either way, the result is one doubled ت with a shadda: ٱتَّ. This is why the three most common Form VIII verbs all start the same way.

| Verb | Root | First root letter | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| ٱتَّبَعَ | ت ب ع | ت | Two ت meet, and they are written as one تّ. |
| ٱتَّقَىٰ | و ق ي | و | The و turns into ت, then it merges with the added ت. Al-Jadwal (2:21) gives the starting form ٱوْتَقَىٰ. |
| ٱتَّخَذَ | أ خ ذ | أ | The hamza turns into ي, then ت, then it merges. Al-Jadwal (2:165) gives the starting form ٱئْتَخَذَ. |

Al-Jadwal says the change of و to ت is regular. In his note on 2:21 he says it holds for any verb whose first letter is و or ي, and for its participles too. His notes that I read did not explain ٱتَّبَعَ on its own. The Corpus gives its root as ت ب ع, so two ت meeting is the reading that fits.

<Note kind="know">The root of ٱتَّخَذَ is disputed. The Corpus gives أ خ ذ, and this is al-Jadwal's first view (he names al-Jawhari). He also gives Ibn al-Athīr's view: the root is ت خ ذ, and the ت is original. The pattern is ٱفْتَعَلَ either way. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn on 2:51 gives the same two views and calls the second one (Abū ʿAlī's) the easier. This lesson uses the Corpus root.</Note>

| First root letter | Added ت becomes | Verb | Starting form | Al-Jadwal on |

Here is a way to find the root of a Form VIII verb that looks changed.

1. Take off the ٱ, or the prefix in the present.
2. Look at what follows. A doubled ت, ط or د, or the letters ط or د after ص ض ز, tell you the ت changed.
3. Put the added ت back and read the first root letter in front of it.
4. Ask which root makes sense. Check the verse and the Corpus.

Form VIII does not have one fixed meaning. In the verses of this lesson you can see a few groups of meaning. They are tendencies, not rules.

- **Doing it with each other:** ٱقْتَتَلُوا۟, “they fought each other” (49:9); ٱخْتَلَفُوا۟, “they differed” (10:93); ٱجْتَمَعَتِ, “came together” (17:88).
- **Doing it for yourself, or taking it:** ٱكْتَسَبَتْ, “it earned” (2:286); ٱتَّخَذَ, “He took” (4:125).
- **Looking for it:** يَبْتَغُونَ, “they seek” (5:2); ٱسْتَمِعْ, “listen” (20:13).
- **No clear link to a Form I verb in the Quran:** ٱفْتَرَىٰ, “he invented”; ٱتَّقَىٰ, “he was mindful”.

The Corpus does not give meanings for these groups. They are Quran Learn's reading of the verses. Learn each verb with its verse, and do not guess a meaning from the form alone.

Two kinds of noun come from a Form VIII verb. You will study them in full later.

- **The active participle**, “the one who does it”, has the shape **مُفْتَعِل** (*muftaʿil*). Al-Jadwal says it takes the present verb, changes the prefix into مُ and puts a kasra on the letter before the last (his notes on 2:2 and 2:16). So يَخْتَلِفُ gives مُخْتَلِف. L3.19 teaches participles.
- **The verbal noun**, “the doing of it”, has the shape **ٱفْتِعَال** (*iftiʿāl*): a kasra on the third letter and an alif before the last. ٱخْتِلَٰف is the verbal noun of ٱخْتَلَفَ (al-Jadwal on 2:164). L3.20 teaches verbal nouns.

The same letter changes happen here. Al-Jadwal says the change of و to ت in ٱتَّقَىٰ also shows in مُتَّقِينَ (2:2). The Corpus has 148 active participle words, 19 passive participle words and 31 verbal noun words tagged (VIII). The most common is مُتَّقِين, with 49 words. These are Quran Learn's counts.

1. **Counting the added ت as a root letter.** The root of ٱخْتَلَفَ is خ ل ف, three letters. The ت is the pattern's.
2. **Looking for the ت in the wrong place.** It comes right after the first root letter, not at the end.
3. **Calling every verb with سْتَ Form X.** The root decides. ٱسْتَمَعَ is Form VIII (س م ع) and ٱسْتَغْفَرَ is Form X (غ ف ر). The same is true of نَسْتَبِقُ (12:17).
4. **Keeping the ٱ in the present.** The prefix takes its place: يَخْتَلِفُ.
5. **Saying the ٱ after وَ or فَ.** You skip it: *wa-ttabaʿa*, *fa-khtalafa*.
6. **Taking the doubled ت of ٱتَّ as the first root letter.** It can hide ت, و or أ: ٱتَّبَعَ, ٱتَّقَىٰ, ٱتَّخَذَ.
7. **Looking for ط as a root letter in ٱصْطَفَىٰ.** The ط is the changed ت. The root is ص ف و.
8. **Looking for د in ٱزْدَادَ as a root letter.** The د is the changed ت, and the root is ز ي د.
9. **Giving Form VIII one meaning.** The verb ٱتَّقَىٰ does not mean “for yourself” in the way ٱكْتَسَبَ does. Read the verse.
10. **Reading a passive as an active.** ٱضْطُرَّ and ٱخْتُلِفَ have a ḍamma and are passive (L3.09).
11. **Expecting every Form VIII verb to be regular.** Roots with و or ي or a doubled letter change shape. 448 of the 963 words have a weak last letter.

        { kind: 'm', text: 'ٱ + first letter', small: 'ٱخْ' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'added ت', small: 'تَ' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'other two letters', small: 'لَفَ' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'Form VIII', small: 'ٱخْتَلَفَ' },

Work out each answer first. Then open the answer. Exercises 3 and 11 use patterns, not verse quotes.

<Exercise n={1} q="Which are Form VIII? (a) ٱجْتَمَعَتِ (Al-Isra 17:88) (b) ٱنفَطَرَتْ (Al-Infitar 82:1) (c) ٱسْتَكْبَرَ (Al-Baqarah 2:34) (d) ٱقْتَرَبَ (Al-Anbiya 21:1)">

Take off ٱ and look for an added ت after the first letter.

- (a) **Form VIII.** Take off ٱ and the ت: ج م ع is left.
- (b) Not Form VIII. It starts with ٱنْ and has no ت: Form VII, root ف ط ر ([L3.18](/learn/level-3/18-verb-forms-vi-vii-ix/)).
- (c) Not Form VIII. It starts with ٱسْتَ: Form X, root ك ب ر ([L3.16](/learn/level-3/16-verb-form-x/)).
- (d) **Form VIII.** Take off ٱ and the ت: ق ر ب is left.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Give the root of each Form VIII verb. (a) ٱكْتَسَبَتْ (Al-Baqarah 2:286) (b) فَٱسْتَمِعْ (Taha 20:13) (c) يَبْتَغُونَ (Al-Maida 5:2) (d) ٱخْتَرْتُكَ (Taha 20:13)">

- (a) **ك س ب.** Take off ٱ and the ت after ك.
- (b) **س م ع.** The little word فَ is not part of the verb. The س is a root letter, and the ت after it is added.
- (c) **ب غ ي.** The ي is the last root letter. It does not show before the wāw.
- (d) **خ ي ر.** The ي is hidden: the alif of ٱخْتَارَ was dropped before the ending (Step 3).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Give the present for “he” and the command for one man of each verb. (a) ٱكْتَسَبَ (b) ٱسْتَمَعَ (c) ٱقْتَرَبَ. These are patterns.">

Drop the ٱ, add يَ, put a kasra on the second root letter. For the command, drop the prefix of the “you” form and add ٱ.

- (a) يَكْتَسِبُ, command ٱكْتَسِبْ.
- (b) يَسْتَمِعُ, command ٱسْتَمِعْ (20:13 has فَٱسْتَمِعْ).
- (c) يَقْتَرِبُ, command ٱقْتَرِبْ (96:19 has وَٱقْتَرِب, from L3.08).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="How do you say these aloud? (a) وَٱتَّبَعَ (An-Nisa 4:125) (b) فَٱصْطَادُوا۟ (Al-Maida 5:2) (c) أَصْطَفَى (As-Saffat 37:153) (d) ٱتَّبِعُوا۟ (Al-Araf 7:3, at the start)">

- (a) *wa-ttabaʿa*. The ٱ is skipped after وَ.
- (b) *fa-ṣṭādū*. The ٱ is skipped after فَ. The ط is the heavy sound.
- (c) *aṣṭafā*. The ٱ is not written, because the question أَ is in front.
- (d) *ittabiʿū*. At the start of a sentence, the helper is said with i.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Undo the change. Give the starting form and the root. (a) فَٱصْطَادُوا۟ (Al-Maida 5:2) (b) ٱزْدَادُوا۟ كُفْرًا (Al-Imran 3:90) (c) ٱضْطُرَّ (Al-Baqarah 2:173) (d) ٱتَّقَىٰ (Al-Layl 92:5) (e) ٱدَّكَرَ (Yusuf 12:45)">

- (a) ٱصْتَادُوا۟, root **ص ي د**. The ت became ط after ص.
- (b) The ت became د after ز. Root **ز ي د**. Al-Jadwal also says the ي changed into an alif.
- (c) ٱضْتُرَّ, root **ض ر ر**. The ت became ط after ض. It is a passive.
- (d) ٱوْتَقَىٰ, root **و ق ي**. The و became ت and merged (al-Jadwal on 2:21).
- (e) ٱذْتَكَرَ, root **ذ ك ر**. The ت became د, then the ذ became د, and the two merged (al-Jadwal on 12:45).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="In this verse the root ك س ب comes twice: لَهَا مَا كَسَبَتْ وَعَلَيْهَا مَا ٱكْتَسَبَتْ (Al-Baqarah 2:286). Which is Form VIII? What does al-Jadwal say about the difference?">

**ٱكْتَسَبَتْ** is Form VIII. Al-Jadwal says كَسَبَتْ goes with good and ٱكْتَسَبَتْ with bad in this verse. It is his reading of this verse. Both verbs mean “earned”, so do not turn it into a rule for every pair of Form I and Form VIII.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="These six words are in Al-Maida 5:2. Which are Form VIII? (a) يَبْتَغُونَ (b) تُحِلُّوا۟ (c) فَٱصْطَادُوا۟ (d) تَعْتَدُوا۟ (e) وَتَعَاوَنُوا۟ (f) وَٱتَّقُوا۟ (Al-Maida 5:2)">

- (a) **VIII**, root ب غ ي.
- (b) Not VIII. It is Form IV, from أَحَلَّ, with the doubled ل of the root ح ل ل.
- (c) **VIII**, root ص ي د.
- (d) **VIII**, root ع د و.
- (e) Not VIII. It is Form VI (L3.18). It starts with تَ and a long ā.
- (f) **VIII**, root و ق ي.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Which weak letter does each verb have, and which lesson teaches it? (a) ٱتَّقَىٰ (b) ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ (Al-Baqarah 2:16) (c) ٱخْتَرْتُكَ (Taha 20:13)">

- (a) ٱتَّقَىٰ, root و ق ي: a first و (it became ت) and a last ي. L4.14 and L4.13.
- (b) ٱشْتَرَوُا۟, root ش ر ي: a last ي. L4.13.
- (c) ٱخْتَرْتُكَ, root خ ي ر: a middle ي. L4.12.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Go through this phrase word by word: وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ (Al-Maida 5:2)">

- وَ is a little word that joins this command to the ones before.
- **ٱتَّقُوا۟** is a command, Form VIII, root و ق ي. It is built on a dropped nūn, and the و is the doer, “you all”. Al-Jadwal: “like” the command of تَعَاوَنُوا۟.
- **ٱللَّهَ** is the object (mafʿūl bihi): accusative, with a fatḥa.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Why is ٱسْتَمَعَ Form VIII and ٱسْتَغْفَرَ Form X? فَٱسْتَمِعْ (Taha 20:13) and وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ (An-Nasr 110:3)">

In ٱسْتَمِعْ only the ت is added: ٱ, س, ت, then م ع. The root is س م ع, three letters. In ٱسْتَغْفِرْ the whole سْتَ is added, and غ ف ر is what is left. The root decides.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={11} q="Make the active participle of each verb from its present. (a) يَجْتَمِعُ (b) يَنتَظِرُ (c) يَتَّخِذُ. These are patterns.">

Change the prefix into مُ. Keep the kasra on the letter before the last.

- (a) مُجْتَمِعٌ
- (b) مُنتَظِرٌ (the Corpus has مُنتَظِرُونَ in 6:158)
- (c) مُتَّخِذٌ (the Corpus has the word in 4:25)

L3.19 teaches participles.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={12} q="Read: إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ بَعْدَ إِيمَٰنِهِمْ ثُمَّ ٱزْدَادُوا۟ كُفْرًا (Al-Imran 3:90). Which verb is Form VIII, what is its root and what happened to the ت?">

**ٱزْدَادُوا۟** is Form VIII, with the root ز ي د. The ت turned into د after ز. The ي turned into an alif. The meaning is “they went on to add to their rejection”. Al-Jadwal reads it as a past verb with the wāw as its doer, and كُفْرًا as a noun that explains what increased.

</Exercise>

  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="joining alif, first root letter, an added ت and the other two root letters make a Form VIII verb"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'ٱ + first letter', small: 'ٱخْ' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'added ت', small: 'تَ' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'other two letters', small: 'لَفَ' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'Form VIII', small: 'ٱخْتَلَفَ' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- Form VIII is the pattern ٱفْتَعَلَ: ٱ in front, and an added ت right after the first root letter. The Corpus tags it (VIII), and it has 963 verb words.
- The past is ٱخْتَلَفَ, the present is يَخْتَلِفُ (no ٱ, a kasra on the second root letter) and the command is ٱتَّبِعْ (ٱ comes back and sounds i).
- The ٱ is said at the start, skipped after وَ and فَ, and not written after a question أَ.
- The ت changes: it becomes ط after ص ض ط, د after ز د ذ, and it merges after ت و أ into ٱتَّ.
- The root decides, not the first letters. ٱسْتَمَعَ is Form VIII and ٱسْتَغْفَرَ is Form X.
- The meaning is shown by the verse. The shape alone does not give it.
- Words from the same verbs: the participle مُفْتَعِل and the verbal noun ٱفْتِعَال. Weak verbs (ٱتَّقَىٰ, ٱشْتَرَىٰ, ٱخْتَارَ) are in Level 4.
