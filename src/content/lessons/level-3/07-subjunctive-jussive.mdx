The verse speaks about the Book. The verb is يَجْعَل, “he puts”. Its root is ج ع ل. The plain present-tense form is يَجْعَلُ, with -u at the end (L3.06).

Here the last letter has a **sukūn**, the mark for “no vowel”. The little word لَمْ stands in front. It changed the ending, and it moved the meaning to the past: “has not put”.

This lesson is about that change. A few little words come in front of a present-tense verb and change its last sound.

The three forms are made from one verb. The little word in front and the last sound are the two differences.

| Ending | Arabic name | The last letter | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plain | رَفْع (rafʿ) | -u | يَجْعَلُ |
| Naṣb, the subjunctive | نَصْب (naṣb) | -a | لَن يَجْعَلَ |
| Jazm, the jussive | جَزْم (jazm) | no vowel | لَمْ يَجْعَلْ |

The grammar books use the same words, *rafʿ* and *naṣb*, for the cases of nouns and for the endings of verbs. In L2.01, L2.04 and L2.05 you met them on nouns. On a verb the books speak of its **mood**. Nouns have no jazm.

The examples above are made-up combinations of a real verb from 18:1. The next steps show each ending in verses.

</Block>

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah*, the short grammar book from L2.01, says four things about the present tense.

- The present tense is **marfūʿ**, with the plain ending, until a word of naṣb or a word of jazm comes in front of it.
- It counts **ten** words of naṣb. This lesson teaches five of them: أَنْ, لَنْ, كَيْ, لِ (the book’s “lām of kay”, لَامُ كَيْ) and حَتَّى.
- It counts **eighteen** words of jazm. This lesson teaches لَمْ, لَا of prohibition and the lām of command. The conditional words إِنْ, مَا and مَنْ are a preview. L4.03 and L4.04 teach them. The other words in the book’s lists are left out here.
- A verb can be marfūʿ, naṣb or jazm. It has no khafḍ, the genitive. A noun is the other way round: it has no jazm.

The table uses a real verb, يَجْعَلُ, a Form I verb. It shows that a sukūn is one sign among several. The same mood shows itself in four ways, depending on the kind of verb.

| Kind of verb | Plain | Naṣb (after لَن) | Jazm (after لَمْ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| One person | يَجْعَلُ | يَجْعَلَ | يَجْعَلْ |
| The five verbs | تَجْعَلُونَ | تَجْعَلُوا۟ | تَجْعَلُوا۟ |
| Women, many | يَجْعَلْنَ | يَجْعَلْنَ | يَجْعَلْنَ |
| Weak last letter ي | يَمْشِي | يَمْشِيَ | يَمْشِ |
| Weak last letter و | يَدْعُو | يَدْعُوَ | يَدْعُ |
| Weak last letter, long ā | يَخْشَىٰ | يَخْشَىٰ | يَخْشَ |

- **One person** (he, she, you (m), I, we): -u becomes -a in naṣb and a sukūn in jazm.
- **The five verbs** end in a nūn. The book lists them as يَفْعَلَانِ، تَفْعَلَانِ، يَفْعَلُونَ، تَفْعَلُونَ، تَفْعَلِينَ. In naṣb and in jazm the nūn is lost. In the table: يَجْعَلَا، تَجْعَلَا، يَجْعَلُوا۟، تَجْعَلُوا۟، تَجْعَلِي.
- **The women’s plural** (they (f), you all (f)) has -na with a sukūn before it. It looks the same in all three moods.
- **A weak last letter** shows naṣb with a visible -a on ي and و, a hidden -a on a long ā, and drops out in jazm. L4.11 to L4.13 teach weak verbs.

The letter لِ at the front of a verb can mean “so that”. It is written joined to the verb: لِ + تُخْرِجَ. The verb ends in **-a**.

Al-Jadwal calls this لِ the lām of reason. It says the -a comes from an أَنْ that is hidden right after the lām. So لِتُخْرِجَ works like “so that you bring out”, with an أَنْ understood between the lām and the verb.

This is not the preposition لِ of L2.07, which stands before a noun, and it is not the lām of command that you meet in Step 3.

The “you all” form is one of the five verbs. In its plain form it ends in a nūn: تَنَالُونَ, تُنفِقُونَ, تُحِبُّونَ. After a word of naṣb the **nūn is lost**.

In this verse, لَن takes the nūn off تَنَالُونَ, and حَتَّىٰ takes it off تُنفِقُونَ. The third verb, تُحِبُّونَ, keeps its nūn, because no word of naṣb or jazm stands right before it. The wāw is the doer, “you all” (L2.04). The alif after it is silent.

So a verb like تَنَالُوا۟ is not a misprint or a noun. The missing nūn is the sign of naṣb. L2.05 named this sign.

<Note kind="know">The book lists حَتَّى among the words of naṣb. For the reader the result is the same: the verb after حَتَّىٰ is in naṣb.</Note>

<Step id="jazm" label="Jazm" title="Words that cut the verb short (jazm)">
<Block id="signs" title="The two signs of jazm, as the book says it">
  <Stage label="From al-Ajurrumiyyah">
    <blockquote class="lx-quote lx-quote-list" lang="ar" dir="rtl">
      <p>وَلِلْجَزْمِ عَلَامَتَانِ: السُّكُونُ وَالْحَذْفُ</p>
      <p>فَأَمَّا السُّكُونُ فَيَكُونُ عَلَامَةً لِلْجَزْمِ فِي الْفِعْلِ الْمُضَارِعِ الصَّحِيحِ الْآخِرِ</p>

    </blockquote>
    <p class="lx-quote-note"><i>Al-Ajurrumiyyah</i>, chapter on the signs of iʿrāb. Vowel marks added by Quran Learn.</p>

The book says jazm has **two signs**.

- The **sukūn**, on a present-tense verb whose last letter is sound (not a weak letter).
- The **dropping** of a letter: the last weak letter of a weak verb, and the nūn of the five verbs, whose plain ending is a nūn that stays.

You saw both in the table of Step 1. The rest of this step shows the little words that bring jazm, with verses.
</Block>

لَمْ is the word of jazm you meet most. It does two things.

- It puts the verb in **jazm**.
- It moves the meaning to the **past**. The verb has a present-tense form, but لَمْ تَرَوْهَا means “you did not see them”.

Al-Jadwal calls لَمْ a little word of negation, of “turning” and of jazm (2:24). The “turning” is the change to the past.

Here the verb is one of the five. The nūn of تَرَوْنَ is gone, so it ends in a wāw and then the attached هَا, “them”. With a sound verb of one person, لَمْ gives a sukūn, as in 18:1 at the start of this lesson.

The Corpus tags لَمْ 353 times in the whole Quran. This is Quran Learn’s count by script on the Corpus file.

        { ar: 'ٱقْرَأْ', en: 'read! (96:1)', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A ban such as لَا تَحْزَنْ ends in a sukūn, and so does a command such as ٱقْرَأْ. They are different forms. The jazm verb keeps its prefix (the تَ of تَحْزَنْ). A command has no prefix. L3.08 teaches commands.

<Block id="mistake-lam-lan" title="Mistake 2: using لَمْ for the future">
  <Stage label="Made-up example">
    <Tiles
      label="لَمْ يَجْعَلْ is past, لَن يَجْعَلَ is future"
      items={[
        { ar: 'لَمْ يَجْعَلْ', en: 'he did not put', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'لَن يَجْعَلَ', en: 'he will not put', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Both say “not”, and both can go in front of the same verb. لَمْ is jazm and points to the past. لَن is naṣb and points to the future.

The five verbs hide the difference: after either word they lose the nūn. Then the meaning and the little word tell you the mood. يَجْعَلُوا۟ after لَمْ is “they did not put”. After لَن it is “they will not put”.

</Block>

<Block id="mistake-nun" title="Mistake 3: thinking a missing nūn is a misprint">
  <Stage label="Same verb, two endings">
    <Tiles
      label="تُحِبُّونَ keeps its nūn, تُنفِقُوا۟ lost it"
      items={[
        { ar: 'تُحِبُّونَ', en: 'you love (3:92)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'تُنفِقُوا۟', en: 'you spend (3:92)', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A reader who knows the plain form تُنفِقُونَ may think the nūn fell out of تُنفِقُوا۟. Nothing fell out. The nūn is the plain ending of the five verbs, and a word of naṣb or jazm takes it away. When you see a verb of this kind without its nūn, look for the little word in front of it.

The same letters also appear on nouns. A noun ending in -ūna is a plural and shows a case (L2.04). A verb ending in -ūna is a plain verb with a wāw as its doer.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-lam-letters" title="Mistake 4: mixing the two lām and the two lā">
  <Stage label="Look at the end of the verb">
    <Tiles
      label="لِتُخْرِجَ ends in -a, لِيُنفِقْ ends in a sukūn"
      items={[
        { ar: 'لِتُخْرِجَ', en: 'so that you bring out (14:1)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'لِيُنفِقْ', en: 'let him spend (65:7)', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The letter لِ before a verb has two jobs, and لَا has two jobs. The first letters of the verb look alike, so check the end of the verb.

- لِ and a verb in **-a**: “so that”.
- لِ and a verb in a **sukūn**: “let him”.
- لَا and a verb with the plain ending: “does not”.
- لَا and a verb in jazm: “do not”.

Both lām are different again from the preposition لِ before a noun (L2.07).
</Block>

  <Stage label="Three signs of jazm">

Naṣb has its own signs: -a, a lost nūn, a hidden -a. The little word in front shows which mood you have when the signs look alike.
</Block>

<Exercise n={1} q="Name the mood of each verb. These are made-up forms of the verb يَجْعَلُ. (a) يَجْعَلُ (b) لَن يَجْعَلَ (c) لَمْ يَجْعَلْ (d) تَجْعَلُونَ (e) لَمْ تَجْعَلُوا۟ (f) لَن تَجْعَلُوا۟">

- (a) **Plain.** It ends in -u and nothing stands in front.
- (b) **Naṣb.** لَن stands in front, and the verb ends in -a.
- (c) **Jazm.** لَمْ stands in front, and the verb ends in a sukūn.
- (d) **Plain.** One of the five verbs, with its nūn.
- (e) **Jazm.** The nūn is lost after لَمْ. It means “you all did not put”.
- (f) **Naṣb.** The nūn is lost after لَن. It looks the same as (e). The word in front decides. It means “you all will not put”.

</Exercise>

لَن brings the naṣb. The verb is one of the five, “they wish”: the plain form is يَتَمَنَّوْنَ. After لَن the **nūn is lost**, so you see يَتَمَنَّوْا. The ـهُ at the end is the attached “it”, so the form is يَتَمَنَّوْهُ. The wāw is the doer, “they”.

<Exercise n={4} q="Say the mood of the verb in each sentence. These are made-up examples. (a) هُوَ يَكْتُبُ ٱلرِّسَالَةَ (b) لَمْ يَكْتُبْ ٱلرِّسَالَةَ (c) لَن يَكْتُبَ ٱلرِّسَالَةَ">

- (b) “He did not write the letter.” **Jazm**, a sukūn, after لَمْ. The meaning is in the past.

- A present-tense verb has the plain ending until a little word of naṣb or jazm comes in front of it.
- **Naṣb** comes after أَنْ, لَن, كَيْ, لِ (“so that”) and حَتَّىٰ. One person ends in -a. The five verbs lose the nūn. A long ā hides the -a.
- **Jazm** comes after لَمْ, لَا of a ban, the lām of command and, as a preview, the words of condition. One person ends in a sukūn. The five verbs lose the nūn. A weak last letter drops.
- The women’s plural looks the same in all three moods. The five verbs look the same in naṣb and jazm. Read the little word.
- لَمْ means “did not” and لَن means “will not”.
- With لَا, the verb’s ending tells a ban (jazm) from a statement (plain ending).
- When a verb has an unusual ending (a -a, a sukūn or no nūn), look for the little word in front of it.
