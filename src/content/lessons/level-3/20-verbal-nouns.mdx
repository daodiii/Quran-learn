ٱلْحَمْدُ is the first word of verse 2 of Al-Fatiha, right after the Bismillah. It means “praise”: the act of praising. It does not mean “the one who praises”, and it does not mean “the one who is praised”.

A word that names an action is called a **verbal noun**, مَصْدَر (*maṣdar*). The Arabic word means “source”. This lesson says “verbal noun”. It says *maṣdar* when it quotes the grammar books.

L3.04 left ٱلْحَمْدُ for this lesson, because it is a noun and not a verb. Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus: the Quran has 668 verbal nouns.

From one root, a noun pattern can give three different words.

- **The doer:** ٱلْحَٰمِدُونَ (At-Tawbah 9:112), “those who praise”. L3.19 teaches it.
- **The one done to:** مَّحْمُودًا (Al-Isra 17:79), “praised”. L3.19 teaches it too.
- **The action itself:** ٱلْحَمْدُ (Al-Fatiha 1:2), “praise”. This lesson teaches it.

L1.05 and L3.03 showed the same three jobs for the root ك ت ب: كَاتِبٌ (2:282), مَكْتُوبًا (7:157) and كِتَٰبًا (78:29). To tell them apart, ask what the word names. A person who does it, a thing it happens to, or the action itself?

<Note kind="tip">A way to remember it. The doer word is the actor. The done-to word is what the action happens to. The verbal noun is the name of the action.</Note>

A verb says who and when: كَتَبَ, “he wrote”. A verbal noun says what: “writing”. It has no tense and no doer inside it.

It is a noun, so it does what a noun does.

- It takes **al-**: ٱلْحَمْدُ (1:2).
- It takes tanwīn: كِتَٰبًا (78:29).
- It takes a case ending, as ٱلْإِيمَٰنُ, ٱلْإِيمَٰنَ and ٱلْإِيمَٰنِ show (L2.04 to L2.06).
- It can be the first word of an idafah: تَنزِيلُ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ (39:1), “the sending down of the Book” (L2.08).

<Note kind="remember">A verbal noun names an action. It has no time and no doer. It is a noun, so it has a case.</Note>

Form I is the plain three-letter verb (L3.04). Its verbal nouns come in many patterns, and no rule says which verb takes which pattern. You meet each one and learn it. The grammar books call this kind of verbal noun **heard**, سَمَاعِيّ (*samāʿī*).

Al-Jadwal’s ṣarf note on ٱلْحَمْدُ (1:2) uses this word. It says «مصدر سماعي» of the verb حَمَدَ يَحْمُدُ, a verb of *bāb naṣara* (L3.04), and gives the pattern as «فعل بفتح فسكون»: فَعْل, a fatḥa and then a sukūn.

So ḥ-m-d gives *ḥamd* on the pattern فَعْل. The other two words above have other patterns, as the next block shows.

Because the patterns are heard, one verb can have more than one verbal noun. Al-Jadwal gives three cases.

- **تَابَ يَتُوبُ**, “he turned back”. On 40:3 it says ٱلتَّوْب is «مصدر سماعي» and that تَوْبَة is “like it”. On 4:17 it calls ٱلتَّوْبَة the verbal noun of تَابَ.
- **جَهِلَ يَجْهَلُ**, “he did not know”. On 4:17 it says جَهَالَة is a heard verbal noun on فَعَالَة, and that the verb has another one, جَهْل.
- **مَالَ يَمِيلُ**, “he leaned”. On 4:27 it gives مَيْل on فَعْل and then lists five more verbal nouns of the same verb.

The same root can also use two doors of Form I. On 2:256 al-Jadwal says ٱلرُّشْد (فُعْل) is the verbal noun of a *bāb naṣara* verb, and ٱلرَّشَد (فَعَل) is the verbal noun of a *bāb faraḥa* verb.

</Block>

Some Form I verbal nouns start with مَ. Al-Jadwal calls them «مصدر ميمي», a verbal noun with a mīm. On 2:207 it says مَرْضَاة is a mīm verbal noun of رَضِيَ on the pattern مَفْعِلَة. On 9:114 it says the same of مَوْعِدَة, from وَعَدَ.

L3.03 listed مَغْفِرَة as a verbal noun. It has the same shape.

These look like the nouns of place and time, مَفْعَل, which L3.21 teaches. The meaning tells them apart: مَوْعِدَة names the action of promising. It does not name a place.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal also has a name for a noun that stands in for a verbal noun but has fewer letters than its verb. On 2:184 it calls طَعَام an *ism maṣdar*, “a noun of the verbal-noun kind”. The regular verbal noun of أَطْعَمَ is إِطْعَام.</Note>

A verbal noun can become an ordinary word with its own meaning.

- On 1:2 al-Jadwal says رَبّ is a verbal noun of رَبَّ يَرُبُّ that came to be used as a describing word, “like عَدْل and خَصْم”.
- On 78:26 it says وِفَاق is a heard verbal noun used in the place of an adjective, to make it stronger: a “fitting” reward.
- On 2:2, L3.03 noted that al-Jadwal calls ٱلْكِتَٰب a fixed noun taken from the action name.

So the meaning of a word can move away from “the action”. Check the meaning in the verse.

This verse has two verbal nouns. They are built in different ways.

- **حَرَجًا** is Form I. Al-Jadwal says it is «مصدر سماعي» of حَرِجَ, on فَعَل. You have to know it.
- **تَسْلِيمًا** comes from سَلَّمَ, which is Form II (L3.12). Al-Jadwal says «مصدر قياسي»: a **regular** verbal noun, on تَفْعِيل.

Regular, قِيَاسِيّ (*qiyāsī*), means the form decides. If you know the form of the verb, you know the pattern of its verbal noun.

Al-Jadwal gives the pattern of each Quran word in its ṣarf notes. It gives the rule in words for two of them.

- **Form X**, on 9:114: the verbal noun is on the pattern of its past verb, «بكسر ثالثه وزيادة ألف قبل الآخر». That means a kasra on the third letter and an alif before the last letter: ٱسْتَغْفَرَ becomes ٱسْتِغْفَار.
- **Form VII**, on 2:256: the same rule. ٱنفَصَمَ becomes ٱنفِصَام. Form VIII follows the same shape: ٱفْتَعَلَ becomes ٱفْتِعَال.
- **Form V**, on 33:33: «تفعّل بوزن الماضي وضمّ ما قبل الآخر». The verbal noun has the shape of the past verb with a ḍamma on the letter before the last: تَبَرَّجَ becomes تَبَرُّج.

The Corpus file has no Form IX verbal noun tagged. L3.18 teaches Forms VI, VII and IX.

L3.07 taught أَنْ as a word of naṣb. It has a second job: it turns the clause after it into a noun.

In this verse, أَن تَعْفُوا is the mubtadaʾ, and the khabar is أَقْرَبُ. But a clause has no case, and a mubtadaʾ is a noun. Al-Jadwal solves this by reading the clause as a noun. It calls أَنْ «حرف مصدري ونصب», a verbal-noun particle that brings naṣb. It calls أَن تَعْفُوا «المصدر المؤوّل», the verbal noun read into the sentence, and says it stands in the place of the nominative, mubtadaʾ: «أي عفوكم», “that is, your forgiving”.

So the sentence says: “Your forgiving is nearer to fearing Allah.” The clause works as the verbal noun عَفْو (*ʿafw*), “forgiving”.

Other little words make a verbal noun from a clause too. L3.11 showed مَا in 2:10. In 9:114, أَنَّهُۥ عَدُوٌّ لِّلَّهِ (“that he was an enemy of Allah”) is read as a verbal noun in the place of a doer, as al-Jadwal says.

<Note kind="know">The word *muʾawwal* means “interpreted”. A *maṣdar muʾawwal* is not a word in the verse. It is the noun that you read into the sentence in place of the little word and the verb.</Note>

<Exercise n={1} q="Which of these name the action itself? Say what the others are. (a) ٱلْحَمْدُ (Al-Fatiha 1:2) (b) ٱلْحَٰمِدُونَ (At-Tawbah 9:112) (c) مَّحْمُودًا (Al-Isra 17:79) (d) كِتَٰبًا (An-Naba 78:29)">

- (a) **ٱلْحَمْدُ** names the action: praise. It is a verbal noun on فَعْل.
- (b) **ٱلْحَٰمِدُونَ** is a doer word: “those who praise”. L3.19 teaches it.
- (c) **مَّحْمُودًا** is a done-to word: “praised”. L3.19 teaches it.
- (d) **كِتَٰبًا** names the action: writing. Al-Jadwal says it means “writing”, or “counting up”, on the pattern فِعَال.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Name the form of the verb and the pattern of each verbal noun. (a) تَسْلِيمًا (An-Nisa 4:65) (b) ٱسْتِغْفَارُ (At-Tawbah 9:114) (c) ٱنفِصَامَ (Al-Baqarah 2:256) (d) تَبَرُّجَ (Al-Ahzab 33:33) (e) إِيتَآئِ (An-Nahl 16:90)">

- (a) **Form II**, from سَلَّمَ. Pattern تَفْعِيل. Al-Jadwal: «مصدر قياسي».
- (b) **Form X**, from ٱسْتَغْفَرَ. Pattern ٱسْتِفْعَال.
- (c) **Form VII**, from ٱنفَصَمَ. Pattern ٱنْفِعَال.
- (d) **Form V**, from تَبَرَّجَ. Pattern تَفَعُّل.
- (e) **Form IV**, from ءَاتَى. Pattern إِفْعَال. Al-Jadwal: «مصدر قياسي».

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Use the rule to build the verbal noun of each verb. (a) أَسْلَمَ (Form IV) (b) سَبَّحَ (Form II) (c) تَضَرَّعَ (Form V) (d) ٱخْتَلَفَ (Form VIII) (e) ٱسْتَكْبَرَ (Form X) (f) عَبَدَ (Form I). These are made-up exercises; the answers are Quran words.">

- (a) **إِسْلَام**, on إِفْعَال (3:19).
- (b) **تَسْبِيح**, on تَفْعِيل (17:44, تَسْبِيحَهُمْ).
- (c) **تَضَرُّع**, on تَفَعُّل (6:63, تَضَرُّعًا).
- (d) **ٱخْتِلَاف**, on ٱفْتِعَال: a kasra on the third letter and an alif before the last (2:164, ٱخْتِلَٰفِ).
- (e) **ٱسْتِكْبَار**, on ٱسْتِفْعَال (35:43, ٱسْتِكْبَارًا).
- (f) **عِبَادَة** (18:110, بِعِبَادَةِ). You cannot build this one by rule, because the verb is Form I. You learn the pattern with the word. Al-Jadwal gives no ṣarf note on it in this verse.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Say each one as a verbal noun, and give its job. (a) وَأَن تَصُومُوا۟ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ (Al-Baqarah 2:184, end) (b) يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ أَن يُخَفِّفَ عَنكُمْ (An-Nisa 4:28, start)">

- (a) **أَن تَصُومُوا** is “your fasting”: al-Jadwal says «أي صيامكم». It is the mubtadaʾ, in the place of the nominative. The khabar is خَيْرٌ.
- (b) **أَن يُخَفِّفَ** is “a lightening”. Al-Jadwal says it stands in the place of the accusative, as the object of يُرِيدُ: Allah wants a lightening for you.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Give the job and case of each verbal noun. (a) تَنزِيلُ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْحَكِيمِ (Az-Zumar 39:1) (b) إِنَّ ٱلدِّينَ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلْإِسْلَٰمُ (Al-Imran 3:19, start) (c) قُلْ قِتَالٌ فِيهِ كَبِيرٌ (Al-Baqarah 2:217, part) (d) وَمَا كَانَ ٱسْتِغْفَارُ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ (At-Tawbah 9:114, start)">

- (a) **تَنزِيلُ** is the mubtadaʾ, nominative (-u), and the first word of an idafah with ٱلْكِتَٰبِ. Al-Jadwal also allows a second reading: the khabar of a left-out mubtadaʾ.
- (b) **ٱلْإِسْلَٰمُ** is the khabar of *inna*, nominative (-u).
- (c) **قِتَالٌ** is the mubtadaʾ, nominative (-un). فِيهِ may be tied to it. كَبِيرٌ is the khabar.
- (d) **ٱسْتِغْفَارُ** is the ism of *kāna*, nominative (-u), and the first word of an idafah with إِبْرَٰهِيمَ.

</Exercise>

- (a) **Heard.** Form I, on فَعَل.
- (b) **Regular.** Form II, on تَفْعِيل.
- (c) **Heard.** Form I, on فَعَل. Al-Jadwal says «مصدر سماعي».
- (d) **Heard.** It comes from a Form III verb, but al-Jadwal calls it «مصدر سماعي» on فِعَال.

<Exercise n={7} q="Fill in the family of each root. (a) ش ك ر, with شُكْرًا (34:13), شَاكِرًا (16:121) and مَّشْكُورًا (17:19) (b) ك ت ب, with كَاتِبٌ (2:282), مَكْتُوبًا (7:157) and كِتَٰبًا (78:29) (c) ح م د, with ٱلْحَٰمِدُونَ (9:112), مَّحْمُودًا (17:79) and ٱلْحَمْدُ (1:2)">

- (c) **ح م د.** Verb حَمَدَ, “he praised”. Doer حَامِد. Done-to مَحْمُود. Verbal noun **حَمْد**, on فَعْل (al-Jadwal, 1:2).

  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Tiles
      label="كَتَبَ, كَاتِبٌ, مَكْتُوبًا, كِتَٰبًا"
      items={[

        { ar: 'مَكْتُوبًا', en: 'done-to' },

- A **verbal noun**, مَصْدَر, names an action. It is a noun: it has no time and no doer, and it takes al-, tanwīn and a case. Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus: 668 in the Quran.
- **Form I** verbal nouns come in many patterns, learnt by hearing: «مصدر سماعي». Examples are فَعْل (ٱلْحَمْدُ), فَعَل (حَرَجًا), فُعْل (شُكْرًا), فُعُول (ٱلسُّجُودِ), فِعَال (كِتَٰبًا) and فَعَالَة (جَهَٰلَةٍ). One verb can have more than one.
- **Forms II to X** have one regular pattern each: تَفْعِيل, إِفْعَال, تَفَعُّل, تَفَاعُل, ٱنْفِعَال, ٱفْتِعَال, ٱسْتِفْعَال. Form III has مُفَاعَلَة or فِعَال, and a few derived verbal nouns are heard.
- A verbal noun can be a mubtadaʾ, a khabar, an ism of *kāna*, or the word after a preposition, and it can carry a phrase.
- **أَنْ and a verb** are read as one verbal noun, «المصدر المؤوّل»: وَأَن تَعْفُوا means “your forgiving”.
- A verb’s own verbal noun in naṣb is the absolute object (L4.07).

**Next:** L3.21 teaches the nouns of place and time, which share the shape مَفْعَل with the mīm verbal nouns. L4.07 teaches the absolute object. L4.11 to L4.15 teach the weak verbs, whose verbal nouns change shape. If a form felt shaky, go back to L3.12 to L3.18.
