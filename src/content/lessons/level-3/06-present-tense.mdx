This question has two present-tense verbs. Read from right to left.

Both start with **ي** and both come from the same verb, عَلِمَ, “he knew”. Look at the end of each one. The first ends in **-ūna**. The second ends in **-u**.

The ending **-ūna** says “they”. The first verb is about people. The second verb is about Allah, so it has a hidden “he” (you met hidden doers in L2.03).

A present-tense verb is called فِعْل مُضَارِع (*fiʿl muḍāriʿ*). It can speak about what is true now, what happens again and again, or what will happen. The words around it tell you which.

In 13:42 the first verb, يَعْلَمُ, says what is true now: Allah knows what each soul earns. The last verb is the same verb with **سَـ** in front: وَسَيَعْلَمُ, “and will know”. Al-Jadwal calls سَـ a little word for the future. The word سَوْفَ does the same job: وَلَسَوْفَ يُعْطِيكَ, “and surely he will give you” (93:5). You met يُعْطِيكَ in L1.04.

A present-tense verb starts with one of four letters: **أ ن ي ت**. The first grammar book you met in L2.01, *al-Ajurrumiyyah*, says: “The present is the verb that has one of the four extra letters at its start, which you can gather in the word *ʾanaytu*.” The word أَنَيْتُ is just a memory aid. It has no meaning.

Each letter points to a person:

- **أَـ**: I.
- **نَـ**: we.
- **يَـ**: he, they two (men), they (men), they (women).
- **تَـ**: she, you (all kinds of “you”), they two (women).

</Block>

Take يَعْلَمُونَ apart. The prefix يَـ says “they”. The stem is the root letters ع ل م with the vowels of this verb. The ending ـُونَ says “men, more than two”.

In the past tense (L3.05) the person sits in the **ending** alone: عَلِمُوا. In the present tense it sits in the **prefix and the ending** together. Together they tell you who does the action, so the verb needs no separate pronoun (L3.01). A short pronoun can also join the very end of the verb as its **object**, like -ka in يُعْطِيكَ, “gives you” (L3.02). That -ka is not the doer.

This is the whole present tense of one verb. عَلِمَ is a Form I verb (L3.04), and the Quranic Arabic Corpus gives its root as ع ل م. The table is a teaching table: the forms follow the pattern. The next blocks show real verses.

Read down the column “Prefix and ending”. Three things to see:

- The prefix gives the person. The ending gives the number and the gender.
- Two pairs look the same: **she** and **you (one man)**, and **they two (women)** and **you two**. The sentence tells you which one is meant.
- The grammar books count 14 persons. This table has 13 rows, because “you two” is one row for men and women.

<Note kind="know">The vowel after the middle letter depends on the verb. عَلِمَ gives يَعْلَمُ (a). نَصَرَ gives يَنْصُرُ (u). ضَرَبَ gives يَضْرِبُ (i). You learn it with each verb. The prefixes and the endings stay the same on all three. Other verb forms use the same four letters, some with a ḍamma, like يُؤْمِنُونَ (L3.13).</Note>

Both verbs start with تَـ and end in -u. They have different doers.

- In 13:42, تَكْسِبُ has a written doer after it: كُلُّ نَفْسٍ, “each soul”. A soul is feminine in Arabic, so the verb means “she earns”. A verb before a written doer is singular and matches its gender (L2.03).

Look at the same verse for the last verb. أَعْلَمُ starts with أَـ, so it means “I know”. Same root, same ending, different prefix, different person.

This verse has the past and the present of the same verb for the same person. عَلِمْتَهُۥ is past: “you knew it”. The ending -ta says “you” (one man), and -hu is “it”. تَعْلَمُ is present: “you know”.

In the past, an ending shows who does it. In the present, a prefix at the front shows it, sometimes with an ending too:

| Person | Past (L3.05) | Present | Where “who” is shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| he | عَلِمَ | يَعْلَمُ | past: no ending for “he”; present: the prefix يَـ |
| she | عَلِمَتْ | تَعْلَمُ | past: the ending ـَتْ; present: the prefix تَـ |
| they (men) | عَلِمُوا | يَعْلَمُونَ | past: the ending ـُوا; present: the prefix يَـ and the ending ـُونَ |
| you (one man) | عَلِمْتَ | تَعْلَمُ | past: the ending ـْتَ; present: the prefix تَـ |
| I | عَلِمْتُ | أَعْلَمُ | past: the ending ـْتُ; present: the prefix أَـ |
| we | عَلِمْنَا | نَعْلَمُ | past: the ending ـْنَا; present: the prefix نَـ |

In 7:62 Nūḥ speaks to his people. He says أَعْلَمُ, “I know”: the prefix أَـ and no ending. At the end of the verse, تَعْلَمُونَ means “you all know”: the prefix تَـ and the ending -ūna. You may know أَعْبُدُ, “I worship”, from Surah Al-Kafirun (109:2). It has the same prefix أَـ.

The verse is about the Messiah, son of Maryam, and his mother. يَأْكُلَانِ is the root أ ك ل (“eat”) with the prefix يَـ and the ending **-āni**, “two people”. You know -āni from the dual nouns of L1.08. On a verb it means two doers.

Al-Jadwal says the alif is the doer, “they two”, and the nūn is the ending. كَانَا is “they two were” (L2.11). With a present-tense verb after it, it gives “they two used to eat”.

<Step id="ending" label="The ending" title="The ending: -u, the nūn, and what changes them">
<Block id="book" title="How the first grammar book says it">
  <Stage label="From al-Ajurrumiyyah">
    <blockquote class="lx-quote lx-quote-list" lang="ar" dir="rtl">
      <p>وَالْفِعْلُ الْمُضَارِعُ الَّذِي لَمْ يَتَّصِلْ بِآخِرِهِ شَيْءٌ</p>
      <p>وَأَمَّا النُّونُ فَتَكُونُ عَلَامَةً لِلرَّفْعِ فِي الْفِعْلِ الْمُضَارِعِ إِذَا اتَّصَلَ بِهِ ضَمِيرُ تَثْنِيَةٍ أَوْ ضَمِيرُ جَمْعٍ أَوْ ضَمِيرُ الْمُؤَنَّثَةِ الْمُخَاطَبَةِ</p>
      <p>وَالْأَفْعَالُ الْخَمْسَةُ وَهِيَ يَفْعَلَانِ وَتَفْعَلَانِ وَيَفْعَلُونَ وَتَفْعَلُونَ وَتَفْعَلِينَ</p>
      <p>وَهُوَ مَرْفُوعٌ أَبَدًا حَتَّى يَدْخُلَ عَلَيْهِ نَاصِبٌ أَوْ جَازِمٌ</p>
    </blockquote>
    <p class="lx-quote-note"><i>Al-Ajurrumiyyah</i>, in order: the verb with nothing attached at its end (its sign is the ḍamma); the nūn as a sign; the five verbs; the present stays raised until a little word comes in. Vowel marks added by Quran Learn.</p>
  </Stage>

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* says four things about the ending of the present tense:

- A present-tense verb with **nothing attached at its end** takes a **ḍamma**: -u.
- The **nūn** is the sign when a pronoun of two, of many, or of one woman “you” is attached.
- That gives **five verbs**: يَفْعَلَانِ، تَفْعَلَانِ، يَفْعَلُونَ، تَفْعَلُونَ، تَفْعَلِينَ. This is the book’s own pattern, with the dummy root ف ع ل.
- The present tense is *marfūʿ* (“raised”): it keeps this plain form until a little word comes in front of it and changes it. The book names two groups of such words, the ones that put the verb in the naṣb and the ones that put it in the jazm. L3.07 teaches them.

The next blocks show each point in a verse.
</Block>

The grammar books call a present-tense verb with the -u ending **marfūʿ**, مَرْفُوع. It is the same word they use for the nominative case of a noun. But a verb is not a noun, and **a verb does not take a case the way a noun does** (L2.04). This course calls the -u form of a verb the **plain form**: the form it has when no little word before it changes it.

Look at 2:77. The little word أَنَّ comes before ٱللَّهَ and puts that noun in the accusative: -a (L2.10). It does not put the verb in the accusative. The verb يَعْلَمُ keeps its plain -u.

These are the five verbs of *al-Ajurrumiyyah*. They end in -āni, -ūna or -īna. They have no -u at the end, because a letter sits there: the alif, the wāw or the yāʾ. That letter is the **doer**. The nūn after it is the **ending**, and it shows the plain form.

Al-Jadwal uses the same words for each verb you met in this lesson: the verb is in the plain form, and the sign is that **the nūn stays**.

- يَعْلَمُونَ (2:77): the wāw is the doer.
- يَأْكُلَانِ (5:75): the alif is the doer.
- تَعْجَبِينَ (11:73): the yāʾ is the doer.

The nūn stays because no little word has changed the verb. You saw in L2.04 that the و in يَأْمُرُونَ is a doer, not a case mark. This is the reason.

Al-Jadwal explains this: the verb is **built on a sukūn**, مَبْنِيّ, and the nūn is the doer. A built word has a fixed ending. A little word cannot change it. And the nūn cannot drop, because it is the doer.

| The ending | -ūna, -āni, -īna | -na |
|---|---|---|
| The nūn is | an ending | the doer |
| The verb is | changeable (the five verbs) | built (fixed) |
| After لَن or أَن | the nūn drops | nothing changes |

Until now, the verbs had three root letters that all showed: ع ل م. In some verbs one root letter is a **weak letter**, و or ي. A weak letter can turn into a long vowel or drop out. The word then looks different from the pattern you learned.

Two kinds are very common:

| Kind | The weak letter is | Example | Share of verbs in the Quran |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hollow | the middle letter | قَالَ, يَقُولُ | 26.7% |
| Defective | the last letter | دَعَا, يَدْعُو | 18.0% |

The shares are Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus. Together the two kinds are about 45 out of 100 verbs. You will meet them often.

The past of يَقُولُ is قَالَ, “he said”. Its root is ق و ل, and the **middle** letter is the و. In the present tense the و becomes a long **ū**: يَقُولُ. In the same way كَانَ, “he was”, has the root ك و ن and gives يَكُونُ.

Read the prefix and the ending as usual. يَـ is “he” and the ending is -u. Al-Jadwal says each doer in 2:117 is a hidden “he”, and the verb is in the plain form. The other persons follow the same prefixes: أَقُولُ (6:50), نَقُولُ and تَقُولُ (50:30).

These two verbs are very common. In Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus, قَالَ is the 7th most common word in the Quran (1,618 times), and كَانَ is the 10th (1,358 times).

Two mistakes look alike.

- **Taking تَـ for one person.** تَـ stands for “she”, for all forms of “you”, and for “they two (women)”. The shape alone does not tell you which. Look for a written doer, or for the speaker (L3.01).
- **Taking any word that starts with أ ن ي ت for a present-tense verb.** نَصَرَ, أَخَذَ and تَابَ start with prefix letters, but the first letter is part of the root. These verbs are past tense. A present-tense verb has a prefix **and** a stem, so three root letters stay when you remove the prefix. Removing the ن of نَصَرَ leaves صَرَ, two letters.
</Block>

        { ar: 'يَعْلَمُونَ', en: 'they (men)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'يَعْلَمْنَ', en: 'they (women)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'يَعْلَمَانِ', en: 'they two', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'تَعْلَمِينَ', en: 'you (one woman)', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Four endings are easy to confuse.

- **-ūna and -na.** The men’s ending has a long vowel (ـُونَ). The women’s ending has a sukūn (ـْنَ). In -na the nūn is the doer, and the form is built.
- **-āni and -ūna.** The dual ends in -āni, with an alif and a nūn that has a kasra. The plural ends in -ūna, with a wāw and a nūn that has a fatḥa.
- **-īna and -ūna.** Both end in a long vowel and a nūn. The yāʾ means one woman. The wāw means many men.

Say the ending aloud, then say who it is.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-form" title="Mistake 3: forgetting the plain form, and the time">
  <Stage label="One verb, three endings">
    <Tiles
      label="يَعْلَمُ, يَعْلَمَ and يَعْلَمْ: the plain form, after أَن, after لَمْ"
      items={[

        { ar: 'يَعْلَمَ', en: 'after أَن: that he knows', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'يَعْلَمْ', en: 'after لَمْ: he did not know', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Three more mistakes.

- **Not looking at the last letter.** -u (or the nūn that stays) is the plain form. An ending in **-a** or a **sukūn**, or a nūn that dropped, tells you that a little word has changed the verb. That changes the meaning (L3.07).
- **Thinking the present tense means just “now”.** It also covers what happens again and again and what comes later. Context decides, and سَـ and سَوْفَ say “will”.
- **Thinking all verbs copy يَعْلَمُ.** The vowel after the middle letter depends on the verb: يَنْصُرُ, يَضْرِبُ, يَعْلَمُ. The other verb forms (II to X) use the same prefix letters and endings with a different stem. L3.12 to L3.18 teach them.
</Block>

- (b) يَعْلَمْنَ: the nūn is the **doer**. The verb is built and does not change.
- (c) تَعْلَمِينَ: an **ending**.
- (d) يَعْلَمُ: **no nūn**. The plain -u shows the plain form.
- (e) تَعْلَمَانِ: an **ending**.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Look at لَن تَنَالُوا۟ ٱلْبِرَّ حَتَّىٰ تُنفِقُوا۟ مِمَّا تُحِبُّونَ (Al-Imran 3:92, start). Which verb kept its nūn? Why did the other two lose it?">

**تُحِبُّونَ** kept the nūn. No little word acts on it, so it is in the plain form.

**تَنَالُوا** and **تُنفِقُوا** lost it. لَن comes in front of the first. حَتَّىٰ, with a hidden أَنْ, acts on the second. On the five verbs, the dropped nūn is the sign of the change. L3.07 teaches these little words.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Which of these present-tense verbs are hollow, which are defective, and which are regular? Use the root. (a) يَقُولُ, root ق و ل (b) يَعْلَمُ, root ع ل م (c) يَدْعُو, root د ع و (d) يَكْتُبُ, root ك ت ب (e) يَهْدِى, root ه د ي (f) يَكُونُ, root ك و ن">

- **Hollow** (the middle root letter is و or ي): (a) يَقُولُ and (f) يَكُونُ.
- **Defective** (the last root letter is و or ي): (c) يَدْعُو and (e) يَهْدِى. Their plain -u is hidden.
- **Regular** (no weak letter): (b) يَعْلَمُ and (d) يَكْتُبُ.

Level 4 teaches how hollow and defective verbs change in each person (L4.12, L4.13).

- A present-tense verb starts with **أ ن ي ت**. It speaks about now, about what happens again and again, or about what will happen. سَـ and سَوْفَ say “will”.
- The **prefix** says who. The **ending** says how many and whether it is a man or a woman. The table has 13 forms. **تَـ** appears in two lookalike pairs.
- The plain form ends in **-u**, or in **a nūn that stays** on the five verbs (-āni, -ūna, -īna). It is what a verb has when no little word before it changes it. A verb does not take a case the way a noun does.
- A little word can change the plain form: on the five verbs the nūn drops (L3.07 teaches the rest).
- The **-na** form is built. The nūn is the doer, and nothing changes it.
- Hollow and defective verbs (about 45 out of 100 verbs in the Quran) look different. The prefixes and the endings stay. The middle or last letter changes, and the plain -u may be hidden. L4.11 to L4.13 teach them.
