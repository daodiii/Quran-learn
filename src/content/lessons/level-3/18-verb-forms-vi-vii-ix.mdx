It is part of a verse about living together. Read it from right to left.

The verb is تَعَاوَنُوا۟, “help one another”. Look at its front: it starts with تَ, and there is a long ā after the first root letter. The root is ع و ن (helping).

English needs three words, “help one another”. Arabic uses one verb with a new shape. This shape is **Form VI**.

L3.03 showed you the ten forms. Seven of them have a lesson of their own: Form I (L3.04) and Forms II, IV, VIII, V, X and III (L3.12 to L3.17). The last three are rare, so they share this lesson.

| Form | Scale word | A word from the Quran | Verb words |
|---|---|---|---|
| VI | تَفَاعَلَ | تَعَاوَنُوا۟ (5:2) | 77 |
| VII | ٱنْفَعَلَ | ٱنفَطَرَتْ (82:1) | 51 |
| IX | ٱفْعَلَّ | ٱسْوَدَّتْ (3:106) | 5 |

The three forms together are 133 verb words. Form VIII alone has 963 (L3.14). These counts are Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus.

So the aim here is to **recognise** these forms: see the shape, find the root, and know what the form often means. You will meet few of them, and you will rarely need to build one.

The scale word of Form VI is تَفَاعَلَ (*tafāʿala*), the “he did” word of L3.03. Put a root into it and you get a Form VI verb.

Peel تَعَاوَنُوا۟ from the verse:

- **تَ** at the front: the *ta-* of Form VI.
- **The root ع و ن.** The first letter, ع, comes right after the تَ.
- **A long ā** between the first and the second root letter. It is not a root letter.
- **ـُوا** at the end: the doer, “you all” (L2.03).

Take off the تَ, the alif and the ending, and the root ع و ن is left.

</Block>

In L3.17, Form III means doing something to another person: ظَٰهَرَ, “he backed (someone)”. Put تَ in front and both sides do it to each other: تَظَٰهَرَ, “they backed one another”.

It is the same step as Form V, which is تَ plus Form II (L3.15). Form VI is تَ plus Form III.

The two verses show the pair. 33:26 has Form III: ظَٰهَرُوهُم, “they backed them”. 66:4 has Form VI: تَظَٰهَرَا, “you two back one another”. The Quran writes the long ā of these verbs as a small sign above the letter (ٰ). The sound is the same as the alif in the tiles.

<Note kind="know">The Quran does not have a Form III verb beside each Form VI verb. For ع و ن, ن ب ز and د ي ن the Corpus lists a Form VI verb and no Form III verb. Use “ta- plus Form III” to read the shape and the meaning. It does not promise that the Form III verb is in the Quran.</Note>

Form VI takes the endings you know from L3.05 and L3.06. The table uses a real Quran verb, تَنَازَعَ. The Quran has the past in 3:152, the “do not” form in 8:46 and the present in 18:21. The table gives teaching forms, written with a full alif.

In the past, the stem تَنَازَعْـ stays the same and the endings are added. The doer of تَنَٰزَعْتُمْ is the ending -tum, as in L2.03.

In the present, the prefix (يَ، تَ، أَ، نَ) goes in front of the تَ of Form VI. With the prefix تَ you get two تَ in a row: تَتَنَازَعُ. The next block shows what happens to them.
</Block>

For “you all”, the present of Form VI is تَتَعَاوَنُونَ, with two تَ. Look at the verse. After لَا the verb is تَعَاوَنُوا۟, with one. Al-Jadwal says that one of the two تَ is dropped to make the word lighter. The verb began as تَتَعَاوَنُوا۟. It says the same about تَنَابَزُوا۟ (49:11), تَظَٰهَرَا (66:4), لِتَعَارَفُوٓا۟ (49:13) and تَسَآءَلُونَ (4:1).

The first تَعَاوَنُوا۟ in this verse (the one in step 1) is a command. A command takes off the prefix (L3.08), and what is left starts with the تَ of Form VI. So the command has one تَ from the start. The two verbs look the same on the page. The first is a command, “help”. The second follows لَا, a “do not” (L3.07), so it is a ban, “do not help”.

**One another.** The common meaning of Form VI is that two sides, or a group, do the action to each other. These verses show it:

| Verb | Root | Verse | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| تَعَاوَنُوا۟ | ع و ن | Al-Maida 5:2 | help one another |
| لِتَعَارَفُوٓا۟ | ع ر ف | Al-Hujurat 49:13 | so that you know one another |
| تَسَآءَلُونَ | س أ ل | An-Nisa 4:1 | you ask one another |
| أَتَوَاصَوْا۟ | و ص ي | Adh-Dhariyat 51:53 | did they urge one another? |
| وَتَنَٰزَعْتُمْ | ن ز ع | Al-Imran 3:152 | and you quarrelled |

**Not always “one another”.** In these verses there is one doer and no second side:

| Verb | Root | Verse | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| تَبَارَكَ | ب ر ك | Al-Aʿraf 7:54 | blessed is (He) |
| تَوَارَتْ | و ر ي | Sad 38:32 | it was hidden |
| تَشَٰبَهَ | ش ب ه | Al-Baqarah 2:70 | it looks like the others |

The verse has to tell you which meaning fits.

**Pretending.** Arabic also has Form VI verbs that mean “pretend”, such as تَمَارَضَ, “he pretended to be ill”. This is an everyday verb, shown here as a made-up example. None of the Form VI verbs in the Quran means “pretend”.

Some Form VI verbs start with ٱ. The تَ of Form VI sits next to the first root letter. In these verbs that letter is د or ث. The تَ turns into the same letter, the two join in a shadda, and a ٱ is added in front so that the word can start. Al-Jadwal explains ٱثَّاقَلْتُمْ like this: it began as تَثَاقَلْتُمْ.

| Verb | Verse | Began as | Root |
|---|---|---|---|
| ٱدَّارَكُوا۟ | Al-Aʿraf 7:38 | تَدَارَكُوا | د ر ك |
| ٱثَّاقَلْتُمْ | At-Tawbah 9:38 | تَثَاقَلْتُمْ | ث ق ل |
| فَٱدَّٰرَْٰٔتُمْ | Al-Baqarah 2:72 | تَدَارَأْتُمْ | د ر أ |

At first this looks like Form VIII (such as ٱتَّبَعُوا۟, L3.14). The difference is the long ā after the doubled letter. Form VI keeps its alif.
</Block>

Form VI is the sixth form. The word for the act, the verbal noun, follows the pattern تَفَاعُلٌ. In 57:20, تَفَاخُرٌۢ is “boasting to one another”, from the root ف خ ر. The word for the doer, the active participle, starts with مُتَ. In 15:47, مُّتَقَٰبِلِينَ is “facing one another”.

L3.19 teaches the participle and L3.20 teaches the verbal noun.
</Block>

</Step>

<Step id="form-vii" label="Form VII" title="Form VII: when it happens to the doer">
<Block id="vii-see" title="Look at this verse">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      size="big"
      roles
      reference="Al-Infitar 82:1"
      groups={[

        { role: 'fiil', hint: 'Form VII: it split', words: [{ ar: 'ٱنفَطَرَتْ', en: 'split apart', say: 'infatarat', type: 'v' }] },
      ]}
      en={['When the sky splits apart.']}
    />
  </Stage>

It is the first verse of a surah about the end of the world. Read it from right to left.

The verb is ٱنفَطَرَتْ, “it split apart”. The sky does not split something. The splitting happens **to** the sky. Nobody is named as the one who does it. This is the idea of Form VII: the action happens to the doer, or by itself.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal reads ٱلسَّمَآءُ as the doer of a verb that is left out, and ٱنفَطَرَتْ as the verb that explains it, because a verb is expected after إِذَا. This course follows al-Jadwal.</Note>

The scale word is ٱنْفَعَلَ (*infaʿala*). It starts with the joining alif ٱ, then a نْ, then the three root letters.

Peel ٱنفَطَرَتْ: ٱنْ, then the root ف ط ر, then ـَتْ, “she”.

The ٱ is silent after another word (L1.03). In فَٱنقَلَبُوا۟ you say *fa-nqalabū*. The present is يَنْفَعِلُ (*yanfaʿilu*): the prefix يَ, then نْ, then the root, with a kasra on the letter before the last.
</Block>

<Block id="vii-trap" title="Is the ن part of the root?">
  <Stage label="Count the letters">
    <Sentence
      reference="Al-Anʿam 6:24 (start)"
      groups={[
        { words: [{ ar: 'ٱنظُرْ', en: 'look', say: 'unzur', type: 'v' }] },

        { words: [{ ar: 'كَذَبُوا۟', en: 'they lied', say: 'kadhabu', type: 'v' }] },
      ]}
      en={['Look how they lied.']}
    />
  </Stage>

Many Form I verbs have ن as their first root letter. Their command can start with ٱ and ن, just like Form VII. ٱنظُرْ is one of them: it is a Form I command from the root ن ظ ر, not a Form VII verb.

Take off the ending, then count the letters after the ٱ:

| Word | Verse | Letters after ٱ | Form |
|---|---|---|---|
| ٱنفَطَرَتْ | Al-Infitar 82:1 | ن ف ط ر: four | Form VII, root ف ط ر |
| ٱنفِرُوا۟ | At-Tawbah 9:38 | ن ف ر: three | Form I command, root ن ف ر |
| ٱنظُرْ | Al-Anʿam 6:24 | ن ظ ر: three | Form I command, root ن ظ ر |
| فَٱنصُرْنَا | Al-Baqarah 2:286 | ن ص ر: three | Form I command, root ن ص ر |

A Form VII verb has the ن and three root letters: four. A Form I command with ن as its first root letter has three. The present works the same way. After the prefix, يَنقَلِبُ has four letters (Form VII). يَنصُرُكُم (3:160) has three, and its root is ن ص ر (Form I).

| X | ٱسْتَفْعَلَ | ٱسْتَ, then the root | ٱسْتَغْفِرُوا۟ (71:10) | L3.16 |
| III | فَاعَلَ | a long ā after the first root letter, no تَ | ظَٰهَرُوهُم (33:26) | L3.17 |

A ٱ followed by a doubled letter and then a long ā (ٱثَّاقَلْتُمْ) is Form VI.

Al-Jadwal gives a ṣarf note for most of the Form VI verbs in this lesson (5:2, 49:11, 49:13, 4:1, 66:4, 8:46, 7:38, 9:38). It has none for ٱنفَطَرَتْ, ٱنقَلَبُوا۟ and ٱسْوَدَّتْ. For those the form comes from the Corpus tag.
</Block>

<Block id="corpus" title="Find the form in the Corpus">
  <Stage label="What the Corpus writes">
    <Tiles
      label="Corpus tags of three verbs"
      items={[
        { ar: 'تَعَاوَنُوا۟', en: '(VI) ROOT:Ewn IMPV 2MP', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ٱنفَطَرَتْ', en: '(VII) ROOT:fTr PERF 3FS', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ٱسْوَدَّتْ', en: '(IX) ROOT:swd PERF 3FS', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The Corpus writes the form in brackets, as (VI), (VII) or (IX). A verb with no bracket is Form I. After ROOT: it spells the root in Latin letters: E is ع, T is ط, w is و, d is د. PERF is the past, IMPF the present and IMPV the command. 2MP is “you all (men)” and 3FS is “she”.

Al-Jadwal names the scale word in its ṣarf notes. For the second تَعَاوَنُوا۟ in 5:2 it gives تَفَاعَلُوا and says one تَ dropped.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-dropped" title="Mistake 2: reading a Form VI with a dropped تَ as Form III">
  <Stage label="Pattern: the vowels tell them apart">
    <Tiles

A Form VI present with the dropped تَ looks like a Form III present: both have a long ā after the first root letter. Check the vowels. The Form III present is يُفَاعِلُ: a ḍamma on the prefix and a kasra before the last letter (L3.17). The Form VI present is يَتَفَاعَلُ: a fatḥa on the prefix and a fatḥa before the last letter. With the dropped تَ you get تَفَاعَلُوا, with fatḥa twice.
</Block>

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="What is the difference between the two verbs of the same root? ثُمَّ ٱنصَرَفُوا۟ صَرَفَ ٱللَّهُ قُلُوبَهُم (At-Tawbah 9:127, part)">

The root is ص ر ف. **صَرَفَ** is Form I and has an object, قُلُوبَهُم: “Allah turned their hearts away”. **ٱنصَرَفُوا۟** is Form VII and has no object: “they turned away”. In Form VII it happens to the doer, or the doer does it by itself.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="These two verbs start with ٱ and a doubled letter. Which form are they, and how did they begin? (a) ٱثَّاقَلْتُمْ (At-Tawbah 9:38) (b) ٱدَّارَكُوا۟ (Al-Aʿraf 7:38)">

Both are **Form VI**. There is a long ā after the doubled letter, and Form VIII has none. (a) began as تَثَاقَلْتُمْ, root ث ق ل. (b) began as تَدَارَكُوا, root د ر ك. The تَ turned into the first root letter, the two joined in a shadda, and ٱ was added so the word can start (al-Jadwal).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="This is a made-up example. Build the Form IX past tense for “she” from the root خ ض ر, and say it with ٱلْأَرْضُ. Then read the Quran’s word: فَتُصْبِحُ ٱلْأَرْضُ مُخْضَرَّةً (Al-Hajj 22:63, part)">

The shape is ٱفْعَلَّتْ, so the root خ ض ر gives **ٱخْضَرَّتْ**, “she turned green”. With ٱلْأَرْضُ: ٱخْضَرَّتِ ٱلْأَرْضُ, “the earth turned green” (made-up). The Quran does not use this verb. In 22:63 it uses the participle مُخْضَرَّةً, “green”, from the same Form IX.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Say the form, the tense, the person and the root. (a) تَسَآءَلُونَ (An-Nisa 4:1, part) (b) فَٱنفَجَرَتْ (Al-Baqarah 2:60, part) (c) وَٱبْيَضَّتْ عَيْنَاهُ (Yusuf 12:84, part) (d) وَلَا تَنَٰزَعُوا۟ (Al-Anfal 8:46, start)">

- (a) **Form VI**, present, “you all”, root س أ ل. One تَ dropped (it began as تَتَسَاءَلُونَ). “You ask one another.”
- (b) **Form VII**, past, “she”, root ف ج ر: “so (the springs) gushed out”.
- (c) **Form IX**, past, “she”, root ب ي ض: “and his eyes turned white”.
- (d) **Form VI**, present with a ban, “you all”, root ن ز ع. Al-Jadwal: one تَ dropped. “And do not quarrel.”

</Exercise>

    <Formula
      label="three rare verb forms: VI, VII and IX"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'Form VI', small: 'تَفَاعَلَ' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'Form VII', small: 'ٱنْفَعَلَ' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'Form IX', small: 'ٱفْعَلَّ' },
      ]}

- The three forms are rare: 77, 51 and 5 verb words (Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus). You need to recognise them.
- **Form VI**, تَفَاعَلَ: تَ plus a long ā after the first root letter. It is Form III with تَ. Often “one another”, as in تَعَاوَنُوا۟, but not always: تَبَارَكَ. In the present, one of two تَ often drops. Some start with ٱ: ٱثَّاقَلْتُمْ.
- **Form VII**, ٱنْفَعَلَ: ٱنْ plus three root letters. The action happens to the doer or by itself: ٱنفَطَرَتْ, ٱنقَلَبُوا۟. Count the letters: a Form I command with ن as a root letter has only three.
- **Form IX**, ٱفْعَلَّ: ٱ, three different root letters and a doubled last letter. Colours: ٱسْوَدَّتْ, ٱبْيَضَّتْ. A doubled root is not Form IX.
- Find the root first, then the form, then read the verse for the meaning.
