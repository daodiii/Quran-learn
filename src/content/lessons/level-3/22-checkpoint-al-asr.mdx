Verse 1 is one written word, but it holds two pieces.

- **وَ** is the oath letter. It means “by” and makes the noun after it genitive (L2.07). It looks like the wāw that means “and”. Look at the noun after it: here it is genitive, and something is being sworn by.
- **ٱلْعَصْرِ** is the noun. It ends in **-i**, the genitive. It has ٱل (L1.06).

Al-Jadwal says the phrase is tied to a verb that is left out, **أُقْسِمُ**, “I swear”. The sentence “I swear by time” is an opening sentence and has no place in a bigger sentence.

Now the part Level 3 added. Peel off ٱل and the oath letter (L3.03), and the root is **ع ص ر**. Al-Jadwal gives the word's build: a plain noun (*ism*), pattern **فَعْل**, with a fatḥa on the first letter and a sukūn on the second. It gives three meanings for the word: time (the age or era), the part of the day after noon, and the ʿaṣr prayer.

Al-Jadwal calls ٱلْعَصْرِ a plain noun. It does not call it a verbal noun, a doer word or a noun of place or time. Mistake 3 comes back to this.

<Note kind="know">Al-Durr al-Maṣūn records a reading with a kasra on the ṣād, and says some scholars allow it when you stop on the word, not when you read on. It also gives another meaning for the word, “the night and the day”. The usual reading, used here, has a sukūn on the ṣād.</Note>

Verse 2 is an inna sentence (L2.10).

- **إِنَّ** is the little word. It has no case.
- **ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ** is the ism inna, the word right after it. It ends in **-a**, the accusative. A word that comes after inna as its first part takes the accusative (L2.10, L2.05).
- **لَفِى خُسْرٍ** is the khabar. It is a phrase, not a single word.

The ٱل on ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ is the kind of ٱل that covers a whole class. Al-Jadwal names it **ٱل al-jinsiyya**, “the ٱل of the kind”, and uses this very verse as its example: it means the whole human kind. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn says the same, and adds that the exception in verse 3 is one proof of it, because an exception takes some people out of a group that held all of them. L1.06 taught ٱل.

The Corpus gives the root of ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ as **أ ن س**. Al-Jadwal gives no pattern for this word, and this lesson does not guess one. What you can say is that it is a plain noun, and it is not a verbal noun or a doer word.

لَفِى خُسْرٍ has three pieces.

| Piece | Job | Taught in |
|---|---|---|
| لَ | The lām that adds emphasis to the khabar. It changes no ending. | [L2.10](/learn/level-2/10-inna-sisters/) |
| فِى | A preposition: “in” | [L2.07](/learn/level-2/07-prepositions-genitive/) |
| خُسْرٍ | The noun after it: genitive, **-in** | [L2.06](/learn/level-2/06-genitive-case/), [L1.06](/learn/level-1/06-definite-article/) |

Al-Jadwal calls this لَ the lām of the oath, and says it stands in for the “slid” lām that you met in L2.10. This is the same lām, and the verse is the answer to an oath. Either name gives the same meaning: “surely”.

Now the build of خُسْرٍ. The root is **خ س ر**. Al-Jadwal does not give a note for this verse, but it parses the same word in 65:9: a verbal noun (*maṣdar*) of the plain verb خَسِرَ, “he lost”, on the pattern **فُعْل** (a ḍamma, then a sukūn). It calls it a *samāʿī* maṣdar: one you learn by hearing it, not by a fixed rule. It adds that the same verb has other verbal nouns: خَسْر, خُسُر, خَسَار, خَسَارَة and خُسْرَان. L3.20 teaches verbal nouns.

The word names the action or state itself: “loss”. The doer word from the same root is خَاسِر, “one who loses” (L3.19).

<Note kind="know">Al-Durr al-Maṣūn records that some readers read the word with a ḍamma on the sīn, خُسُرٍ, and says it is like عُسُر and يُسُر. The usual reading, used here, has a sukūn.</Note>

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal does not list the words of 103:2 one by one. The khabar is a phrase tied to a left-out khabar, as al-Jadwal writes for a phrase that is a khabar in 2:147. L2.01 gave the plainer wording: the phrase is the khabar.</Note>

Verse 3 has the pattern that L3.11 taught: a joining noun, then clauses that finish it.

- **إِلَّا** is the little word of exception: “except”.
- **ٱلَّذِينَ** is the joining noun, “those who”, for men or a mixed group (L3.11).
- The **ṣila** is a run of four verbal sentences: ءَامَنُوا۟, then عَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ, then تَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْحَقِّ, then تَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ. وَ joins each one to the sentence before it.

**إِلَّا** is a little word, so it has no case. The Corpus tags it as a word of exception, and al-Jadwal says it is “for exception”.

**ٱلَّذِينَ** is the *mustathnā*, the word set apart. It is a joining noun, and L3.11 taught that it keeps one shape. A built word still has a case, and the books say it is “in the place of” that case. Al-Jadwal says ٱلَّذِينَ here is **in the place of the accusative, as the excepted word**.

So the verse says: all of mankind is in loss, except one group. The group is described by its four ṣila sentences. L4.05 teaches how the exception works and how the case of the excepted word is decided. This lesson names it and goes no further.

<Note kind="tip">إِنَّ and إِلَّا look almost the same. Check the letters: إِنَّ has a nūn with a shadda, and إِلَّا has a lām with a shadda and a final alif. In verse 2, ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ comes after inna. In verse 3, ٱلَّذِينَ comes after illā. Both end up accusative, for different reasons.</Note>

All four verbs of verse 3 share one ending, **-ū**. In the Quran text a small round mark sits over the alif that follows the wāw: عَمِلُوا۟. It tells you not to say the alif (L3.05).

- **Tense:** past. It names a finished action (L3.05).
- **Person and number:** third person, masculine plural: “they”. The ending -ū is the doer. The Corpus tags all four as `PERF` and `3MP`.
- **Voice:** active. The vowels show it (L3.09), and the Corpus has no `PASS` tag on any of them.
- **The ending** is a pronoun joined to the verb. It does the job of the doer (L2.03, L3.02). Al-Jadwal writes it as “the wāw is the doer”.

The ending tells you who did it. It does not tell you the form. The form comes from the stem in front of the ending. The three blocks after this one take the stems one by one.

**ءَامَنُوا۟** is a **Form IV** verb. The Corpus tags it `PERF`, `(IV)`, root **أ م ن**, `3MP`.

- **Root:** أ م ن. Other words from it are أَمْن, “safety”, and أَمَانَة, “trust”.
- **Pattern:** أَفْعَلُوا. Form IV has a hamza at the front (L3.13). The first root letter is also a hamza here, so two hamzas meet: the first has a fatḥa and the second has a sukūn. **تَوَاصَوْا۟** appears twice. The Corpus tags it `PERF`, `(VI)`, root **و ص ي**, `3MP`.

- **Root:** و ص ي. Look at the written word: you see two of the three letters: و and ص. The third letter, ي, is gone.
- **Pattern:** the Form VI pattern is تَفَاعَلَ, with تَ at the front and a long ā after the first root letter (L3.18). The “they” form is **تَفَاعَوْا**. Al-Jadwal parses the same word in 51:53 and gives this pattern, تَفَاعَوْا.

<Block id="m1" title="Mistake 1: counting all the written letters as root letters">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="written letters against root letters"
      items={[
        { ar: 'ءَامَنُوا', en: 'written: ءا م ن و ا', type: 'v' },

The root has three letters, and the written word often shows more or fewer.

- In ءَامَنُوا۟ the written word has more: the second hamza became the long ā, and the ending -ū adds و and an alif. The root is أ م ن.
- In تَوَاصَوْا۟ the written word shows the تَ of Form VI, the long ā and the ending. It does not show the last root letter, ي, because it dropped. The root is و ص ي.

Peel off the ending, the prefix and the pattern letters. Then pour the root back into the pattern to check it (L3.03).
</Block>

<Block id="m2" title="Mistake 2: reading the form from the ending">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles
      label="the same ending -ū on three forms"

All the verbs of verse 3 end in -ū, and they are of three forms. The ending names the doer. It says nothing about the form.

A long ā after the first root letter also needs care. L3.04 taught that it can mean Form III. In تَوَاصَوْا۟ it comes with a تَ at the front, which makes it Form VI. Check the front of the word before you decide.
</Block>

<Block id="m3" title="Mistake 3: calling ٱلْعَصْرِ a noun of time">
  <Stage>
    <Tiles

      items={[

ٱلْعَصْرِ names a time, so it can seem to belong to L3.21. It does not. A noun of place or time in L3.21 is built from a verb on a pattern such as مَفْعَل. ٱلْعَصْرِ is not on that pattern. Al-Jadwal gives it as a plain noun (*ism*) on the pattern فَعْل, and it adds three meanings: time, the afternoon and the ʿaṣr prayer.

The meaning of a word does not decide its build. The pattern does.
</Block>

Three words in the surah are verbal nouns: خُسْرٍ, ٱلْحَقِّ and ٱلصَّبْرِ. One is a doer word: ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ. They look alike, because all of them come from a root and name something about it.

| Kind | What it names | Example in the surah | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verbal noun | The action or state itself | خُسْرٍ, ٱلصَّبْرِ | فُعْل, فَعْل |
| Doer word | The one who does it, or has the quality | ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ | فَاعِل |

Ask: does the word name the action, or the one who does it?

<Exercise n={4} q="The same root appears in a different form: وَوَصَّىٰ بِهَآ إِبْرَٰهِۦمُ بَنِيهِ (Al-Baqarah 2:132, start). Meaning: “And Ibrahim told his sons to hold to it.” Give the root and the form of وَصَّىٰ, and say how it differs from تَوَاصَوْا۟.">

The root is **و ص ي**, the same as in تَوَاصَوْا۟. The form is **Form II** (the Corpus tag `(II)`), and al-Jadwal gives the pattern **فَعَّلَ**: the middle root letter ص is doubled, with a shadda. The last letter is a weak yāʾ that turned into an alif, so the written word ends in an alif. The doer is written: إِبْرَٰهِۦمُ.

تَوَاصَوْا۟ is **Form VI**: it has a تَ at the front and a long ā after the first root letter, and no shadda. Compare the two clues:

| Form | Clue | Example |
|---|---|---|
| II | Doubled middle letter | وَصَّىٰ |
| VI | تَ in front and a long ā | تَوَاصَوْا۟ |

L3.12 teaches Form II and L3.18 teaches Form VI.

| Form I verb | صَلَحَ | 13:23: وَمَن صَلَحَ مِنْ ءَابَآئِهِمْ | “was good” |
| Form IV verb | أَصْلَحَ | 2:182: فَأَصْلَحَ بَيْنَهُمْ | “put right, made peace” |
| Doer word, Form I | ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ | 103:3 | “the good deeds” |
| Doer word, Form IV | مُصْلِحُونَ | 2:11: إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُصْلِحُونَ | “ones who put right” |
| Verbal noun, Form IV | إِصْلَٰحًا | 2:228: إِنْ أَرَادُوٓا۟ إِصْلَٰحًا | “making right” |
| A plain noun | صُلْح | 4:128: وَٱلصُّلْحُ خَيْرٌ | “peace, settlement” |

The Form IV doer word is built on مُفْعِل: al-Jadwal gives the rule in 2:8 (the doer word of a Form IV verb takes the pattern of its present, with a mīm in place of the prefix and a kasra before the last letter). The Form IV verbal noun is built on إِفْعَال (L3.20).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Two verses use تَوَاصَوْا۟ again. Find the verb in each, give its form, and say what the phrase after it is tied to: وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ (Al-Balad 90:17, end) and أَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِهِۦ (Adh-Dhariyat 51:53, start). Meaning of the second: “Did they urge each other to it?”">

**90:17.** Both verbs are **تَوَاصَوْا۟**: Form VI, root و ص ي, past, they (men), active. The phrase بِٱلصَّبْرِ is tied to the first, and بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ to the second. Al-Jadwal gives the same two ties. ٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ has the root ر ح م and is genitive after بِ.

**51:53.** The verb is **تَوَاصَوْا۟** again: Form VI. Look at the first letter. The opening **أَ** is not Form IV. It is the question word, “did they …?”. Al-Jadwal says: “the hamza is for a question of denial”, and the Corpus tags it as a question prefix. The phrase بِهِۦ is tied to تَوَاصَوْا۟. Al-Jadwal also gives the same pattern, تَفَاعَوْا, and the same reason for the dropped alif.

L3.04 taught that an opening أَ can be a sign of Form IV. Check the rest of the word before you decide.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="The word خُسْر comes up in three places. Compare them: إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَفِى خُسْرٍ (Al-Asr 103:2), وَكَانَ عَٰقِبَةُ أَمْرِهَا خُسْرًا (At-Talaq 65:9, end) and فَقَدْ خَسِرَ خُسْرَانًا مُّبِينًا (An-Nisa 4:119, end). Give the root, the kind of word and the case of the noun خُسْر (or its relative) in each.">

The root is **خ س ر** in all three.

- **103:2: خُسْرٍ.** A verbal noun on the pattern فُعْل. It is genitive, **-in**, after فِى.
- **65:9: خُسْرًا.** The same verbal noun. It is the khabar of كَانَ, so it is accusative, **-an** (L2.11). Al-Jadwal gives the build in this verse: a *samāʿī* maṣdar on فُعْل.
- **4:119: خَسِرَ خُسْرَانًا.** The verb is خَسِرَ: Form I, past, he, active. The noun is خُسْرَان, another verbal noun of the same verb, on the pattern فُعْلَان, accusative, **-an**. Al-Jadwal parses it as an absolute object (*mafʿūl muṭlaq*): the noun repeats the verb to add strength. L4.07 teaches this.

One verb gives many verbal nouns, and each verse picks one. Al-Jadwal lists six for this verb in 65:9.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="The same words appear in two more verses. Say the job of ٱلَّذِينَ in each, and what is different: إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ (At-Tin 95:6, start) and إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ (Al-Bayyinah 98:7, start).">

**95:6.** The frame is the same as Al-Asr: إِلَّا, then ٱلَّذِينَ and its ṣila. ٱلَّذِينَ is the excepted word (*mustathnā*): in the place of the accusative, as al-Jadwal says. The clauses of the ṣila are the same: no place.

**98:7.** The little word is **إِنَّ**, not إِلَّا. Now ٱلَّذِينَ is the **ism inna**, and it is also in the place of the accusative. Al-Jadwal gives this reading for the same four words after إِنَّ in 2:277: “a joining noun, built, in the place of the accusative, the ism of inna”.

In both verses ٱلَّذِينَ is accusative by place, for different reasons. The ṣila is the same, and it has no place in both. What changes is the little word before ٱلَّذِينَ, and so the job.

</Exercise>

- Surah Al-ʿAsr has **three verses and fourteen written words**. After you split the words, you find 25 pieces: 10 little words, 4 verbs and 11 nouns and pronouns.
- **Verse 1** is an oath: an oath letter and a plain noun, ٱلْعَصْرِ, on the pattern فَعْل. **Verse 2** is an inna sentence, the answer to the oath. Its khabar has a verbal noun, خُسْرٍ, on the pattern فُعْل.
- **Verse 3** is an exception. ٱلَّذِينَ is finished by four past verbs: ءَامَنُوا۟ (Form IV), عَمِلُوا۟ (Form I) and تَوَاصَوْا۟ twice (Form VI). All are third person, masculine plural, active. The ending -ū is the doer.
- ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ is a doer word used as the object, so its kasra is accusative. ٱلْحَقِّ and ٱلصَّبْرِ are verbal nouns on فَعْل, after بِ.
- The nine roots are ع ص ر, أ ن س, خ س ر, أ م ن, ع م ل, ص ل ح, و ص ي, ح ق ق and ص ب ر.

L4.13 and L4.15 teach the verbs that change shape, such as تَوَاصَوْا۟ and ءَامَنُوا۟. L4.07 teaches the absolute object, such as خُسْرَانًا in 4:119.
