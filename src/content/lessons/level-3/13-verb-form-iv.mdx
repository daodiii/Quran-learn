This is a verbal sentence ([L2.03](/learn/level-2/03-verbal-sentence/)). Take off the ending -nā (“we”, the doer) and the attached -hu (“it”, the object, [L3.02](/learn/level-3/02-attached-pronouns/)). What is left is أَنزَلَ, *anzala*, “he sent down”.

Its letters are أ ن ز ل. The root is ن ز ل, “coming down” ([L3.03](/learn/level-3/03-root-system/)). The أَ in front is not a root letter. It was added. That is **Form IV**.

In L3.04 the pattern فَعَلَ stood for any Form I verb. Form IV has its own pattern, **أَفْعَلَ** (*afʿala*):

- **أَ**: a hamza with fatḥa, added in front.
- **ف**: the first root letter, with a **sukūn**.
- **ع**: the second root letter, with fatḥa.
- **ل**: the third root letter, with fatḥa.

Form I has nothing added. Form IV adds one letter, and the first root letter loses its vowel. Al-Jadwal calls such a verb *al-rubāʿī*, “the four-letter verb” (for example on أَخْرَجَ in 2:85).

<Note kind="know">The Tanzil text of 97:1 writes أَنزَلْنَٰهُ without a sukūn on the ن. The ن is still said with a sukūn: *an-za-la*. The tables in this lesson write the sukūn, because it is part of the shape.</Note>

The hamza of Form IV is *hamzat al-qaṭʿ*, هَمْزَة الْقَطْع, “the cutting hamza”. You say it, even in the middle of a sentence: *wa-**a**nzala*. The opening letter of Forms VII to X is the joining alif ٱ (*hamzat al-waṣl*, [L3.08](/learn/level-3/08-imperative/)). After وَ it is not said: *wa-stakbara*.

| Form | Past pattern | A Quran verb | Opening letter |
|---|---|---|---|
| IV | أَفْعَلَ | أَنزَلَ (3:3) | أَ, said |
| VII | ٱنْفَعَلَ | ٱنطَلَقَ (38:6) | ٱ, joining |
| VIII | ٱفْتَعَلَ | ٱقْتَرَبَ (21:1) | ٱ, joining |
| IX | ٱفْعَلَّ | very rare | ٱ, joining |
| X | ٱسْتَفْعَلَ | ٱسْتَكْبَرَ (2:34) | ٱ, joining |

Forms VII, VIII and X come in [L3.18](/learn/level-3/18-verb-forms-vi-vii-ix/), [L3.14](/learn/level-3/14-verb-form-viii/) and [L3.16](/learn/level-3/16-verb-form-x/). Here you just need the difference: أَ is said, ٱ is not.

Form IV has 3,487 verb words in the Quran, 18% of the 19,356 verb words. It is the second most common form. Form I has 12,356, and Form II has 1,300 (Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus, curriculum review section 1).

I split the 3,487 with a short script on the Corpus file, counting each word once by its tags. By tense: 1,518 active and 215 passive past words, 1,396 active and 69 passive present words, and 289 commands. They come from 261 different roots.

Six verbs make up 1,359 of the words, about 39%: ءَامَنَ (537), ءَاتَى (271), أَنزَلَ (183), أَرَادَ (139), أَرْسَلَ (130) and أَخْرَجَ (99). So “to believe”, “to give”, “to send down” and “to want” are all Form IV.

<Note kind="know">1,910 of the 3,487 words (55%) come from a root with و, ي or a hamza. Those verbs change shape, and step 4 shows how to recognise them. The old lesson said Form IV is less common than Form II. The count shows the opposite.</Note>

The verb is أَنْزَلَ, a real Quran verb. The table is a teaching table: the Quran does not use all 13 forms. The stem أَنْزَلْـ stays the same. The endings are the ones you learned in [L3.05](/learn/level-3/05-past-tense/): the same 13, because the endings do not care which form the verb is. Real forms are أَنزَلْنَا (-nā, 22:5) and أَنزَلْتَ (-ta, 3:53).

Compare the present with the past أَخْرَجَ. Three things changed:

- The **أَ is gone**. The present has no hamza of its own in front.
- The prefix (يُـ, تُـ, أُـ, نُـ) has a **ḍamma**. In Form I it has a fatḥa: يَخْرُجُ ([L3.06](/learn/level-3/06-present-tense/)).
- The second root letter has a **kasra**, and the third letter takes the ending ḍamma. The pattern is **يُفْعِلُ** (*yufʿilu*).

Al-Jadwal explains the dropped hamza on 2:3. For يُقِيمُونَ it says the past is أَقَامَ, with the pattern أَفْعَلَ, and the present drops the hamza and has the pattern يُفْعِلُونَ, with ḍamma on the yāʾ. The prefix and endings are the same as for any present verb. The “I” form أُخْرِجُ has a hamza, but it is the prefix of “I”, not the dropped one.

L3.08 made a command from the present: take the present for “you”, end it like the jussive, take off the prefix. For Form IV the present is تُنْزِلُ. The jussive is تُنْزِلْ. Without تُ the word starts with a silent ن, so Arabic adds a hamza. Here it is **أَ with fatḥa**, a hamza that is said, as in the past. The middle letter keeps its kasra: أَنْزِلْ. The five endings are the same as in L3.08.

The extra أَ often says “make it happen”, but not in all verbs. Four things to know:

- **Some verbs mean “become” or “enter”.** أَظْلَمَ (2:20) is “it became dark”. The Form I verb ظَلَمَ of the same root means “he did wrong”, which is a different idea.
- **Some meanings are learned as they are.** ءَامَنَ is “he believed”. The Form I verb أَمِنَ is “he felt safe” (7:99). The two are linked by the idea of safety and trust. أَسْلَمَ and أَحْسَنَ also have to be learned with their meanings.
- **Many Form IV verbs have no Form I partner in the Quran.** I checked the Corpus: of the 261 roots with a Form IV verb, 146 also have a Form I verb. The other 115, such as أَرْسَلَ and أَسْلَمَ, do not.
- **A shared root is not a promise.** نَذَرَ (2:270) is “he vowed”, and أَنذَرَ is “he warned”.

So a dictionary gives the meaning, and the pattern gives a hint.

[L3.04](/learn/level-3/04-verb-form-i/) warned about أَكَلَ and أَخَذَ: the hamza is the first root letter. Take off the أَ and just two letters are left (كَلَ, خَذَ), so nothing was added. The same holds for أَتَىٰٓ (root أ ت ي) and أَمِنَ (root أ م ن). [L4.15](/learn/level-4/15-hamzated-verbs/) teaches verbs with a hamza in the root.

Now look at the Form IV partners of these two roots: ءَاتَى (“he gave”) and ءَامَنَ (“he believed”). They start with ءَا, a hamza and a long ā written together. Al-Jadwal explains this on 2:3: the long ā in آمَنَ is made of two hamzas, the first with fatḥa and the second with sukūn, أَأْمَنَ, and its pattern is أَفْعَلَ. On 2:277 it says the same for آتَوا: the long ā hides أَأْتَوْا. So the first أَ is the Form IV letter, and the second is the root hamza.

**The test:** take off the first أَ. If three root letters are left, the أَ was added. If two are left, it belongs to the root.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal gives the pattern of ءَاتَوُا۟ in 2:277 as أَفْعَوْا. The weak final letter drops before the wāw of “they”. L4.13 teaches this.</Note>

More than half of the Form IV verb words come from roots with a weak letter (step 1). They look different, but the Form IV frame is still there. Look for the hamza in front, or ءَا, then the long ā or ī.

| Past | Present | Root | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| أَقَامَ | يُقِيمُ | ق و م | the و becomes ā in the past and ī in the present |
| أَرَادَ | يُرِيدُ | ر و د | the same: و becomes ā, then ī |
| ءَاتَى | يُؤْتِى | أ ت ي | ءَا for two hamzas; the last letter shows as ā or ī |
| ءَامَنَ | يُؤْمِنُ | أ م ن | ءَا for two hamzas; the present keeps one hamza |

Weak verbs are taught in [L4.11](/learn/level-4/11-weak-verbs-intro/). أَقَامَ and أَرَادَ are hollow ([L4.12](/learn/level-4/12-hollow-verbs/)). ءَاتَى ends in a weak letter ([L4.13](/learn/level-4/13-defective-verbs/)). ءَامَنَ and ءَاتَى also have a hamza ([L4.15](/learn/level-4/15-hamzated-verbs/)).         { ar: 'أَفْعِلْ', en: 'command: أَ + kasra', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'أُفْعِلَ', en: 'passive: أُ + kasra', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

1. Peel off the prefix and the endings ([L3.03](/learn/level-3/03-root-system/)).
2. Look at the start. A said hamza with a sukūn on the next letter is the past. A prefix with ḍamma and a kasra before the last letter is the present. A said hamza with a kasra is the command.
3. Take off the first أَ. If three root letters remain, it was added. If two remain, the hamza is a root letter (step 4).
4. Check the Corpus tag: `(IV)`.

The old lesson also said to count letters (four for a basic Form IV) and to ask whether the verb means “make it happen”. Counting works for a strong root. The meaning is just a hint (step 3).

</Block>
</Step>

<Block id="mistakes" title="Common mistakes">
  <Stage label="Wrong and right">
    <Tiles
      label="Four wrong readings and the right ones"
      items={[
        { ar: 'يُؤَخْرِجُ', en: 'wrong: the hamza kept in the present', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'يُخْرِجُ', en: 'right', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ٱنزِلْ', en: 'wrong: a joining alif in the command', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'أَنزِلْ', en: 'right', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

1. **Taking any verb that opens with أَ for Form IV.** أَكَلَ, أَخَذَ, أَمَرَ and أَتَى are Form I: the hamza is a root letter. Take off the أَ and count.
2. **Keeping the hamza in the present.** The present of أَخْرَجَ is يُخْرِجُ. The أَ drops, and the prefix has a ḍamma.
3. **Opening the command with ٱ.** A Form IV command starts with a said hamza: أَنزِلْ. The joining alif belongs to Form I commands like ٱدْخُلْ.
4. **Mixing Form II and Form IV.** Form II has a shadda (نَزَّلَ). Form IV has an added hamza (أَنزَلَ).
5. **Reading the noun أَحْسَنُ as a verb.** Look at the last vowel and at the words around it.
6. **Mixing the active and passive present.** يُخْرِجُ (kasra) is active. يُخْرَجُ (fatḥa) is passive.
7. **Assuming Form IV carries the action over to someone else.** Many meanings are learned one by one: ءَامَنَ, أَسْلَمَ. A Form I verb of the same root can mean something quite different (نَذَرَ, أَنذَرَ).
8. **Looking for a Form IV partner of each Form I verb.** Many have none. Learn each pair from the dictionary.
</Block>

- (d) **Form I.** Root أ ت ي. Two letters are left without the أَ.

<Exercise n={2} q="From the root خ ر ج, make the Form IV verb: (a) past, “he” (b) present, “he” (c) command, “you (one man)” (d) past, “we”. Check (d) with فَأَخْرَجْنَا (Fatir 35:27).">

- (a) **أَخْرَجَ.**
- (b) **يُخْرِجُ.** The أَ drops.

<Exercise n={3} q="How do you say the start of وَأَنزَلَ (Al-Imran 3:3) and of وَٱسْتَكْبَرَ (Al-Baqarah 2:34)? Why do they differ?">

- وَأَنزَلَ is *wa-anzala*. The hamza of Form IV is a cutting hamza, so you say it.
- وَٱسْتَكْبَرَ is *wa-stakbara*. It is Form X (the tag is `(X)`), and its ٱ is a joining alif, which you drop after وَ.

The hamza is gone. The prefix يُـ has a ḍamma, and the middle letter has a kasra: yufʿilu. يَخْرُجُونَ is Form I: the prefix has a fatḥa and the middle letter a ḍamma.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Take the phrase لَئِنْ أُخْرِجُوا۟ لَا يَخْرُجُونَ (Al-Hashr 59:12). Which verb is Form I and which is Form IV? What does each mean?">

- يَخْرُجُونَ is **Form I**: “they go out”.
- أُخْرِجُوا۟ is **Form IV, passive**: “they are driven out” (L3.09). The ḍamma on the hamza and the kasra before the last letter show the passive.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Take apart ءَامَنُوا۟ (Al-Baqarah 2:277). Give the root, the form, the pattern and the meaning of the ending.">

The long ā hides two hamzas, أَأْ (al-Jadwal on 2:3). The root is أ م ن. The form is IV, pattern أَفْعَلَ. The ending -ū is “they”, the doer. The meaning is “they believed”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Give the root and form of أَقَامُوا۟ and ءَاتَوُا۟ (Al-Baqarah 2:277). Why does أَقَامُوا۟ have an ā in the middle?">

أَقَامُوا۟: root ق و م, Form IV. The middle و of the root turned into ā, and the original is أَقْوَمُوا۟ (al-Jadwal). ءَاتَوُا۟: root أ ت ي, Form IV: ءَا is two hamzas, and the weak final letter drops before the و of “they”.

</Exercise>

(a) A **Form IV verb**: ends in fatḥa, has a hidden doer and the object كُلَّ شَىْءٍ. (b) A **noun**, “the best”: ends in ḍamma and is followed by “of the creators”.

A verb, present, Form IV, dictionary form أَخْرَجَ, root خ ر ج, third person masculine singular: “he brings out”.

<Exercise n={11} q="Why does أَدْخِلْنِى (Al-Isra 17:80) start with أَ, and ٱدْخُلُوا۟ (Al-Baqarah 2:58) with ٱ?">

أَدْخِلْنِى is Form IV: its opening hamza is said, and the meaning is “make me enter”. ٱدْخُلُوا۟ is the Form I command: its alif is a joining alif, and the meaning is “enter!”.

<Exercise n={12} q="A learner reads أُنزِلَ (Al-Baqarah 2:185) as a Form I verb because the first letter has a ḍamma. What is wrong?">

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="root plus pattern equals a Form IV verb"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'root', small: 'ن ز ل' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'pattern', small: 'أَفْعَلَ' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'Form IV verb', small: 'أَنزَلَ' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- Form IV adds a hamza in front of the root and a sukūn on the first root letter: **أَفْعَلَ**. It has 3,487 verb words, 18% of all (Quran Learn’s count from the Corpus).
- The hamza is a cutting hamza and is said, even after وَ. The joining alif ٱ of Forms VII to X is not.
- The present is **يُفْعِلُ**: the أَ drops, the prefix has a ḍamma, and the middle letter a kasra. The command is **أَفْعِلْ**. The passive is أُفْعِلَ.
- Often Form IV carries the action over to someone else (نَزَلَ, أَنزَلَ). Many meanings are learned as they are (ءَامَنَ).
- An opening أَ does not make a verb Form IV (أَكَلَ, أَخَذَ, أَتَى, أَمِنَ). Take off the أَ and count the letters left.
- Weak Form IV verbs are common (أَقَامَ, ءَاتَى, ءَامَنَ, أَرَادَ). Recognise them now. L4.11 to L4.15 teach them.
- Nouns built on Form IV: مُفْعِل (L3.19), مُفْعَل and إِفْعَال (L3.20).
