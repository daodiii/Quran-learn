This is a verbal sentence ([L2.03](/learn/level-2/03-verbal-sentence/)). The verb comes first: كَتَبَ, *kataba*, “he wrote”. Here “wrote” means “made a rule”: Allah has made mercy a rule for Himself. The doer is رَبُّكُمْ, “your Lord”.

Look only at the verb. It has three letters, ك ت ب, and three short vowels. Nothing is added in front, between or after. That is **Form I**.

The Arabic name for Form I is *al-fiʿl al-mujarrad*, فِعْل مُجَرَّد. *Mujarrad* means “bare” or “stripped”. A Form I verb is the root with vowels, and nothing else.

The other forms are called *mazīd*, مَزِيد, “increased”. They add a letter or double one. They are Forms II to X, and they come in later lessons.

Al-Jadwal uses the same word. On ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ in 1:6 it says that the plain verb of *istaqāma* (“he stood straight”) is *qāma*, “he stood”.

**How common is Form I?** There are 12,356 Form I verbs in the Quran. That is 64% of all verbs (Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus). The next form, Form IV, has 3,487. So most verbs you meet in a verse are Form I.

<Note kind="know">A few Form I verbs have four root letters, such as وَسْوَسَ, “he whispered” (Ta-Ha 20:120). They are bare too, and the Corpus gives them no form tag. This lesson is about the three-letter ones.</Note>
</Block>

Arabic grammar needs a short way to say “three root letters with these vowels”. It uses the verb فَعَلَ (*faʿala*, “he did”) as a ruler.

- **ف** stands for the first root letter.
- **ع** stands for the second.
- **ل** stands for the third.

So فَعَلَ means: first letter with fatḥa, second letter with fatḥa, third letter with fatḥa. Pour a root into it and you get a verb:

| Root | Pattern | Verb | Say it | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ك ت ب | فَعَلَ | كَتَبَ | kataba | he wrote |
| ذ ه ب | فَعَلَ | ذَهَبَ | dhahaba | he went |
| خ ل ق | فَعَلَ | خَلَقَ | khalaqa | he created |
| ن ص ر | فَعَلَ | نَصَرَ | naṣara | he helped |
| ع ب د | فَعَلَ | عَبَدَ | ʿabada | he worshipped |

Al-Jadwal writes the pattern of a word after *wazn-hu*, “its pattern”. For رَفِيقًا in 4:69 it says the pattern is فَعِيل.

</Block>

A Form I verb gives the plain meaning of the root. The root ك ت ب carries the idea of writing, and كَتَبَ means “he wrote”.

A Form I verb can name an action (he wrote), a state (he knew) or a quality (he was good).

The other forms change the plain meaning. From the root ع ل م, Form I is عَلِمَ, “he knew”, and Form II is عَلَّمَ, “he taught”. You will see both in verses in step 3. Form IV often adds “make it happen”: نَزَلَ is “he came down” and أَنزَلَ is “he sent down”. Form X often means “ask for”: غَفَرَ is “he forgave” and ٱسْتَغْفَرَ is “he asked forgiveness”.

<Note kind="remember">Form I is the bare verb: three root letters and vowels, with nothing added. The other forms add something.</Note>

The past tense is *māḍī*, مَاضِي. The “he” form of a regular Form I verb has three root letters and three short vowels. The first and the last vowel are fatḥa. Only the middle vowel changes, and it can be one of three:

- **faʿala**, فَعَلَ: fatḥa in the middle. كَتَبَ, “he wrote”.
- **faʿila**, فَعِلَ: kasra in the middle. عَلِمَ, “he knew”.
- **faʿula**, فَعُلَ: ḍamma in the middle. حَسُنَ, “he was good”.

The root letters do not tell you the middle vowel. A dictionary gives it, and you learn it with each verb.

This is the “he” form only. [L3.05](/learn/level-3/05-past-tense/) teaches the endings for “she”, “they”, “you”, “I” and “we”.

</Block>

Each of these verses has a Form I verb in the “he” form:

- خَلَقَ (*khalaqa*, “he created”) is faʿala.
- سَمِعَ (*samiʿa*, “he heard”) is faʿila.
- حَسُنَ (*ḥasuna*, “he was good”) is faʿula.

All three have the same shape: three letters, nothing added. Only the middle vowel is different.

The doer comes after the verb in each verse: ٱللَّهُ after سَمِعَ, and أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ after حَسُنَ. In 55:3 the doer is hidden inside the verb: “he”.

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* is a short grammar book by Ibn Ājurrūm (died 723 AH). It starts its chapter on verbs with three kinds:

- **Past**, *māḍī*. The book says the past verb ends in fatḥa.
- **Present**, *muḍāriʿ*. The book says it starts with one of four extra letters, joined in the word أَنَيْتُ: **أ ن ي ت**. In the “he” form the letter is يَ (*ya-*).
- **Command**, *amr*. [L3.08](/learn/level-3/08-imperative/) teaches it.

Its own example is ضَرَبَ, يَضْرِبُ, اضْرِبْ: “he struck”, “he strikes”, “strike!”. All three are Form I. Look at the middle vowel: ضَرَبَ has fatḥa, but يَضْرِبُ has kasra.
</Block>

The present tense is *muḍāriʿ*, مُضَارِع. Its “he” form has a fixed shape:

- the prefix يَ (*ya-*), which is not a root letter
- the first root letter with a sukūn
- the second root letter with a vowel: ḍamma, kasra or fatḥa
- the third root letter with ḍamma at the end

As patterns: **yafʿulu**, يَفْعُلُ, **yafʿilu**, يَفْعِلُ, and **yafʿalu**, يَفْعَلُ.

The middle vowel of the present is not always the one from the past. كَتَبَ has fatḥa in the past and ḍamma in the present. So learn the two forms as a pair.

The last example in the panel is 58:1 again. Its start has the past سَمِعَ (kasra in the middle), and here is the present يَسْمَعُ (fatḥa in the middle).

[L3.06](/learn/level-3/06-present-tense/) teaches the whole present tense. Here you only need to recognise the pattern.

A Form I verb has a past vowel and a present vowel. Together they make a pair. The grammar books call each kind of pair a *bāb*, “door”, and name it after one verb. Al-Jadwal uses these names in its notes on word patterns. There are six doors:

| Door | Past | Present | Middle vowel, past and present | A Quran verb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 *bāb naṣara* | نَصَرَ | يَنْصُرُ | fatḥa, ḍamma | كَتَبَ يَكْتُبُ |
| 2 *bāb ḍaraba* | ضَرَبَ | يَضْرِبُ | fatḥa, kasra | ضَرَبَ يَضْرِبُ |
| 3 *bāb fataḥa* | فَتَحَ | يَفْتَحُ | fatḥa, fatḥa | فَتَحَ يَفْتَحُ |
| 4 *bāb samiʿa* | سَمِعَ | يَسْمَعُ | kasra, fatḥa | عَلِمَ يَعْلَمُ |
| 5 *bāb ḥasiba* | حَسِبَ | يَحْسِبُ | kasra, kasra | few verbs |
| 6 *bāb karuma* | كَرُمَ | يَكْرُمُ | ḍamma, ḍamma | كَبُرَ يَكْبُرُ |

Al-Jadwal’s notes show the names at work (vowel marks added by Quran Learn):

- On 2:214 it calls the pair نَصَرَ يَنْصُرُ “the first door”.
- On 2:60 it says شَرِبَ يَشْرَبُ is *bāb faraḥa*, its name for door 4.
- On 3:123 it says ذَلَّ يَذِلُّ is *bāb ḍaraba*.
- On 4:69 it says the verb with the root ر ف ق can follow three doors: *naṣara*, *karuma* and *faraḥa*.

So one verb can follow more than one door.

  <Stage label="Quran Learn’s count">
    <Tiles

I counted the regular Form I verbs in the Quranic Arabic Corpus with a short script. “Regular” means: three root letters, no و, ي or hamza among them, and no doubled letter. There are 123 such verbs. By the middle vowel of the past:

| Middle vowel of the past | Verbs |
|---|---|
| fatḥa (faʿala) | 89 |
| kasra (faʿila) | 29 |
| ḍamma (faʿula) | 5 |

Seventy of the 123 also appear in a *ya-* present form. The middle vowel of that present form:

| Past | Verbs | Present: fatḥa | Present: kasra | Present: ḍamma |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| faʿala | 52 | 10 | 17 | 25 |
| faʿila | 17 | 17 | 0 | 0 |

What this tells you:

- **faʿala** is the biggest group, and it can go with any of the three present vowels.
- **faʿila** verbs all have fatḥa in the present, in this count.
- **faʿula** is small. The five verbs are كَبُرَ (be big), كَثُرَ (be many), ضَعُفَ (be weak), حَسُنَ (be good) and خَبُثَ (be bad). All five name a quality. Only one of them, كَبُرَ, is among the seventy with a *ya-* present form. It has ḍamma: يَكْبُرُ (Al-Isra 17:51).
- **faʿila** verbs often name a feeling or a state: فَرِحَ (be glad), كَرِهَ (dislike), غَضِبَ (be angry), عَلِمَ (know). But not all do: عَمِلَ (do) and شَرِبَ (drink) are faʿila too. So the meaning gives a hint, not a rule.

<Note kind="tip">Look at the root letters of a faʿala verb. All 10 faʿala verbs with fatḥa in the present have a throat letter (ع, ح, غ, خ or ه) as the second or third root letter: جَعَلَ يَجْعَلُ, ذَهَبَ يَذْهَبُ, فَتَحَ يَفْتَحُ. But 4 of the 14 faʿala verbs with such a letter take kasra or ḍamma: دَخَلَ يَدْخُلُ, رَجَعَ يَرْجِعُ. So a throat letter is a hint too.</Note>

The word مَشْرَب (*mashrab*, “a place to drink”) comes from the verb شَرِبَ, “he drank”. Al-Jadwal says its pattern is مَفْعَل, with fatḥa on the middle letter. The reason it gives: the middle letter of the present, يَشْرَبُ, has fatḥa.

So the present vowel also decides the shape of words made from the verb. [L3.21](/learn/level-3/21-nouns-place-time/) teaches nouns of place and time.

        { words: [{ ar: 'إِنَّا', en: 'indeed we', say: 'inna', type: 'p' }] },
        { words: [{ ar: 'ذَهَبْنَا', en: 'we went', say: 'dhahabna', type: 'v' }] },
        { words: [{ ar: 'نَسْتَبِقُ', en: 'racing each other', say: 'nastabiqu', type: 'v' }] },
      ]}
      en={['Indeed, we went off racing each other.']}
    />
  </Stage>

This verse has two verbs. One is Form I. To find out which, use three steps.

1. **Take off what is joined to the verb.** In front: little words like وَ and فَ. At the end: endings and attached pronouns. In the present, also the prefix (*ya-*, *ta-*, *a-*, *na-*). ذَهَبْنَا loses the ending -nā (“we”) and gives ذَهَبَ.
2. **Find the root and count.** ذَهَبَ has the root ذ ه ب: three letters.
3. **Look for anything added.** A doubled letter. A long ā after the first letter. A letter in front of the root. For ذَهَبَ there is nothing, so it is Form I, faʿala.

Now نَسْتَبِقُ. Take off the prefix نَ and you have سْتَبِقُ. The root is س ب ق. The letter ت is not one of the root letters. It is added, so this verb is not Form I. The Corpus agrees: it tags it (VIII).

You find the root with the method of [L3.03](/learn/level-3/03-root-system/). The endings are [L3.05](/learn/level-3/05-past-tense/) and [L3.06](/learn/level-3/06-present-tense/).

The same verse has a Form I verb, جَعَلَ (root ج ع ل, “he made”), and a Form IV verb, أَنزَلَ (root ن ز ل, “he sent down”). أَنزَلَ has an extra أَ in front of the root. That is what Form IV looks like.

Here is what the other forms add to the “he” form of the past. Each is taught in its own lesson.

| Form | Pattern | What is added | A Quran verb | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| II | فَعَّلَ | the middle letter is doubled | عَلَّمَ | [L3.12](/learn/level-3/12-verb-form-ii/) |
| III | فَاعَلَ | a long ā after the first letter | قَٰتَلَ | [L3.17](/learn/level-3/17-verb-form-iii/) |
| IV | أَفْعَلَ | أَ in front of the root | أَنزَلَ | [L3.13](/learn/level-3/13-verb-form-iv/) |
| V | تَفَعَّلَ | تَ in front and the middle letter doubled | تَوَلَّىٰ | [L3.15](/learn/level-3/15-verb-form-v/) |
| VI | تَفَاعَلَ | تَ in front and a long ā | تَبَٰرَكَ | [L3.18](/learn/level-3/18-verb-forms-vi-vii-ix/) |
| VII | اِنْفَعَلَ | ان in front | ٱنطَلَقَ | [L3.18](/learn/level-3/18-verb-forms-vi-vii-ix/) |
| VIII | اِفْتَعَلَ | ا in front and ت after the first letter | ٱقْتَرَبَ | [L3.14](/learn/level-3/14-verb-form-viii/) |
| X | اِسْتَفْعَلَ | است in front | ٱسْتَكْبَرَ | [L3.16](/learn/level-3/16-verb-form-x/) |

Form IX, اِفْعَلَّ, is very rare: it appears five times in the whole Quran (Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus). L3.18 covers it.

The Quran examples are عَلَّمَ (55:4), قَٰتَلَ (9:30), أَنزَلَ (2:22), تَوَلَّىٰ (80:1), تَبَٰرَكَ (67:1), ٱنطَلَقَ (38:6), ٱقْتَرَبَ (21:1) and ٱسْتَكْبَرَ (2:34). Form I has nothing in this column, because nothing is added.

<Note kind="know">The patterns above are for the “he” form of the past. In the present and with endings the added letters move around. Each form’s own lesson shows how.</Note>

<Block id="corpus" title="The Corpus tag: no bracket means Form I">
  <Stage label="Quranic Arabic Corpus">
    <Tiles
      label="Two verbs from Yusuf 12:17 and what the Corpus says about their form"
      items={[

      ]}

The Quranic Arabic Corpus tags each verb with its tense, its root and its person. A verb that is not Form I also carries its form number in brackets, such as (II) or (VIII). A verb with no form number is Form I.

| Word | What the Corpus says | Form |
|---|---|---|
| ذَهَبْنَا | verb, past (PERF), root ذ ه ب, “we” (1P), no form number | I |
| نَسْتَبِقُ | verb, present (IMPF), root س ب ق, “we” (1P), (VIII) | VIII |
| عَلَّمَهُ (55:4) | verb, past (PERF), root ع ل م, “he” (3MS), (II) | II |
| أَنزِلْنِى (23:29) | verb, command (IMPV), root ن ز ل, “you” (2MS), (IV) | IV |

You can use this when you are unsure. Look the word up in the Corpus and check for a number in brackets. The tags show the tense too: PERF is past, IMPF is present and IMPV is a command.

<Block id="mistake-hamza" title="Mistake 2: an opening أَ means Form IV">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      roles
      reference="Yusuf 12:17 (end)"
      groups={[
        { role: 'fiil', hint: 'Form I', words: [{ ar: 'فَأَكَلَهُ', en: 'and ate him', say: 'fa-akalahu', type: 'v' }] },
        { role: 'fail', words: [{ ar: 'ٱلذِّئْبُ', en: 'the wolf', say: 'adh-dhibu' }] },
      ]}
      en={['And the wolf ate him.']}
    />
  </Stage>

أَنزَلَ starts with أَ, and it is Form IV. أَكَلَ (“he ate”) also starts with أَ, and it is Form I. How can you tell?

Take off the أَ and count what is left.

- أَنزَلَ gives نَزَلَ: three letters. The أَ was added, so it is Form IV.
- أَكَلَ gives كَلَ: two letters. The hamza is the first root letter (the root is أ ك ل), so it is Form I.

The Corpus agrees: أَكَلَ has no form number, and أَنزَلَ has (IV). أَخَذَ (“he took”) is Form I in the same way. Verbs with a hamza in the root are called hamzated verbs. [L4.15](/learn/level-4/15-hamzated-verbs/) covers them.

<Block id="mistake-vowel" title="Mistake 3: guessing the vowel">
  <Stage label="Both mean an action">
    <Tiles
      label="Two verbs for an action, with different middle vowels"
      items={[
        { ar: 'ضَرَبَ', en: 'faʿala: he struck', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'شَرِبَ', en: 'faʿila: he drank', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

ضَرَبَ (“he struck”) and شَرِبَ (“he drank”) are both actions. But ضَرَبَ is faʿala and شَرِبَ is faʿila. Their presents are يَضْرِبُ and يَشْرَبُ.

You cannot guess the vowel from the meaning or from the root letters. The same goes for the present: كَتَبَ and ضَرَبَ are both faʿala, but one has يَكْتُبُ and the other يَضْرِبُ. Learn the past and the present together.
</Block>

  <Stage>

A command from a Form I verb starts with a connecting alif, ٱ. It is not a letter of the root and it is not the Form IV letter. ٱضْرِب (“strike!”) comes from ضَرَبَ يَضْرِبُ, the verb that al-Ajurrumiyyah uses as its example.

A Form IV command starts with أَ with a fatḥa. In 23:29, أَنزِلْنِى (“send me down”) comes from أَنزَلَ. The Corpus gives ٱضْرِب no form number and أَنزِلْنِى (IV).

[L3.08](/learn/level-3/08-imperative/) teaches the command. For now: ٱ is a connecting alif, and أَ with fatḥa is Form IV.

<Block id="mistake-long" title="Mistake 5: a long ā means Form III">
  <Stage label="Same sound, different roots">
    <Tiles
      label="قَٰتَلَ is Form III. قَالَ is Form I."
      items={[
        { ar: 'قَٰتَلَ', en: 'Form III: root ق ت ل', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'قَالَ', en: 'Form I: root ق و ل', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Form III puts a long ā after the first root letter: قَٰتَلَ (“he fought”), root ق ت ل. The ā is added.

قَالَ (“he said”) also has a long ā after its first letter. But its root is ق و ل, with a و in the middle. The ā stands in for that و. Nothing is added, so قَالَ is Form I.

Find the root first. Then see if the ā is extra. If only two root letters show besides the ā, the ā may stand for a weak letter. [L4.12](/learn/level-4/12-hollow-verbs/) explains these verbs.

</Block>

<Block id="mistake-prefix" title="Mistake 6: counting ya- as extra, or reading است as Form X">
  <Stage label="The root decides">
    <Tiles
      label="Three present-tense verbs, three different forms"
      items={[
        { ar: 'نَعْبُدُ', en: 'Form I (1:5)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'نَسْتَعِينُ', en: 'Form X (1:5)', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'نَسْتَبِقُ', en: 'Form VIII (12:17)', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

There are two traps here.

**The tense prefix is not an extra letter.** نَعْبُدُ (“we worship”, Al-Fatiha 1:5) has a نَ in front, but that is the present-tense prefix. Take it off before you count. The root ع ب د is bare, so it is Form I.

**سْتَ at the start of a present verb does not always mean Form X.** Right next to it in 1:5, نَسْتَعِينُ (“we ask for help”) has the root ع و ن. Everything between the prefix and the root is added: Form X. But in نَسْتَبِقُ (12:17) the root is س ب ق, so the س is a root letter, and only ت is added: Form VIII.

So the root decides. [L3.03](/learn/level-3/03-root-system/) shows how to find it.
</Block>

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal reads رَفِيقًا as a *tamyīz*. *Al-Durr al-Maṣūn* gives two readings: a tamyīz or a *ḥāl* (the state: how?). Both put it in the accusative. It adds that رَفِيق is singular but can mean “companions”, as one word for one or many.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="practice-1" title="Practice: which form is it?">
  <Stage label="For each verb, say">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Take off</b> prefixes, endings and pronouns.</li>
      <li><b>Root:</b> which letters, and how many?</li>
      <li><b>Added:</b> a shadda, a long ā or an extra letter?</li>
      <li><b>Pattern:</b> faʿala, faʿila or faʿula?</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

Work out each answer first. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Which of these four verbs is Form I? Say why for each. (a) كَتَبَ (Al-Anʿam 6:54) (b) عَلَّمَ (Ar-Rahman 55:4) (c) أَنزَلَ (Al-Baqarah 2:22) (d) قَٰتَلَ (At-Tawbah 9:30)">

- (a) **Form I.** Root ك ت ب, three letters, nothing added. Pattern faʿala. The Corpus gives it no form number.
- (b) **Form II.** The middle letter has a shadda.
- (c) **Form IV.** An added أَ is in front of the root ن ز ل. The Corpus gives (IV).
- (d) **Form III.** A long ā is added after the first letter of the root ق ت ل.

Only (a) is Form I.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Make the “he” past verb in the faʿala pattern from the root ذ ه ب, “going”. Then say its present. The verse has the same verb with the ending -nā: ذَهَبْنَا (Yusuf 12:17).">

Put the letters into فَعَلَ: **ذَهَبَ**, *dhahaba*, “he went”. The present is **يَذْهَبُ**, *yadhhabu*, with fatḥa in the middle (door 3). The second root letter ه is a throat letter, and the throat-letter hint in step 2 points to fatḥa.

In the verse, ذَهَبْنَا is the same verb with the ending -nā, “we”: “we went”. L3.05 teaches the ending. The Corpus gives it no form number, so it is Form I.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Use the three steps on نَصَرَكُمُ (Al-Imran 3:123). What is the “he” verb, its root, its pattern and its form?">

1. **Take off** كُمُ (“you all”, an attached pronoun, L3.02). What is left is **نَصَرَ**.
2. **Root:** ن ص ر, three letters (the idea of helping).
3. **Added:** nothing. No shadda, no long ā, no extra letter.

The pattern is fatḥa, fatḥa: **faʿala**. The form is **Form I**, and the Corpus gives it no number. Its present is يَنْصُرُ, door 1. The verse says: “Allah helped you at Badr”. The doer ٱللَّهُ comes after the verb.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Three verbs from the root ع ل م: (a) يَعْلَمُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:102) (b) يُعَلِّمُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:102) (c) يَتَعَلَّمُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:102). Which is Form I, and what are the other two?">

- (a) **Form I.** Take off the prefix يَ and the ending -ūna. The root ع ل م is bare. The middle vowel of the present is fatḥa, which fits عَلِمَ (faʿila).
- (b) **Form II.** The middle letter ل has a shadda. The Corpus gives (II). L3.12 teaches it.
- (c) **Form V.** After the prefix يَ there is a تَ, and the middle letter has a shadda. The Corpus gives (V). L3.15 teaches it.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Speed drill. Is each verb Form I? (a) خَلَقَ (Ar-Rahman 55:3) (b) سَمِعَ (Al-Mujadila 58:1) (c) ٱقْتَرَبَ (Al-Anbiya 21:1) (d) أَنزَلَ (Al-Baqarah 2:22) (e) تَرَكْنَا (Yusuf 12:17) (f) ٱسْتَكْبَرَ (Al-Baqarah 2:34)">

- (a) **Yes.** Root خ ل ق, faʿala.
- (b) **Yes.** Root س م ع, faʿila.
- (c) **No, Form VIII.** The ت after the first letter ق is added, and the root is ق ر ب.
- (d) **No, Form IV.** The أَ in front of ن ز ل is added.
- (e) **Yes.** Take off the ending -nā: تَرَكَ, root ت ر ك.
- (f) **No, Form X.** است is added in front of the root ك ب ر.

Three of the six are Form I: (a), (b) and (e).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="A learner says: “جَآءَ (Al-Anʿam 6:54) is Form I, because it has no shadda and no extra letter in front.” Is this right?">

**Yes.** The Corpus gives جَآءَ no form number. Its root is ج ي أ.

But it does not look like كَتَبَ. The middle letter is weak (ي), and it shows as a long ā. The last root letter is a hamza. So the verb changes shape with endings: its “I” form is جِئْتُ. The test still works: nothing is added to the root. Weak verbs are taught in L4.11, and hollow verbs in L4.12.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Exercise n={7} q="The past عَلِمَ has kasra in the middle. What is the present “he” form? Check with وَٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ وَأَنتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ (Al-Imran 3:66).">

عَلِمَ is faʿila, door 4. Its present has fatḥa in the middle: **يَعْلَمُ**, yafʿalu. The verse has it: “And Allah knows, and you do not know.” The second verb, تَعْلَمُونَ, is the same verb with “you all”.

In Quran Learn’s count, all 17 faʿila verbs that have a *ya-* present form take fatḥa.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Which middle vowel does the present show in each? (a) يَفْعَلُ (Al-Imran 3:40) (b) يَخْلُقُ (Al-Imran 3:47) (c) يَضْرِبُ (Ibrahim 14:25) Then name the door of each.">

- (a) **Fatḥa**: yafʿalu. The past is فَعَلَ, faʿala, so this is door 3.
- (b) **Ḍamma**: yakhluqu. The past is خَلَقَ, faʿala, so this is door 1.
- (c) **Kasra**: yaḍribu. The past is ضَرَبَ, faʿala, so this is door 2.

All three verbs are faʿala in the past, and each has a different vowel in the present. This is why you learn the pair.

In (a) the ع is a throat letter. In (b) and (c) the second and third root letters are not throat letters.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Is أَكَلَ (Yusuf 12:17) Form IV, because it starts with أَ?">

**No.** Take off the أَ and two letters are left: كَلَ. So the hamza is a root letter, and the root is أ ك ل. The Corpus gives أَكَلَ no form number: it is Form I.

Compare أَنزَلَ. Without the أَ, three letters are left: نَزَلَ. So there the أَ is added, and it is Form IV.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Why is نَسْتَبِقُ (Yusuf 12:17) Form VIII and not Form X, although it starts with نَسْتَ?">

Take off the prefix نَ. What is left is سْتَبِقُ. Its root is س ب ق, so the س belongs to the root. Only the ت is added. That is Form VIII, and the Corpus gives (VIII).

In Form X, the whole of سْتَ is added in front of three root letters, as in نَسْتَعِينُ (root ع و ن). So find the root before you decide.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={11} q="The learner says: “يَكْتُبُ has a ي in front, so it is not Form I.” What is wrong?">

The يَ is the present-tense prefix. It is not an added letter of a different form. Take it off and the root ك ت ب is bare: Form I. Every present-tense verb starts with one of the four tense letters (أ ن ي ت). So a يَ in front shows that the verb is in the present. It does not tell you the form.

</Exercise>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="root plus pattern equals a Form I verb"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'root', small: 'ك ت ب' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'pattern', small: 'فَعَلَ' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'Form I verb', small: 'كَتَبَ' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- Form I, *al-fiʿl al-mujarrad*, is the bare verb: root letters and vowels, nothing added. It is 64% of the verbs in the Quran.
- فَعَلَ stands for any three-letter root: ف is the first letter, ع the second and ل the third.
- The “he” form of the past has three patterns: faʿala, faʿila and faʿula. The middle vowel is fatḥa, kasra or ḍamma.
- The “he” form of the present is *ya-* with a middle vowel: yafʿulu, yafʿilu or yafʿalu. Learn the past and the present together. The grammar books group the pairs into six doors.
- To spot Form I, take off prefixes and endings, find the root, and look for a shadda, a long ā or an extra letter. The Corpus gives Form I no form number.
- Two traps: an opening أَ may be a root letter (أَكَلَ), and سْتَ may belong partly to the root (نَسْتَبِقُ).
- Some very common Form I verbs, like قَالَ and كَانَ, change shape. [L3.05](/learn/level-3/05-past-tense/) and [L4.11](/learn/level-4/11-weak-verbs-intro/) explain.
