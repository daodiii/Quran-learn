Allah speaks to the angels. Read from right to left and look at the third word, جَاعِلٌ.

It ends in **-un**, like a noun. It is the khabar of *inna* (L2.10). But it also has an **object**: خَلِيفَةً, “a successor”, with **-an**. A plain noun such as “house” does not take an object. This word does, because it was made from a verb.

<Block id="from-verb" title="A noun made from a verb">
  <Stage label="One root, a verb and a noun">
    <Tiles
      label="جَعَلَ, then جَاعِلٌ: he made, one who makes"
      items={[
        { ar: 'جَعَلَ', en: 'he made', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'جَاعِلٌ', en: 'one who makes' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Both words come from the root **ج ع ل** (L3.03). The verb is جَعَلَ, “he made”. The noun is جَاعِلٌ, “one who makes”, a maker.

You met the same step in L2.03: خَلَقَ is a verb, and خَالِق is a noun, “creator”. The grammar books call a noun like this **ism fāʿil**, اِسْم فَاعِل, “name of the doer”. English calls it an **active participle**. This lesson says **doer word** too.

<Note kind="know">Do not mix up two names. A <i>fāʿil</i> is a role: the doer of a verb in a verbal sentence (L2.03). An <i>ism fāʿil</i> is a kind of noun. The next steps show that a doer word keeps being a noun, even when it has an object.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="two" title="Two kinds: the doer and the done-to">
  <Stage label="Same root, two nouns">
    <Tiles
      label="كَتَبَ, كَاتِبٌ, مَكْتُوبٌ: he wrote, one who writes, written"
      items={[
        { ar: 'كَتَبَ', en: 'he wrote', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'كَاتِبٌ', en: 'one who writes' },
        { ar: 'مَكْتُوبٌ', en: 'written, what is written' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A verb gives two nouns.

- The **doer word** names the one who does the action: كَاتِبٌ, “one who writes”. You find it in 2:282, “a scribe”.
- The **done-to word** names the one the action is done to: مَكْتُوبٌ, “written”. It is **ism mafʿūl**, اِسْم مَفْعُول, “name of the one done to”. English calls it a **passive participle**. You met مَكْتُوبًا in 7:157, and L3.09 taught the passive verb. The done-to word is the passive in noun form.

<Note kind="know">Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus: 2,956 words in the Quran are active participles and 550 are passive. Doer words are more than five times as common.</Note>

</Block>

<Step id="building" label="Building them" title="How a doer word and a done-to word are built">
<Block id="build-active" title="Form I doer word: فَاعِل">
  <Stage label="Pattern: a real verb, جَعَلَ">
    <Formula
      label="the root ج ع ل poured into فَاعِل gives جَاعِلٌ"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'root', small: 'ج ع ل' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'pattern', small: 'فَاعِل' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'جَاعِلٌ', small: 'one who makes' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Pour the three root letters into the pattern **فَاعِل** (L3.03):

| Place in the pattern | What it is | In جَاعِلٌ |
|---|---|---|
| ف | first root letter, with fatḥa | جَ |
| ا | a long ā (alif) | ا |
| ع | second root letter, with kasra | عِ |
| ل | third root letter, then the case ending | لٌ |

Al-Jadwal gives this pattern, فَاعِل, for جَاعِل in 2:30, for فَالِق in 6:95 and for شَاكِر in 2:158. It says each time that the word is an *ism fāʿil*.

<Check n={4} q="Which doer word comes from the root ظ ل م (to wrong)?" options={['مَظْلُومٌ', 'ظَالِمٌ', 'مُظْلِمٌ']} answer={1} why="ظَالِمٌ fits فَاعِل: alif after the first letter, kasra on the second. مَظْلُومٌ is the done-to word." />
</Block>

<Block id="build-passive" title="Form I done-to word: مَفْعُول">
  <Stage label="Pattern: a real verb, ظَلَمَ">
    <Formula
      label="the root ظ ل م poured into مَفْعُول gives مَظْلُومٌ"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'root', small: 'ظ ل م' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'pattern', small: 'مَفْعُول' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'مَظْلُومٌ', small: 'wronged' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The done-to word has an extra **مَ** in front and a long **ū** (و) before the last letter:

| Place in the pattern | What it is | In مَظْلُومٌ |
|---|---|---|
| مَ | an added mīm, with fatḥa | مَ |
| ف | first root letter, with sukūn | ظْ |
| ع | second root letter, with ḍamma | لُ |
| و | a long ū (waw) | و |
| ل | third root letter, then the case ending | مٌ |

You see مَظْلُومًا in 17:33, and al-Jadwal gives its pattern as مَفْعُول. In the same verse مَنصُورًا (“helped”) has the same pattern.

<Note kind="tip">Doer word: an **alif** after the first letter. Done-to word: a **مَ** in front and a **و** near the end.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="rule" title="Forms II to X: مُ plus the present verb">
  <Stage label="The rule">
    <Formula
      label="the present verb with مُ in place of the prefix gives the doer word"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'present verb', small: 'يُصَدِّقُ' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'مُ instead of يُ', small: 'kasra before the last letter' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'مُصَدِّقٌ', small: 'one who confirms' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Forms II to X use one rule. Al-Jadwal states it for مُصَدِّقًا in 2:41 and for مُؤْمِنِينَ in 2:8: the word has the shape of its present verb, with the prefix (يَ or يُ) replaced by **مُ**, and a **kasra** on the letter before the last.

- **Doer word:** يُصَدِّقُ becomes مُصَدِّقٌ. The letter before the last has **kasra**.
- **Done-to word:** يُكَرَّمُ becomes مُكَرَّمٌ. The letter before the last has **fatḥa**.

Form I does not use this rule. Its words are فَاعِل and مَفْعُول. L3.06 teaches the present verb, and L3.12 to L3.18 teach each form.

</Block>

In Forms II to X the doer word and the done-to word differ by one vowel. You find مُخْرِجُ in 6:95 and مُخْرَجِينَ in 15:48. Al-Jadwal calls the first a doer word and the second a done-to word, from the verb أَخْرَجَ.

Written without vowel marks, both are مخرج. The Quran text has the marks, so read the marks. If the marks are missing, the meaning decides.

When the last two letters are the same letter doubled, the vowel is hidden inside the shadda. Al-Jadwal says of مُصْفَرًّا in 30:51 that the doer word and the done-to word have one shape, and only the meaning separates them.

Many doer words come from weak roots, roots with و or ي. The letters change, so the word does not look like فَاعِل.

| Verb | Doer word | What changed | Al-Jadwal |
|---|---|---|---|
| قَامَ, he stood | قَائِمٌ | the middle و becomes a hamza (ئ) | 3:18 |
| أَقَامَ, he set up | مُقِيمٌ | the و turns into ي | 14:40 |
| بَقِيَ, he remained | بَاقٍ | the last ي drops before the tanwīn | 16:96 |

Al-Jadwal says of قَائِمًا in 3:18 that a hollow verb turns its weak letter into a hamza in the فَاعِل shape. It calls بَاقٍ a *manqūṣ* noun: the ي dropped, and the ḍamma is hidden on it.

You only need to recognise these words now. L4.11 gives an overview of weak verbs, and L4.12 and L4.13 teach the changes.

<Step id="noun-verb" label="Noun and verb" title="A noun that can work like a verb">
<Block id="noun" title="A participle is a noun">

    <Sentence

        { role: 'mubtada', words: [{ ar: 'وَأَنتُمْ', en: 'and you', say: 'wa-antum' }] },

A doer word is a noun, so it behaves like one.

- It takes **case endings**. Here ظَٰلِمُونَ is the khabar, nominative, shown by -ūna (L2.04). Al-Jadwal says the same.
- It takes **ٱلْ** and **tanwīn**: ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ, مُؤْمِنٌ.
- It can do the jobs of a noun. In the verses of this lesson: khabar of *inna* (جَاعِلٌ in 2:30), khabar (ظَٰلِمُونَ in 2:92), the first word of an idafah (فَالِقُ in 6:95), a delayed mubtadaʾ (هَادٍ in 13:7) and an adjective (مَقْطُوعَةٍ in 56:33).

In L2.12 you met شَانِئَكَ in 108:3, “the one who hates you”. It is a doer word from the verb شَنِئَ. It was the *ism inna*, accusative, and the first word of an idafah. The ending told you its job, as it does for any noun.
</Block>

<Block id="not-every" title="Not every describing word is a participle">

The root ع ل م gives a doer word, عَٰلِمُ (6:73), on the pattern فَاعِل. It also gives عَلِيمٌ (4:26), on the pattern فَعِيل (L2.01). Both describe someone who knows, but only the first is a participle.

A describing word is a participle when its pattern is فَاعِل or مَفْعُول (Form I), or مُ plus the present shape (Forms II to X). Other patterns give other describing words.

- Al-Jadwal calls ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ and ٱلرَّحِيمِ (1:3) adjectives (naʿt) of ٱللَّهِ. The Corpus tags both as adjectives, not participles.
- Al-Jadwal calls ٱلصَّمَدُ (112:2) a *ṣifa mushabbaha*, صِفَة مُشَبَّهَة, “a word that looks like a participle”, on the pattern فَعَل.
- Al-Jadwal says ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ (1:2) is a plural of عَالَم, a word for “all the worlds” with no single form from its own letters. The Corpus tags it as a noun.

<Exercise n={1} q="Build the doer word (فَاعِل) from each verb: (a) نَصَرَ (he helped) (b) ظَلَمَ (he wronged) (c) شَكَرَ (he thanked) (d) فَتَحَ (he opened). These are made-up exercises with real words.">

- (a) نَاصِرٌ, “helper”.
- (b) ظَالِمٌ, “wrongdoer”.
- (c) شَاكِرٌ, “one who thanks”.
- (d) فَاتِحٌ, “opener”.

Alif after the first letter, kasra on the second.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Build the done-to word (مَفْعُول) from each verb: (a) ذَكَرَ (he remembered) (b) شَكَرَ (he thanked) (c) فَعَلَ (he did) (d) غَفَرَ (he forgave). These are made-up exercises with real words.">

- (a) مَذْكُورٌ, “remembered”, as in 76:1.
- (b) مَشْكُورٌ, “thanked”, as in 17:19.
- (c) مَفْعُولٌ, “done”, as in 4:47.
- (d) مَغْفُورٌ, “forgiven”.

A مَ in front, sukūn on the first letter, ḍamma on the second, و before the last.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Give the doer word and the done-to word of each present verb: (a) يُصَدِّقُ (b) يُرْسِلُ (c) يَسْتَضْعِفُ. These are made-up exercises with real words.">

- (a) مُصَدِّقٌ (kasra before the last letter) and مُصَدَّقٌ (fatḥa).
- (b) مُرْسِلٌ and مُرْسَلٌ.
- (c) مُسْتَضْعِفٌ and مُسْتَضْعَفٌ. You see the second in ٱلْمُسْتَضْعَفِينَ (4:75).

Replace the prefix with مُ, then choose kasra or fatḥa before the last letter.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Doer word or done-to word? (a) فَالِقُ (Al-Anʿam 6:95) (b) مَّمْدُودٍ (Al-Waqiah 56:30) (c) مُّكَرَّمَةٍ (Abasa 80:13) (d) مُخْرَجِينَ (Al-Hijr 15:48) (e) مُصَدِّقًا (Al-Baqarah 2:41)">

- (a) **Doer word**: فَاعِل, “splitter”.
- (b) **Done-to word**: مَفْعُول, “stretched out”.
- (c) **Done-to word**: Form II, fatḥa before the last letter (before the ة).
- (d) **Done-to word**: Form IV, fatḥa before the ين ending.
- (e) **Doer word**: Form II, kasra before the last letter.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Find the doer word and its object: وَلَآ ءَآمِّينَ ٱلْبَيْتَ ٱلْحَرَامَ (Al-Maidah 5:2, part). Meaning: “and do not [harm] those heading for the Sacred House.”">

ءَآمِّينَ is the doer word, “those heading for”, plural, with -īna. Its object is ٱلْبَيْتَ, accusative (-a). ٱلْحَرَامَ describes ٱلْبَيْتَ. Al-Jadwal calls ٱلْبَيْتَ “the object of the doer word”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="What is the job of the doer word, and why does it end in -ūna? وَأَنتُمْ ظَٰلِمُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:92, end). Meaning: “and you were wrongdoers.”">

ظَٰلِمُونَ is the **khabar** of أَنتُمْ. It is nominative, and the mark is -ūna: a sound masculine plural shows the nominative with a و (L2.04). It comes from the root ظ ل م.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="What is the root of the doer word, and what is its job? وَمَا عِندَ ٱللَّهِ بَاقٍ (An-Nahl 16:96, part). Meaning: “and what is with Allah remains.”">

بَاقٍ comes from the root **ب ق ي**. The last ي dropped before the tanwīn, so al-Jadwal calls it a *manqūṣ* noun. It is the **khabar** of مَا, and its ḍamma is hidden.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Block id="practice-2" title="Practice: verb or participle, agreement and review">
<Exercise n={8} q="One verse has a verb and a doer word with almost the same meaning. Find both and say what each does: يُخْرِجُ ٱلْحَىَّ مِنَ ٱلْمَيِّتِ وَمُخْرِجُ ٱلْمَيِّتِ مِنَ ٱلْحَىِّ (Al-Anʿam 6:95, part).">

- يُخْرِجُ is a **verb** (present, Form IV). Its object ٱلْحَىَّ is accusative.
- مُخْرِجُ is a **doer word** (kasra before the last letter). It is the first word of an idafah, so ٱلْمَيِّتِ is genitive.

Both say “brings out”. Al-Jadwal says the verse switches from the verb to the doer word in its notes on style.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Why are both words feminine, single and genitive? لَّا مَقْطُوعَةٍ وَلَا مَمْنُوعَةٍ (Al-Waqiah 56:33).">

Both describe فَٰكِهَةٍ, “fruit” (56:32), which is feminine, single, indefinite and genitive. A describing word copies its noun (L2.09). So each ends in -atin.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Is each of these a participle? ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ (Al-Fatihah 1:3)">

No. Both are adjectives of ٱللَّهِ on the patterns فَعْلَٰن and فَعِيل. A participle needs فَاعِل, مَفْعُول or the مُ shape.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={11} q="Review L2.12: what is شَانِئَكَ (Al-Kawthar 108:3, part), and which case is it in?">

شَانِئَ is a doer word on the pattern فَاعِل, from the verb شَنِئَ, “he hated”, root ش ن أ. In 108:3 it is the *ism inna*, so it is accusative (-a). كَ is its second word in an idafah: “the one who hates you”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={12} q="Build both words from the root ر ز ق (to provide), then use one in a sentence. This is a made-up example.">

The doer word is رَازِقٌ, “provider”. The done-to word is مَرْزُوقٌ, “provided for”.

Made-up sentence: ٱللَّهُ رَازِقٌ, “Allah is a provider.” It is a nominal sentence: ٱللَّهُ is the mubtadaʾ and رَازِقٌ is the khabar, with -un.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="a root plus the doer pattern equals a doer word"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'root', small: 'ك ت ب' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'pattern', small: 'فَاعِل or مَفْعُول' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'participle', small: 'كَاتِب, مَكْتُوب' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A **doer word** (*ism fāʿil*, active participle) names the one who does the action. A **done-to word** (*ism mafʿūl*, passive participle) names the one the action is done to.
- Form I: **فَاعِل** and **مَفْعُول**. Forms II to X: **مُ** plus the present shape, with **kasra** before the last letter for the doer word and **fatḥa** for the done-to word.
- Both are nouns: they take case endings, ٱلْ, tanwīn, gender and number, and they can describe a noun.
- A doer word can take an object: accusative after a tanwīn or a plural -īna, genitive when it is the first word of an idafah. It stays a noun, so the sentence stays nominal.
- It has no time of its own. The sentence tells you when.
- Not every describing word is a participle. ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ, ٱلرَّحِيمِ and عَلِيمٌ are not.
- Weak roots hide letters: قَائِمٌ, مُقِيمٌ, بَاقٍ. L4.11 to L4.13 teach the changes.

**Next:** L3.20 teaches the verbal noun, the noun for the action itself. L3.21 teaches nouns of place and time. L4.01 teaches the ḥāl, where doer words are common.
