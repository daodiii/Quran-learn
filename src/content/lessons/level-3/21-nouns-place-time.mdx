Moses tells his young helper that he will keep walking until he reaches the meeting place of the two seas.

Look at مَجْمَع (*majmaʿ*), “a meeting place”. It starts with مَ, and its root is ج م ع, the root of gathering. Al-Jadwal calls it a **noun of place**, اِسْم مَكَان (*ism makān*): a word for the place where an action happens. The action is gathering. The place is where things gather.

</Block>

  <Stage label="مَفْعَل, one letter at a time">

      items={[

A noun of place has a fixed shape, a **pattern** (*wazn*, وَزْن). In [L3.03](/learn/level-3/03-root-system/) the letters ف ع ل stood for the first, second and third root letters. For a noun of place, the pattern is **مَفْعَل** (*mafʿal*):

- **مَ** is added in front.
- The first root letter has a sukūn.
- The second root letter has a vowel. Here it is fatḥa. Step 2 shows when it is kasra instead.
- The third root letter ends the word.

So the root ج م ع gives مَجْمَع. Al-Jadwal gives this pattern for the word, مَفْعَل, with fatḥa on the mīm and on the ʿayn (the middle letter).

<Note kind="know">The Arabic name for a word of time is **noun of time**, اِسْم زَمَان (*ism zamān*). It has the same shape as the noun of place. The next block shows why.</Note>

</Block>

This is from the rules of the pilgrimage. The offering is an animal that the pilgrim sends to Makkah.

The word مَحِلَّهُ (*maḥillahu*, “its place or time”) comes from the verb حَلَّ يَحِلُّ, which al-Jadwal explains as “to come out of the pilgrim's state”. Al-Jadwal calls the noun a **noun of place or of time**. The verse can mean the place where the animal is released, or the time when it is released.

The same shape serves both jobs. In its notes on 17:80, al-Jadwal says these nouns are made for the time or the place where an action happens, and that **the sentence tells you which one is meant**.

<Note kind="know">The root of مَحِلّ is ح ل ل. Its second and third letters are the same, so they join into one doubled letter. That is why you see a shadda and not three letters.</Note>

<Block id="zarf" title="Not the same as a place word">
  <Stage label="Al-Baqarah 2:115">
    <Tiles

Do not mix up two names that sound close. Al-Jadwal uses both in Al-Baqarah 2:115.

- ٱلْمَشْرِقُ, “the east”, is a **noun of place**, اِسْم مَكَان. It is made from a verb by a pattern. It has a root: ش ر ق.
- ثَمَّ, “there”, is a **place word**, ظَرْف مَكَان (*ẓarf makān*). Al-Jadwal explains it as a noun that points to something far away. It is not made from a verb.

*Ẓarf* names a job in the sentence. You saw it in [L2.01](/learn/level-2/01-nominal-sentence/), where عِندَ, “with”, was a khabar. A noun of place is a kind of word, and its shape shows it.
</Block>
</Step>

<Step id="patterns" label="Two patterns" title="Mafʿal or mafʿil?">
<Block id="two" title="Two patterns, one difference">
  <Stage label="Fatḥa or kasra on the middle letter">
    <Tiles
      label="the two patterns, with a Quran word for each"
      items={[
        { ar: 'مَفْعَل', en: 'مَجْمَع: fatḥa in the middle' },
        { ar: 'مَفْعِل', en: 'مَجْلِس: kasra in the middle' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A Form I noun of place has two patterns. Both start with مَ and both give the first root letter a sukūn. They differ in one vowel: the vowel on the **middle root letter**.

- **مَفْعَل** (*mafʿal*): fatḥa. مَجْمَع, “a meeting place”, from ج م ع.
- **مَفْعِل** (*mafʿil*): kasra. مَجْلِس, “a place where people sit”, from ج ل س.

You cannot tell which pattern a root takes by looking at its letters. The next block shows what decides it.
</Block>

<Block id="rule" title="The rule: look at the present tense">
  <Stage label="The present vowel decides">
    <Tiles
      label="three verbs in the present tense, and the noun each gives"
      items={[
        { ar: 'يَقْعُدُ', en: 'ḍamma, so مَقْعَد', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'يَجْمَعُ', en: 'fatḥa, so مَجْمَع', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'يَجْلِسُ', en: 'kasra, so مَجْلِس', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

In [L3.04](/learn/level-3/04-verb-form-i/) you learned that the present tense of Form I has a vowel on its middle letter: ḍamma, fatḥa or kasra. That vowel decides the noun of place.

| Vowel on the middle letter of the present | Pattern | Example | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| ḍamma | مَفْعَل | قَعَدَ يَقْعُدُ gives مَقْعَد | Al-Imran 3:121 |
| fatḥa | مَفْعَل | جَمَعَ يَجْمَعُ gives مَجْمَع | Al-Kahf 18:60 |
| kasra | مَفْعِل | جَلَسَ يَجْلِسُ gives مَجْلِس | Al-Mujadila 58:11 |

Al-Jadwal states the rule in its notes on 17:80: a noun of place or time is made on مَفْعَل when the middle letter of the present has ḍamma or fatḥa, and on مَفْعِل when it has kasra. It also gives the reason for single words. On 3:121 it says the middle letter of the present of قَعَدَ has ḍamma. On 9:25 it says the middle letter of the present of وَطَنَ has kasra. On 2:60 it says the middle letter of the present of شَرِبَ has fatḥa. That last verse is the one L3.04 used for مَشْرَب, “a place to drink”.

Ḍamma and fatḥa give the same result. Kasra is the one that differs.

<Note kind="remember">Kasra in the present gives kasra in the noun: مَفْعِل. Any other vowel gives fatḥa: مَفْعَل.</Note>

<Note kind="know">L3.03 showed مَرْقَد (36:52), “a place of lying down”, as the pattern مَفْعَل. Now you can see why. Al-Jadwal says it comes from رَقَدَ, whose present has ḍamma, so the noun has fatḥa.</Note>

Allah tells the believers to make room for one another where they sit. The word ٱلْمَجَٰلِسِ is a plural. Al-Jadwal says its singular is مَجْلِس, a noun of place from جَلَسَ. It also says the verb is *bāb ḍaraba*, the door of ضَرَبَ يَضْرِبُ in L3.04: the present is يَجْلِسُ, with kasra.

So the noun has kasra too. The plural is Step 3.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal says that a verb whose first letter is weak, like وَعَدَ, also gives مَفْعِل, whatever its present vowel: مَوْعِد, “a promised place or time”. Weak verbs are taught in Level 4: [L4.14](/learn/level-4/14-assimilated-verbs/) has the verbs whose first letter is و. For verbs that end in a weak letter, al-Jadwal gives مَرْمًى and مَسْعًى as its examples ([L4.13](/learn/level-4/13-defective-verbs/)).</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="exceptions" title="Eleven words that break the rule">
  <Stage label="Present with ḍamma, noun with kasra">
    <Tiles
      label="three common words that break the rule"
      items={[
        { ar: 'مَشْرِق', en: 'يَشْرُقُ has ḍamma' },
        { ar: 'مَغْرِب', en: 'يَغْرُبُ has ḍamma' },
        { ar: 'مَسْجِد', en: 'يَسْجُدُ has ḍamma' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Three common words look like a mistake. Their verbs have ḍamma in the present, so the rule says fatḥa: مَشْرَق, مَغْرَب, مَسْجَد. But the words have kasra.

Al-Jadwal says so itself. On Al-Baqarah 2:115 it says that مَشْرِق, from شَرَقَ, and مَغْرِب, from غَرَبَ, have kasra “against the rule”. On 2:114 it says the same of مَسْجِد, from سَجَدَ. In its notes on 17:80 it lists eleven words like these:

<blockquote class="lx-quote" lang="ar" dir="rtl">
  <p>منسك ومطلع ومشرق ومغرب ومرفق ومفرق ومجزر ومنبت ومسقط ومسكن ومسجد</p>
</blockquote>
<p class="lx-quote-note">From al-Jadwal, notes on 17:80. The original has no vowel marks.</p>

Learn these eleven as they are. Use the rule for the other words.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal does not agree with itself about two of the eleven. Its list includes مطلع and مسكن with kasra. But its notes on 97:5 and 9:24 give fatḥa for them: مَطْلَع and مَسْكَن. This lesson follows the note on the verse, and the Corpus text has fatḥa in 97:5.</Note>

A verb with more than three letters (Forms II to X) has no choice of vowels. Al-Jadwal says, in its notes on 17:80, that its noun of place or time is made on the shape of the **passive participle**: مُ in front, and fatḥa on the second letter from the end. You will study that participle in [L3.19](/learn/level-3/19-active-passive-participles/).

| Form | Verb | Noun | Shape | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IV | أَدْخَلَ | مُدْخَل | مُفْعَل | Al-Isra 17:80 |
| VIII | ٱلْتَحَدَ | مُلْتَحَد | مُفْتَعَل | Al-Kahf 18:27 |
| X | ٱسْتَقَرَّ | مُسْتَقَرّ | مُسْتَفْعَل | Al-Baqarah 2:36 |
| X | ٱسْتَوْدَعَ | مُسْتَوْدَع | مُسْتَفْعَل | Al-Anam 6:98 |

In 2:36 Allah tells Adam and Hawwa to go down to the earth. مُسْتَقَرٌّ is the mubtadaʾ, put later. Al-Jadwal calls it a noun of place from the six-letter verb ٱسْتَقَرَّ. The khabar comes first, as in [L2.01](/learn/level-2/01-nominal-sentence/).

<Note kind="know">The Corpus tags مُسْتَقَرّ, مُدْخَل and مُلْتَحَد as passive participles. Al-Jadwal calls them nouns of place. Both are about the same shape. This lesson follows al-Jadwal.</Note>

The same shape can name a place, a time, or the action itself. When it names the action, al-Jadwal calls it a **maṣdar mīmī**, مَصْدَر مِيمِيّ: a verbal noun that starts with mīm. [L3.20](/learn/level-3/20-verbal-nouns/) teaches the verbal noun.

- **18:58.** For مَوْئِل al-Jadwal gives three readings: a verbal noun with mīm (“returning”), a noun of time, or a noun of place. The sense here is a place to run to.
- **17:80.** Al-Jadwal reads مُدْخَل, and مُخْرَج after it, as verbal nouns with mīm: “entering” and “leaving”. مُدْخَلَ is an object that repeats the action of the verb, a *mafʿūl muṭlaq* ([L4.07](/learn/level-4/07-maf-ul-mutlaq/)): “let me enter an entering”.
- **2:36.** Al-Jadwal reads مُسْتَقَرّ as a noun of place, and adds that it may be a verbal noun with mīm, “staying”.
- **97:5.** Al-Jadwal says مَطْلَع is a verbal noun with mīm from طَلَعَ: “the rising”. Step 4 goes through that verse.

Al-Jadwal ends its notes on 17:80 with the same point: for nouns of place and time, the verbal noun with mīm, and the passive participle of the longer verbs, **one shape serves all**, and the sentence shows which one is meant.

A noun of place has a **broken plural** (*jamʿ taksīr*): a plural made by changing the shape of the word. Its pattern is **مَفَاعِل** (*mafāʿil*). Al-Jadwal names this pattern for مَوَاطِن in 9:25.

| Singular | Plural | Meaning | Plural in |
|---|---|---|---|
| مَسْجِد | مَسَاجِد | mosque | Al-Baqarah 2:114 |
| مَقْعَد | مَقَاعِد | seat | Al-Imran 3:121 |
| مَجْلِس | مَجَالِس | a place to sit | Al-Mujadila 58:11 |
| مَسْكَن | مَسَاكِن | house | At-Tawbah 9:24 |
| مَوْطِن | مَوَاطِن | place | At-Tawbah 9:25 |
| مَقْبَرَة | مَقَابِر | graveyard | At-Takathur 102:2 |

Compare the singular with the plural. The pattern مَفْعَل or مَفْعِل becomes مَفَاعِل: a long ā comes in after the first root letter, and the middle vowel is a kasra. The plural is the same for both patterns.

In 3:121 al-Jadwal says مَقَاعِد is the plural of مَقْعَد, a noun of place from قَعَدَ, with fatḥa because the present has ḍamma. The word is the second object of تُبَوِّئُ.
</Block>

    <Tiles

These plurals are **diptotes**: words kept from the full ending ([L2.06](/learn/level-2/06-genitive-case/)). Al-Ajurrumiyyah says the diptote is put in the genitive with a fatḥa. Al-Jadwal says this about مَوَاطِن in 9:25: the mark of the genitive is the fatḥa, because the word is kept from the full ending.

What the five words show:

- **Without al-**, the word takes no tanwīn: -u in 9:24 (and not -un), -a in 3:121 (and not -an), and -a in the genitive in 9:25 (and not -in).
- **With al- in front**, as in 58:11, or as **the first word of an idafa**, as in 14:45, the genitive ends in the usual -i. In both verses the Corpus tags the word as genitive.

So a plural of the مَفَاعِل pattern is not decided by its ending alone. Look at what is around the word.

<Step id="practice" label="Practice" title="Practice">
<Block id="method" title="How to spot one, and what to avoid">
  <Stage label="Four questions">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Start:</b> does the word start with مَ (or مُ for a longer verb), with a root after it?</li>
      <li><b>Root:</b> take away the مَ and find the root letters.</li>
      <li><b>Pattern:</b> Form I: look at the vowel of the present. Kasra gives مَفْعِل, anything else gives مَفْعَل. Longer verb: the shape of the passive participle.</li>
      <li><b>Job:</b> place, time or action? The sentence decides.</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

Work through the four questions on a word. A word that starts with مَ can be something else. Two traps:

- A **passive participle** of Form I looks alike. It is مَفْعُول, with a long ū after the middle letter: مَكْتُوب, “written”. A noun of place has no long ū.
- The مَ can be a root letter. In مَوْت, “death”, and مَلِك, “a king”, the م is the first root letter, and nothing is added.

<Note kind="warn">
Common mistakes:

- Choosing the pattern from the **past** vowel. جَلَسَ and قَعَدَ have the same past vowel and give different nouns. Look at the present.
- Treating مَشْرِق, مَغْرِب and مَسْجِد as regular. They are among the eleven that break the rule.
- Mixing up the passive participle مَفْعُول with a noun of place. Look for the long ū.
- Counting the مَ as a root letter, or taking any word that starts with مَ for a noun of place. Check the root.
- Reading a plural of the مَفَاعِل pattern from its ending alone. It takes no tanwīn, and its genitive has -a, or -i with al- or in an idafa.
- Mixing up the **noun of place** (a kind of word, made from a verb) and the **place word**, ẓarf (a job in the sentence, like عِندَ or ثَمَّ).
</Note>
</Block>

<Exercise n={1} q="Each verb is shown with its present tense. Which pattern gives its noun of place, مَفْعَل or مَفْعِل? (a) رَجَعَ يَرْجِعُ (b) قَعَدَ يَقْعُدُ (c) جَمَعَ يَجْمَعُ">

- (a) The present has kasra, so the pattern is **مَفْعِل**: مَرْجِع. Yunus 10:4 has إِلَيْهِ مَرْجِعُكُمْ. The present يَرْجِعُونَ is in Al-Baqarah 2:18.

- (c) The present has fatḥa, so the pattern is **مَفْعَل**: مَجْمَع, “a meeting place” (Al-Kahf 18:60). The present يَجْمَعُ is in Ash-Shura 42:15.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Give the root, the pattern and the case of the noun of place: مَقْعَدِ صِدْقٍ (Al-Qamar 54:55, part). Meaning: “a seat of truth”.">

The root is **ق ع د** (sitting). The pattern is **مَفْعَل**, with fatḥa on the middle letter, because the present يَقْعُدُ has ḍamma. It is a noun of place: “a seat”.

مَقْعَدِ is **genitive** (-i) after فِى, and it is the first word of an idafa. صِدْقٍ is the second word, “of truth”, genitive (-in). Al-Jadwal reads the whole phrase فِى مَقْعَدِ صِدْقٍ as a second khabar of إِنَّ from the verse before.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Say what each part is: إِلَيْهِ مَرْجِعُكُمْ (Yunus 10:4, start). Meaning: “To Him is your return.”">

- إِلَيْهِ, “to Him”: the **khabar, put first**. Al-Jadwal: a phrase tied to a khabar that is left out.
- مَرْجِعُ: the **mubtadaʾ, put later**, nominative (-u). It has the pattern **مَفْعِل**, from رَجَعَ يَرْجِعُ. It is the first word of an idafa.
- كُمْ, “your”: the second word of the idafa.

The sentence says “to Him is your returning”. Place, time or action: the sentence decides. Here the meaning is the returning.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="What kind of word is مُلْتَحَدًا, and from which form of the verb? وَلَن تَجِدَ مِن دُونِهِۦ مُلْتَحَدًا (Al-Kahf 18:27, end). Meaning: “and you will not find a refuge besides Him.”">

The root is **ل ح د**. The Corpus says it comes from **Form VIII**. Al-Jadwal: a noun of place from the five-letter verb ٱلْتَحَدَ, “he took refuge”, with the shape **مُفْتَعَل**: ḍamma on the mīm and fatḥa on the second letter from the end.

It is **accusative** (-an) and it is the **first object** of تَجِدَ. The phrase مِن دُونِهِ is the second object, as al-Jadwal reads it.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Two nouns of place in one verse. Give the form and the shape of both: فَمُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمُسْتَوْدَعٌ (Al-Anam 6:98, part). Meaning: “a place to stay and a place of keeping”.">

Both come from **Form X**: ٱسْتَقَرَّ (root ق ر ر) and ٱسْتَوْدَعَ (root و د ع). Both have the shape **مُسْتَفْعَل**: ḍamma on the mīm and fatḥa on the second letter from the end. It is the shape of the passive participle.

Al-Jadwal calls مُسْتَوْدَع a noun of place from the six-letter verb ٱسْتَوْدَعَ, and adds that it may be a verbal noun with mīm, “the keeping”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="What is the singular of مَسَٰكِنُ, and why does it end in -u and not -un? وَمَسَٰكِنُ تَرْضَوْنَهَآ (At-Tawbah 9:24, part). Meaning: “and houses that you are pleased with”.">

The singular is **مَسْكَن**, a noun of place from سَكَنَ, “he stayed”. Al-Jadwal says its pattern is مَفْعَل, with fatḥa, because the present has ḍamma.

مَسَٰكِنُ is a broken plural of the **مَفَاعِل** pattern, a diptote. It has no word like “the” in front, so it takes no tanwīn: -u, not -un.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Why does مَسَٰكِنِ end in -i here, when مَوَاطِنَ ended in -a in 9:25? فِى مَسَٰكِنِ ٱلَّذِينَ (Ibrahim 14:45, part). Meaning: “in the houses of those who …”">

Both words are genitive. مَوَاطِنَ stood alone after فِى, and a diptote has -a in the genitive.

Here مَسَٰكِنِ is the **first word of an idafa**: “the houses of …”, with ٱلَّذِينَ as the second word. The usual -i comes back. The Corpus tags the word as genitive.

</Exercise>
</Block>

<Exercise n={8} q="Place, time or action? Say what al-Jadwal says about each word. (a) مَحِلَّهُۥ (Al-Baqarah 2:196, part) (b) مَطْلَعِ (Al-Qadr 97:5) (c) لِمَهْلِكِهِم (Al-Kahf 18:59, part)">

- (a) **A noun of place or of time**, from حَلَّ يَحِلُّ (door 2). The sentence could mean the place or the time of release.
- (b) **A verbal noun with mīm** (*maṣdar mīmī*) from طَلَعَ: “the rising”. The pattern is مَفْعَل.
- (c) **A verbal noun with mīm** from هَلَكَ: “their destruction”. Al-Jadwal gives the pattern مَفْعِل, with kasra, and says the verb is *bāb ḍaraba* and *bāb fataḥa*.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Two of these four words are not nouns of place or time: مَسْجِد، مَكْتُوب، مَشْرِق، مَوْت. Which two, and why?">

**مَكْتُوب** is the passive participle of كَتَبَ, “written”. It has a long ū after the middle letter: the shape مَفْعُول ([L3.19](/learn/level-3/19-active-passive-participles/)).

**مَوْت**, “death”, has the م as its first root letter (م و ت). Nothing is added in front.

مَسْجِد and مَشْرِق are nouns of place. Both are among the eleven words that break the rule.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Write the family of each root: the verb, the active participle, the verbal noun and the noun of place. (a) ق ع د (b) س ج د. The Corpus lists these words under the roots.">

- (a) **قَعَدَ**, “he sat” (present يَقْعُدُ). **قَاعِد**, “sitting” (the active participle, [L3.19](/learn/level-3/19-active-passive-participles/)). **قُعُود**, “sitting” (the verbal noun, [L3.20](/learn/level-3/20-verbal-nouns/)). **مَقْعَد**, “a seat”: مَفْعَل because the present has ḍamma.
- (b) **سَجَدَ**, “he bowed down” (present يَسْجُدُ). **سَاجِد**, “one who bows down”. **سُجُود**, “bowing down”. **مَسْجِد**, “a place of bowing down”: here the present has ḍamma, so the rule says مَسْجَد, but the word has kasra. It is one of the eleven.

The Corpus does not list a passive participle مَسْجُود for the root س ج د in the Quran.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={11} q="Made-up example. The verb كَتَبَ يَكْتُبُ has ḍamma in the present (door 1 in L3.04). Build the noun of place, “a place of writing”. Then add the tāʾ marbūṭa.">

The present has ḍamma, so the pattern is **مَفْعَل**: مَكْتَب, “a desk or an office”.

With the tāʾ marbūṭa: مَكْتَبَة, the pattern **مَفْعَلَة**, “a library”.

Neither word is in the Quran. This is the old lesson's example, kept for practice. Al-Jadwal says words like مَدْرَسَة take the tāʾ marbūṭa because the Arabs said them so, and that there is no rule for it.

</Exercise>
</Block>

What you can now do:

1. A noun of place or time is a word made from a verb root with مَ in front. It names where or when an action happens.
2. For a Form I verb, look at the vowel of the present tense. Kasra gives **مَفْعِل**. Ḍamma or fatḥa gives **مَفْعَل**.
3. Eleven words break the rule, among them مَشْرِق, مَغْرِب and مَسْجِد.
4. A longer verb makes the noun in the shape of the passive participle: مُدْخَل, مُلْتَحَد, مُسْتَقَرّ.
5. One shape can name a place, a time or the action. The sentence decides. Al-Jadwal calls the action word a maṣdar mīmī.
6. The plural is **مَفَاعِل**, a diptote: no tanwīn, and -a in the genitive unless al- or an idafa is there.
7. A noun of place is not the same as a place word (ẓarf).
