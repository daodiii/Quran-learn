You met this word in L1.04. It is a **command**, أَمْر (*amr*): a verb that asks someone to do something. In Arabic it is one word. In English it is one word too: “Read!”

Who is told to read? **You**, the person spoken to. Nobody writes أَنتَ (“you”). The “you” is hidden inside the verb, as in L2.03. Al-Jadwal reports that most commentators say these opening verses were the first revelation.

Commands are common. The Quranic Arabic Corpus tags 1,876 words as commands, and 1,181 of them are Form I, the basic verb (L3.04). This is Quran Learn's count of the words whose verb is tagged as a command. A command is easy to learn, because it is the present tense with a few small changes.

<Block id="recipe" title="Make a command in three moves">
  <Stage label="From present to command">
    <Tiles
      label="تَقْرَأُ, then تَقْرَأْ, then قْرَأْ, then ٱقْرَأْ"

        { ar: 'قْرَأْ', en: '2. take off تَ', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'ٱقْرَأْ', en: '3. add ٱ: read!', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />

Start with the present tense for “you”, one man: تَقْرَأُ, “you read” (L3.06). Then make three moves.

1. **End in a sukūn.** The ending -u becomes ـْ. This is what the jussive does to a present verb (L3.07). تَقْرَأُ becomes تَقْرَأْ.
2. **Take off the prefix تَ.** What is left is قْرَأْ.
3. **Add a helper if the word now starts with a silent letter.** The قْ starts with a sukūn, and Arabic does not start a word with a silent letter. So add the **helping alif**, ٱ, called هَمْزَة الْوَصْل (*hamzat al-waṣl*, “the joining hamza”). Now you have ٱقْرَأْ.

<Note kind="tip">After move 2, try to say the word. If you cannot say it without a vowel in front, you need the helper ٱ.</Note>

<Note kind="know">The jussive (جَزْم, *jazm*) is a mood of the present tense, taught in L3.07. You only need its endings here: a sukūn, a dropped nūn, or a dropped weak letter. Step 3 of this lesson shows how the command uses them.</Note>

<Block id="helper" title="The helping alif sounds u or i">
  <Stage label="Which vowel?">
    <Tiles
      label="three commands with a helper: usjud, idhhab, ijlis"
      items={[
        { ar: 'ٱسْجُدْ', en: 'usjud: bow down!', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ٱذْهَبْ', en: 'idhhab: go!', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ٱجْلِسْ', en: 'ijlis: sit!', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

In the Quran the helper is written ٱ with no vowel mark. You choose its sound. For a Form I verb, look at the present tense for “he” and find the vowel on the **middle** root letter.

| Verb (root) | Present “he” | Vowel on the middle letter | Command | The helper sounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| س ج د | يَسْجُدُ | ḍamma | ٱسْجُدْ | u |
| ذ ه ب | يَذْهَبُ | fatḥa | ٱذْهَبْ | i |
| ج ل س | يَجْلِسُ | kasra | ٱجْلِسْ | i |
| ق ر أ | يَقْرَأُ | fatḥa | ٱقْرَأْ | i |
| ن ح ر | يَنْحَرُ | fatḥa | ٱنْحَرْ | i |

A ḍamma gives **u**. A fatḥa or a kasra gives **i**. Some learner books write the helper as أُ or إِ. It is the same sound.

You say the helper at the start of a sentence. After a little word in front, it is silent. The Quran writes ٱ either way, but you say وَٱنْحَرْ as *wa-nḥar* (108:2) and وَٱسْجُدِى as *wa-sjudī* (3:43).

<Note kind="warn">Not every command starts with ٱ. A Form IV command starts with أَ, a hamza that is said even after وَ. Step 4 shows it.</Note>

</Block>

</Step>

A command is said to “you”, and Arabic has five ways to say “you”: one man, one woman, two people, a group of men (or a mixed group), and a group of women. So there are five commands. The stem ٱسْجُدْ stays the same, and the ending says who is told.

The table is a pattern for one real verb. ٱسْجُدْ is in 96:19, ٱسْجُدِى is in 3:43 and ٱسْجُدُوا۟ is in 53:62. The forms for “you two” and “you women” are shown as part of the pattern.

Where is the doer? In ٱسْجُدْ it is the hidden “you”. In the other four, the ending **is** the doer: the ى is “you, one woman”, the ا is “you two”, the و is “you all”, and the نَ is “you women”. L2.03 taught this for the endings -tu, -ū and -nā.

<Note kind="remember">A command has a doer, and the doer is “you”. For one man it is hidden. For the other four it is the ending.</Note>

The three moves work for all five persons. Only move 1 changes: the command takes the ending that the jussive would give.

| Who | Present “you” | Move 1: the jussive ending | Command |
|---|---|---|---|
| one man | تَسْجُدُ | تَسْجُدْ | ٱسْجُدْ |
| one woman | تَسْجُدِينَ | تَسْجُدِى | ٱسْجُدِى |
| two people | تَسْجُدَانِ | تَسْجُدَا | ٱسْجُدَا |
| men | تَسْجُدُونَ | تَسْجُدُوا۟ | ٱسْجُدُوا۟ |
| women | تَسْجُدْنَ | تَسْجُدْنَ | ٱسْجُدْنَ |

Three present forms end in a nūn that the jussive drops: -īna, -āni and -ūna. The women's form keeps its nūn. In تَسْجُدْنَ the nūn is the doer, “you women”, so it is not an ending that can drop.

<Note kind="know">*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* says the nūn is the sign of the nominative in these present forms, and that dropping it is the sign of the jussive. The book calls them “the five verbs”: the forms for two people, a group and one woman. L3.07 teaches them.</Note>

This verse has two commands for a group: ٱسْجُدُوا۟, “bow down”, and ٱعْبُدُوا۟, “worship”. Each ends in -ū. The little words فَ and وَ come in front, so the helper ٱ is silent: you say *fa-sjudū* and *wa-ʿbudū*. The small circle over the last alif means the alif is written but not said.

Commands for a group of men and for one man are the most common. In the Corpus, 951 command words are for one man and 870 are for a group of men. That is 1,821 of the 1,876. The other three persons have only 55 words between them: 27 for one woman, 20 for two people and 8 for women. These are counts of the Corpus person tags, made by Quran Learn.

<Step id="built" label="A fixed ending" title="Why the ending does not change">
<Block id="mabni" title="A command is built, not moved">
  <Stage label="Three kinds of fixed ending">
    <Tiles
      label="three commands: built on a sukūn, on a dropped nūn, on a dropped weak letter"
      items={[
        { ar: 'ٱنْحَرْ', en: 'built on a sukūn', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ٱقْنُتِى', en: 'built on a dropped nūn', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'صَلِّ', en: 'built on a dropped weak letter', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A noun changes its ending with its job: -u, -a or -i (L2.04 to L2.06). A present-tense verb changes its ending with its mood (L3.07). A command does not change. ٱنْحَرْ stays ٱنْحَرْ, whatever words are around it.

The grammar books call a word with a fixed ending **built**, مَبْنِي (*mabnī*). Al-Jadwal says a command is built on whatever the jussive would show:

- a **sukūn**, as in ٱنْحَرْ (108:2);
- a **dropped nūn**, as in ٱقْنُتِى (3:43);
- a **dropped weak letter**, as in صَلِّ (108:2).

You know all three from the present tense: it is the same ending that you chose in move 1 of the recipe. L2.12 showed ٱنْحَرْ and صَلِّ in Al-Kawthar, and their endings are different for this reason.

The women's form ends in a sukūn, like the form for one man. The nūn there is the doer, so it is not dropped. The weak verb ٱدْعُ is in step 4.

A built word has no case and no mood. So when you go through a command word by word, you give three things: it is a command, what it is built on, and who the doer is.

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* is a short grammar book by Ibn Ājurrūm (died 723 AH). It names three kinds of verb: past, present and command. For the command it says it is **jussive** (*majzūm*). It then says the jussive has two signs: the sukūn, and dropping a letter.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal says the command is **built**. Al-Ajurrumiyyah says it is **jussive**. The two books name the same endings: a sukūn, a dropped nūn, a dropped weak letter. They give the endings a different label. This course follows al-Jadwal. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn on 2:21 and 2:110 does not discuss the ending of the command, so it does not settle the point.</Note>
</Block>

The command form is only for “you”. Arabic has no command form for “he”, “she” or “they”. To tell someone who is not there, Arabic puts the little word لِ (*lām al-amr*, “the lām of command”) in front of a present verb. The Corpus tags the verb as jussive.

This verse has both kinds side by side. ٱتَّقُوا۟ is a command for “you all”. It has no prefix, and its ending is a dropped nūn. وَلْتَنظُرْ is a command for “a soul”, which is “she”. It keeps the prefix تَ, and the لِ in front makes the command.

L3.07 teaches the little words that put a present verb in the jussive: the لِ of this command, and the لَا of “do not”.

<Note kind="remember">If a verb has a present-tense prefix (يَ, تَ, نَ, أَ) and means a command, it is لِ plus the jussive. It is not the command form.</Note>

The other verb forms make commands in the same way: take the present for “you”, end it like the jussive, take off the prefix, and add a helper only if the word starts with a silent letter. Form I is the most common among the commands (1,181), then Form IV (289), Form VIII (160) and Form II (105). Quran Learn made these counts from the Corpus form tags.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| III | تُجَٰدِلُ | جَٰدِلْ | the stem letter, no helper | L3.17 |

A command can carry a short pronoun on its end. Here نَا is “us”, and it is the **object**. The command ٱنصُرْ keeps its shape in front of it. The doer is still the hidden “you”.

You know the endings that show the doer: ـِى, ـَا, ـُوا۟ and ـْنَ. An object pronoun is a different ending. It comes after them. L3.02 teaches the attached pronouns.

A command is not always strict. The believers in this verse are asking. Al-Jadwal calls a command that is a request to Allah an *amr duʿāʾī*, “a command that is a prayer”. The form is the same. The situation decides the meaning: an order, advice or a request. ٱهْدِنَا in 1:6 is ٱهْدِ with نَا, a prayer of the same kind.

Here are three clues for a command form.

1. **It has no present-tense prefix** (يَ, تَ, نَ, أَ) in front of the stem. Forms V and VI are the exception, because their تَ is part of the form (L3.15, L3.18).
2. **Its ending is one of the five:** a sukūn, ـِى, ـَا, ـُوا۟ or ـْنَ. A weak verb may drop its last letter instead.
3. **It tells “you” to do something.**

In this verse, لَا تُطِعْهُ looks like a command because it ends in a sukūn. But it has the prefix تُ and it follows لَا, so it is a present verb that says “do not”. L3.07 teaches that. The next two words have no prefix, so they are commands.

<Block id="mistakes" title="Common mistakes">
  <Stage label="Three look-alikes">
    <Tiles
      label="ٱكْتُبْ is right. تَكْتُبْ keeps the prefix. كْتُبْ has no helper."

1. **Keeping the prefix.** تَكْتُبْ ends in a sukūn like the command, but it keeps the prefix تَ. It is a jussive. ٱكْتُبْ, “write!”, is a command. تَكْتُبُ, “you write”, is only a statement.
2. **Leaving out the helper.** كْتُبْ cannot be said, because Arabic does not start a word with a silent letter. Write ٱكْتُبْ.
3. **Using the wrong helper sound.** Look at the present “he” form. A ḍamma on the middle letter gives u, as in ٱسْجُدْ. A fatḥa or a kasra gives i, as in ٱذْهَبْ and ٱجْلِسْ.
4. **Calling every short command “hollow”.** Only قُلْ is hollow. كُلْ and خُذْ begin with a hamza, and ٱدْعُ ends in a weak letter. L4.11 introduces all of them.
5. **Looking for a written “you”.** A command has a doer, but it is hidden in ٱقْرَأْ and it is the ending in ٱسْجُدِى, ٱسْجُدَا, ٱسْجُدُوا۟ and ٱسْجُدْنَ.
6. **Reading “do not” as a command.** لَا تُطِعْهُ is a present verb after لَا. It keeps the prefix.
7. **Looking for a command form for “he” or “she”.** There is none. Arabic uses لِ and the jussive: وَلْتَنظُرْ نَفْسٌ.
8. **Thinking every command starts with ٱ.** A Form IV command starts with أَ, a hamza that is said.
9. **Thinking every command is strict.** A command can be a request or a prayer, as in 3:147.
</Block>

<Exercise n={1} q="Make the command for one man from each present form. Say which sound the helper takes. (a) تَذْكُرُ, “you remember” (b) تَضْرِبُ, “you strike” (c) تَدْخُلُ, “you enter” (d) تَذْهَبُ, “you go”">

Use the three moves: end in a sukūn, take off تَ, add ٱ. For the sound, look at the present “he” form.

- (a) تَذْكُرُ gives ٱذْكُرْ. يَذْكُرُ has a ḍamma on the middle letter, so the helper sounds **u**: *udhkur*.
- (b) تَضْرِبُ gives ٱضْرِبْ. يَضْرِبُ has a kasra, so **i**: *iḍrib*.
- (c) تَدْخُلُ gives ٱدْخُلْ. يَدْخُلُ has a ḍamma, so **u**: *udkhul*.
- (d) تَذْهَبُ gives ٱذْهَبْ. يَذْهَبُ has a fatḥa, so **i**: *idhhab*.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Which of these are command forms? (a) قُلْ (b) يَقُولُ (c) ٱسْجُدْ (d) سَجَدَ (e) ٱقْرَأْ (f) تَقْرَأُ (g) وَلْتَنظُرْ">

- (a) **Command.** “Say!”, from تَقُولُ.
- (b) Present, “he says”. It has the prefix يَ.
- (c) **Command.** “Bow down!”
- (d) Past, “he bowed down”.
- (e) **Command.** “Read!”
- (f) Present, “you read”. It has the prefix تَ and the ending -u.
- (g) Not a command form. It is a present verb with the prefix تَ, and the لِ in front makes the meaning a command, for “she” (59:18).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Find the commands in this verse, and say what the other verb is: كَلَّا لَا تُطِعْهُ وَٱسْجُدْ وَٱقْتَرِب (Al-ʿAlaq 96:19). Meaning: “No! Do not obey him. Bow down and come near.”">

- **وَٱسْجُدْ** is a command, Form I, for one man. It is built on a sukūn.
- **وَٱقْتَرِب** is a command, Form VIII, for one man. The helper sounds i. The doer of both is a hidden “you”.
- **لَا تُطِعْهُ** is not a command form. It is a present verb, Form IV, with the prefix تُ. After لَا it says “do not obey him”. Al-Jadwal calls this لَا *nāhiya*, “the لَا that forbids”, and says it makes the verb jussive.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Give the command for all five “you” forms of نَصَرَ, “he helped”. The present is تَنْصُرُ.">

يَنْصُرُ has a ḍamma on the middle letter, so the helper sounds u.

1. One man: ٱنصُرْ (*unṣur*)
2. One woman: ٱنصُرِى (*unṣurī*)
3. Two people: ٱنصُرَا (*unṣurā*)
4. A group of men: ٱنصُرُوا۟ (*unṣurū*)
5. A group of women: ٱنصُرْنَ (*unṣurna*)

The Quran has the form for one man in وَٱنصُرْنَا (3:147), with نَا, “us”, on the end.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Make a command for “Write!”, said to one woman, from تَكْتُبِينَ. This is a made-up example.">

تَكْتُبِينَ is the present for one woman. The jussive drops the nūn: تَكْتُبِى. Take off the تَ: كْتُبِى. Add the helper: **ٱكْتُبِى** (*uktubī*). It is built on a dropped nūn, and the ى is the doer. The helper sounds u, because يَكْتُبُ has a ḍamma on the middle letter.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="How do you say these aloud, and why do they differ? وَٱرْكَعِى (Al-Imran 3:43) and وَأَقِيمُوا۟ (Al-Baqarah 2:110)">

- وَٱرْكَعِى is *wa-rkaʿī*. The helper ٱ is silent after وَ. Only the و is heard, and then the word starts with رْ.

- وَ is a little word that joins this sentence to the one before.

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="the present tense for you, plus three small changes, equals a command"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'present “you”', small: 'تَقْرَأُ' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'three small changes', small: 'sukūn, no تَ, add ٱ' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'command', small: 'ٱقْرَأْ' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A command is a verb that asks “you” to do something. Make it from the present for “you”: end like the jussive, take off the prefix, and add a helper if the word starts with a silent letter.
- The helper ٱ sounds u after a ḍamma and i after a fatḥa or a kasra. It is silent after وَ and فَ.
- There are five commands: one man (sukūn), one woman (ـِى), two people (ـَا), men (ـُوا۟) and women (ـْنَ). The doer is hidden for one man and is the ending for the other four.
- A command is built. It is built on a sukūn, a dropped nūn or a dropped weak letter, and it has no case and no mood. Al-Ajurrumiyyah calls it jussive.
- “He” and “she” have no command form. Arabic uses لِ and the jussive. “Do not” uses لَا and the jussive (L3.07).
- Weak verbs make short commands (قُلْ, ٱدْعُ, خُذْ). Other forms have their own commands: Form IV starts with أَ.
