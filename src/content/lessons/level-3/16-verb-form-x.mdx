The middle verb is وَٱسْتَكْبَرَ, “and he was proud”. Take off the وَ and you have ٱسْتَكْبَرَ. Take off ٱسْتَ and three letters are left: ك ب ر. This is the root of كَبِير, “big”. The verb says that Iblis acted big. In this verse it is about Iblis, who refused to bow to Adam.

This verb has **Form X**. The Quranic Arabic Corpus marks it (X). You met Form X in the table of L3.03 and in the trap of L3.04. A form is a root with extra letters added (L3.04), and Form X adds three: ٱ, س and ت.

The scale word of Form X is **ٱسْتَفْعَلَ**. The letters ف ع ل stand for the three root letters, as in L3.03. Put غ ف ر in their place and you get ٱسْتَغْفَرَ, “he asked forgiveness”.

Three marks tell you the shape:

1. **ٱسْتَ** comes first. The س has a sukūn and the ت has a fatḥa.
2. **The first root letter has a sukūn.** In ٱسْتَغْفَرَ it is the غ.
3. **The second and third root letters have a fatḥa** in the past.

Al-Jadwal calls Form X a **six-letter verb** (*sudāsī*). The past verb ٱسْتَغْفَرَ has six letters: ا س ت غ ف ر. Three of them are added.

<Note kind="remember">Form X has a sukūn on the س and a sukūn on the first root letter. Two silent letters in a row inside the word show the form.</Note>

The Quranic Arabic Corpus tags 369 verb words as Form X, out of 19,356 verb words. That is about 1.9%. It is the sixth form by count, after Form I (12,356 words), Form IV (3,487), Form II (1,300), Form VIII (963) and Form V (414). Form III has 334. These are Quran Learn's counts from the Corpus tags.

The Corpus writes the form number at the end of a verb's tags. A verb with no number is Form I. L3.04 shows how to read these tags, and L3.03 showed that وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ (110:3) is a Form X command with a joined pronoun.

Al-Jadwal does not use numbers. It gives the **scale word**. For يَسْتَخْفُونَ (4:108) it gives يَسْتَفْعُونَ, and for ٱسْتَغْشَوْا۟ (71:7) it gives ٱسْتَفْعَوْا, with a fatḥa on the ع. For مُسْتَكْبِرُونَ (16:22) it gives مُسْتَفْعِل. You can use whichever you find easier. This course writes “Form X” and the scale word.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal calls ٱسْتَكْبَرَ a six-letter verb (*sudāsī*, 16:22). It calls ٱسْتَوَىٰ a five-letter verb (*khumāsī*, 2:29). The difference matters: ٱسْتَوَىٰ looks like Form X and is not. Step 4 explains this.</Note>

  <Stage label="Three ways to start">

The ٱ at the start of a past Form X verb is the **joining alif**, هَمْزَة الْوَصْل (*hamzat al-waṣl*). L3.08 met it in commands. It is a helper: Arabic does not start a word with a silent letter, and the س of ٱسْتَ has a sukūn.

- At the start of speech you say **ista-**: *istakbara*.
- After a little word such as وَ or فَ the ٱ is silent. You say *wa-stakbara* and *fa-staghfarū*. This is how you read وَٱسْتَكْبَرَ in 2:34.
- In the present the alif is gone. The prefix يَ, تَ, أَ or نَ takes its place: ٱسْتَكْبَرَ becomes يَسْتَكْبِرُ.

The Quran writes the joining alif as ٱ with no vowel mark. You choose the sound. For a Form X verb the sound is **i** at the start of speech.

</Block>

The present is the past with two changes. First, the **prefix** يَ, تَ, أَ or نَ goes in front, and the ٱ goes. Second, the second root letter changes its vowel from a fatḥa to a **kasra**: ٱسْتَغْفَرَ becomes يَسْتَغْفِرُ. The scale word is **يَسْتَفْعِلُ**.

The prefix and ending work as in L3.06. The table shows nine of the thirteen forms. The dual forms (“they two” and “you two”) and “you all (women)” follow the same pattern.

So the present has the marks: prefix, then سْتَ, then the first root letter with a sukūn, then the second root letter with a kasra. When you see يَسْتَ, تَسْتَ, أَسْتَ or نَسْتَ, think of Form X, then check the root (step 4).

To find the root of a Form X verb, peel in this order, as in L3.03.

1. Take off a وَ or فَ, any prefix (يَ, تَ, أَ, نَ) and any ending or joined pronoun.
2. Take off ٱسْتَ, or سْتَ if a prefix came before it.
3. Count what is left.

| Word (verse) | What is left | Root | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| ٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟ (7:75) | كْبَر | ك ب ر | three letters: a sound root |
| يَسْتَعْجِلُ (10:50) | عْجِل | ع ج ل | three letters: a sound root |
| ٱسْتَجَابَ (3:195) | جَاب | ج و ب | the ā stands for a hidden و |
| يَسْتَخْفُونَ (4:108) | خْف | خ ف ي | only two letters: the last letter dropped |
| ٱسْتَقَرَّ (7:143) | قَرَّ | ق ر ر | a shadda: two same letters in one |

A **sound root** keeps all three letters. A **weak root** has a و, ا or ي that changes or drops. A shadda at the end often hides two identical letters.

Each weak shape has its own lesson in Level 4: L4.12 (hollow verbs, with a weak middle letter, as in ٱسْتَجَابَ), L4.13 (defective verbs, with a weak last letter, as in يَسْتَخْفُونَ) and L4.15 (verbs with a hamza). L4.11 introduces them all. Here you only need to see that Form X verbs of these kinds are common, and to know the root is not what you see at first.

<Block id="lookalikes" title="Look-alikes: not every ٱسْتَ is Form X">
  <Stage label="Four words that start with ٱسْتَ or نَسْتَ">
    <Tiles
      label="four look-alikes: three are Form VIII and one is Form X"
      items={[
        { ar: 'ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ', en: 'Form VIII (2:29)', type: 'v' },

        { ar: 'نَسْتَبِقُ', en: 'Form VIII (12:17)', type: 'v' },

The spelling ٱسْتَ is not proof. In Form VIII the س is the first **root letter**, and the ت is added after it (with the ٱ in front). Compare:

- ٱسْتَبَقَا (12:25): peel ٱسْتَ and only بَقَ is left. That is two letters. So the س belongs to the root: **س ب ق**. It is Form VIII, with the added ت after the first root letter (L3.14).
- ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ (2:29): peel ٱسْتَ and only وَىٰ is left. The root is **س و ي**. Form VIII again. Al-Jadwal calls it a five-letter verb.
- نَسْتَبِقُ (12:17) is the present of the same verb as ٱسْتَبَقَا. L3.04 showed it next to نَسْتَعِينُ (1:5), which is Form X with the root ع و ن.

The test is the same each time: **peel ٱسْتَ and look at what is left**. Three letters left is Form X. Two letters left may be Form X with a weak or doubled root (جَاب, قَرَّ, خْف). They may also be Form VIII with س as the first root letter (بَقَ, وَىٰ). So a short remainder is a warning: stop and check the Corpus tag, (X) or (VIII).

A Form X verb has related words. These are taught in full later, so only recognise them now.

- **The doer word** has the scale word **مُسْتَفْعِل**: مُّسْتَكْبِرُونَ (16:22), “proud ones”, and ٱلْمُسْتَقِيم, “the straight”, which you know from 1:6 and which is also in 6:126 as مُسْتَقِيمًا. Al-Jadwal gives مُسْتَفْعِل as the scale word of مُسْتَكْبِرُونَ. L3.19 teaches it.
- **The passive word** is **مُسْتَفْعَل**, with a fatḥa on the second root letter: ٱلْمُسْتَضْعَفِينَ (4:75), مُسْتَقَرٌّ (2:36).
- **The verbal noun** is **ٱسْتِفْعَال**, with a kasra on the ت and a long ā: ٱسْتِكْبَارًا (35:43, 71:7) and ٱسْتِغْفَارُ (9:114). L3.20 teaches it.

In 71:7 the verbal noun repeats the verb. Al-Jadwal calls ٱسْتِكْبَارًا a *mafʿūl muṭlaq*, “an absolute object”: it strengthens the verb. Here the verb ٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟ and its verbal noun stand side by side.

1. **Reading every ٱسْتَ as Form X.** ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ (2:29) and ٱسْتَبَقَا (12:25) are Form VIII. Peel ٱسْتَ and count what is left.
2. **Keeping the ٱ in the present.** The prefix takes its place. You write يَسْتَغْفِرُ and not يَٱسْتَغْفِرُ.
3. **Mixing up the vowels in the present.** The active present has a kasra on the second root letter: يَسْتَغْفِرُ. A fatḥa there with a ḍamma on the prefix, يُسْتَغْفَرُ, is passive.
4. **Reading the weak root at face value.** In ٱسْتَجَابَ you see ج ا ب, but the root is ج و ب. The ā stands for a و.
5. **Saying wa-istaghfir.** After وَ and فَ the joining alif is silent: *wa-staghfir*.
6. **Treating the meaning groups as a law.** “Asking for” fits ٱسْتَغْفَرَ and ٱسْتَسْقَىٰ, but ٱسْتَطَاعَ means “was able”. Learn the verb.
7. **Forgetting that the long vowel drops in a command.** ٱسْتَقِمْ has a short vowel, and فَٱسْتَقِيمُوٓا۟ has a long one.
8. **Mixing up the verb and the verbal noun.** ٱسْتِغْفَارُ (9:114) is a noun with a kasra on the ت. The verb has a fatḥa: ٱسْتَغْفَرَ.

<Exercise n={1} q="Which of these verbs is Form X? Peel each one and check the Corpus tag. (a) ٱسْتَغْفَرُوا۟ (Al-Imran 3:135) (b) ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ (Al-Baqarah 2:29) (c) وَٱسْتَبَقَا (Yusuf 12:25) (d) وَيَسْتَكْبِرْ (An-Nisa 4:172) (e) نَسْتَعِينُ (Al-Fatiha 1:5)">

- (a) **Form X.** Peel ٱسْتَ and غَفَرُوا is left, with the ending ـُوا. The root is غ ف ر.
- (b) **Form VIII.** Peel ٱسْتَ and only وَىٰ is left. The س is a root letter. The root is س و ي, and the Corpus tags it (VIII). Al-Jadwal calls it a five-letter verb.
- (c) **Form VIII.** Peel ٱسْتَ and only بَقَ is left. The root is س ب ق, with a ت added after the first root letter.
- (d) **Form X.** Peel وَيَ and سْتَ, and كْبِرْ is left: ك ب ر. It ends in a sukūn because it follows the condition word مَن, and the Corpus tags it jussive (L3.07).
- (e) **Form X.** Peel نَ and سْتَ and عِينُ is left. The root is ع و ن: the ī stands for the و.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Peel each word and give its root. Say whether it is a sound or a weak root. (a) ٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟ (Al-Aʿraf 7:75) (b) يَسْتَعْجِلُ (Yunus 10:50) (c) ٱسْتَجَابَ (Al-Imran 3:195) (d) يَسْتَخْفُونَ (An-Nisa 4:108) (e) ٱسْتَقَرَّ (Al-Aʿraf 7:143)">

- (a) **ك ب ر.** Sound. Peel ٱسْتَ and the ending ـُوا.
- (b) **ع ج ل.** Sound. Peel يَ and سْتَ.
- (c) **ج و ب.** Weak, with a hollow middle: the ā in جَابَ stands for the و.
- (d) **خ ف ي.** Weak, with a last letter that dropped: only خ ف are left after you peel the ending ـُونَ.
- (e) **ق ر ر.** A doubled root: the shadda on the ر is two identical letters, ر and ر.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Use ٱسْتَكْبَرَ, “he was proud”, to write these forms. They are patterns, not verse quotes. (a) past, they (men) (b) present, he (c) present, you all (men) (d) command, you all (men) (e) past, I">

- (a) **ٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟** (*istakbarū*). The ending ـُوا۟ is “they (men)”. The Quran has this form in 7:75.
- (b) **يَسْتَكْبِرُ** (*yastakbiru*). Prefix يَ, ٱ gone, kasra on the ب.
- (c) **تَسْتَكْبِرُونَ** (*tastakbirūna*). Prefix تَ and the ending ـُونَ.
- (d) **ٱسْتَكْبِرُوا۟** (*istakbirū*). Drop the nūn of (c), take off the تَ, add the joining alif.
- (e) **ٱسْتَكْبَرْتُ** (*istakbartu*). The sukūn is on the ر before the ending ـْتُ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="From the root meaning to the Form X meaning. (a) غَفَرَ is “he forgave”. What is ٱسْتَغْفَرَ? (b) سَقَىٰ is “he gave water”. What is ٱسْتَسْقَىٰ (Al-Baqarah 2:60)? (c) The root ض ع ف is about weakness. What is ٱسْتَضْعَفُونِى (Al-Aʿraf 7:150)? (d) The root ح ب ب is about love. What is ٱسْتَحَبُّوا۟ (At-Tawbah 9:23)?">

- (a) “He asked forgiveness”. A Form X verb often means asking for what the root is about.
- (b) “He asked for water”. Musa asked for water for his people.
- (c) “They thought me weak” and treated me so. The meaning is **counting as**.
- (d) “They preferred”. Al-Jadwal says the verb carries the meaning of choosing, and the phrase with عَلَى goes with it.

Groups (a) and (b) are **asking for**. Groups (c) and (d) are **counting as** or **choosing**.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Go through ٱلْقَوْمَ ٱسْتَضْعَفُونِى وَكَادُوا۟ (Al-Aʿraf 7:150) word by word. Break ٱسْتَضْعَفُونِى into its parts. Meaning: “the people thought me weak, and they almost killed me”">

- **ٱلْقَوْمَ** is the noun of إِنَّ in the words before. It is accusative, with a fatḥa.
- **ٱسْتَضْعَفُونِى** is one word with three parts. **ٱسْتَضْعَفَ** is a past verb, Form X, root ض ع ف. **ـُوا** is the doer, “they”. **ـنِى** is the object, “me”: the ن protects the verb, and the ى is “me” (L3.02). Al-Jadwal says the whole verb sentence is the khabar of إِنَّ.
- **وَكَادُوا۟** is a new verb, “and they almost”, with the doer ـُوا.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Look at إِنَّكَ لَن تَسْتَطِيعَ مَعِىَ صَبْرًا (Al-Kahf 18:67). Give the person, the tense, the mood and the root of تَسْتَطِيعَ. Meaning: “Indeed, you will not be able to be patient with me.”">

- **Person:** “you”, one man.
- **Tense:** present.
- **Mood:** subjunctive. The ending is a fatḥa, because لَن puts the present verb in the subjunctive (L3.07). The Corpus tags it so.
- **Root and form:** ط و ع, Form X. The long ī in the middle stands for the و. It is a hollow verb (L4.12).

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Why does the command فَٱسْتَقِمْ (Hud 11:112) have a short vowel, while فَٱسْتَقِيمُوٓا۟ (Fussilat 41:6) has a long ī?">

The verb is ٱسْتَقَامَ, root ق و م, and the present is تَسْتَقِيمُ. In the command for one man the word ends in a sukūn. The long ī and the sukūn meet, and Arabic drops the long vowel: ٱسْتَقِمْ. In the command for a group the ending ـُوا۟ follows, so there is no final sukūn and the ī stays: ٱسْتَقِيمُوا۟.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Compare the two verbs. Which is active and which is passive, and who is the doer in each? (a) ٱسْتَضْعَفُونِى (Al-Aʿraf 7:150) (b) ٱسْتُضْعِفُوا۟ (Al-Aʿraf 7:75)">

- (a) **Active.** The doer is the ـُوا, “they”. The object is ـنِى, “me”.
- (b) **Passive.** The ḍamma on the ت and the kasra before the last letter show it. The ـُوا۟ is the stand-in for the doer: “they”, the ones who were thought weak. The one who did it is not named.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="A learner says ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ (Al-Baqarah 2:29) starts with ٱسْتَ, so it is Form X. What is wrong with this?">

The spelling ٱسْتَ is not proof. Peel ٱسْتَ from ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ and only وَىٰ is left, which is too short to be a root on its own. The Corpus tags the verb (VIII), so the س belongs to the root: **س و ي**. The ت is the one added letter, and the verb is Form VIII. The Corpus tags it (VIII), and al-Jadwal calls it a five-letter verb. A Form X verb is a six-letter verb.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Say what each word is: a verb, a doer word or a verbal noun. (a) وَٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟ (Nuh 71:7) (b) مُّسْتَكْبِرُونَ (An-Nahl 16:22) (c) ٱسْتِكْبَارًا (Fatir 35:43)">

- (a) A **verb**: past, Form X, “and they were proud”.
- (b) A **doer word**, scale word مُسْتَفْعِل. It is the doer word of ٱسْتَكْبَرَ: “proud ones”. L3.19 teaches this.
- (c) A **verbal noun**, scale word ٱسْتِفْعَال. L3.20 teaches this.

All three come from the root ك ب ر.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={11} q="How do you say وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ (An-Nasr 110:3) aloud, and what are its parts?">

You say *wa-staghfirhu*. The joining alif is silent after وَ. The parts are: **وَ**, “and”; **ٱسْتَغْفِرْ**, a command for one man, Form X, with the hidden doer “you”; and **هُ**, “him”, the object (L3.02). The verb is built on a sukūn. Meaning of the whole verse: “So glorify with the praise of your Lord, and ask his forgiveness.”

</Exercise>

- Form X is a root with **ٱسْتَ** in front and a sukūn on the first root letter. The scale word is ٱسْتَفْعَلَ. The Corpus tags 369 verb words as Form X.
- The past is ٱسْتَغْفَرَ. The present is يَسْتَغْفِرُ, with a kasra on the second root letter. The command is ٱسْتَغْفِرْ. The passive past is ٱسْتُفْعِلَ.
- The joining alif is said at the start of speech and silent after وَ or فَ. In the present the prefix takes its place.
- Form X often means **asking for** what the root is about, **counting as**, or **being or acting in a way**. Some verbs, like ٱسْتَطَاعَ, are learned whole.
- To find the root, peel ٱسْتَ and count. A long ā or ī may stand for a و or ي, and a verb may drop a last letter.
- Not every ٱسْتَ is Form X. ٱسْتَوَىٰ and ٱسْتَبَقَ are Form VIII.
- The doer word is مُسْتَفْعِل, the passive word is مُسْتَفْعَل, and the verbal noun is ٱسْتِفْعَال.
