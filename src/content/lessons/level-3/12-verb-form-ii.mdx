Read it from right to left. The first word is نَزَّلَ, “he sent down”. Look at the ز. It has a **shadda**, ـّ, the mark that doubles a letter.

The root is ن ز ل, the idea of coming down (L3.03). The verb is not Form I. The middle letter is written twice, and that is **Form II**. The doer is a hidden “he”: Allah, who is named in 3:2.

The last word, مُصَدِّقًا, “confirming”, also has a shadda. It comes from the same family, as step 3 shows.

Both verbs come from ن ز ل. The first has no shadda. It is Form I, نَزَلَ, *nazala*: “came down”. The one who comes down is the doer, ٱلرُّوحُ.

The second has the shadda. It is Form II, نَزَّلَ, *nazzala*: “sent down”. Now a new doer, the hidden “he”, does the sending. The thing that is sent is the object, ٱلْكِتَٰبَ, with **-a** (L2.05).

So the doubling added a doer who makes the thing happen. This is one use of Form II. Step 3 shows others.

Form IV (أَنزَلَ) is the topic of L3.13. The verse before 26:193, which is 26:192, has تَنزِيلُ, a noun from the Form II verb. You meet it in step 3.</Note>

L3.03 taught the scale letters ف ع ل. ف stands for the first root letter, ع for the second and ل for the third. In Form II the second letter is doubled, so the scale word is **فَعَّلَ**, *faʿʿala*.

One letter is written, with a shadda. It is said twice: the first copy is silent (sukūn), the second has the vowel. Read نَزَّلَ as *naz-zala*.

The vowels of the past “he” form are three fatḥas: on the first letter, on the doubled letter and on the last letter.

| Root | Form I | Form II | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| ن ز ل | نَزَلَ, came down | نَزَّلَ, sent down | 26:193, 3:3 |
| ع ل م | عَلِمَ, knew | عَلَّمَ, taught | 2:60, 96:5 |
| ك ذ ب | كَذَبَ, lied | كَذَّبَ, called false | 39:32 (both) |

The shape changes. The root stays the same, and it still has three letters. The second letter is **ل** in ع ل م, and it is the pattern that writes it twice (L3.03 said the same of عَلَّمَ).

L3.04 showed that the Quranic Arabic Corpus writes the form of a verb in brackets. A verb with no bracket is Form I.

| Word | What the Corpus says | Form |
|---|---|---|
| نَزَّلَ (3:3) | verb, past (PERF), root ن ز ل, “he” (3MS), (II) | II |
| نَزَلَ (26:193) | verb, past (PERF), root ن ز ل, “he” (3MS), no bracket | I |
| مُصَدِّقًا (3:3) | noun, active participle (ACT PCPL), root ص د ق, (II) | II |
| نُزِّلَ (16:44) | verb, past (PERF), passive (PASS), (II) | II |

The Corpus also puts (II) on some nouns, the ones built from a Form II verb. So the bracket says “this word is built in the Form II way”, and the word type tells you if it is a verb.

Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus: **1,300 verb words** are Form II (the third most common form after Form I and Form IV). A script counted them by tense in the Corpus file: 656 past, 539 present and 105 commands. 140 of the 1,300 are passive.

The present of Form II is **يُفَعِّلُ**, *yufaʿʿilu*. Compare it with the Form I present, يَفْعَلُ (L3.06):

- the prefix (يـ، تـ، أ، نـ) takes a **ḍamma**, not a fatḥa,
- the second root letter is doubled,
- the letter before the last takes a **kasra**,
- the ending is the same as in Form I.

| Form I present | Form II present |
|---|---|
| يَعْلَمُ, he knows | يُعَلِّمُ, he teaches |
| يَنزِلُ, he comes down | يُنَزِّلُ, he sends down |

Al-Jadwal says يُعَلِّمُكَ is like the verb before it, يَجْتَبِيكَ: a present verb, nominative, with the ending -ka as the object and رَبُّكَ as the doer. The phrase مِن تَأْوِيلِ ٱلْأَحَادِيثِ is tied to the verb.

<Note kind="remember">A ḍamma on the prefix fits Form II, Form III (L3.17), Form IV (L3.13) and the passive (L3.09). The doubled middle letter then tells you it is Form II.</Note>

The table has thirteen rows. Some forms look the same: تُعَلِّمُ is “she” and “you (a man)”, and تُعَلِّمَانِ is “they two (women)” and “you two”. The sentence tells you which one is meant.

The moods work as in Form I ([L3.07](/learn/level-3/07-subjunctive-jussive/)). In 8:33 you find لِيُعَذِّبَهُمْ: the present of عَذَّبَ after لِ, in the subjunctive, with -a on the last letter. In 3:164 (step 4) the present يُزَكِّيهِمْ has a weak last letter, which step 3 explains.

L3.08 gave the recipe for a command: take the present for “you”, end it like the jussive and take off the prefix. For Form II the present for “you” is تُكَبِّرُ. Take off the prefix تُ and you have كَبِّرُ. End it like the jussive and you have **كَبِّرْ**, *kabbir*. The word starts with the stem letter, so no helper is needed.

[L3.09](/learn/level-3/09-passive-voice/) gave the rule for the passive: a ḍamma on the first letter and a kasra before the last (past), a ḍamma on the prefix and a fatḥa before the last (present). It works in Form II too. The doubled letter stays.

| Tense | Active | Passive | A real passive verb |
|---|---|---|---|
| Past | فَعَّلَ, نَزَّلَ | فُعِّلَ, نُزِّلَ | نُزِّلَ (16:44) |

In 16:44 the passive نُزِّلَ has a hidden stand-in. Al-Jadwal says it is a hidden “he”, meaning “it”, and that it is also the returning pronoun of مَا (L3.11). The same verse has the active present لِتُبَيِّنَ, from بَيَّنَ, Form II. The prefix has a ḍamma and the letter before the last has a kasra.

تُوَفَّىٰ is the present passive of وَفَّىٰ (step 3). Its middle letter has a fatḥa, as the rule says, and the weak last letter has become an alif.

Many Form II verbs have a weak letter (و or ي) in the root. Three of them are common: صَلَّىٰ (root ص ل و), وَفَّىٰ (root و ف ي) and سَمَّىٰ (root س م و). Quran Learn counted in the Corpus file: 323 of the 1,300 Form II verb words have a و or ي among the root letters.

In وَفَّىٰٓ you can still see the shadda on the ف. The last letter has turned into an alif, written ـّىٰ. Al-Jadwal says the last letter was a ي, and it turned into an alif because it had a fatḥa before it. It gives the pattern as **فَعَّلَ**: the same as for a strong root.

In صَلَّىٰ (75:31, which you go through word by word in step 4) al-Jadwal says the alif comes from a ي, and the ي comes from a و. The sign is the plural of the noun: صَلَوَات, with a و. It gives the pattern as فَعَّلَ.

The weak last letter shows differently from tense to tense. In the past it is an alif: صَلَّىٰ. In the present it stays as a ي: يُزَكِّيهِمْ, يُصَلِّى. In the command it drops: صَلِّ (L2.12, L3.08).

<Note kind="know">L4.11 gives the first look at weak verbs. L4.13 teaches the verbs with a weak last letter (defective verbs) in full. This lesson asks you to recognise them.</Note>

        { ar: 'يَتَعَلَّمُونَ', en: 'Form V: they learn', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

Form V also has a doubled middle letter. It has an extra **تَ** in front of the stem: تَعَلَّمَ, *taʿallama*, “he learned”. The scale word is تَفَعَّلَ. L3.15 teaches it.

L3.04 showed the verse 2:102 with the three verbs. Look at the front of the verb. A ḍamma on the prefix and a doubled letter after it is Form II (يُعَلِّمُونَ). An extra تَ after the prefix and a doubled letter after it is Form V (يَتَعَلَّمُونَ).
</Block>

<Exercise n={1} q="Which of these verbs are Form II? (a) نَزَلَ بِهِ ٱلرُّوحُ ٱلْأَمِينُ (Ash-Shuara 26:193) (b) نَزَّلَ عَلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ (Al-Imran 3:3) (c) وَضَلَّ عَنْهُم (Al-Anam 6:24) (d) عَلَّمَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ (Al-Alaq 96:5)">

- (a) **Form I.** نَزَلَ has no shadda.
- (b) **Form II.** The shadda is on the middle letter ز.
- (c) **Form I.** The shadda is there, but the root is ض ل ل. The Corpus gives no form number.
- (d) **Form II.** The shadda is on the middle letter ل. The last verb of 96:5, يَعْلَمْ, is Form I.

So (b) and (d) are Form II.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Form I نَزَلَ means “came down” (Ash-Shuara 26:193). What does Form II نَزَّلَ mean in Al-Imran 3:3? Say who the doer is and what the object is.">

- نَزَّلَ means “sent down”.
- In 26:193 the one who comes down is the doer: ٱلرُّوحُ. There is no object.
- In 3:3 a new doer, a hidden “he” (Allah), does the sending. The object is ٱلْكِتَٰبَ, “the Book”, with -a.

The doubling added a doer who makes the thing happen. Quran Learn does not claim this holds for all Form II verbs.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Say what each form means. These are teaching forms of عَلَّمَ. (a) عَلَّمْتُ (b) عَلَّمَتْ (c) عَلَّمُوا (d) عَلَّمْنَ">

- (a) **I taught.** The ending -tu is “I”.
- (b) **She taught.** The ending -at is “she”.
- (c) **They (men) taught.** The ending -ū is “they”.
- (d) **They (women) taught.** The ending -na is “they (women)”.

In all four the stem عَلَّمـ is the same, with the shadda.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Give the present of كَذَّبَ, “he called false”, for (a) he (b) they (men) (c) I. These are teaching forms.">

- (a) **يُكَذِّبُ.** Prefix يُ with a ḍamma, the ذ doubled, a kasra before the last letter.
- (b) **يُكَذِّبُونَ.** The same stem with the ending -ūna.
- (c) **أُكَذِّبُ.** The prefix is أُ, with a ḍamma.

The Form I present would be يَكْذِبُ, with a fatḥa on the prefix. It is a different verb: يَكْذِبُ means “he lies”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Read the command in سَبِّحِ ٱسْمَ رَبِّكَ ٱلْأَعْلَى (Al-Ala 87:1). (1) Which form is it? (2) What is the root? (3) Which present verb does it come from? (4) Why does it end in a kasra?">

1. **Form II.** The shadda is on the ب, and the Corpus gives (II).
2. The root is **س ب ح**.
3. It comes from **تُسَبِّحُ**, the present for “you”. Take off the prefix, end it like the jussive, and you have سَبِّحْ.
4. The next word, ٱسْمَ, starts with a silent letter. The kasra keeps the two apart: سَبِّحِ ٱسْمَ. The same happened to رَتِّلِ in 73:4.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Find the two Form II verbs in وَنَحْنُ نُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِكَ وَنُقَدِّسُ لَكَ (Al-Baqarah 2:30). For each say the tense, the person and the root. Does the Quran have a Form I verb from the same root?">

- **نُسَبِّحُ**: present, “we”, root س ب ح. The Corpus has 2 Form I words of this root (يَسْبَحُونَ, “they float”, 21:33 and 36:40). They do not mean “glorify”.
- **نُقَدِّسُ**: present, “we”, root ق د س. The Corpus has no Form I verb of this root. It has this one Form II word.

Both have the prefix نُ with a ḍamma, which is “we” in the present.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="Look at مَا نُزِّلَ إِلَيْهِمْ (An-Nahl 16:44). (1) Is نُزِّلَ active or passive? (2) Which tense? (3) Which form? (4) What is the root? (5) What is its active verb?">

1. **Passive.** It has a ḍamma on the first letter and a kasra under the doubled letter.
2. **Past.**
3. **Form II.** The ز is doubled, and the Corpus gives (II).
4. The root is **ن ز ل**.
5. The active verb is **نَزَّلَ**, “he sent down”. The stand-in for the doer is a hidden “it”.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Find the weak letter in each verb. Say its root and how the last letter shows. (a) وَإِبْرَٰهِيمَ ٱلَّذِى وَفَّىٰٓ (An-Najm 53:37) (b) هُوَ سَمَّىٰكُمُ ٱلْمُسْلِمِينَ (Al-Hajj 22:78)">

- (a) **وَفَّىٰٓ**: root **و ف ي**. The last letter is an alif (written ىٰ). Al-Jadwal says it was a ي. The ف has the shadda.
- (b) **سَمَّىٰكُمُ**: root **س م و**. The last letter is an alif, followed by the attached pronoun كُمُ, “you all”, as the object. The Corpus tags ٱلْمُسْلِمِينَ as accusative: the second object, “named you Muslims”.

L4.11 and L4.13 teach these roots in full.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Say the form, tense, person and number of each verb. (a) فَٱقْطَعُوٓا۟ أَيْدِيَهُمَا (Al-Maidah 5:38) (b) وَقَطَّعْنَ أَيْدِيَهُنَّ (Yusuf 12:31)">

- (a) **Form I**, a command, “you all (men)”. The ending -ū is the doer.
- (b) **Form II**, past, “they (women)”. The ending -na is the doer.

The two verses cut hands. In (b) the shadda goes with many cuts, since several women cut their hands.

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="a root plus a doubled middle letter makes a Form II verb"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'root', small: 'ع ل م' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'middle letter doubled', small: 'فَعَّلَ' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'Form II verb', small: 'عَلَّمَ, he taught' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- Form II doubles the middle root letter: **فَعَّلَ**. The root keeps its three letters. The Corpus tags it (II). There are 1,300 verb words in the Quran.
- **Past:** فَعَّلَ, with the endings of Form I. **Present:** يُفَعِّلُ, a ḍamma on the prefix and a kasra before the last letter. **Command:** فَعِّلْ. **Passive:** فُعِّلَ and يُفَعَّلُ.
- Test a shadda: Is it a verb? Is it on the middle root letter? Is there nothing in front of the stem? A shadda on a doubled root (وَضَلَّ) is Form I.
- The doubling can mean “a new doer makes it happen” (نَزَّلَ, عَلَّمَ, طَهِّرْ), “call it false or true” (كَذَّبَ, صَدَّقَ) or “many times” (قَطَّعَ). It does not follow one rule. 84 of the 171 Form II verbs have no Form I verb in the Quran.
- Weak letters change at the end: صَلَّىٰ, وَفَّىٰ, يُزَكِّي, and the command صَلِّ.
- Form II also builds nouns: مُصَدِّقًا (مُفَعِّل), ٱلْمُقَرَّبُونَ (مُفَعَّل) and تَنزِيل (تَفْعِيل).
