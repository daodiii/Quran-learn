The verse has two verbs from the same root, ق ت ل (“killing”). The first is a command, “fight!”. The second is in the present tense, “they fight you”.

In both, the first letter ق is followed by a long **ā**. The Quran writes it as a small alif above the ق. A Form I verb from this root is قَتَلَ, “he killed” (L3.04). The long ā makes a different verb: “he fought”. A verb with a long ā after the first root letter is **Form III**.

Form III is not one of the most common forms. The Quranic Arabic Corpus has 334 Form III verb words. Forms I, IV, II, VIII, V and X all have more. These are Quran Learn's counts of the Corpus form tags. This is why Form III comes after those forms in the course.

Form III keeps the three root letters and adds one letter: a long ā after the first root letter.

| Form | Root | Pattern | Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | ق ت ل | فَعَلَ | قَتَلَ | he killed |
| III | ق ت ل | فَاعَلَ | قَاتَلَ | he fought |

ف ع ل stand for the first, second and third root letters, as in L3.03. In the pattern فَاعَلَ (*fāʿala*) the ā sits between the first and second letters. Form I has three vowel patterns (L3.04). In the past, Form III has one: fatḥa, ā, fatḥa, fatḥa.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal counts the ā and calls a verb like جَاهَدَ a four-letter verb (*rubāʿī*). The root still has three letters, and this course counts the root. On corpus.quran.com a Form III verb carries the tag (III). In 2:190 both قَٰتِلُوا۟ and يُقَٰتِلُونَكُمْ have it.</Note>

The Quran text often writes the long ā of Form III as a small alif above the first letter: قَٰتَلَ, جَٰهَدَ. Some Form III verbs have a full alif instead: هَاجَرَ, عَاقَبَ, نَادَىٰ. Verse 8:72 has both, side by side: وَهَاجَرُوا۟ وَجَٰهَدُوا۟, “and they migrated and strove”.

The sound and the form are the same. The tables in this lesson write the full alif, so the ā is easy to see. The verses keep the Quran's own spelling.

The table is a pattern for one real verb. The Form III stem is **جَاهَدْ** (*jāhad*), and it stays the same in all 13 forms. The endings change, and they are the ones you know from L3.05. Nothing new has to be learned here.

The Quran has some of these forms: جَٰهَدَ (he), جَٰهَدُوا۟ (they, 8:72), جَٰهَدَاكَ (the two of them, with كَ, “you”: 29:8), عَٰهَدتُّم (you all, 9:4, from عَاهَدَ, “he made a treaty”, root ع ه د) and وَٰعَدْنَا (we, 2:51, from وَاعَدَ, root و ع د). In عَٰهَدتُّم the ت carries a shadda in the Quran text.

<Note kind="know">Counted by the Corpus person tags (Quran Learn's count; some tags are unreliable), the Quran has 126 active past-tense Form III words. Of these, 42 are “he” and 45 are “they (men)”. The full table is still worth knowing, because the endings do not change from verb to verb.</Note>

The present of Form III is built on the stem **يُفَاعِلُ** (*yufāʿilu*). Compare it with Form I, where the prefix has a fatḥa and the first root letter has a sukūn: يَجْهَدُ (the pattern of L3.06). Form III changes three things:

- The prefix takes a **ḍamma**: يُـ, تُـ, أُـ, نُـ. All four prefixes show it in the tiles above.
- The long ā comes after the first root letter: جَا.
- The middle root letter takes a **kasra**: هِ. The prefixes and endings are the ones from L3.06.

Forms II and IV also have a ḍamma on the prefix (L3.12, L3.13). The long ā is what shows Form III. A present verb with a ḍamma on its prefix and a long ā after the first root letter is Form III.

This one verse shows Form III in the past, in the jussive and in the subjunctive. It also has a Form I verb, ٱقْتُلُوهُمْ, from the same root.

- **لَا تُقَٰتِلُوهُمْ.** After لَا, which forbids, the verb is jussive (L3.07). The nūn of -ūna has dropped. Al-Jadwal says the same.
- **حَتَّىٰ يُقَٰتِلُوكُمْ.** After حَتَّىٰ the verb is subjunctive, with a hidden أَنْ (L3.07). The nūn has dropped again.
- **قَٰتَلُوكُمْ.** The past tense. The ending ـُوا is the doer, “they”, and كُمْ is the object, “you all”.

Compare the verse before. In 2:190, يُقَٰتِلُونَكُمْ keeps its nūn, because nothing in front of it changes the verb. The moods work as in Form I.

The three moves of L3.08 give a Form III command. Take the present for “you”, end it like the jussive, and take off the prefix. Then you have جَاهِدْ. You do not add the helper alif ٱ. The word now starts with the first root letter and its fatḥa, so you can say it: *jāhid*. (A Form I command often needs the helper, as in ٱسْجُدْ.)

The Quran has three of the five: **وَجَٰهِدْهُم** for one man (25:52), **وَجَٰهِدُوا۟** for a group (22:78) and **فَقَٰتِلَآ** for two people (5:24). In 5:24 al-Jadwal says the alif is the doer: “you two”. Of the 41 Form III command words in the Corpus, 27 are for a group of men, 13 for one man and 1 for two people. No command is for women. These are Quran Learn's counts of the Corpus person tags.

The doer of وَجَٰهِدْهُم is a hidden “you”. The attached هُم is the object, “them”.

L3.09 taught the passive: a ḍamma on the first letter, a kasra before the last letter in the past, and a fatḥa before the last letter in the present. Form III follows this, with one change: the long **ā becomes ū**, written و. So قَاتَلَ gives قُوتِلَ, and نَادَىٰ gives نُودِىَ.

In the present, the prefix already has its ḍamma. The ā stays, and the middle letter gets a fatḥa: يُقَاتِلُ becomes يُقَاتَلُ. The Corpus tags 11 past and 13 present Form III words as passive, so the passive is rare. The passive نُودِىَ is parsed word by word at the end of this lesson.

A participle is a noun for the one who does the action. For Form III it has the pattern **مُفَاعِل** (*mufāʿil*). Take the present يُجَاهِدُ, put مُـ in the place of يُـ, and keep the ā and the kasra: مُجَاهِد, “one who strives”.

<Step id="practice" label="Check yourself" title="Mistakes, four verses word by word, practice">
<Block id="mistakes" title="Common mistakes">
  <Stage label="Three wrong forms">
    <Tiles
      label="جَاهِدْ is right. ٱجَاهِدْ has a helper alif. يَجَاهِدُ has the wrong prefix."
      items={[
        { ar: 'جَاهِدْ', en: 'right: strive!', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ٱجَاهِدْ', en: 'wrong: no helper alif', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'يَجَاهِدُ', en: 'wrong: the prefix needs a ḍamma', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

1. **Reading every long ā as Form III.** Find the root first. قَالَ and كَانَ are hollow Form I verbs, ءَامَنَ is Form IV and ٱلْقَٰعِدُونَ is a Form I participle.
2. **Missing the small alif.** In the Quran text قَٰتَلَ looks like قَتَلَ at a glance. They are two verbs: “he fought” and “he killed”. Look for the small alif above the first letter.
3. **Giving the prefix a fatḥa.** The Form III present is يُفَاعِلُ. A prefix with a fatḥa and a long ā, like يَجَاهِدُ, is not a Form III form.
4. **Adding a helper alif to the command.** The command is جَاهِدْ, not ٱجَاهِدْ. The helper is for words that would start with a silent letter (L3.08).
5. **Translating every Form III verb as “with each other”.** In 3:146 it is “with”, in 2:190 it is “against”, and in 8:72 هَاجَرُوا۟ has no partner. Read the verse.
6. **Thinking Form III needs a preposition.** وَقَٰتِلُوهُمْ (2:193) and وَجَٰهِدْهُم (25:52) take their object with no preposition. A preposition such as مَعَ in 3:146 adds to the picture.
7. **Mixing the two participles.** ٱلْمُجَٰهِدُونَ has مُـ and is Form III. ٱلْقَٰعِدُونَ has no مُـ and is Form I.
8. **Looking for the ā in the passive.** It becomes و: قُوتِلُوا۟, نُودِىَ.
9. **Mixing up Form III and Form VI.** Form VI has تَ in front: تَبَٰرَكَ (L3.18).

<Exercise n={2} q="Which form is each verb: I, II or III? Say what shows it. (a) يُقَٰتِلُونَ (At-Tawbah 9:111) (b) يُقَتِّلُونَ (Al-Aʿraf 7:141) (c) يَقْتُلُونَ (At-Tawbah 9:111)">

- (b) **Form II.** A ḍamma on the prefix and a shadda on the middle letter ت (L3.12).

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="the first root letter, a long ā, and the other two root letters make a Form III verb"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'first root letter', small: 'قَ' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'a long ā', small: 'ا' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'the other two letters', small: 'تَلَ' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'Form III verb', small: 'قَاتَلَ' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- Form III is a verb with a long ā after the first root letter: فَاعَلَ. The Quran often writes the ā as a small alif: قَٰتَلَ.
- The past takes the endings of L3.05 on the stem جَاهَدْ. The present is يُفَاعِلُ: a ḍamma on the prefix, the ā, and a kasra on the middle letter. The command is جَاهِدْ, with no helper alif.
- The passive turns the ā into و: قُوتِلَ, نُودِىَ.
- In many verses the action has someone on the other side: with, against or towards. Some Form III verbs have nobody there, like هَاجَرَ and سَارَعَ. Read the verse.
- Find the root first. قَالَ and كَانَ are hollow Form I verbs, ءَامَنَ is Form IV, and ٱلْقَٰعِدُونَ is a Form I participle.
- The participle is مُفَاعِل (ٱلْمُجَٰهِدُونَ). The verbal nouns are فِعَال (جِهَاد, قِتَال) and مُفَاعَلَة. Form VI is Form III with تَ in front.

**Next:** L3.18 teaches Forms VI, VII and IX. L3.19 teaches the participles and L3.20 the verbal nouns. L4.11 to L4.15 teach the weak verbs behind نَادَىٰ and وَٰعَدْنَا. Before you go on, review L3.04 and L3.12 if the difference between the forms felt new.
