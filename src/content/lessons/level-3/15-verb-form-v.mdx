The first word is a verb: تَذَكَّرُوا۟ (*tadhakkarū*), “they remember”. Read it from right to left. Two things stand out. There is a **تَ in front**, and the middle letter, ك, has a **shadda**.

A تَ in front and a doubled middle letter make **Form V**. The Quranic Arabic Corpus marks this verb (V). L3.04 showed how the Corpus writes the form in brackets.

</Block>

The root is ذ ك ر (L3.03). Form I is ذَكَرَ, as in وَذَكَرَ ٱسْمَ رَبِّهِۦ (87:15), “and he mentioned the name of his Lord”. Form II doubles the middle letter. You meet it in the command فَذَكِّرْ (87:9), “so remind”.

**Form V is Form II with تَ in front.** The doubled letter stays, and تَ comes before it. L3.12 teaches Form II in full. This lesson is about what the تَ changes.

<Check n={2} q="What do you add to Form II (فَعَّلَ) to make Form V?" options={['A تَ in front', 'A أَ in front', 'An alif after the first letter']} answer={0} why="Form V is تَ + Form II: تَفَعَّلَ. An alif after the first letter would be Form III." />
</Block>

The scale word of Form V is تَفَعَّلَ (*tafaʿʿala*). The present is يَتَفَعَّلُ (*yatafaʿʿalu*). Al-Jadwal gives the same patterns in its word-form (ṣarf) notes: for تَلَقَّىٰ (2:37) it says the pattern is تَفَعَّلَ, and for يَذَّكَّرُ (3:7) it says يَتَفَعَّلُ.

This lesson uses **تَوَكَّلَ** (*tawakkala*), “he relied, he put his trust”, root و ك ل, for its tables. All its tenses are in the Quran, in the verses you will read. The root starts with و, but in Form V the و comes after the تَ, so nothing changes.

<Note kind="know">In its word-form notes al-Jadwal calls a Form V verb *khumāsī*, “five-lettered”: three root letters and two extra ones, the تَ and the doubled middle letter.</Note>

Take the stem تَوَكَّلْ and add the endings you know from L3.05. The table is a teaching table: the forms follow the pattern, and the Quran does not use all 13 forms of this verb. The verse shows one real form: تَوَكَّلْتُ, with ـْتُ, “I”. Al-Jadwal says the verb is built on a sukūn and the ـتُ is the doer, and that عَلَيْهِ belongs to the verb.

In all 13 forms the تَ stays in front and the shadda stays on the middle letter. The end is what changes. In 7:201 you saw the same stem with ـُوا, “they”: تَذَكَّرُوا۟.

The present is the person prefix plus the Form V stem: يَـ + تَوَكَّلُ. The Form V تَ stays after the prefix. The endings are the ones from L3.06.

The prefix of Form V has a **fatḥa**. Compare the two verbs in 2:221. وَيُبَيِّنُ is Form II, and its prefix has a **ḍamma**. يَتَذَكَّرُونَ is Form V, and its prefix has a **fatḥa**.

| Form | Past | Present “he” | Vowel on the prefix |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | كَتَبَ | يَكْتُبُ | fatḥa |
| II | عَلَّمَ | يُعَلِّمُ | ḍamma |
| IV | أَنزَلَ | يُنزِلُ | ḍamma |
| V | تَوَكَّلَ | يَتَوَكَّلُ | fatḥa |

<Note kind="tip">Take off the prefix. If a تَ is left and the next letter has a shadda, the verb is Form V.</Note>

For “she” and “you”, the present prefix is تَ. Then comes the Form V تَ. That gives two tāʾs in a row: تَتَنَزَّلُ (*tatanazzalu*), as in 41:30.

Often one of the two is not written. In 97:4 the same verb is تَنَزَّلُ. Al-Jadwal says: one of the two tāʾs is dropped. It says the same about تَكَلَّمُ in 11:105, which was تَتَكَلَّمُ. On a third verb, تَبَرَّجْنَ (33:33), it adds that the tāʾ is dropped to make the word lighter.

The Corpus tags both تَتَنَزَّلُ and تَنَزَّلُ as the same verb, Form V, present, “she”. The angels are many, but the verb is the “she” form.

      en={['…that he goes to and fro between them.']}

Sometimes the Form V تَ is not written at all. The word starts with يَ and then a doubled letter, as in يَذَّكَّرُ. Al-Jadwal’s word-form notes explain two such verbs.

- **يَذَّكَّرُ (3:7).** The original is يَتَذَكَّرُ. The تَ became ذ, because it stands in front of ذ, the first root letter. The two ذ letters then merged, because they are alike.
- **يَطَّوَّفَ (2:158).** The original is يَتَطَوَّفُ. The sounds of ت and ط are close, so the تَ became ط to make the word lighter. The two ط letters then merged.

The verb in 87:10, سَيَذَّكَّرُ, has the same shape, and the Corpus tags it as the same verb as 3:7. In 2:221 the same root is written in full: يَتَذَكَّرُونَ.

<Note kind="tip">To read a merged verb, put the تَ back: يَذَّكَّرُ becomes يَتَذَكَّرُ. Then you see the root and the form.</Note>

<Block id="practice-1" title="Practice: recognise and read">
  <Stage label="Four questions for a verb">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Front:</b> is there a تَ in front, or a shadda right after the prefix?</li>
      <li><b>Middle:</b> does the middle root letter have a shadda?</li>

Work out each answer first. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Which of these are Form V? (a) تَذَكَّرُوا۟ (Al-Aʿraf 7:201) (b) فَتَابَ (Al-Baqarah 2:37) (c) فَذَكِّرْ (Al-Aʿla 87:9) (d) تَوَكَّلْتُ (Yusuf 12:67) (e) وَتَعَاوَنُوا۟ (Al-Maida 5:2)">
- (a) **Form V.** تَ in front, shadda on ك. Past, “they”.
- (b) **Not Form V.** Form I. The ت is the first root letter (ت و ب). No shadda.
- (c) **Not Form V.** Form II, a command. The shadda is there, but no تَ in front.
- (d) **Form V.** Past, “I”.
- (e) **Not Form V.** Form VI: an alif after the first root letter, no shadda.
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Name the tense and the person: (a) تَذَكَّرُوا۟ (Al-Aʿraf 7:201) (b) يَتَذَكَّرُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:221) (c) فَتَوَكَّلْ (Al-Imran 3:159) (d) فَتَمَنَّوُا۟ (Al-Baqarah 2:94)">
- (a) Past, “they” (men).
- (b) Present, “they” (men). The prefix يَ and the ending ـُونَ.
- (c) Command, “you” (one man).
- (d) Command, “you all” (men). The nūn is dropped. The و is the doer. The Corpus tags it as a Form V command.
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Put the تَ back: يَذَّكَّرُ (Al-Imran 3:7), سَيَذَّكَّرُ (Al-Aʿla 87:10) and يَطَّوَّفَ (Al-Baqarah 2:158).">
- يَذَّكَّرُ becomes **يَتَذَكَّرُ**.
- سَيَذَّكَّرُ becomes **سَيَتَذَكَّرُ**.
- يَطَّوَّفَ becomes **يَتَطَوَّفَ**. The fatḥa at the end is the naṣb after أَن.

Al-Jadwal’s notes on 3:7 and 2:158 say the تَ turned into ذ and ط and merged.
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="One tāʾ or two? تَنَزَّلُ (Al-Qadr 97:4) and تَكَلَّمُ (Hud 11:105) each show one تَ. Write the full forms.">
- تَنَزَّلُ is **تَتَنَزَّلُ**: the form in 41:30.
- تَكَلَّمُ is **تَتَكَلَّمُ**.

In both, one of the two tāʾs is dropped. Both are present, Form V, “she”.
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="Form II or Form V? وَيُبَيِّنُ (Al-Baqarah 2:221) and يَتَذَكَّرُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:221). How can you tell?">
- وَيُبَيِّنُ is **Form II**: the prefix has a **ḍamma** and the middle letter has a shadda. There is no تَ after the prefix.
- يَتَذَكَّرُونَ is **Form V**: the prefix has a **fatḥa**, then a تَ, then a doubled ك.
</Exercise>
</Block>

<Exercise n={6} q="Make the command for “you” from these present verbs. (a) تَتَذَكَّرُ (b) تَتَوَكَّلُونَ (c) تَتَقَبَّلِينَ. These are made-up examples.">
- (a) **تَذَكَّرْ**: one man, a sukūn.
- (b) **تَوَكَّلُوا**: men. The nūn drops and the و is the doer.
- (c) **تَقَبَّلِي**: one woman. The nūn drops and the ـِي is the doer.

<Exercise n={7} q="What do the two verbs mean, and how are they related? فَذَكِّرْ (Al-Aʿla 87:9) and سَيَذَّكَّرُ (Al-Aʿla 87:10).">
فَذَكِّرْ is Form II, a command: “remind!”. سَيَذَّكَّرُ is Form V, present with سَ: “will remember”. The root is ذ ك ر. The person who is reminded is the one who remembers. The Form V verb is merged: the original is سَيَتَذَكَّرُ.
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Find the Form V verb and the Form V participle: عَلَيْهِ يَتَوَكَّلُ ٱلْمُتَوَكِّلُونَ (Az-Zumar 39:38, end). Meaning: “On Him those who rely will rely.”">
The verb is **يَتَوَكَّلُ**, present, “he relies”. The participle is **ٱلْمُتَوَكِّلُونَ**, “those who rely”, on the pattern مُتَفَعِّل. عَلَيْهِ, “on Him”, belongs to the verb (al-Jadwal). The -ūna shows the participle is nominative: it follows the verb.
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Which is the Form V verbal noun, and which is the Form II one? تَطْهِيرًا (Al-Ahzab 33:33) and تَبَرُّجَ (Al-Ahzab 33:33). What is the pattern of each?">
**تَبَرُّجَ** is Form V: pattern تَفَعُّل, with a ḍamma on the doubled letter. **تَطْهِيرًا** is Form II: pattern تَفْعِيل, with a long ī. Al-Jadwal gives both patterns in its word-form notes on 33:33.
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Why does تَلَقَّىٰٓ end in ىٰ, and what is its Form? فَتَلَقَّىٰٓ ءَادَمُ (Al-Baqarah 2:37, start)">
It is **Form V**: تَ in front and a shadda on ق. The root is ل ق ي. The last root letter is a weak letter. Al-Jadwal: the original is تَلَقَّيَ, and the ي turned into an alif, so the verb is built on a hidden fatḥa. L4.13 teaches verbs like this.
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={11} q="Go through this phrase word by word: عَلَيْهِ تَوَكَّلْتُ وَعَلَيْهِ فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ ٱلْمُتَوَكِّلُونَ (Yusuf 12:67, end). Meaning: “In Him I put my trust, and in Him let those who rely, rely.”">
- **عَلَيْهِ**, “on Him”: a phrase that belongs to تَوَكَّلْتُ.
- **تَوَكَّلْتُ**: past, Form V. Built on a sukūn. The ـتُ is the doer, “I”.
- **وَ**: joins this to the sentence before.
- **عَلَيْهِ**: belongs to فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ.
- **فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ**: present, Form V, with لِ of command. It is in the jussive (L3.07), and a kasra moves the last letter because the next word starts with ٱل.
- **ٱلْمُتَوَكِّلُونَ**: the doer, “those who rely”. A Form V participle. Nominative, shown by -ūna.
</Exercise>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="Form II plus a ta in front equals Form V"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'Form II', small: 'فَعَّلَ' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'a تَ in front', small: 'تَـ' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'Form V', small: 'تَفَعَّلَ' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- Form V has a **تَ in front** and the **middle root letter doubled**: تَفَعَّلَ, يَتَفَعَّلُ. The Corpus tag is (V). There are 414 verb words in the Quran, from 81 verbs (Quran Learn’s count from the Corpus).
- The past takes the usual endings: تَوَكَّلْتُ. The present has a **fatḥa** on the prefix: يَتَوَكَّلُ. The command drops the first تَ: تَوَكَّلْ.
- In the present, one of the two tāʾs can drop (تَنَزَّلُ), or the تَ can merge into the next letter (يَذَّكَّرُ, يَطَّوَّفَ). Put the تَ back to read it.
- Next to Form II it often shows the receiver of the action: teach and learn, remind and remember. It is a tendency to check in each verse: 41 of the 81 roots have no Form II verb in the Quran.
- Form V has a participle **مُتَفَعِّل** (ٱلْمُتَوَكِّلُونَ) and a verbal noun **تَفَعُّل** (تَبَرُّجَ).
- Verbs that end in a weak letter (تَلَقَّىٰ, تَوَلَّىٰ, تَوَفَّىٰ) look different at the end: L4.13.

**Next:** L3.16 teaches Form X. L3.18 teaches Form VI, which also starts with تَ. L3.19 and L3.20 teach participles and verbal nouns. L4.11 starts the weak verbs. Review L3.12 (Form II) if the comparisons felt new. The [verb forms hub](/learn/verb-forms/) shows all ten forms together.
