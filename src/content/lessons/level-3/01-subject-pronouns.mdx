The verse speaks to men about their wives. The sentence starts with **هُنَّ**, “they”, for women. It is one word, and it points back to نِسَآئِكُمْ, “your women”, a few words earlier in the verse.

A word like this is a **pronoun**, ضَمِير (*ḍamīr*). It stands for someone the listener already knows, so the speaker does not need to say the name again.

You have already met all three ways.

- **Detached**, ضَمِير مُنْفَصِل (*ḍamīr munfaṣil*): a pronoun that is a word of its own. This lesson teaches it.
- **Attached**, ضَمِير مُتَّصِل (*ḍamīr muttaṣil*): a short pronoun joined to the end of another word. You met it after prepositions (L2.07) and as the doer ending of a verb (L2.03). [L3.02](/learn/level-3/02-attached-pronouns/) teaches it in full.
- **Hidden**, ضَمِير مُسْتَتِر (*ḍamīr mustatir*): a doer understood inside a verb, with nothing written for it (L2.03).

<Note kind="know">Detached pronouns come in two sets. The twelve in this lesson stand where a nominative word would stand. A second set, the words that start with إِيَّا, stands in the place of an object. Step 3 gives a first look at it.</Note>
</Block>

Two points from earlier lessons now have a full answer.

- **A pronoun counts as a noun.** L1.09 sorted sentences by their first word. A sentence that starts with a pronoun starts with a noun, so it is a nominal sentence.
- **A pronoun is definite.** In L1.06, *al-Ajurrumiyyah* lists five kinds of definite noun, and the pronoun comes first. A pronoun already points to one person, so it takes no ٱلْ.

A definite word is what a mubtadaʾ usually is (L2.02). That is one reason a pronoun starts a nominal sentence so easily.

Ask three questions about any pronoun.

1. **Who?** The speaker (I, we), the one spoken to (you), or the one spoken about (he, she, they).
2. **How many?** One, two, or more than two. Arabic has its own form for two (L1.08).
3. **Man or woman?** Arabic asks this for “you” and “they”, not for “I” and “we”.

English has seven pronouns: I, you, he, she, it, we, they. Arabic has twelve. English “you” is one word. Arabic has five.

Arabic has no word for “it”. هُوَ is “he”, or “it” for a masculine noun. هِيَ is “she”, or “it” for a feminine noun (L1.07).

- **-mā** is two people. **-m** is many men, or men and women together. **-nna** is many women. The “you” row and the “they” row use the same three endings.
- **One man or one woman.** For “you”, the last vowel is what changes: أَنْتَ (-a) and أَنْتِ (-i). For “he” and “she”, more changes: هُوَ and هِيَ.
- **I and we** do not change for a man or a woman who is speaking.
- **A mixed group** takes the masculine form. L2.02 showed this with the believing men and women in 9:71.

*Al-Ajurrumiyyah* lists the twelve in its chapter on the mubtadaʾ and the khabar. It says the mubtadaʾ is of two kinds, a written noun and a pronoun, and then names the pronouns. Its word for a pronoun is **مُضْمَر** (*muḍmar*), “put away, hidden”. Today's books say ضَمِير.

The twelve in the book are the twelve in your table, in the same order. The first وَهِيَ in the quote means “and they are”. The second one is “she”.

The book's own example is **أَنَا قَائِمٌ**, “I am standing”, and **نَحْنُ قَائِمُونَ**, “we are standing”. Each one is a nominal sentence. The pronoun is the mubtadaʾ and the khabar ends in -un or -ūna.
</Block>

Both sentences start with a pronoun: هُوَ, “he”, and أَنَا۠, “I”. A pronoun counts as a noun, so each sentence is nominal. The pronoun is the **mubtadaʾ**, and the khabar follows it. Both khabars end in -u or -un: ٱلْقَاهِرُ, خَيْرٌ.

In 7:12 the speaker is Iblis, who is answering Allah's question.

This is the plainest job of a written pronoun. A nominal sentence has no verb to carry the doer. The pronoun is the starting point of the sentence, so it has to be written.

<Block id="built" title="A pronoun has no ending to show its case">
  <Stage label="How the grammar books say it">
    <Tiles
      label="the books’ words for أَنَا۠ in 7:12"
      items={[
        { ar: 'ضَمِير', en: 'pronoun' },
        { ar: 'مُنْفَصِل', en: 'detached' },
        { ar: 'مَبْنِيّ', en: 'built: one fixed shape' },
        { ar: 'فِي مَحَلّ رَفْع', en: 'in the place of the nominative' },

A noun like ٱللَّهُ changes its ending with its job: -u, -a or -i (L1.10). A pronoun does not. أَنَا۠ looks the same whatever its job.

That is why the books, al-Jadwal among them, call a pronoun **مَبْنِيّ** (*mabnī*), “built”: its shape is fixed. They still give it a job. A mubtadaʾ is nominative, so a pronoun that is a mubtadaʾ stands **in the place of** the nominative, فِي مَحَلّ رَفْع (*fī maḥall rafʿ*).

The line in the stage reads, one word at a time: “a detached pronoun, built, in the place of the nominative, a mubtadaʾ.”

Many books call the twelve **ضَمَائِر الرَّفْع**, “pronouns of the nominative”. The name fits, because each one takes the place of a nominative word.

<Note kind="warn">Do not add an ending to a pronoun that is not there. Say that it is nominative in place, and name its job.</Note>

Arabic has a form for exactly two people (L1.08), and the dual pronouns have one shape for men and women. In 9:40 **هُمَا** means the two men in the cave. In 28:35 **أَنتُمَا** means the two people Allah is speaking to.

In both verses the dual pronoun is the mubtadaʾ. In 9:40 the khabar is the phrase فِى ٱلْغَارِ. In 28:35 the khabar is ٱلْغَٰلِبُونَ, and ٱلْغَٰلِبُونَ shows its nominative with the letter و (L2.04). The word مَن, “whoever”, is joined to أَنتُمَا by “and”, so it is joined to the mubtadaʾ.

The dual pronouns are rare. Quran Learn found هُمَا twice and أَنتُمَا once as words of their own.

</Block>

The pronoun for a mixed group is the masculine one (L2.02).

<Block id="when" title="When is a pronoun written?">
  <Stage label="Three reasons">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>It starts a nominal sentence.</b> هُوَ ٱلْقَاهِرُ, أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌ.</li>
      <li><b>It stresses or repeats a doer.</b> ٱسْكُنْ أَنتَ, إِنَّنِىٓ أَنَا ٱللَّهُ.</li>

A verb already says who does the action. So a written pronoun beside a verb is a choice, and it adds something: weight, a contrast, or a repeat.

A nominal sentence is different. There the pronoun is the mubtadaʾ itself, so the sentence needs it. It is not a stress.

The next blocks show the second and third reasons in verses.
</Block>

Al-Jadwal names أَنتَ this way. Then وَزَوْجُكَ, “and your wife”, is joined to the doer by “and”. So the verse tells both of them to live in the garden.

<Block id="mistake-stress" title="Mistake 1: adding a pronoun by habit, or hearing stress in each pronoun">
  <Stage label="Made-up example">
    <Tiles

Learners make two errors here, in opposite directions.

- **Adding a pronoun to a verb that does not need one.** A verb already says who. A written pronoun beside it adds stress (2:35, 109:3). Add one when you mean the stress.
- **Reading stress into each pronoun.** In a nominal sentence the pronoun is the mubtadaʾ. هُوَ ٱلْقَاهِرُ (6:18) is “He is the One with full power”, not “HE is…”.

  <Stage>

<Block id="mistake-attached" title="Mistake 4: calling an attached pronoun detached">
  <Stage label="Same letters, different job">
    <Tiles

A pronoun can look the same in two forms. In لَّهُنَّ the هُنَّ is joined to لِ, so it is attached. The هُنَّ that starts a sentence is a word of its own.

A learner may think a pronoun has to be written after particles such as إِنَّ. What follows إِنَّ is a pronoun joined to it, as in إِنَّا. It is an attached pronoun, and [L3.02](/learn/level-3/02-attached-pronouns/) teaches it. A detached pronoun may follow, as نَحْنُ does in 15:9, but then it is a separate word.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-ending" title="Mistake 5: looking for a case ending on a pronoun">
  <Stage label="Seen and not seen">

Do not hunt for an ending, and do not invent one. Say what the books say: “built, in the place of the nominative”.

<Exercise n={1} q="Match each pronoun to its meaning. (a) أَنَا (b) هُوَ (c) نَحْنُ (d) هُمْ (e) أَنْتَ (f) هِيَ (g) أَنْتُمْ (h) هُنَّ. Meanings: she, I, they (women), he, you (one man), we, you all (men), they (men). This is from the table of the lesson.">

- (a) أَنَا is **I**.
- (b) هُوَ is **he**.
- (c) نَحْنُ is **we**.
- (d) هُمْ is **they (men)**, or men and women together.
- (e) أَنْتَ is **you (one man)**.
- (f) هِيَ is **she**.
- (g) أَنْتُمْ is **you all (men)**, or men and women together.
- (h) هُنَّ is **they (women)**.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Is the pronoun written, attached or hidden? (a) وَبِٱلْءَاخِرَةِ هُمْ يُوقِنُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:4, end): both “they”. (b) نَذَرْتُ (Al-Imran 3:35, part): the “I”. (c) خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ (An-Nahl 16:3, start): the “he”. (d) وَأَنتُمْ لِبَاسٌ لَّهُنَّ (Al-Baqarah 2:187, part): the هُنَّ at the end.">

- (a) There are two “they”. **هُمْ** is **written** (detached): it is the mubtadaʾ. The ending **و** in يُوقِنُونَ is **attached**: it is the doer.
- (b) **Attached.** The ending -tu is “I”, the doer.
- (c) **Hidden.** The doer is a “he” inside the verb.
- (d) **Attached.** This هُنَّ is joined to لِ: “for them”. The written هُنَّ is at the start of the verse part.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Find each “we”, and say which is written and which is attached: قَالَ ٱلْحَوَارِيُّونَ نَحْنُ أَنصَارُ ٱللَّهِ ءَامَنَّا بِٱللَّهِ (Al-Imran 3:52, part). Meaning: “The disciples said: We are the helpers of Allah. We believed in Allah.”">

There are two “we”.

- **نَحْنُ** is **written**. It is the mubtadaʾ of the first sentence. Its khabar is **أَنصَارُ**, nominative (-u). ٱللَّهِ is the second word of an idafah, genitive.
- The ending **-nā** in ءَامَنَّا is **attached**. It is the doer of the verb.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Which pronouns are written in these two verses, and what is the verse doing with them? وَلَآ أَنتُمْ عَٰبِدُونَ مَآ أَعْبُدُ (Al-Kafirun 109:3) and وَلَآ أَنَا۠ عَابِدٌ مَّا عَبَدتُّمْ (Al-Kafirun 109:4). Meaning: “And you are not worshippers of what I worship. And I am not a worshipper of what you worshipped.”">

The written pronouns are **أَنتُمْ**, “you all”, in 109:3 and **أَنَا۠**, “I”, in 109:4.

The verses set “you” against “I”. The pronouns carry the contrast. There are also pronouns inside the verbs: أَعْبُدُ has a hidden “I”, and عَبَدتُّمْ ends in the attached -tum, “you all”.

<Exercise n={5} q="Find the mubtadaʾ and the khabar. (a) قَالَتْ هُوَ مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ (Al-Imran 3:37, part). Meaning: “She said: It is from Allah.” (b) قَالَ هِىَ عَصَاىَ (Ta-Ha 20:18, start). Meaning: “He said: It is my staff.”">

- (a) **هُوَ**, “it”, is the **mubtadaʾ**. The khabar is the phrase **مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ**, “from Allah”.
- (b) **هِىَ**, “it”, is the **mubtadaʾ**. A staff, عَصًا, is a feminine noun, so the pronoun is feminine. The khabar is **عَصَا**. It is nominative, but you cannot see the -u: the word ends in a long ā, so the ḍamma is hidden. The ending **-ya** is “my”, the second word of an idafah.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={6} q="Fill in the pronoun. (a) ___ عَالِمَةٌ (she is a scholar) (b) ___ مُسْلِمُونَ (we are Muslims) (c) ___ طَالِبَانِ (you two are students) (d) ___ ٱلْخَالِقُ (he is the Creator) (e) ___ صَادِقٌ (you, one man, are truthful). These are made-up examples.">

- (a) **هِيَ** عَالِمَةٌ. The khabar is feminine, so the pronoun is “she”.
- (b) **نَحْنُ** مُسْلِمُونَ.
- (c) **أَنْتُمَا** طَالِبَانِ. The khabar ends in -āni, the dual (L2.04).
- (d) **هُوَ** ٱلْخَالِقُ.
- (e) **أَنْتَ** صَادِقٌ. For a woman it would be أَنْتِ.

In all five sentences the pronoun is the mubtadaʾ. Nothing else needs to be written.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="(a) Who does هُمَا stand for in إِذْ هُمَا فِى ٱلْغَارِ (At-Tawbah 9:40, part), and how many are they? (b) Find the mubtadaʾ and the khabar in أَنتُمَا وَمَنِ ٱتَّبَعَكُمَا ٱلْغَٰلِبُونَ (Al-Qasas 28:35, end). Meaning: “You two and whoever follows you two are the winners.”">

- (a) **Two** people, the two men in the cave. هُمَا is the mubtadaʾ and the phrase فِى ٱلْغَارِ is the khabar.
- (b) **أَنتُمَا**, “you two”, is the mubtadaʾ. **وَمَنِ**, “and whoever”, is joined to it. The khabar is **ٱلْغَٰلِبُونَ**, nominative with the letter و.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Find the mubtadaʾ and the khabar, and say why the mīm has a ḍamma: وَأَنتُمُ ٱلْأَعْلَوْنَ (Al-Imran 3:139, part). Meaning: “And you are the highest.”">

**أَنتُمُ** is the mubtadaʾ, a pronoun. **ٱلْأَعْلَوْنَ** is the khabar, nominative with the letter و (L2.04).

The ḍamma on the mīm is the joining vowel you saw in step 1. It is placed before a word that starts with ٱلْ. It is still أَنْتُمْ.

Al-Jadwal reads the whole sentence as a **ḥāl** (a state: “while you are the highest”). L4.01 teaches the ḥāl.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Nominal or verbal? (a) هُمْ يُوقِنُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:4, end) (b) يُوقِنُونَ (c) أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌ مِّنْهُ (Al-Aʿraf 7:12, part) (d) خَلَقَ (e) هِىَ رَٰوَدَتْنِى (Yusuf 12:26, start)">

The first word decides (L1.09).

- (a) **Nominal.** It starts with the pronoun هُمْ. The khabar is the verbal sentence يُوقِنُونَ.
- (b) **Verbal.** It starts with a verb. The “they” is an attached doer.
- (c) **Nominal.** The pronoun is the mubtadaʾ and خَيْرٌ is the khabar.
- (d) **Verbal.** It starts with a verb. The doer is a hidden “he”.
- (e) **Nominal.** It starts with هِىَ. The khabar is the verbal sentence رَٰوَدَتْنِى, with a hidden “she” as its doer.

</Exercise>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="person plus number and gender equals one of twelve pronouns"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'person', small: 'I, you, he' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'number and gender', small: 'one, two, many; man or woman' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'one of twelve words', small: 'ضَمَائِر' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A **pronoun** stands for someone already known. It can be **detached** (a word of its own), **attached** (joined) or **hidden** (inside a verb).
- The twelve detached pronouns: **I, we**; **you** in five forms; **he, she, they** in five forms. The dual has one form for men and women. A mixed group takes the masculine plural.
- A pronoun counts as a noun and is definite. A sentence that starts with it is nominal.
- A pronoun has one fixed shape. It is **built** and shows no ending. A pronoun that is a mubtadaʾ stands **in the place of** the nominative.
- A written pronoun starts a nominal sentence. Beside a verb it repeats or stresses a doer, or sets one person against another.
- After إِنَّ the pronoun that is joined to it is attached. A detached pronoun may follow it as a separate word.
- إِيَّاكَ belongs to a second set of detached pronouns, for the object.

Verb endings come in [L3.05](/learn/level-3/05-past-tense/), and demonstratives and relatives, the other pronouns, in [L3.10](/learn/level-3/10-demonstrative-pronouns/) and [L3.11](/learn/level-3/11-relative-pronouns/).
