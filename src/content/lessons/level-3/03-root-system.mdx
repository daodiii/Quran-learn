You saw this idea in L1.05: many Arabic words are a root plus a pattern. This lesson gives you the method: how to find the root, and how to name the pattern.

Level 2 taught **naḥw**, نَحْو: how words work together in a sentence. This level teaches **ṣarf**, صَرْف: how one word is built.

Al-Jadwal is Maḥmūd Ṣāfī’s book on the grammar of the Quran. Its notes on a verse come in two parts. First the iʿrāb, which you know. Then, for some verses, a short section headed الصرف. There it says وَزْنُهُ, “its pattern is”, and names the pattern. Reading those notes is one aim of this lesson.

Three skills:

1. **Find the root** of a word with pieces stuck to it (step 2).
2. **Name the pattern** with the scale ف ع ل (this step, and step 3).
3. **Spot a root that changes**: one with a weak letter, a doubled letter or a fourth letter (step 3).

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal does not give a pattern for every word. Where it gives one, this lesson uses it. Where it does not, we read the pattern off with the scale and say so.</Note>

Think of the root as the bricks and the pattern as the plan. The grammar books write the plan with three stand-in letters, ف ع ل. The word فَعَلَ means “he did”, so it is easy to remember.

- **ف** stands for the first root letter (*fāʾ al-fiʿl*).
- **ع** stands for the second root letter (*ʿayn al-fiʿl*).
- **ل** stands for the third root letter (*lām al-fiʿl*).

To read a word on the scale, put the root letters where ف ع ل stand. Keep the vowels and the extra letters of the scale word. Here the root **ك ت ب** (writing) is poured into four scale words. Each result is a word of the Quran.

| Scale word | Word you get | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| فَعَلَ | كَتَبَ | Al-Anʿam 6:12 |
| يَفْعُلُونَ | يَكْتُبُونَ | Al-Baqarah 2:79 |
| فَاعِل | كَاتِبٌ | Al-Baqarah 2:282 |
| مَفْعُول | مَكْتُوبًا | Al-Aʿraf 7:157 |

Everything in the scale word apart from ف ع ل is the pattern. فَاعِل has a long ā after the first letter. مَفْعُول has an extra م at the front and a long ū after the second letter. Al-Jadwal gives فَاعِل for كَاتِب and مَفْعُول for مَكْتُوب. The other two rows are the scale read directly.

</Block>

The verb in this phrase has the root ع ل م. It also has a prefix يُ and a doubled ل. The scale shows both. When the scale word has a mark that is not part of ف ع ل, that mark belongs to the pattern.

| Scale word | Root poured in | Word | The clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| فَعَلَ | ك ت ب | كَتَبَ | Only vowels. No extra letter. |
| فَعَّلَ | ع ل م | عَلَّمَ | The second letter is doubled (shadda on ع). |
| يُفَعِّلُ | ع ل م | يُعَلِّمُ | يُ in front, and the second letter doubled. |
| مَفْعُول | ك ت ب | مَكْتُوب | An extra م in front and an extra و after the second letter. |

A letter you can read in the scale word that is not ف, ع or ل is an **extra letter**. An extra letter belongs to the pattern, not to the root. The Corpus calls عَلَّمَ a **Form II** verb. Step 3 shows all ten forms, and L3.12 teaches Form II.

A word in the Quran often has pieces stuck to it. Before you look for the root, peel them off, from the outside in. The word above, from Al-Jumua 62:2, is one example.

| Piece | Examples | Taught in |
|---|---|---|
| A joining word or little prefix | وَ، فَ، بِ، لِ، كَ | [L1.04](/learn/level-1/04-three-word-types/), [L2.07](/learn/level-2/07-prepositions-genitive/) |
| The article | ٱلْ | [L1.06](/learn/level-1/06-definite-article/) |
| A pronoun ending | ـهُ، ـهُمْ، ـكُمْ، ـنَا | [L2.07](/learn/level-2/07-prepositions-genitive/), [L3.02](/learn/level-3/02-attached-pronouns/) |
| A tense prefix or doer ending on a verb | يَ، تَ، نَ، أَ and ـُوا، ـُونَ، ـْتُ | [L3.05](/learn/level-3/05-past-tense/), [L3.06](/learn/level-3/06-present-tense/) |
| A plural, dual or feminine ending | ـِينَ، ـُونَ، ـَانِ، ـَاتٌ، ـَةٌ | [L1.08](/learn/level-1/08-singular-dual-plural/), [L2.04](/learn/level-2/04-nominative-case/) |

What is left is the **stem**. The root is inside the stem. The stem can still hold pattern letters, and the next blocks show how to see them.

Now check the answer by pouring the root back into the pattern. The scale word is يُفَعِّلُ. Put ع ل م into it and you get يُعَلِّمُ. It is the word you started with, so the root is right.

The pattern has two clues: the prefix يُ and the doubled second letter. Together they say: a present-tense verb of Form II. L3.06 teaches the present tense.

Three names, one peeling method. Each has the article ٱلْ and a case ending ـُ to peel.

| Word | Left after peeling | Root | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| ٱلْخَٰلِقُ | خَالِق | خ ل ق | فَاعِل |
| ٱلْبَارِئُ | بَارِئ | ب ر أ | فَاعِل |
| ٱلْمُصَوِّرُ | مُصَوِّر | ص و ر | مُفَعِّل |

In ٱلْمُصَوِّرُ the م is an extra letter of the pattern, and the و is a **root letter**, doubled by the pattern. Al-Jadwal gives the pattern مُفَعِّل for it. It gives no pattern for the other two. They are doer words (active participles in the Corpus), so the usual doer pattern فَاعِل fits. L3.19 teaches doer words. In ٱلْبَارِئُ the last root letter is a hamza, written on a ي-shaped seat.

<Note kind="know">Al-Jadwal calls the verb صَوَّرَ “four-letter” (*rubāʿī*), because the doubled letter counts twice. The root ص و ر still has three letters. A four-letter root is a different thing, and you meet one in step 3.</Note>
</Block>

<Block id="pour-back" title="Check your root: pour it back">
  <Stage label="Two tries for مَكْتُوبٌ">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Try 1:</b> the root م ك ت. Pour it into مَفْعُول and you get مَمْكُوتٌ. This is not the word.</li>
      <li><b>Try 2:</b> the root ك ت ب. Pour it into مَفْعُول and you get مَكْتُوبٌ. This is the word.</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

When you have a root, test it. Pour the root into the pattern you think the word has. If you get the same word back, the root is right.

In try 1 the first letter م was taken for a root letter. In مَكْتُوبٌ it is an extra letter of the pattern مَفْعُول. The scale word has its own م, so the extra م cannot be a root letter as well.

The test needs a good guess at the pattern. If the first try fails, try another pattern or another peeling.

A dictionary of the Quran lists words under their roots. The Quranic Arabic Corpus has one on its website (corpus.quran.com). It groups the words of the Quran by root. To use it:

1. Peel the word and find its root.
2. Find the root’s first letter in the order above. Then the second letter. Then the third.
3. Read what the dictionary says about the root, then find your word in the list.

The six roots in the picture are in that order: ح, then ر, then س, then ع, then ق, then ك. So ع ل م comes before ك ت ب.

The 500 most common roots cover 59.2% of all the words in the Quran (Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus, as in L1.05). So each root you learn helps you with many words.

A Form I verb is the plain verb. In the other forms the root is wrapped in extra letters, or one letter is doubled. The Quran has ten forms, numbered I to X. The root stays the same in all ten. Only the shape around it changes.

The table gives the scale word for each form (the “he did” word), a word from the Quran, and how many verb words use the form. The counts are Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus: 19,356 verb words in all.

| II | فَعَّلَ | عَلَّمَ (96:5) | 1,300 | [L3.12](/learn/level-3/12-verb-form-ii/) |
| IV | أَفْعَلَ | أَنزَلَ (2:231) | 3,487 | [L3.13](/learn/level-3/13-verb-form-iv/) |

Form I is 12,356 of the 19,356 verb words, about 64%. The table is in the order of the lessons. After Form I, the lessons go from the more common forms to the rarer ones: II, IV, VIII, V, X, III. The three rare forms share one lesson. Form IV has more verb words than Form II, so the order follows use roughly, not exactly. The counts are in the table.

Two things you will see in al-Jadwal’s ṣarf notes:

- **A Form I verb named by an example.** Al-Jadwal writes *bāb* (type) and then a verb, such as *bāb naṣara*. This names the vowels of a Form I verb. L3.04 teaches them.
- **A pattern letter that changes.** In ٱصْطَادُوا۟ (Al-Maida 5:2) al-Jadwal says the ت of the Form VIII pattern turned into ط next to ص. The word began as ٱصْتَادُوا۟. So the scale word اِفْتَعَلَ is the plan, and a letter in it can change.

| The place of an action, اِسْم مَكَان | مَفْعَل | مَّرْقَدِنَا (36:52) | ر ق د | [L3.21](/learn/level-3/21-nouns-place-time/) |

Al-Jadwal names the pattern of غَافِرِ as فَاعِل. It says قَابِلِ is “like” it. For ٱلتَّوْبِ it gives فَعْل, an action name. For مَرْقَد it gives مَفْعَل, a noun of place. L1.05 showed مَفْعُول on مَكْتُوبًا.

There are many patterns for the action name, not only فَعْل. L3.20 shows them. The root ت و ب has a و in the middle. That is a weak letter, and a later block in this step returns to it.
</Block>

Many describing words, and many names of Allah, have a pattern of their own. Each row below gives the pattern, the word, the root, and what al-Jadwal says about it.

| Pattern | A word from the Quran | Root | Al-Jadwal says |
|---|---|---|---|
| فَعِيل | ٱلْحَكِيمُ (2:32) | ح ك م | gives the pattern |
| فَعَل | ٱلصَّمَدُ (112:2) | ص م د | a describing word, صِفَة مُشَبَّهَة |
| فَعَّال | ٱلْفَتَّاحُ (34:26) | ف ت ح | an intensive form, صِيغَة مُبَالَغَة |

A **describing word**, صِفَة مُشَبَّهَة, names a lasting quality. An **intensive form**, صِيغَة مُبَالَغَة, says “very much”, so ٱلْفَتَّاحُ is the one who opens abundantly. The same pattern appears in تَوَّابًا in 110:3, which a later block reads word by word.

Some roots hold a letter that makes the word change shape. The weak letters are **و** and **ي**, and the hamza can behave like them. This preview names the kinds. It does not teach their conjugation. Level 4 does that.

| Kind of root | The special letter | Example | Root | Taught in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sound | none | بَعَثَنَا (36:52) | ب ع ث | no special rules |

<Block id="hidden" title="A hidden letter: how to notice it">
  <Stage>
    <Sentence
      reference="Al-Baqarah 2:183 (part)"
      groups={[
        { words: [{ ar: 'كُتِبَ', en: 'it was written', say: 'kutiba', type: 'v' }] },
        { words: [{ ar: 'عَلَيْكُمُ', en: 'on you', say: 'alaykumu', type: 'p' }] },
        { words: [{ ar: 'ٱلصِّيَامُ', en: 'the fasting', say: 'as-siyamu' }] },
      ]}
      en={['Fasting has been made a duty for you.']}
    />
  </Stage>

A word with a hidden letter often gives you a clue. Here are four.

1. **Only two letters are left after peeling.** If you cannot find three, a letter is hidden. In يَعِدُكُمُ (2:268) only ع and د are left. The root is و ع د, and the first letter has dropped. Al-Jadwal says the first letter is dropped in the present, so the pattern has no letter for it: يَعِلُ.
2. **A long vowel sits where a root letter should be.** In قَالُوا۟ the long ā stands where the و of ق و ل belongs.
3. **A weak letter shows as a different letter.** In ٱلصِّيَامُ the root is ص و م. Al-Jadwal says the word began as ٱلصِّوَامُ, and the و turned into ي. In يُزَكِّيهِمْ (62:2) the root is ز ك و, and the last و shows as ي.
4. **The dictionary or the Corpus tag tells you.** When a clue is not enough, look the word up. The Corpus lists the full root even when the word shows less.

كُتِبَ in the same verse comes from ك ت ب, the family of writing. It is a Form I verb in a form that does not name the doer. L3.09 teaches it.

<Block id="mistake-pattern" title="Mistake 1: counting a pattern letter as a root letter">
  <Stage label="Three letters, not four">
    <Tiles
      label="مَكْتُوبًا: the wrong root has four letters, the right root has three"
      items={[
        { ar: 'مَكْتُوبًا', en: 'written', type: 'n' },
        { ar: 'م ك ت ب', en: 'wrong: four letters', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'ك ت ب', en: 'right: three letters', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

The wrong root م ك ت ب has four letters. The first م belongs to the pattern مَفْعُول. Some letters at the front are often pattern letters:

- **م** in مَفْعُول, مَفْعَل, and in the doer words of the later forms, such as مُفْعِل and مُفَعِّل.
- **ت** in Forms V and VI, as in تَعَاوَنُوا۟.
- **ٱسْتَ** in Form X, as in وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ.
- **ٱنْ** in Form VII, as in ٱنفَطَرَتْ.

Say “often”, because a letter at the front can also be a root letter. In مَلِك (Al-Hashr 59:23) the م is the first root letter, م ل ك. Pour the root back (step 2) and see whether you get the word.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-doubled" title="Mistake 2: counting a doubled letter twice">
  <Stage label="One root letter, written twice">
    <Tiles
      label="عَلَّمَ has the root ع ل م, not ع ل ل"
      items={[
        { ar: 'عَلَّمَ', en: 'he taught', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'ع ل ل', en: 'wrong', type: 'x' },
        { ar: 'ع ل م', en: 'right', type: 'x' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

In عَلَّمَ the shadda doubles the second root letter. That is the pattern فَعَّلَ. The root is ع ل م, and it has one ل.

Do not go too far the other way. Some roots really have two equal letters. رَبِّكَ (110:3) is ر ب ب, and كُلِّ (64:11) is ك ل ل. How can you tell? Undo the shadda and count the letters. If you get only two letters, the root has a repeated letter, as in ر ب ب. If you still have three different letters, as in ع ل م, the shadda came from the pattern. When you are unsure, look at the Corpus tag.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-vowel" title="Mistake 3: counting a long vowel as a root letter, or missing one">
  <Stage label="Two words with a long ā">
    <Tiles
      label="قَٰتَلَهُمُ has the root ق ت ل; قَالُوا۟ has the root ق و ل"
      items={[
        { ar: 'قَٰتَلَهُمُ', en: 'root ق ت ل: the ā is the pattern', type: 'v' },
        { ar: 'قَالُوا۟', en: 'root ق و ل: the ā hides a و', type: 'v' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

A long ā can be two different things.

- In قَٰتَلَهُمُ (9:30) it is the extra letter of the Form III pattern فَاعَلَ. After you peel the ending هُمُ you find the three letters ق ت ل, with the ā between the first two. The root is ق ت ل.
- In قَالُوا۟ (36:52) the long ā stands for a root letter. Only ق and ل show. The root is ق و ل.

The clue from step 3 helps. If a long vowel sits between two letters and no third consonant shows, a root letter is hidden. If three consonants already show, the long vowel is a pattern letter.
</Block>

<Block id="mistake-noroot" title="Mistake 4: looking for a root in every word">
  <Stage label="Words with no root">

      ]}

  </Stage>

Not every word has a root. The Corpus tags no root on pronouns such as هُوَ, on little words such as مِن and فِيهِ (fī and the pronoun hi), or on some names, such as مُوسَىٰ. In the whole Quran, 27,462 of the 77,429 words (35%) have no root tag (Quran Learn’s count from the Quranic Arabic Corpus).

Three consonants in a row do not make a root. فِيهِ has three letters ف ي ه, but they are the word فِي and the pronoun هِ.

<Block id="mistake-lookup" title="Mistake 5: looking a word up under its first letter">

- Look in the order of the Arabic letters (step 2). Count ع before ك.

<Step id="practice" label="Practice" title="Practice">
<Block id="practice-1" title="Practice: peel, root and pattern">
  <Stage label="To find a root">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Peel</b> little prefixes, the article, pronoun endings and case endings.</li>
      <li><b>Peel</b> tense prefixes and doer endings if it is a verb.</li>
      <li><b>Find</b> the extra letters and undo any doubling.</li>
      <li><b>Pour back</b>: put the root into the pattern and see whether you get the word.</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

Work out each answer first, on paper or in your head. Then open the answer.

<Exercise n={1} q="Peel the word and name its root and pattern: ٱلْقُدُّوسُ (Al-Hashr 59:23). Meaning: “the Holy One”.">

Peel ٱلْ and the ending ـُ. Left: قُدُّوس.

The root is **ق د س**. The pattern is **فَعُّول**: the second letter د is doubled, and there is an extra long ū before the last letter. Al-Jadwal gives this pattern and calls the word a describing word from a root meaning “pure”.

Pour back: ق د س into فَعُّول gives قُدُّوس.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} q="Which of these words has the root ع ل م? (a) عَلَّمَ (Al-Alaq 96:5) (b) ٱلْقَلَمِ (Al-Alaq 96:4) (c) يَعْلَمْ (Al-Alaq 96:5) (d) عَلِيمٌ (At-Taghabun 64:11)">

- (a) **Yes.** The doubled ل is one root letter.
- (b) **No.** ٱلْقَلَمِ has the root ق ل م. After you peel ٱلْ and the ending you find ق ل م, which is a different root.
- (c) **Yes.** Peel the prefix يَ.
- (d) **Yes.** The pattern is فَعِيل.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} q="Peel each word, then give its root and its form. (a) فَسَبِّحْ (An-Nasr 110:3) (b) بِحَمْدِ (An-Nasr 110:3) (c) وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ (An-Nasr 110:3)">

- (a) Peel فَ. Left: سَبِّحْ. Root **س ب ح**. The doubled ب shows **Form II**, scale word فَعِّلْ.
- (b) Peel بِ and the ending ـِ. Left: حَمْد. Root **ح م د**. This is a plain noun with no extra letters, so the scale word is فَعْل.
- (c) Peel وَ and هُ. Left: ٱسْتَغْفِرْ. The extra letters ٱسْتَ show **Form X**, scale word ٱسْتَفْعِلْ. Root **غ ف ر**.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={4} q="Pattern practice: pour the root ح م د (praise) into three scale words: فَاعِل, مَفْعُول and فَعِيل. Write the three words.">

- فَاعِل gives **حَامِد**, one who praises. The Quran has its plural, ٱلْحَٰمِدُونَ (At-Tawba 9:112).
- مَفْعُول gives **مَحْمُود**, praised. The Quran has مَّحْمُودًا (Al-Isra 17:79).
- فَعِيل gives **حَمِيد**, worthy of praise. The Quran has حَمِيدٌ (Hud 11:73).

The root gives the idea of praise. Each pattern gives the word its job.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={5} q="A student says the root of مَّغْفِرَةً (Al-Baqarah 2:268) is م غ ف ر. What went wrong, and what is the right root?">

The student counted the first م. It is an extra letter of the pattern.

Peel the ending ـَةً (the feminine ـَة and tanwīn). Left: مَغْفِر. Take away the extra م. The root is **غ ف ر**. Pour back: غ ف ر into مَفْعِلَة gives مَغْفِرَة.

Al-Jadwal gives no pattern for this word. The scale word مَفْعِلَة is our own reading. The same root appears in غَافِرِ (40:3) and in ٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ (110:3).

</Exercise>
</Block>

  <Stage label="The kinds of root">
    <ol class="lx-card-list">
      <li><b>Sound:</b> three strong letters.</li>
      <li><b>Doubled:</b> the second and third letters are the same.</li>
      <li><b>Assimilated:</b> the first letter is و or ي.</li>
      <li><b>Hollow:</b> the middle letter is و or ي.</li>
      <li><b>Defective:</b> the last letter is و or ي.</li>
      <li><b>Hamzated:</b> one letter is a hamza.</li>
    </ol>
  </Stage>

<Exercise n={6} q="Put these roots in dictionary order: ك ت ب, ع ل م, ح م د, س ل م, ر ح م.">

The order is **ح م د, ر ح م, س ل م, ع ل م, ك ت ب**.

Look at the first letters in the order of the Arabic alphabet: ح comes before ر, then س, then ع, then ك.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={7} q="How many root letters does each word have? (a) ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ (An-Nas 114:4) (b) ٱلْخَنَّاسِ (An-Nas 114:4)">

- (a) **Four.** The root is و س و س. The pair و س repeats.
- (b) **Three.** The root is خ ن س. The doubled ن in the word comes from the pattern فَعَّال.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={8} q="Which of these verbs has a weak letter in its root? (a) قَالُوا۟ (Ya-Sin 36:52) (b) بَعَثَنَا (Ya-Sin 36:52) (c) وَعَدَ (Ya-Sin 36:52)">

- (a) **Yes.** The root is ق و ل: the middle letter is و, hidden in the long ā (a hollow root).
- (b) **No.** The root is ب ع ث, three strong letters (a sound root).
- (c) **Yes.** The root is و ع د: the first letter is و (an assimilated root).

L4.12 and L4.14 teach how these verbs behave.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={9} q="Name the pattern and the job of each word. (a) ٱلْفَتَّاحُ (Saba 34:26) (b) مَّرْقَدِنَا (Ya-Sin 36:52) (c) غَافِرِ (Ghafir 40:3)">

- (a) **فَعَّال**, an intensive form (al-Jadwal), root ف ت ح. It says the one who opens very much.
- (b) **مَفْعَل**, a noun of place (al-Jadwal), root ر ق د. It means the place of resting.
- (c) **فَاعِل**, a doer word (al-Jadwal), root غ ف ر. It means the one who forgives.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={10} q="Each word hides a root letter. Which letter, and where is it? (a) ٱلصِّيَامُ (Al-Baqarah 2:183) (b) يَلِدْ (Al-Ikhlas 112:3)">

- (a) The root is **ص و م**. The middle letter و shows as ي in ٱلصِّيَامُ. Al-Jadwal says the word began as ٱلصِّوَامُ.
- (b) The root is **و ل د**. The first letter و has dropped in يَلِدْ, as it did in يَعِدُكُمُ (2:268). The Corpus gives the root و ل د, and L1.11 met it in both يَلِدْ and يُولَدْ.

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={11} q="Walk through a dictionary lookup for وَيَأْمُرُكُم (Al-Baqarah 2:268, start). Meaning: “and he orders you”.">

1. **Peel** وَ, “and”. Left: يَأْمُرُكُم.
2. **Peel** كُم, “you all”. Left: يَأْمُرُ.
3. **Peel** the prefix يَ and the vowel ending ـُ. Left: أْمُر.
4. **Root:** أ م ر. The first letter is a hamza. The Corpus writes it as أ.
5. **Look up:** use the order of the letters. Find أ, then م, then ر.

The hamza is a root letter here, so the word is a hamzated verb. L4.15 teaches how such verbs behave.

</Exercise>

<Block id="done" title="You have finished the lesson">
  <Stage label="The idea">
    <Formula
      label="root plus pattern equals a word"
      items={[
        { kind: 'm', text: 'root', small: 'the idea: ع ل م' },
        { kind: 'k', text: 'pattern', small: 'the shape: فَعِيل' },
        { kind: 's', text: 'word', small: 'عَلِيمٌ, all-knowing' },
      ]}
    />
  </Stage>

- A word is a **root** plus a **pattern**. The root gives the idea. The pattern gives the shape and the job.
- The **scale ف ع ل** writes a pattern: ف, ع and ل stand for the three root letters. Any other letter in the scale word is an extra letter of the pattern.
- To find a root, **peel** the word from the outside in. Peel little prefixes, the article, pronoun endings, tense prefixes, doer endings, plural and case endings. Then find the extra letters and undo any doubling.
- **Pour the root back** into the pattern to test it. If you get the word back, the root is right.
- A dictionary lists words under their **roots**, in the order of the Arabic letters.
- A verb has one of ten **forms**, I to X. Form I is about 64% of the verb words in the Quran. L3.04 teaches it, and L3.12 to L3.18 teach the others.
- Noun patterns do jobs: the doer (L3.19), the one done to (L3.19), the action (L3.20) and the place (L3.21).
- Most roots have three letters. 40 of the 1,642 roots have four.
- A **weak** letter or a hamza can hide or change. If only two letters show after peeling, suspect a hidden letter. L4.11 to L4.15 teach these roots.

**Next:** L3.04 uses the scale to build the plain verb, Form I. After that you meet the tenses and the other forms one at a time.
