# Quranic Arabic Learning Roadmap

A clear, phased plan for going from "I can't read the Arabic alphabet" to "I can do a full grammatical breakdown of any Quranic verse." Built around the 80-lesson curriculum on this site, with realistic timelines for different study commitments.

This roadmap assumes **30 minutes a day, 5 days a week** as the baseline. Adjust the timelines proportionally for your own pace — and don't worry about hitting them exactly. Consistency beats speed.

## The five-level plan at a glance

<GrammarTable>
| Phase | Level | What you’ll learn | Lessons | Time at 30 min/day |
|-------|-------|-------------------|---------|---------------------|
| **Phase 1** | Level 1: Foundation | Alphabet, vowels, word types, case endings | 11 | 4–6 weeks |
| **Phase 2** | Level 2: Core Grammar | Sentence types, the three I’rab cases, idafa | 12 | 6–8 weeks |
| **Phase 3** | Level 3: Intermediate | Verb forms I–X, conjugations, participles | 22 | 10–12 weeks |
| **Phase 4** | Level 4: Advanced | Conditionals, weak verbs, balagha (rhetoric) | 19 | 8–10 weeks |
| **Phase 5** | Level 5: Applied Study | Full I’rab analysis, Quranic patterns, narratives | 16 | 8–10 weeks |
</GrammarTable>

**Total: 80 lessons, 36–46 weeks (roughly 9–11 months) at 30 min/day.**

If you can commit 60 min/day, halve the timeline. If you study 15 min/day, double it. The lessons themselves don't get harder when you go faster — the bottleneck is retention, which depends on review frequency, not session length.

## Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1–6)

**Goal:** read Arabic letters and short words. Recognize what I’rab markers look like.

What you'll do:

- Master the [Arabic alphabet](/learn/level-1/01-arabic-script-vowels/) — all 28 letters in their isolated, initial, medial, and final forms.
- Internalize the [reading marks](/learn/level-1/02-reading-marks/) — long vowels, sukūn, shadda, tanwīn.
- Read the [Bismillah](/learn/level-1/03-reading-bismillah/) word by word — your first complete Quranic phrase.
- Learn the [three word types](/learn/level-1/04-three-word-types/) — every Arabic word is either an *ism* (noun), *fiʿl* (verb), or *ḥarf* (particle).
- Understand the [root system](/learn/level-1/05-root-system-preview/) at a high level. You'll go deep in Phase 3.
- Get comfortable with [gender](/learn/level-1/07-gender-masculine-feminine/), [number](/learn/level-1/08-singular-dual-plural/), and [case endings](/learn/level-1/10-case-endings/).
- Finish with the [Al-Ikhlas checkpoint](/learn/level-1/11-checkpoint-al-ikhlas/) — your first complete short surah.

<Callout type="tip" title="Phase 1 success looks like">

By the end of Phase 1 you should be able to:
- Read any Arabic word slowly but accurately, including the diacritics
- Identify every letter in connected script
- Tell whether a word is a noun, verb, or particle
- Recognize the three case-ending markers (-u, -a, -i) when you see them

You won't *understand* much yet. That's fine. The goal is fluent recognition.

</Callout>

## Phase 2: Core Grammar (Weeks 7–14)

**Goal:** parse simple Arabic sentences. Identify subject, verb, object, and the role of each word.

What you'll do:

- Distinguish [nominal sentences](/learn/level-2/01-nominal-sentence/) from [verbal sentences](/learn/level-2/03-verbal-sentence/).
- Learn the [subject and predicate](/learn/level-2/02-subject-predicate/) of nominal sentences.
- Master the three I’rab cases — [Marfuʻ](/learn/level-2/04-nominative-case/), [Mansub](/learn/level-2/05-accusative-case/), and [Majrur](/learn/level-2/06-genitive-case/). The [I’rab cases hub](/learn/irab-cases/) ties them together.
- Internalize [prepositions and the genitive](/learn/level-2/07-prepositions-genitive/) — the most reliable I’rab trigger.
- Get comfortable with [idafa](/learn/level-2/08-possessive-idafah/) — possessive constructions are everywhere in the Quran.
- Learn [adjective agreement](/learn/level-2/09-adjective-agreement/) (gender, number, definiteness, case — all four must match).
- Understand [inna and her sisters](/learn/level-2/10-inna-sisters/) and [kāna and her sisters](/learn/level-2/11-kaana-sisters/) — these particles flip case assignments.
- Capstone with the [Al-Kawthar checkpoint](/learn/level-2/12-checkpoint-al-kawthar/).

<Callout type="tip" title="Phase 2 success looks like">

By the end of Phase 2 you should be able to:
- Identify the subject, predicate/object, and modifiers in a 5–10 word verse
- Explain *why* each word has the case ending it has
- Recognize an idafa chain at sight
- Predict how an inna or kāna will affect the words after it

This is where Quranic Arabic stops feeling like memorization and starts feeling like structure.

</Callout>

## Phase 3: Intermediate (Weeks 15–26)

**Goal:** parse any verb in the Quran. Recognize verb forms I–X on sight.

What you'll do:

- Master the full [root system](/learn/level-3/01-root-system/) — extracting roots reliably from any conjugated form.
- Walk through [Form I](/learn/level-3/02-verb-form-i/) in detail, then [past tense](/learn/level-3/03-past-tense/) and [present tense](/learn/level-3/04-present-tense/) conjugations.
- Add the [subjunctive and jussive moods](/learn/level-3/05-subjunctive-jussive/) and the [imperative](/learn/level-3/06-imperative/).
- Learn [passive voice](/learn/level-3/07-passive-voice/).
- Internalize the pronoun system — [subject](/learn/level-3/08-subject-pronouns/), [attached](/learn/level-3/09-attached-pronouns/), [demonstrative](/learn/level-3/10-demonstrative-pronouns/), [relative](/learn/level-3/11-relative-pronouns/).
- Master the derived forms — [II](/learn/level-3/12-verb-form-ii/), [III](/learn/level-3/13-verb-form-iii/), [IV](/learn/level-3/14-verb-form-iv/), [V](/learn/level-3/15-verb-form-v/), [VI](/learn/level-3/16-verb-form-vi/), [VII–VIII](/learn/level-3/17-verb-forms-vii-viii/), [IX–X](/learn/level-3/18-verb-forms-ix-x/). The [verb forms hub](/learn/verb-forms/) ties them together.
- Add the verb-derived nouns: [active and passive participles](/learn/level-3/19-active-passive-participles/) and [verbal nouns (maṣdar)](/learn/level-3/20-verbal-nouns/).
- Capstone with [Al-‘Asr](/learn/level-3/22-checkpoint-al-asr/).

<Callout type="tip" title="Phase 3 success looks like">

By the end of Phase 3 you should be able to:
- See a verb in the Quran and identify its root, form, tense, and person
- Recognize a participle as such (not mistake it for a verb)
- Understand what "form X means seeking" intuitively, without looking it up
- Read a short surah like Al-‘Asr or Al-Ikhlas with full grammatical comprehension

This is the longest phase. It's also the most rewarding — the Quran's verb-rich style starts opening up.

</Callout>

## Phase 4: Advanced (Weeks 27–36)

**Goal:** handle the rare and tricky structures — conditionals, weak verbs, the special accusatives.

What you'll do:

- Master the special accusatives: [ḥāl](/learn/level-4/01-hal-clauses/), [tamyīz](/learn/level-4/02-tamyiz/), [mafʿūl muṭlaq](/learn/level-4/07-maf-ul-mutlaq/), [mafʿūl li-ajlih](/learn/level-4/08-maf-ul-li-ajlih/), [mafʿūl maʿah](/learn/level-4/09-maf-ul-ma-ah/).
- Walk through [conditional sentences](/learn/level-4/03-conditional-sentences/) and [conditional particles](/learn/level-4/04-conditional-particles/) — the Quran is dense with conditionals.
- Learn [exception particles](/learn/level-4/05-exception-particles/) (illā, ghayr) and [emphasis/affirmation](/learn/level-4/06-emphasis-affirmation/).
- Add [negation particles](/learn/level-4/10-negation-particles/) — Arabic has many ways to say "not".
- Master [weak verbs](/learn/level-4/11-weak-verbs-intro/) — [hollow](/learn/level-4/12-hollow-verbs/), [defective](/learn/level-4/13-defective-verbs/), [assimilated](/learn/level-4/14-assimilated-verbs/), and [hamzated](/learn/level-4/15-hamzated-verbs/) verbs all behave irregularly.
- Add [numbers and counting](/learn/level-4/16-numbers-counting/) — number agreement is its own micro-system.
- Get an [intro to balagha (rhetoric)](/learn/level-4/17-intro-balagha/) and [figures of speech](/learn/level-4/18-figures-of-speech/).
- Capstone with [Ad-Duha](/learn/level-4/19-checkpoint-ad-duha/).

<Callout type="tip" title="Phase 4 success looks like">

By the end of Phase 4 you should be able to:
- Parse any verb, including weak roots and irregular conjugations
- Recognize a conditional structure and predict what mood the result clause will use
- See a fronted object and explain *why* it's fronted (emphasis vs. exclusivity vs. specificity)
- Read advanced surahs of Juz Amma with full I’rab

This is the phase where you stop being surprised by the Quran's grammar.

</Callout>

## Phase 5: Applied Study (Weeks 37–46)

**Goal:** independently produce a full grammatical breakdown of any verse.

What you'll do:

- Master the technique of [full I’rab analysis](/learn/level-5/01-full-irab-analysis/).
- Walk through complete surah analyses — [Al-Fatiha](/learn/level-5/02-surah-al-fatiha/), [Ayat al-Kursi](/learn/level-5/03-ayat-al-kursi/), [Al-Ikhlas](/learn/level-5/04-surah-al-ikhlas/), [Al-Falaq and An-Nas](/learn/level-5/05-surah-al-falaq-an-nas/).
- Identify [Juz Amma patterns](/learn/level-5/06-juz-amma-patterns/), [duʿāʾ patterns](/learn/level-5/07-dua-patterns/), and [oath formulas](/learn/level-5/08-oath-formulas/).
- Study extended narratives — [Prophet Ibrahim](/learn/level-5/09-prophet-ibrahim-narrative/) and [Prophet Musa](/learn/level-5/10-prophet-musa-narrative/).
- Get comfortable with [dialogue patterns](/learn/level-5/11-dialogue-patterns/), [parallelism and repetition](/learn/level-5/12-parallelism-repetition/), [rhetorical questions](/learn/level-5/13-rhetorical-questions/), and [word order for emphasis](/learn/level-5/14-word-order-emphasis/).
- Synthesize everything in [Nahw synthesis](/learn/level-5/15-nahw-synthesis/) and [Sarf synthesis](/learn/level-5/16-sarf-synthesis/).

<Callout type="rule" title="Phase 5 success looks like">

By the end of Phase 5 you should be able to:
- Pick any verse, parse every word, and explain its grammatical role
- See a rhetorical structure (iltifāt, fronting, parallelism) and explain its effect
- Read tafsir works that discuss grammar without losing the thread
- Continue learning independently — you have the tools

The journey doesn't end here. Quranic Arabic is a lifetime study. But by the end of Level 5, you have the structural fluency to keep going on your own.

</Callout>

## Adjusting the timeline

The 9–11 month estimate assumes 30 minutes a day, 5 days a week, with steady review. Real learners adjust:

<GrammarTable>
| Daily commitment | Total time | Honest expectation |
|------------------|-----------|--------------------|
| 15 min/day | 18–22 months | Very sustainable. Slow but real progress. |
| 30 min/day | 9–11 months | The recommended baseline. |
| 60 min/day | 5–6 months | Aggressive but doable. Watch for burnout. |
| 2 hours/day | 3–4 months | Possible only with a non-school schedule. |
</GrammarTable>

The biggest predictor of finishing isn't how fast you go — it's whether you keep going. Skipping a day is fine. Skipping a month is when momentum dies.

## Three habits that compound

1. **Daily Quran reading**, even 1–2 verses, looking up grammar as you go. This forces application from week 1.
2. **A vocabulary spaced-repetition system** (Anki, Memrise) for the [200 most-used words](/resources/200-most-used-words/) — they cover ~70% of Quranic word occurrences.
3. **A weekly review session** where you re-read past lessons and re-parse a verse. Without review, retention drops sharply after 2–3 weeks.

## When to start

The best time to start was a year ago. The second-best time is today. Begin with [Lesson 1.01: Arabic Script &amp; Vowels](/learn/level-1/01-arabic-script-vowels/) and don't worry about the finish line.

If you've already started and want to know where you are, the [progress tracker](/learn/) on each level page will show what you've completed.

If you're serious enough about the Quran to read this far, you're serious enough to finish.
