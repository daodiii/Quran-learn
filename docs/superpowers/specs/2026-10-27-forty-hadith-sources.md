## Arabic text

- [Arabic Wikisource](https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/الأربعون_النووية) has the full text,
  but with essentially no vowel marks. It is good for checking words, not for display.
- Abu Amina Elias's pages show hadith 1 with full tashkeel
  ("إِنَّمَا الْأَعْمَالُ بِالنِّيَّاتِ"), but that site is under copyright as a whole.
- The classical Arabic itself (13th century) is not under copyright. Vowel marks added by
  a modern editor can be, so the tashkeel needs care.

**Result: no single free source with full tashkeel confirmed.** The workable approach is
to take the unvowelled text from Wikisource and add the tashkeel myself, checking each
hadith against two printed or online vocalised editions for disagreements.

## Options for the English, and my recommendation

1. **Write my own translation from the Arabic**, checking meaning against the editions
   above. It will be close in meaning, but the sentences are mine. No permission needed.
   *Recommended.*
2. **Ask Abu Amina Elias for permission** to use his translation with credit. He is the
   most likely to say yes, because he publishes it free. Slower, and it depends on his
   answer.
3. **Follow a published edition closely** (what you asked for on 2026-10-27). This is the
   one with the legal risk: close paraphrase of a copyrighted translation is still a copy,
   and the PDF would spread it further.

Option 1 and option 2 can run together: I start on my own translation now, and if he
agrees, we can align to his wording later.

## How the Arabic is checked (added during the first batch)

- **Wording** is checked against Arabic Wikisource, which carries Nawawi's own text. A
  script strips vowel marks and confirms every phrase of each page appears in it. The few
  misses are Wikisource typos (for example "أ تدري", "أحد كم", "مالا") and the blessing
  written as ﷺ.
- **Vowel marks** are cross-checked word by word against Abu Amina Elias's page wherever
  the same word appears. His page is **not** a faithful copy of Nawawi's text: his hadith 8
  is Jabir's wording, not Ibn Umar's. So it is used for vowels only, never for wording.
- A model summary of a web page is not a source for "verbatim" Arabic. The raw page was
  downloaded and read directly instead.
