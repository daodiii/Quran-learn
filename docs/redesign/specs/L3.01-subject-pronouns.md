# Content spec: L3.01 Subject Pronouns

Written 2026-10-28. This lesson was L3.08 on `main` (`08-subject-pronouns.mdx`). It is now the first lesson of Level 3, before any verb is taught (past tense is L3.05, present L3.06). The lesson file is `src/content/lessons/level-3/01-subject-pronouns.mdx`. It follows the definition of a complete lesson at the end of `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Name the twelve detached pronouns, say who each one is (person, number, gender) and read them in a verse.
2. Tell a detached pronoun from an attached one (L2.07, L3.02) and from a hidden doer (L2.03).
3. Say why a pronoun is a noun (L1.09) and definite (L1.06), so a sentence that starts with one is nominal.
4. Say why a pronoun shows no case ending: it is built (mabnī) and stands "in the place of" the nominative (the promise of L1.10 and L2.01).
5. Find a pronoun that is the mubtadaʾ, one that repeats a hidden doer (tawkīd), and one that follows inna.
6. Go through four verses word by word, with the pronoun, its role, and an `irab` line.
7. Recognise, as a preview, that a verb carries the same pronouns as endings (taught in L3.05, L3.06, L3.08) and that a second set of detached pronouns, the words that start with إِيَّا, exists.

Points that needed a source or a decision:
- **Why the twelve are called ḍamāʾir al-rafʿ.** The lesson says the name means "pronouns of the nominative" and "fits, because each one takes the place of a nominative word". This comes from the old lesson and general grammar knowledge. It is not quoted from a book. Al-Jadwal's own words (ḍamīr munfaṣil mabnī fī maḥall rafʿ) are used for the reading of the pronoun. **Needs a source if the owner wants the origin of the name stated.**
- **The reason for the joining ḍamma in هُمُ and أَنتُمُ** (step 1, `common`; exercise 8): "a joining vowel, placed before a word that starts with ٱلْ". The Corpus tags show the forms humu (82) and antumu (5). The reason is standard grammar but not quoted from the approved sources. The lesson does not say why the vowel is needed.
- **The statement that the "iyyā" pronoun is for an object put first.** Al-Jadwal says: "detached pronoun in the place of the accusative, a fronted object". The lesson does not add the reason an attached pronoun cannot be put first.
- **A pronoun counts as a noun and is definite.** L1.09 and L1.06 teach these (al-Ajurrumiyyah's five definite nouns start with the pronoun). The lesson repeats them with those links.

## Measured counts (step 1, `common`)

Method: a script in a temporary folder read `src/data/morphology/quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt`. A word counts as a detached pronoun when its only STEM row is tagged PRON and it has no `l:P+` or `bi+` prefix (those are a pronoun joined after li- or bi-). Words with a stem and a suffix (a verb or noun with an attached pronoun) have two or more rows and are not counted. The script was deleted afterwards.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. **"Required after particles"** (إِنَّهُ, لَٰكِنَّهُمْ, كَأَنَّهَا): those are attached pronouns, not independent ones. Now `after-inna` and `mistake-attached`.
2. **A contradiction:** the lesson said pronouns appear "for emphasis, not necessity", then said they are required in nominal sentences. Now: a pronoun is the mubtadaʾ in a nominal sentence; beside a verb it adds stress.
3. **Observation 4** listed أَنْتَ, أَنْتِ and the rest under "First person starts with أَ".
4. **"Subject pronouns take nominative case."** A pronoun shows no ending; it is built and stands in the place of the nominative (L1.10, L2.01 promise).
5. **"Without هُوَ there would be no subject"** and "HE (and no one else)" for 112:1: unsourced, and the reading of هُوَ there is disputed (L1.11).
6. **The "royal we" explanation** (majestic plural for grandeur) was unsourced. The lesson says only that the speaker in 15:9 is Allah and that a plural pronoun does not always mean several speakers.
7. **Example 4 (107:1)** was labelled "second person pronoun after a particle". It has no written pronoun.
8. **The second أَنَا in 20:14** was called "required grammatically after exceptive constructions" with no source.
