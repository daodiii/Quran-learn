# Content spec: L3.04 The Basic Verb: Form I

Written 2026-10-28. This says what the rewritten L3.04 teaches, with which verses, and how each verse is parsed. It follows the model spec (`L2.01-nominal-sentence.md`) and the definition of a complete lesson (end of `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`). The lesson is `src/content/lessons/level-3/04-verb-form-i.mdx` (old file on `main`: `02-verb-form-i.mdx`, old number L3.02).

L3.04 is now fourth in Level 3, after the root system (L3.03). The full past-tense endings are L3.05 and the present tense is L3.06, so this lesson teaches only the "he" form, the present vowel as a pattern to recognise, meanings, and how to spot Form I.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say what Form I is (the bare verb: root letters and vowels, nothing added) and that it is 64% of the verbs in the Quran.
2. Read فَعَلَ as a pattern: ف, ع, ل stand for the first, second and third root letters.
3. Read the three vowel patterns of the "he" past form: faʿala, faʿila, faʿula.
4. Read the present "he" form as ya- plus a middle vowel (yafʿulu, yafʿilu, yafʿalu), know the past and present vowels go in pairs, and know the six "doors" (abwāb) that al-Jadwal uses.
5. Spot Form I in a verse: take off prefixes and endings, find the root, look for a shadda, a long ā or an extra letter; read the Corpus (no form number means Form I).
6. Avoid the traps: an opening أَ can be a root letter (أَكَلَ); a long ā can stand for a weak letter (قَالَ); سْتَ can belong partly to the root (نَسْتَبِقُ); ya- is the tense prefix, not an extra letter.
7. Go through four verses word by word: a faʿala, a faʿila and a faʿula past verb, and a present verb.

## The lesson, step by step

5 steps, 30 blocks (5, 7, 5, 10, 3; the first block of step 1 is the uncounted overview). 11 exercises, 12 quick checks, 4 `Parse` panels.

**Step 1: What Form I is** (`start`)
- `overview` (uncounted): Formula root + pattern = Form I verb; `Start` with six terms.
- `see`: 6:54 (part) as a `Sentence`; the verb has three letters and nothing added. Check 1.
- `bare`: Form I is the bare verb (mujarrad) and the others are mazīd; al-Jadwal's use of "mujarrad" (1:6); 64% (12,356 of the verbs; Form IV 3,487); note on four-letter verbs (20:120).
- `pattern`: فَعَلَ as a ruler; table of five roots; al-Jadwal's "wazn-hu" (4:69 رَفِيقًا فعيل). Check 2.
- `meaning`: plain meaning of the root; action, state, quality; Form II "taught", Form IV "make it happen", Form X "ask for".

**Step 2: Three vowel patterns, past and present** (`patterns`)
- `three`: faʿala, faʿila, faʿula; the first and last vowel are fatḥa; no prediction from the root letters; endings go to L3.05. Check 3.
- `verses`: 55:3, 58:1 (start), 4:69 (end).
- `first-book`: al-Ajurrumiyyah on the three kinds of verb, its own example ضَرَبَ يَضْرِبُ اضْرِبْ, the four tense letters أَنَيْتُ.
- `present`: ya- plus a middle vowel; three pairs as `Tiles`; 58:1 (part) shows يَسْمَعُ. Check 4.
- `doors`: the six doors and al-Jadwal's own uses of the names (2:214, 2:60, 3:123, 4:69). Check 5.
- `counts`: Quran Learn's count of regular verbs (123), the past vowel (89, 29, 5) and the present vowel (70 verbs); meaning hints (damma verbs name a quality); throat-letter tip. Check 6.
- `matters`: 2:60 مَشْرَب, the present vowel decides the noun of place (al-Jadwal).

**Step 3: How to spot Form I in a verse** (`spot`)
- `method`: three steps on 12:17 (ذَهَبْنَا, نَسْتَبِقُ). Check 7.
- `other-forms`: 2:22 (Form I next to Form IV); table of what Forms II to X add; Form IX rare (five times).
- `same-root`: عَلِمَ and عَلَّمَ, 55:4; 2:102 has the root in three forms. Check 8.
- `corpus`: no bracket means Form I; table of four Corpus lines (ذَهَبْنَا, نَسْتَبِقُ, عَلَّمَهُ, أَنزِلْنِى). Check 9.
- `weak`: قَالَ, كَانَ, جَآءَ are Form I but change shape; 1,618 and 1,358 (curriculum review); L3.05, L4.11.

**Step 4: Six mistakes, and four verses word by word** (`word-by-word`)
- `mistake-three` (shadda; Check 10), `mistake-hamza` (12:17 end; Check 11), `mistake-vowel`, `mistake-command` (2:60 part), `mistake-long` (Check 12), `mistake-prefix` (نَعْبُدُ, نَسْتَعِينُ, نَسْتَبِقُ).
- `parse-1` 6:54, `parse-2` 2:60, `parse-3` 4:69 (with the al-Durr note), `parse-4` 14:25.

**Step 5: Practice** (`practice`)
- `practice-1`: Ex 1 to 6. `practice-2`: Ex 7 to 11. `done`: summary, next lessons, `Sources`.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. **Mistake 4 contradicts itself.** The Arabic is اِكْتُبْ with a kasra on the hamza, but the transliteration is "uktub", and the text gives the Form I command as اِفْعِلْ only. A command from yaktubu is اُكْتُبْ; the vowel of the connecting alif follows the present vowel. The new lesson names the opening ٱ as a connecting alif (hamzat al-waṣl) and does not give a pattern; L3.08 teaches it. *Certain.*
2. **"Remove the أَ: if the rest makes sense, it is likely Form IV"** is not a test, and "an opening أَ means Form IV" is wrong for Form I verbs whose root starts with a hamza (أَكَلَ, أَخَذَ). The new test counts the letters left. *Certain* (the Corpus tags أَكَلَ and أَخَذَ with no form number).
3. **"Form I has exactly 3 consonants"** ignores four-letter Form I verbs such as وَسْوَسَ (20:120). *Checked* in the Corpus (no form tag).
4. **The checklist reads an opening اِسْتَ / سْتَ as Form X** without looking at the root. In نَسْتَبِقُ (12:17) the س is a root letter, and the Corpus tags it (VIII). *Checked.*
5. **"No long vowel between the first and second root letters"** is given as a Form I test, but قَالَ, كَانَ and جَاءَ (Form I) have a long ā there. Only the last exercise of the old lesson mentioned weak verbs. The new lesson says it in `weak` and `mistake-long`. *Certain.*
6. **Example 4 shows no verb.** The verse (1:2) has no Form I verb, so the "Form I verification" is run on حَمِدَ, which is not in the verse, to explain the noun ٱلْحَمْدُ. A weak example rather than an error. Replaced, and the noun is left for L3.20 (verbal nouns).
7. **"Fatḥa is the most common class, kasra second, ḍamma least"** had no source. Now measured: 89, 29 and 5 regular verbs (method below).
8. **The jāʾa exercise** says the root is "ج-ي-أ or ج-و-أ". The Corpus gives ج ي أ. The new answer says the middle letter is weak and the last is a hamza.
9. **Links** named lessons by the old numbers (L3.03 Past Tense, L3.04 Present Tense, L3.06 Imperative, L3.13 Form III, L3.14 Form IV). They were right before the reorder and are stale now. The new lesson uses the new numbers and addresses.
10. **Example 2 quoted 55:1-2** as one example with ٱلْقُرْآنَ; the Tanzil text is ٱلْقُرْءَانَ. The new lesson uses 55:3 and 55:4. *Checked.*

## Counts and how they were measured

Script run on `src/data/morphology/quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt` (scratch script, deleted after use). A Form I verb is a `V STEM` word with a ROOT and no bracketed form tag. A **regular** verb has a three-letter root with none of w, y, alif or hamza (Buckwalter `w y A > < & } '`) and no doubled letter, and its lemma has the shape C a C V C a that matches the root. The middle vowel V of the lemma is the past vowel: 123 verbs, fatḥa 89, kasra 29, ḍamma 5. The **present vowel** comes from a token of the same lemma that matches `ya C1 o C2 V C3 u` (a `ya-` form): 70 of the 123 verbs have one. fatḥa verbs: 52 (present fatḥa 10, kasra 17, ḍamma 25); kasra verbs: 17 (all fatḥa); ḍamma verbs: 1 (كَبُرَ, يَكْبُرُ in 17:51; the Corpus also files يَكْبَرُوا in 4:6 under the same lemma, so the lesson gives no table row for it). All 10 fatḥa-present verbs among the fatḥa verbs have a ع, ح, غ, خ or ه as second or third root letter; 14 fatḥa verbs have one, and the other four (دَخَلَ, بَلَغَ, زَعَمَ, رَجَعَ) take ḍamma or kasra. The five ḍamma verbs: كَبُرَ, كَثُرَ, ضَعُفَ, حَسُنَ, خَبُثَ.

Counts quoted from `docs/curriculum/CURRICULUM-REVIEW.md`: Form I 12,356 (64% of verbs), Form IV 3,487, Form IX five times; قَالَ 1,618 words and كَانَ 1,358 words.
