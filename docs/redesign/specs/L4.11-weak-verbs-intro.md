# Content spec: L4.11 Weak Verbs Introduction

Written 2026-10-28. This is the old L4.11 "Weak Verbs Introduction (al-Fi'l al-Mu'tall)", kept at the same number. It follows the definition of a complete lesson (end of `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`). The lesson is `src/content/lessons/level-4/11-weak-verbs-intro.mdx`. It opens the weak-verb run L4.11 to L4.15 and gives the map; L4.12 to L4.15 teach each kind.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Name the six kinds of verb (sound, hollow, defective, assimilated, hamzated, doubled), with the Arabic names, and say where the special letter sits in the root.
2. Say what the weak letters are (و, ي and the long ā), that a root is written only with و or ي, and that the hamza and a repeated letter are not weak but change shape too.
3. Peel a verb in four steps (take off what was added, count the root letters, find the missing letter in the Corpus, name the kind), and use six clues in the word to suspect a kind.
4. Say that a root can have two kinds (جَآءَ, ءَاتَى, تَوَلَّىٰ) and that the kind belongs to the root, in every verb form and participle.
5. Name the three changes of a weak letter (turns into another letter, gets a sukūn, drops out) and match each to a verse word, with al-Jadwal's note.
6. Tell merged from apart in a doubled verb (مَسَّ, يَمْسَسْكُمْ).
7. Name the kind of each of the five short commands of L3.08.
8. Read four verses word by word, with the kind and the change of each verb.

## The lesson, step by step

5 steps, 27 blocks, 15 `Check`s, 11 exercises, 4 `Parse` panels (4, 8, 5 and 8 cards).

**Step 1: The map** (hero; 5 blocks)
1. `overview`: Start, formula "root, kind, what you see".
2. `see`: 3:47, ten verbs, nine not sound; list of the shapes. Check 1.
3. `weak`: كَتَبَ and قَالَ in four forms each; the three weak letters; the hamza. Check 2.
4. `six`: table of the six kinds with Arabic names and an example each; weak verbs and the other two. Check 3.
5. `count`: Corpus counts and the method; the 945 against 872; the weak-letter and two-kind counts. Check 4.

**Step 2: Root and kind** (5 blocks)
6. `peel`: the four steps on ٱهْدِنَا (1:6). Check 5.
7. `clues`: six clues table. Check 6.
8. `assim`: assimilated, sound in the past (56:1), short in the present (يَعِدُ, يَجِدْ). Check 7.
9. `two`: 9:18 with four verbs; two kinds; lafīf; table of six roots with counts. Check 8.
10. `forms`: the kind belongs to the root; ق و م in six words; a table of earlier-lesson words. Check 9.

**Step 3: What changes** (6 blocks)
11. `three`: the three changes with al-Jadwal's terms (table). Check 10.
12. `turn`: قَضَىٰٓ, قِيلَ, ٱتَّقَىٰ; qāla applied by analogy; the ā-spelling tip. Check 11.
13. `sukun`: نَسْتَعِينُ in three steps; يَقُولُ by the same account. Check 12.
14. `drop`: 2:217; command, jazm, heavy ending; table of plain form and result. Check 13.
15. `doubled`: merged and apart; رَبِّكَ; the Form II warning. Check 14.
16. `five`: the five commands by kind; no كُلْ in the Quran. Check 15.

**Step 4: Word by word, and mistakes** (8 blocks)
17. to 20. `parse-1` to `parse-4`: 1:5, 3:47 (end), 2:268 (part), 3:140 (part).
21. `mistake-root`: reading the root off the word. 22. `mistake-aa`: every long ā is weak (قَاتَلَ). 23. `mistake-kinds`: hamza and doubled are not weak; a root can have two kinds. 24. `mistake-always`: a rule with "always" (15:53).

**Step 5: Practice** (3 blocks)
25. `practice-1`: exercises 1 to 5. 26. `practice-2`: exercises 6 to 11. 27. `done`: summary, next, links, Sources.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. **Four categories.** The old lesson teaches four kinds and leaves out the doubled verb, which L3.03 promised to L4.11. The new lesson has six kinds, with the sound verb as the baseline.
2. **ٱهْدِنَا.** The old lesson says the command ٱهْدِ shows the ي and that the ي "drops before pronoun suffixes". The command ٱهْدِ has no ي, and al-Jadwal says the verb is built by dropping the weak letter (origin تَهْدِينَا in the present). `peel` says so.
3. **The alif as a root letter.** The old lesson lists "ا" as one of the weak letters of the root and in the step "check if the root contains و، ي، ا، or ء". In a root the weak letters are و and ي (the Corpus writes only these); the alif stands for one of them. `weak` and `mistake-root` say so.
4. **Hamzated: "otherwise regular, only the spelling changes".** Not so: the hamza of أَكَلَ and أَخَذَ drops in the command (كُلْ, خُذْ, L3.08). The new lesson says hamzated verbs also change shape, mostly in the spelling, and sends the rules to L4.15.
5. **Assimilated: "the initial و drops in the imperative and the present (يَجِدُ not يَوْجِدُ). Otherwise fairly regular".** Too absolute: تَوْجَلْ (15:53, root و ج ل) keeps its و. `mistake-always` says so; the rule goes to L4.14.
6. **Exercise 3 quotes two phrases that are not in the Quran:** وَجَدَ ٱللَّهَ غَفُوُرًا رَّحِيمًا (the verse has يَجِدِ ٱللَّهَ غَفُورًا رَّحِيمًا, 4:110) and كَانَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَِيمًا حَكَِيمًا (with stray vowel marks; the verse reads وَكَانَ ٱللَّهُ عَلِيمًا حَكِيمًا, 4:17). The new exercise 3 quotes the verses.
7. **Exercise 4: "Al-Fatiha contains at least 3 weak verbs".** The Corpus tags four verbs in 1:1 to 1:7: نَعْبُدُ and أَنْعَمْتَ are sound; نَسْتَعِينُ and ٱهْدِنَا are weak. Two, not three (the old answer key also listed two). Exercise 4 says so and treats ٱلضَّآلِّينَ as a participle from a doubled root.
8. **ٱُكْتُبْ** in the old text has a stray ḍamma on the helping alif. The new lesson writes ٱكْتُبْ.
9. **Weak-letter list in the hook ("waw و, ya ي, or alif ا/hamza ء").** Mixed the hamza in with the alif as if they were one letter. `weak` separates them.
10. **جَاءَ and the recognition table** list جَاءَ as hollow only (root ج-ي-ء). It is hollow and hamzated. `two` and `mistake-kinds` say so.
11. **"This is the most challenging topic in Arabic verb morphology"** and "exponentially easier": inflated claims, dropped.
12. **"The final ي drops ... when we add نَا"** also appears in the Step 4 line of the worked example ("the ي drops: ٱِهْدِ + نَا"), with the stray kasra under the alif (ٱِهْدِ). Replaced by the corrected steps.

## What is not checked

- The English meanings under the verses and the Arabic parse lines are unreviewed by an Arabic speaker.
- **The six kinds, the six clues, the 4 steps and the tip about the written ā** are a teaching summary. No approved book states the weak-verb rules as such. The lesson says so in two notes and in `Sources`. The Arabic names of the kinds are the classical ones; they were not confirmed against a named approved source.
- **The three changes** are named from al-Jadwal's own notes (1:5, 2:217, 3:47, 1:6, 2:268, 2:21, 2:11). Where the lesson applies a rule to a verb the book does not discuss, it says so: قَالَ (و to ā), يَقُولُ (the vowel moves back), كُن, the doubled verbs' apart/merged rule (the online copy has no note on يَمْسَسْ or يَرْتَدِدْ).
- "The ā is never a root letter; the Corpus writes only و and ي for the weak letters": checked in the Corpus (the 943 verb roots use w and y for the weak letters, and the letter A for the hamza, as in جَآءَ = jyA). One exception to the reading of A as a hamza: هَاتُوا۟ (root hAt, 4 words) has an alif in its root as the Corpus writes it. The counts treat it as hamzated by the letter A, so hamzated is 4 words too high if هَاتُوا۟ is read differently. Not mentioned in the lesson.
- Corpus tags are not reliable for every word; the root of نَادَىٰ and تَرَاضٍ is written with و by the Corpus. Counts follow the Corpus tags.
- 56:1: sentence level only in al-Jadwal.
- The claim "no كُلْ in the Quran" comes from a search of `ROOT:Akl` with `IMPV` in the Corpus (2D, 2MP and 2FS only).
