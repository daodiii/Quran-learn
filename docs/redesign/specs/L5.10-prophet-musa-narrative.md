## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say, for each speech in 20:9 to 20:71 and 28:7 to 28:12, who speaks and which clue shows it (a written name, a called name, the ending of قَالَ, a question and its answer, the pronoun in the words said, what the words say).
2. Read the shape "قَالَ + what is said" and the shape "a command or ban + a reason sentence" (al-Jadwal's taʿlīliyya), with the فَ that al-Jadwal reads as the link of an answer to a left-out condition (20:12, 20:49).
3. Read a question and its answer (20:17 and 20:18, 20:49 and 20:50), including an answer that says more than was asked (iṭnāb, al-Jadwal's name).
4. Tell a verb with one object (أَجِدُ, 20:10) from a verb with two (أَعْطَىٰ, أَرَيْنَٰهُ, نَتَّخِذَهُۥ), and read the passive of a two-object verb (أُوتِيتَ سُؤْلَكَ).
5. Name the words al-Jadwal says are left out: a condition, a preposition, objects, a mubtadaʾ, a khabar, a sentence, an answer; and not write them into the verse.
6. Read the feminine endings of 28:7 to 28:12 (-at, -ti, -ī, -ki), and the dual and plural قَالَا and قَالُوا.
7. Say what the lesson does not claim: no purpose for a passive or a choice of preposition; no names for people who are not named.

## What is not checked

- **Who speaks.** Al-Jadwal parses the words and does not name speakers, except the hidden doer of قَالَتْ in 28:12 (the sister of Musa, al-Jadwal) and the written doer in 28:9. Every other "who" (Allah in 20:19, 20:21, 20:36 and 20:46; Musa; Pharaoh in 20:49 and 20:71; the mother in 28:11; the sister in 28:11 for بَصُرَتْ; Musa and Harun in 20:45) is our reading of the story, from the surrounding verses. The lesson says so in `speakers`, `v36`, `v45`, `v71` and `Sources`. The counts "four Allah, four Musa, one Pharaoh" depend on it.
- **Word-level lines that al-Jadwal does not give on its page.** The standard parse is used for: the verbs رَءَا, قَالَ, ءَانَسْتُ, ٱخْلَعْ, أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟, أَعْطَىٰ, هَدَىٰ, أُلْقِىَ, قَالُوا, ءَامَنَّا, قَالَتْ, تَقْتُلُوهُ, أَدُلُّكُمْ, يَكْفُلُونَهُ; the nouns نَارًا, ٱلْمُقَدَّسِ, كُلَّ, شَىْءٍ, سُجَّدًا, نَٰصِحُونَ; the mood or mark of a verb (built on a sukūn or ḍamma). The Arabic formula on each card is a paraphrase.
