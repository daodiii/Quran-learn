# Content spec: L5.11 Dialogue Patterns in the Quran

Written 2026-10-28. Old lesson: `git show main:src/content/lessons/level-5/11-dialogue-patterns.mdx`. New lesson: `src/content/lessons/level-5/11-dialogue-patterns.mdx`. Level 5 rules: `docs/redesign/LEVEL-5-NOTES.md`. The old lesson sorted the Quran's speech into four "types" and quoted about 30 verses. That sorting is the old author's, not a source's. This lesson keeps it only as a teaching summary, sorted by the opening word, and teaches the grammar of speech that the verses and al-Jadwal support: the verb قَالَ and its doer, the quoted words as "what is said" (in the place of accusative), the command قُلْ, the call (يَا, يَٰٓأَيُّهَا), who answers whom, and the same verbs on the Last Day.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Find the verb that opens a speech (قَالَ, قُلْ, يَقُولُ, or another such as نَادَىٰ) and say where its doer is: in the verb's ending, written after it, or a hidden pronoun.
2. Mark where the quoted words start and end, and say that al-Jadwal puts them in the place of accusative ("what is said", مَقُول الْقَوْل) and that the sentence of the verb has no place.
3. Read قُلْ as a command with a hidden "you", and name what can follow it (a statement, a question, a condition), without giving the verse a purpose that the grammar does not state.
4. Read a call (يَا and a noun; يَٰٓأَيُّهَا and the noun after it; a call with the call word left out), and use it and a pronoun "you" to say who is spoken to.
5. See a change of speaker in a new verb with a new doer (7:59 to 7:61, 19:19 to 19:21, 10:31).
6. Read speech on the Last Day with the same grammar, and say what marks it as the Last Day (a time word), not the tense.
7. Say what the counts show (1,321 verses with a form of قَالَ, 332 words of قُلْ, 143 words of يَٰٓأَيُّهَا) and what they do not.
8. Go through four verses word by word.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

The old L5.11 is not in `CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`, so these were found by reading it against the Corpus (Tanzil text), al-Jadwal and the lesson files. 14 errors.

1. "Over 40% of Quranic verses contain dialogue". No source. The Corpus has a form of قَالَ in 1,321 of 6,236 verses (21%); other speech verbs exist, so the true share is not known (`count`).
2. Ash-Shuʿara **26:49** is quoted as قَالَ فِرْعَوْنُ ءَامَنتُم بِهِۦ قَبْلَ أَنْ ءَاذَنَ لَكُمْ and used for a "written doer". Those words are 7:123. 26:49 reads قَالَ ءَامَنتُمْ لَهُۥ قَبْلَ أَنْ ءَاذَنَ لَكُمْ …, with no doer written (`mistake-doer`, Exercise 5).
3. Yunus **10:31** is quoted as قُلْ مَن يَرْزُقُكُم مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ. The verse has مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ. The old words are the start of 34:24 (`qul-turns` uses the real text).
4. "The Quran often uses past tense for future eschatological events to convey certainty", with the term الْمَاضِي بِمَعْنَى ٱلْمُسْتَقْبَلِ and "treats the future as a done deal". No source in the project states it, and al-Jadwal's pages for 33:67, 41:29 and 75:10 do not. Dropped (`lastday`).
5. **89:15** (فَيَقُولُ رَبِّىٓ أَكْرَمَنِ) is filed under the Last Day as "Allah's response". The speaker is the man, and no time word puts it on the Last Day (`mistake-day`).
6. **25:30** is quoted as وَيَقُولُ ٱلرَّسُولُ and described as "the Prophet's complaint on the Day of Judgment". The Tanzil text is وَقَالَ ٱلرَّسُولُ, and the verse names no day (`mistake-day`).
7. "قُلْ verses are ALWAYS Allah's words to the Prophet", and the "functions" (declaration, response to objections, worship instructions). The Corpus tags قُلْ as "you, one man", and al-Jadwal says only that the doer is "you". The lesson does not say "always" and drops the functions (`qul`, `qul-after`).
8. "يَٰٓأَيُّهَا appears 154 times". The Corpus has 143 words. "Al-Hujurat contains 5": 5 with ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟, and 6 with 49:13 (`ayyuha`).
9. "Prophetic address", with the speaker given as "Prophet/Allah" and the audience as believers or humanity. A call has no verb of saying, so the verse does not mark a speaker. The label has no source and is dropped (`ayyuha`, `mistake-types`).
10. The structure of يَٰٓأَيُّهَا: "أَيُّ = which one (attention-grabbing device)", "هَا = demonstrative", "ٱلنَّاسُ = the addressee noun (definite, nominative)". Al-Jadwal: أَيُّ is the called noun (an indefinite noun that means a certain person, built on ḍamma), هَا a word of attention, ٱلنَّاسُ a badal of أَيُّ (`ayyuha`, Parse 3).
11. Fussilat **41:29** is quoted with ٱللَّذَيْنِ. The Tanzil text has ٱلَّذَيْنِ (Exercise 9 uses the right text).
12. Exercise 1 says the second قَالَ in 19:10 is "Allah responding to Zakariyya". The verse and al-Jadwal's page do not name the second speaker (Exercise 1).
13. Exercise 4 files 19:24 to 19:26 as "narrative dialogue (the verb نَادَىٰ introduces the speech)" under a list of four types that has no place for نَادَىٰ. Al-Jadwal names the doer of نَادَىٰ as Jibril or ʿIsa, and reads أَنْ as a word that explains. The sorting now says it leaves such verses out (`mistake-types`, Exercise 4).
14. Marks: the old lesson writes tanwin with ۢ or ۭ where Tanzil has none, in 6 verses (2:168, 18:110, 12:4, 3:45, 49:13, 75:10; 25:30 has the same mark and a wrong word). Of the 33 `ArabicExample` quotes in the old file, a script (NFC, against the Corpus text) found 21 exact, 3 that differ only in a mark (2:168, 18:110, 12:4), and 9 mismatches. The 9 are 7 distinct verses: 4 with a wrong word (errors 2, 3, 6 and 11 above: 26:49, 10:31, 25:30, 41:29) and 3 that differ in a mark only or are cut from a longer verse (3:45, 49:13, and 75:10, which the old lesson quotes 3 times). The old 12:4 word list also has an empty gloss for عَشَرَ. The new lesson's quotes come from `verse.py`.
