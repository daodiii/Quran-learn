# Content spec: L2.10 Inna and her sisters

Written 2026-10-27. Lesson: `src/content/lessons/level-2/10-inna-sisters.mdx`. Parses follow al-Jadwal (Mahmud Safi). The grammar book is al-Ajurrumiyyah (Arabic text from the Wikisource copy, vowel marks added by Quran Learn).

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Name the six words (inna, anna, lakinna, kaʾanna, layta, laʿalla) and their meanings as al-Ajurrumiyyah gives them.
2. Find the ism (accusative) and the khabar (nominative) after any of them, including when the ism is a joined pronoun.
3. Name the kind of khabar, and spot a khabar put first (indefinite ism).
4. Spot the lam of emphasis on the khabar or on the ism, and not confuse it with li-.
5. Tell inna from in and innama, and lakinna from light lakin.
6. Go through four verses word by word.

Points that follow the sources and are worded with care:
- Where al-Jadwal ties the fronted phrase to a left-out khabar (73:7, 26:8), the lesson calls the phrase the khabar and gives al-Jadwal's wording in a "Good to know" note (as L2.01 does for 7:128).
- The "slid-back" explanation of the lam is only the meaning of al-Jadwal's term al-lam al-muzahlaqa. No further reason is claimed.
- "Anna and its two parts together fill the place of what the verb needs" is al-Jadwal's wording on 2:235.
- The lesson does not claim a count for inna in the Quran (the old lesson said "over 1,500 times", not measured).

## The lesson, step by step

5 steps, 28 blocks.

1. **The six words** (5): overview, see (2:218 vs 2:173), name (al-Ajurrumiyyah rule, ism and khabar inna), sisters (six meanings), marks (accusative marks, ism that never changes).
2. **The six, one by one** (6): anna, lakinna, kaʾanna, layta, laʿalla, glance.
3. **What can follow inna** (5): pronoun (ism joined to inna), kinds (khabar kinds), fronted (khabar first), lam-khabar, lam-ism.
4. **Five mistakes and four verses word by word** (9): mistake-both, mistake-in, mistake-lakin, mistake-object, mistake-lam; parse-1 (12:2), parse-2 (2:243), parse-3 (26:8), parse-4 (31:34).
5. **Practice** (3): practice-1 (exercises 1 to 4), practice-2 (exercises 5 to 9), done.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. Example 2 (103:3): the excepted ٱلَّذِينَ was called "accusative to match the ism inna". Wrong reason and not al-Jadwal's reading; dropped.
2. Old "predicate of inna remains nominative (though here expressed as prepositional phrase)": confused. The new lesson says a phrase or sentence has no ending and stands in the place of the nominative.
3. Unlabelled made-up lines passed as quotations: عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلِيمٌ, لَٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ قَدِيرٌ, لَيْتَ ٱلشَّبَابَ يَعُودُ, ٱلصَّلَوٰةُ نُورٌ, كَأَنَّ ٱلْجِبَالَ ٱلْعِهْنُ (that last one is not a verse; 101:5 reads differently), and إِنَّ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ إِخْوَةٌ (the real verse 49:10 uses innamā).
4. Cut quotations without a mark: كَأَنَّهُمْ حُمُرٌ (74:50) and لَعَلَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحْدِثُ (65:1). Now marked "(part)" or replaced.
5. "Anna = that (conjunction)" was the only meaning given. al-Ajurrumiyyah gives emphasis for inna and anna.
6. "Over 1,500 times in the Quran": not measured, dropped.
7. The old L1.10 exercise suggested inna could make a second word accusative ("ٱلصَّمَدَ might signal inna"). The new lesson says clearly that after inna only the first part changes.
