# Content spec: L2.01 The Nominal Sentence

Written 2026-10-27. This says what the new L2.01 will teach, with which verses, and how each verse is parsed. Nothing is written in the lesson file yet. It follows the definition of a complete lesson that the owner accepted (end of `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`).

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say whether a sentence is nominal, and find its mubtadaʾ and khabar, also when the khabar comes first.
2. Say why both are nominative, and show the mark: -u, -un, a hidden ending, or no ending on a pronoun.
3. Name the kind of khabar: a single word (mufrad), a phrase with a preposition, a place or time word (ẓarf), a verbal sentence, or a nominal sentence.
4. Tell a sentence (ٱلْكِتَابُ جَدِيدٌ) from a phrase (ٱلْكِتَابُ ٱلْجَدِيدُ).
5. Parse a short verse word by word: role, case, the mark, and the reason.
6. Recognise that kāna changes the khabar to accusative. This is a preview; L2.11 teaches it.

Points that need a source before they go into the lesson:
- **Why both parts are nominative.** The lesson will say "because of their place in the sentence, not because one copies the other". The fuller classical account (the mubtadaʾ is nominative because it starts the sentence, *ibtidāʾ*; the khabar because of the mubtadaʾ) is the common Basran view. It goes in a "Good to know" note only if a source is agreed.
- **The definitions and the kinds of khabar** come from *al-Ajurrumiyyah*, chapter on the mubtadaʾ and khabar: the mubtadaʾ is a nominative noun with no word before it acting on it; the khabar is a nominative noun said about it; the khabar is either single (mufrad) or not single, and the not-single kinds are four: a preposition phrase, a place or time word, a verb with its doer, a mubtadaʾ with its khabar. A university thesis quotes the opening definition in these words; the four kinds are from my knowledge of the text. **To check against a printed edition before quoting.**
- **When the khabar must come first** (indefinite mubtadaʾ with a phrase khabar, as in 2:10): *al-Durr al-Maṣūn* on 2:10 calls the phrase khabar in فِى قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ a "fronted khabar that must come first" (*khabar muqaddam wājib al-taqdīm*), the same pattern as وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ. Source found.

## The lesson, step by step

About 34 steps in nine parts. Each step keeps the Stage look: example panel, short text, at most two notes, and often a quick check (✓ below).

**Part 1. Meet it**
1. Start: goal, about 30 minutes, what to know first (L1.09, L1.10), new terms.
2. See it: هُوَ ٱلْأَوَّلُ. Count the words: Arabic two, English three. ✓
3. No "is": Arabic puts two words side by side. Note: there is no Arabic word for "is" here.
4. The name: *jumla ismiyya*, "noun sentence", because it starts with a noun (ism). Why it matters: ٱللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ, which you say in every prayer, is one (labelled "said in prayer").

**Part 2. The two parts**
5. Mubtadaʾ ("the starting point") and khabar ("the news"), with 3:68. Note (Good to know): many books say subject and predicate.
6. Both nominative, both -u, with 3:68. The reason, plainly. ✓
7. A pronoun can be the mubtadaʾ: أَنتَ shows no ending, it still counts.
8. A hidden ending: مَوْلَىٰنَا cannot show -u. Note (Watch out): a word ending in ى keeps its case, you just cannot see it. ✓
9. Two khabars: 4:26. Root and pattern of عَلِيم and حَكِيم (faʿīl), short.
10. How the first grammar book says it: the al-Ajurrumiyyah definition, Arabic and plain English (once the text is checked).

**Part 3. What can the khabar be?**
11. Single (mufrad): one word, or a group like وَلِىُّ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ. Note: "single" means "not a sentence", not "one word".
12. A preposition phrase: 7:128. ✓
13. A verbal sentence: 2:15. The doer of يَسْتَهْزِئُ is a hidden "he".
14. A nominal sentence: 3:14, a sentence inside a sentence. ✓
15. A place or time word (ẓarf): عِندَ. Shown in 6:59, taught fully in Part 4.
16. Summary table of the five kinds, each with its verse.

**Part 4. Which comes first?**
17. The usual order: mubtadaʾ then khabar.
18. By choice: 109:6, both halves. The terms khabar muqaddam, mubtadaʾ muʾakhkhar. The hidden "my" in دِينِ. ✓
19. When the mubtadaʾ is indefinite: 2:10, the khabar comes first. ✓
20. A place word first: 6:59. Note (Tip): find the word with -u that is not after a preposition; that is the mubtadaʾ.

**Part 5. Sentence or phrase?**
21. ٱلْكِتَابُ جَدِيدٌ (a sentence) and ٱلْكِتَابُ ٱلْجَدِيدُ (a phrase), made-up. The usual sentence is definite + indefinite.
22. In 2:10, عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ is a phrase (noun and adjective) and the whole phrase is the mubtadaʾ. ✓
23. Gender: 2:259 هِىَ خَاوِيَةٌ, and 36:38 تَجْرِى (feminine verb).

**Part 6. A look ahead**
24. kāna: 4:26 next to 4:17. The subject stays -u; the khabar becomes -an. Full lesson in L2.11. ✓

**Part 7. Common mistakes**
25. Adding a verb for "is".
26. Mixing up the mubtadaʾ and the fāʿil (doer of a verb). Preview of L2.03.
27. Thinking word order is free: moving the khabar first changes the emphasis.
28. New: stopping at the preposition phrase. In وَٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ قَدِيرٌ the khabar is قَدِيرٌ; the phrase belongs to it.

**Part 8. Parse it with me** (one word at a time, role, case, mark, reason)
29. 3:68.
30. 2:15.
31. 3:14.
32. 2:10.

**Part 9. Practice and finish**
33. Exercises (below), each "try it, then see the answer".
34. Summary, related lessons (L1.09, L1.10, L2.02, L2.03, L2.04, L2.11), sources and "Found a mistake?".

## Exercises

1. **Find the parts:** 3:163, وَٱللَّهُ بَصِيرٌۢ بِمَا يَعْمَلُونَ. Answer: ٱللَّهُ mubtadaʾ, بَصِيرٌ khabar, بِمَا يَعْمَلُونَ tied to بَصِيرٌ.
2. **Two sentences, two orders:** 3:189. Answer: لِلَّهِ khabar muqaddam, مُلْكُ mubtadaʾ muʾakhkhar; then ٱللَّهُ mubtadaʾ, قَدِيرٌ khabar, with the phrase before it.
3. **Full parse:** 2:39, هُمْ فِيهَا خَٰلِدُونَ. Answer: هُمْ mubtadaʾ (pronoun), فِيهَا tied to the khabar, خَٰلِدُونَ khabar, nominative shown by -ūna (sound plural; L2.04).
4. **Hidden ending:** 3:150, بَلِ ٱللَّهُ مَوْلَىٰكُمْ وَهُوَ خَيْرُ ٱلنَّٰصِرِينَ. Two nominal sentences; مَوْلَىٰ has a hidden -u; هُوَ is a pronoun mubtadaʾ.
5. **What kind of khabar?** Five short items from the lesson's verses, one of each kind.
6. **Build it** (made-up): ٱلْعِلْمُ + نُورٌ → ٱلْعِلْمُ نُورٌ, with a short parse.
7. **Gender** (made-up): ٱلسَّمَاءُ عَالِيَةٌ and ٱلْبَحْرُ عَمِيقٌ. Why the -a on one and not the other.
8. **Sentence or phrase?** Four items: two made-up pairs and two verse phrases.

## I'rab book: the options

All four are Arabic works in the classical tradition. I read each one's text on tafsir.app (Al-Baheth Al-Qurani), which shows them verse by verse, and compared them on 2:10 and 3:14 (the two hardest parses in the spec). **A and B agree with the spec on both. C agrees on 2:10; on 3:14 only its likely copy on surahquran.org was read, which agrees. D agrees on 2:10 and does not parse the 3:14 clause.** Publication details come from library catalogues and the Shamela listing, as noted. All are under copyright: cite and paraphrase, do not copy.
