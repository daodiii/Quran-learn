# Content spec: L4.02 Tamyiz (specification)

Written 2026-10-28. The old lesson (`git show main:src/content/lessons/level-4/02-tamyiz.mdx`) was not in `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`, so it is inventoried here. The lesson is `src/content/lessons/level-4/02-tamyiz.mdx`. It follows the definition of a complete lesson at the end of the inventory.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Spot a tamyiz and say what it makes clear: "of what?" or "in what way?".
2. Name its three marks (accusative, indefinite, after a complete sentence) from *al-Ajurrumiyyah*.
3. Use the number patterns: 11 to 99 take a singular accusative tamyiz; 3 to 10, 100 and 1000 take an idafah (genitive).
4. Read a tamyiz after a measure, after "more" (أَفْعَل) and after verbs like طَابَ and ٱزْدَادَ, and see that it was often "turned" from the doer or the object (19:4, 54:12).
5. Tell a tamyiz from a ḥāl (12:4 has both) and from an object (54:12).
6. Know where scholars differ (رَفِيقًا in 4:69, سِنِينَ and تِسْعًا in 18:25, نَفْسَهُۥ in 2:130, شَيْبًا in 19:4) and say which reading the lesson follows.
7. Go through four verses word by word (12:4, 19:4, 18:34, 54:12).

- 4:4: نَفْسًا تمييز منصوب محوّل عن فاعل; طِبْنَ فعل ماض مبني على السكون، والنون ضمير فاعل.
- 4:69: رَفِيقًا تمييز منصوب (a footnote says رفيق is singular for one or many); أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ فاعل. al-Durr: tamyiz or ḥāl; under tamyiz, turned from the doer or not turned.
- Exercise verses: 2:74 قَسْوَةً, 18:103 أَعْمَٰلًا, 11:77 ذَرْعًا, 38:23 نَعْجَةً: each "تمييز منصوب". 2:130 نَفْسَهُۥ: مفعول به (al-Jadwal); al-Durr lists seven readings, prefers the object, reports the Kufan "tamyiz" and rejects a definite tamyiz.

## The lesson, step by step

5 steps, 29 blocks, 19 `Check`s.

**Step 1: Of what?** (hero; 4 blocks)
1. `overview`: Start, formula "a vague part plus a tamyiz equals a clear meaning".
2. `see`: 19:4 (part). Check 1.
3. `name`: al-Ajurrumiyyah's definition (and the ḥāl's, by contrast); mumayyaz; the two kinds named by al-Jadwal (mufrad, jumla). Check 2.
4. `rules`: three marks (accusative, indefinite, after a complete sentence). Check 3.

**Step 2: One vague word: measures and numbers** (7 blocks)
5. `measure`: 3:91; made-up رِطْلًا عَسَلًا; min and idafah (al-Jadwal's note). Check 4.
6. `eleven`: 12:4; singular accusative after 11 to 19; L4.16 pointer. Check 5.
7. `tens`: 2:51; the tens; al-Ajurrumiyyah's examples. Check 6.
8. `idafa-numbers`: 2:29; 3 to 10; naming note. Check 7.
9. `thousand`: 29:14; 100 and 1000 against 50. Check 8.
10. `left-out`: 18:25; badal and tamyiz; both readings. Check 9.
11. `kam`: 19:74; كَمْ; made-up كَمْ كِتَابًا and كَذَا دِرْهَمًا (tendency). Check 10.

**Step 3: A vague sentence** (4 blocks)
12. `fail`: 19:4 taken apart: turned from the doer. Check 11.
13. `maful`: 54:12: turned from the object; the "turn it back to X of Y" tip. Check 12.
14. `better`: 28:34; made-up مَا أَحْسَنَ زَيْدًا وَجْهًا; al-Ajurrumiyyah's أَكْرَمُ مِنْكَ أَبًا. Check 13.
15. `becomes`: 4:4; ٱزْدَادَ ٱلْمَاءُ ٱرْتِفَاعًا. Check 14.

**Step 4: Look-alikes, mistakes and parsing** (11 blocks)
16. `hal` (mistake 1): 12:4 (part 2); table tamyiz and ḥāl. Check 15.
17. `object`: tamyiz against object (54:12); the three questions. Check 16.
18. `disagree`: 4:69; al-Durr's two readings. Check 17.
19. `m-definite` (mistake 2): ٱلْـ; 2:130 note.
20. `m-case` (mistake 3): wrong case or number after a number. Check 18.
21. `m-after-number` (mistake 4): ثَلَاثُ سِنِينَ and ثَلَاثُونَ سَنَةً. Check 19.
22. `m-left-out` (mistake 5): a bare number.
23. to 26. `parse-1` to `parse-4`: 12:4, 19:4, 18:34, 54:12 (4, 4, 6 and 4 cards: 18).

**Step 5: Practice** (3 blocks)
27. `practice-1`: exercises 1 to 6. 28. `practice-2`: exercises 7 to 10. 29. `done`: summary, next, links, Sources.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

Not in `CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`. Found by reading the old file against the Corpus text, al-Jadwal, al-Durr and *al-Ajurrumiyyah*. 14:

1. Verse text: the 12:4 quote used a different tanwīn mark (كَوْكَبًۭا for Tanzil's كَوْكَبًا). The 18:22 quote has shadda and fatḥa in a different order from Tanzil (same look, other code points). The other three verse quotes match. (Checked by comparing with `verse.py`.)
2. Example 1 (18:25) calls سِنِينَ a tamyiz, "genitive with yāʾ because it follows the number pattern". The yāʾ is the accusative sign of a sound plural, and al-Jadwal and al-Durr read it as a badal or ʿaṭf bayān (a tamyiz of "hundred" would be singular genitive). Now `left-out` and exercise 8.
3. "This follows the 3 to 10 rule, extended to مِا۟ئَةٍ": no such rule. 100 and 1000 take a singular genitive (al-Jadwal's note on 99:7).
4. The rules say a tamyiz "must be accusative", then give an "exception" of a genitive tamyiz for 3 to 10. It contradicts itself and the book's definition. In al-Jadwal's parse of 2:29 the word is muḍāf ilayh. Now an idafah, with a note that al-Jadwal's own overview also calls it tamyiz.
5. Exercise 3's history ("the 3 to 10 construction evolved from a possessive ... the genitive is a remnant") has no source. Dropped.
6. Mistake 5: ثَلَاثُ سِنِينَ "could be a tamyiz or an idafah". It is an idafah (genitive). Now `m-after-number`.
7. The hook shows only "and they increased nine" in Arabic, with a meaning that adds "and remained three hundred years".
8. Example 3 (18:22) calls ثَلَٰثَةٌ a predicate with an "implicit tamyiz: people". al-Jadwal: ثَلَٰثَةٌ is the khabar of an omitted "they", and رَابِعُهُمْ كَلْبُهُمْ is its naʿt. There is no tamyiz. Dropped.
9. Example 2 calls تِسْعًا both an "object of verb" and an "implicit tamyiz" in one entry. al-Jadwal: tamyiz. al-Durr: object, with the counted word dropped. Now both views.
10. The rule box says a tamyiz is "not in possessive construction (iḍāfah)", which is unclear: a tamyiz can follow an idafah (مِلْءُ ٱلْأَرْضِ ذَهَبًا). Dropped.
