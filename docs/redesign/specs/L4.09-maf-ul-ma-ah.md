## The honest count (read this first)

The Quran has very few clear cases of a noun after a wāw of accompaniment. The numbers below were counted by script, not by eye.

- **Corpus** (`quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt`): `grep -c "w:COM"` gives **3**, the "comitative" wāw: 3:142 (before a verb, وَيَعْلَمَ), 5:36 (before a noun, وَمِثْلَهُۥ), 10:71 (before a noun, وَشُرَكَآءَكُمْ). A script over the file finds **803** words that are an accusative noun (N or PN, ACC) with an ordinary joining wāw (`w:CONJ`) in front, so the two noun cases are rare against that. مَعَ (lemma maE, tag LOC): **159** segments in **152** verses.
- **al-Jadwal** (Maḥmūd Ṣāfī): I fetched the page for each of the **6,236** verses (`https://tafsir.app/aljadwal/<s>/<v>`, no failed fetches) and searched the commentary for "واو المعية" and "مفعول معه" with the marks removed. **6,218** pages had commentary text; **18** had none my script could find (3:1, 3:2, 3:178, 5:106, 5:107, 5:108, 8:10, 9:84, 9:85, 10:6, 10:107, 14:6, 14:7, 14:8, 16:25, 18:75, 36:1, 47:15). So the counts are "at least what the book says on those pages". 59 pages matched. Many pages cover a range of verses and repeat the same note, so the matches were assigned to the verse that holds the word. Pages 2:174, 3:128, 9:82, 17:29 to 17:38 and 57:26 to 57:27 matched only on general grammar notes (not a reading of a word) and are not in the table.

| What al-Jadwal says | Verses |
|---|---|
| Noun, "along with" as the **first** reading | 34:10 (no doubt: واو المعية، الطير مفعول معه); 6:112 ("واو المعية أو عاطفة", ما مفعول معه); 6:137 (repeats 6:112 by reference) |
| Noun, "along with" only as the **second** reading (footnote or "possible") | 3:20, 5:36, 5:59, 10:71, 11:112, 21:79, 25:17, 37:22, 51:40, 73:11 (10 verses) |
| **Verb** after a wāw of accompaniment, first reading (hidden أَنْ) | 2:188, 3:142, 4:19, 6:27 |
| **Verb**, second reading | 2:42, 7:127, 8:27 |

- **Overlap of the two sources for a noun: none.** The Corpus's two noun verses (5:36, 10:71) are plain "and" in al-Jadwal's first reading. al-Jadwal's own first readings (34:10, 6:112, 6:137) are plain `CONJ` in the Corpus. 3:142 is the one verse where both say "accompaniment", and it is a verb.
- al-Jadwal quotes Ibn Hishām twice (notes to 37:22 and 73:8 to 73:14) as saying the Quran has no wāw of accompaniment. In its notes to 10:71 it says Ibn Hishām followed al-Fārisī in allowing it there. The lesson reports both and says it did not check Ibn Hishām's own text.

The lesson says all this in steps 1 and 3 and does not stretch any verse to fit.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say what a mafʿūl maʿah is (an accusative noun after a wāw that means "along with") and name the three parts: the action, wāw al-maʿiyya, the companion.
2. Tell wāw al-maʿiyya from wāw al-ʿaṭf with three questions (one action or two doers; can the second noun do the action; "and" or "along with"), and know that the ending alone does not decide.
3. Use the book's two examples: جَاءَ ٱلْأَمِيرُ وَٱلْجَيْشَ and ٱسْتَوَى ٱلْمَاءُ وَٱلْخَشَبَةَ.
4. Say that the Quran has very few clear cases, give the counts, and give the common reading and the other reading where scholars differ (10:71, 6:112, 5:36).
5. Recognise the same wāw before a verb (hidden أَنْ, subjunctive) and say that it is not a mafʿūl maʿah.
6. Say that مَعَ is a noun of place and بِـ a preposition, and neither is a mafʿūl maʿah.
7. Write the iʿrāb line of the companion (visible noun, joining noun, verb after the wāw, "and").
8. Place the job among the accusative jobs taught so far (seven) and use the right lesson numbers.
9. Go through four verses word by word.

## The lesson, step by step

5 steps, 29 blocks, 17 `Check`s, 9 `Exercise`s, 4 `Parse` panels (7, 6, 7 and 10 cards: 30 in all), 9 `Sentence`s.

**Step 1: What "along with" is** (hero; 5 blocks)
1. `overview`: Start, formula "an action, along with, a companion".
2. `see`: 34:10 (part). Check 1.
3. `book`: al-Ajurrumiyyah's chapter (definition, two examples, the list of fifteen accusatives). Check 2.
4. `parts`: the three parts and their names (action, wāw al-maʿiyya, companion). Check 3.
5. `rare`: it is rare; the Corpus's three tags; al-Jadwal's first readings; a "Good to know" note on lists of examples. Check 4.

**Step 2: With, or and?** (5 blocks)
6. `and`: wāw al-ʿaṭf (made-up جَاءَ زَيْدٌ وَعَمْرٌو). Check 5.
7. `with`: wāw al-maʿiyya (made-up سِرْتُ وَٱلنَّهْرَ). Check 6.
8. `impossible`: when "and" cannot work (the book's ٱسْتَوَى ٱلْمَاءُ وَٱلْخَشَبَةَ). Check 7.
9. `both`: when both work (-u or -a, أَكْرَمْتُكَ وَزَيْدًا; teaching summary note). Check 8.
10. `decide`: three questions. Check 9.

**Step 3: The Quran: a few cases, and the doubts** (6 blocks)
11. `maa`: مَعَ (20:46), noun of place, count 159 in 152. Check 10.
12. `drop`: 6:112, a joining noun as companion; other reading in a note. Check 11.
13. `shuraka`: 10:71, the famous doubt; both readings; note. Check 12.
14. `corpus`: the Corpus's three tags against al-Jadwal (3:142, 5:36, 10:71). Check 13.
15. `verb`: the same wāw before a verb (3:142, made-up fish and milk); the condition (negation or request); the verse list. Check 14.
16. `count`: the full count table; the Ibn Hishām note. Check 15.

**Step 4: I'rab and mistakes** (10 blocks)
17. `irab-line`: the lines for the wāw, a visible noun, a joining noun, a verb, "and". Check 16.
18. `review`: the seven accusative jobs with lesson links, and the marks. Check 17.
19. to 22. `mistake-case`, `mistake-verb`, `mistake-maa`, `mistake-force`: four mistakes (accusative after a wāw proves nothing; a verb is not a mafʿūl maʿah; مَعَ and بِـ are not the mafʿūl maʿah; forcing a verse).
23. to 26. `parse-1` to `parse-4`: 34:10, 6:112, 10:71, 3:142 (30 cards).

**Step 5: Practice** (3 blocks)
27. `practice-1`: exercises 1 to 5. 28. `practice-2`: exercises 6 to 9. 29. `done`: summary, next, links, Sources.

The old lesson is not in `CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`. These were found by reading it against the Corpus, al-Jadwal and al-Ajurrumiyyah.

1. The table listed the ḥāl lesson as **L4.05**. It is L4.01.
2. The table listed the tamyīz lesson as **L4.06**. It is L4.02.
3. The opening verse 29:48 has no accompaniment (the old text said so itself: "this particular verse uses a prepositional phrase"), and the goal "identify accompaniment patterns in Surah Hud" was never taught. The hook now is 34:10.
4. The lesson said "FIVE accusative functions" and "ALL FIVE" while its own table listed six, and later "all six". The book lists fifteen; the lesson says seven jobs so far.
5. 20:46: مَعَ was called a **preposition** and كُمَا "the object of the preposition". مَعَ is a noun of place (ẓarf, accusative; al-Jadwal: ظرف منصوب), and كُمَا is its muḍāf ilayh.
6. The marks callout said the dual takes an alif, and the sound masculine plural "alif-nun (ينَ)". The accusative dual ends in -ayni (yāʾ) and the sound masculine plural in -īna (L2.05).
7. Criterion 3 said that after a negative or a command the wāw "often signals maʿiyya" and used لَا تَأْكُلِ ٱلسَّمَكَ وَتَشْرَبَ ٱللَّبَنَ, where the word after the wāw is a **verb** (تَشْرَبَ), not a noun, so it is not a mafʿūl maʿah. The transliteration `wa-tashrab al-labana` also did not match the Arabic. The negation or request is the condition for the hidden أَنْ before a verb (al-Jadwal, notes to 17:29), not for a noun.
8. The recognition checklist said "check if the noun is accusative" to find the wāw of accompaniment. An accusative after "and" is just as likely a second object or ḥāl (أَكْرَمْتُ زَيْدًا وَعَمْرًا).
9. 10:71 was given as a mafʿūl maʿah example, with the translation "and [call upon] your partners" (words added). al-Jadwal's first reading is plain "and" (with a missing word), and the Corpus tags the wāw comitative. "You resolve a plan, not people" is al-Jadwal's reason for the missing word, not a proof of maʿiyya. The lesson now gives al-Jadwal's reading first and the other in a note.
10. One claim with no source was dropped: "classical poetry and prose use maf'ul ma'ah more frequently". The lesson gives counted numbers for the Quran instead.
11. Two bad examples were dropped. سِرْتُ وَطُلُوعَ ٱلشَّمْسِ was glossed "at sunrise", a time reading, and it has no source. جَاءَ رَاكِبًا عِشْرِينَ فَرَسًا ("came riding twenty horses") is not a sound sentence, and عِشْرِينَ was called "part of number construct" with no job. The exercise uses جَاءَ ٱلْأَمِيرُ وَٱلْجَيْشَ رَاكِبًا instead.
12. The direct object was defined by "different root from the verb". That is not a rule (the question "what?" is the test). The column was dropped.
13. "You can now analyze ANY accusative noun in the Quran using these six categories" is false: the book names fifteen jobs. Dropped.
14. The old lesson never said how few Quran examples there are in numbers, and gave the Quran example of a preposition under the heading of the object of accompaniment. Now counted (see "The honest count").

## What moved to or from another lesson

- The ḥāl, tamyīz, muṭlaq and li-ajlih are named in `review` with a question each and a link. They are taught in L4.01, L4.02, L4.07 and L4.08.
- The wāw before a verb (hidden أَنْ, "the verb after wāw al-maʿiyya") is only recognised here. The subjunctive is L3.07, and the particles are not taught in a lesson of their own.
- The other kinds of wāw (the wāw of the state, the wāw of the oath, the extra wāw) are not taught here. Only the ḥāl wāw appears, in 3:142.

## What is not checked

- The English meanings under the verses and the Arabic parse lines are unreviewed by an Arabic speaker.
- The counts of the Corpus (3 comitative wāws, 803 accusative nouns with a joining wāw, 159 مَعَ in 152 verses) come from scripts over `quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt`. The 803 counts words whose first stem is N or PN with ACC and a `w:CONJ` prefix; the Corpus tags are not reliable for every word.
- The al-Jadwal count is a text search over the online copy (see "The honest count"). 18 pages had no commentary text, the online copy has gaps (".." inside a sentence), and the search matched only the words "wāw of accompaniment" and "mafʿūl maʿah", so a reading that uses other words would be missed. The count is "at least". I did not read every matched page in full: 34:10, 6:112, 6:137, 10:71, 3:142, 5:36, 2:42, 9:82, 17:29, 20:46, 29:48, 57:26 were read in full.
- The lesson's three questions (`decide`) and the "-u or -a" pair (`both`) are a teaching summary. The book gives only a definition and two examples. The note on أَكْرَمْتُكَ وَزَيْدًا is al-Jadwal's, passing on Ibn Hishām's *Mughnī*.
- What Ibn Hishām himself said about the wāw of accompaniment in the Quran is **not checked**: al-Jadwal reports a denial (37:22, 73:11) and an allowance (10:71) in different notes. The lesson reports both as al-Jadwal's.
- The al-Ajurrumiyyah line is from the Wikisource text (`ajurrumiyyah-wikisource.txt`, the same copy the earlier Level 4 writers used), re-vowelled by Quran Learn; a printed edition was not compared. The fifteen accusatives are from the same text.
- The reading of ٱسْتَوَى ٱلْمَاءُ وَٱلْخَشَبَةَ as "rose level with the wood" is the usual teaching gloss, not a quotation from the book.
