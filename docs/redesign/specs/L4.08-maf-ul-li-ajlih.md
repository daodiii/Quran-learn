# Content spec: L4.08 The object of purpose (mafʿūl li-ajlih)

Written 2026-10-28. This is the old L4.08 "The Maf'ul li-Ajlih (Object of Cause)". It follows the definition of a complete lesson (end of `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`). The old lesson is not in the error inventory, so it was read against the Corpus, al-Jadwal, al-Durr and *al-Ajurrumiyyah*. The lesson is `src/content/lessons/level-4/08-maf-ul-li-ajlih.mdx`. Title kept; `titleArabic` added (the old file had none).

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say what a mafʿūl li-ajlih is (a noun in naṣb that says why the action was done), from al-Ajurrumiyyah's definition, and name its three names (li-ajlih, lahu, min ajlihi).
2. Spot it by three marks: naṣb, a verbal noun, and the question "why?".
3. Name what the reasons in the Quran usually have: the same doer as the verb (named by al-Jadwal and al-Durr), a feeling or a wish, the same time (the last two are a teaching summary).
4. Read its three shapes: a bare word with tanwīn (بَغْيًا), the first word of an idafah with no tanwīn (حَذَرَ ٱلْمَوْتِ), two reasons joined by وَ.
5. Tell it from a reason given with min (مِّنْ إِمْلَٰقٍ), lām (لِوَجْهِ ٱللَّهِ), min ajli (مِنْ أَجْلِ ذَٰلِكَ) or bi-sabab: a preposition and a genitive noun, not the role.
6. Tell an accusative verbal noun that is a reason from one that is a ḥāl (8:47, 4:38) or an absolute object (33:33), and know that scholars read some both ways.
7. Give root and pattern of the reason words (ٱبْتِغَآء, تَثْبِيتًا, مَرْضَاتِ, حَذَر).
8. Go through four verses word by word (2:19, 2:207, 17:31, 21:107).

## The lesson, step by step

5 steps, 24 blocks (4, 6, 4, 7, 3).

**Step 1: Why did they do it?** (hero; 4 blocks)
1. `overview`: Start, formula "a complete action plus a reason in naṣb equals a mafʿūl li-ajlih".
2. `see`: 2:19 (part), the reason حَذَرَ, the min phrase beside it. Check 1.
3. `name`: al-Ajurrumiyyah's chapter, definition and two examples; the three names. Check 2.
4. `marks`: tiles حَذَرَ ٱلْمَوْتِ, ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ ٱللَّهِ, حَسَدًا; naṣb, verbal noun, "why?". Check 3.

**Step 2: What a good reason looks like** (6 blocks)
5. `doer`: 2:109; the same doer (al-Jadwal 30:24, al-Durr 13:12). Check 4.
6. `feeling`: 17:31; a feeling and the same time (teaching summary; note). Check 5.
7. `shapes`: 10:90; bare, first word of an idafah, joined by وَ; note on the ḥāl reading. Check 6.
8. `two-reasons`: 2:265; Corpus count of ٱبْتِغَآء; note on the other readings. Check 7.
9. `differ`: 13:12; the doers differ; al-Jadwal, al-ʿUkbarī, al-Zamakhshari, Ibn Kharūf, 30:24, 32:16. Check 8.
10. `list`: five conditions and where each comes from. Check 9.

**Step 3: Other ways to give a reason** (4 blocks)
11. `min`: 6:151 beside 17:31; 2:19 again (min sababiyya). Check 10.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

The old L4.08 is not in `CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`. Found by reading it against the Corpus, al-Jadwal, al-Durr and *al-Ajurrumiyyah*. 8:

1. Exercise 1, item 3: the root of احْتِرَام is given as ح-ت-ر-م. The root is ح ر م; the ت belongs to the Form VIII pattern. *Certain* (the same item says "Form VIII"). The example is replaced by the book's examples, and `m-root` teaches root against pattern with real words.
2. Example 3 quotes 2:265 as "وَٱلَّذِينَ يُنفِقُونَ أَمْوَٰلَهُمُ …" and translates "And those who spend …". The verse starts وَمَثَلُ ٱلَّذِينَ ("The likeness of those who …"); the opening word was changed. *Checked* (Corpus, and `docs/curriculum/verse-text-check.md`).
3. The heading of Example 2 (2:207) is "Desire for Worldly Gain". The verse is about seeking Allah's pleasure. *Certain.*
4. "The maf'ul li-ajlih can appear in THREE forms", and the third (لِـ and a noun) is then called "technically a prepositional phrase, not an accusative object" (also exercise 4). It contradicts itself. The lesson separates the accusative shapes from the phrases (`shapes`, `lam`).
5. The four conditions are given as one fixed classical rule. Only the same doer is stated in an approved book here (al-Jadwal on 30:24, al-Durr on 13:12, which also record scholars who do not require it). The "feeling" and the "same time" (with "or slightly before" and the counter-example "yesterday's failure", "grammarians debate this edge case") are not in them. The lesson names the same doer as the usual rule and labels the other two a teaching summary. *Needs a source* for those two.
6. 2:19 and 2:265 are given with one reading only. Al-Jadwal and al-Durr record a second one (2:19: an omitted absolute object; 2:265: a ḥāl, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya's objection). Added as notes.
7. 2:19, yajʿalūna: "Subject: embedded pronoun 'they'". The doer is the attached wāw (الواو فاعل), not a hidden pronoun. *Certain* (al-Jadwal).
8. Exercise 2, item 2: the transliteration "dakha la" for دَخَلَ. It is dakhala. Fixed in exercise 4.
