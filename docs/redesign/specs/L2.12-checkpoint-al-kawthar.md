# Content spec: L2.12 Checkpoint, Surah Al-Kawthar

Written 2026-10-27. Lesson file: `src/content/lessons/level-2/12-checkpoint-al-kawthar.mdx`. Every parse follows al-Jadwal (Maḥmūd Ṣāfī); al-Durr al-Maṣūn for disputes.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Read all three verses of Al-Kawthar word by word: role, case, mark, reason, and the Level 2 lesson that taught the point.
2. Find the ism inna and khabar inna in verses 1 and 3, and say why each has its case.
3. Take apart أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ (verb, doer -nā, first object -ka) and tell the two -nā apart.
4. Read a command with a hidden doer, a preposition phrase tied to a verb, and an idafah with a pronoun.
5. Give both readings of هُوَ in 108:3 and say what stays the same.
6. Use the same tools on review verses from earlier lessons.

## Checked against al-Jadwal and al-Durr (2026-10-27)

Read with `jadwal.mjs 108:1 108:2 108:3` (the page covers all three verses). Al-Jadwal names: the فَ as joining for cause; لِرَبِّكَ tied to صَلِّ; هُوَ as a faṣl pronoun, with the footnote allowing a mubtadaʾ whose khabar is ٱلْأَبْتَرُ and the sentence as khabar of inna; the places of all sentences (أعطيناك in place of rafʿ as khabar inna; صل and انحر no place; the whole verses). It does not list each single word's role. Those follow the same parses al-Jadwal gives in other verses: 9:103 (صَلِّ built on dropping the weak letter, doer "you"; صَلَوٰتَكَ ism inna with the kāf as muḍāf ilayh; خذ doer "you"), 2:153 (command with the wāw as doer, joined genitive), and 15:87 as accepted in L2.03 (two objects).

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. "Two accusatives" in verse 1: there are three by place (-nā of inna, -ka, ٱلْكَوْثَرَ). The -ka was left out.
2. Exercise 2 said "two genitive words" and then counted a third in a note: there are three.
3. هُوَ was stated as a separating pronoun and "not the grammatical subject" as the only reading. Al-Jadwal and al-Durr give two readings (and al-Durr names a third and rejects it).
4. The note on رَبِّ ("visible as kasra on the base form, though the attached pronoun replaces the tanwin") was garbled: the kasra comes from لِ.
5. The idafah rule was stated as if the first word's case came from the idafah; now the lesson says the first word takes its case from its own place.
6. The surah was called "the shortest in the Quran" in the old intro; the new lesson says only "three verses, ten written words" (counted in the Corpus).
7. Em dashes in the English meanings.

## What is not checked

- "Built on dropping the weak letter" for صَلِّ is al-Jadwal's wording in 9:103; "built on sukūn" for ٱنْحَرْ is the standard formula and was not read in al-Jadwal for a command.
