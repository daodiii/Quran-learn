# Content spec: L4.17 Introduction to Balagha

Written 2026-10-28. This is the old L4.17 "Introduction to Balagha (Rhetoric)", kept at the same number. It follows the definition of a complete lesson (end of `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`). The lesson is `src/content/lessons/level-4/17-intro-balagha.mdx`. It is the map for L4.18 (figures of speech in detail, written at the same time by another writer): it gives the three branches and the idea of fitting speech to its situation, and names one figure per branch only where al-Jadwal names it.

No approved book on balagha exists in this project. The rule used throughout: a reason why a verse chose a form is taught only where al-Jadwal's balagha paragraph for that verse says it, and the lesson says "al-Jadwal says". Definitions of the field and of each figure are teaching summaries (listed under "What is not checked").

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say what balagha asks: why a verse chose one correct wording over another (a teaching summary), and give the classical phrase muṭābaqat al-kalām li-muqtaḍā l-ḥāl.
2. Name the three branches (maʿānī, bayān, badīʿ) with the question each asks, and say that one verse can use all three (11:44).
3. Tell a grammar question from a balagha question about the same verse.
4. See how a change of grammar changes the wording, with real verses: the khabar put first (64:1), the object put first (1:5, 2:3), the noun put first (2:2 against 37:47), a verbal sentence and a nominal sentence in one verse (2:212), a plain report and two strengthened ones (36:14 to 36:16), a passive with no doer and a left-out object (11:44, 36:14), restriction with وَمَا … إِلَّا (3:144, 36:17).
5. Recognise kināya (25:27), istiʿāra (14:1) and tashbīh (2:17) as figures of bayān, and ṭibāq (53:43 to 53:45) and takrīr (55:13) as devices al-Jadwal names. Detail is L4.18.
6. Sort a statement about a verse into grammar, a named reading, or a claim with no named source, and not teach a reading of one verse as a rule.
7. Go through four verses word by word.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. 1:5: "the standard نَعْبُدُ إِيَّاكَ ... would be rhetorically weaker". No source. The lesson gives al-Jadwal's reading of 1:5 (the fronting limits worship and asking for help to Allah; the repeat of إِيَّاكَ keeps each object separate).
2. 55:1 to 2: "teaching the Quran" called a kināya meaning memorising, understanding and applying it. Al-Jadwal prints no balagha note on 55:1 or 55:2. Dropped.
3. 55:13: claims that the refrain divides the surah into sections, creates "rhythmic meditation" and an emotional effect. Replaced by what al-Jadwal says: it is takrīr, it confirms each favour in turn and rebukes the denial.
4. Table row "يَوْمَ يَقُولُ كُن فَيَكُونُ: Bayan, ellipsis and brevity": ellipsis is maʿānī in the old lesson's own table; the words are a cut of 6:73 (وَيَوْمَ يَقُولُ); al-Jadwal names كُن فَيَكُونُ an istiʿāra tamthīliyya on 2:117 and 16:40. Row dropped.
5. Table row "ٱلْكِتَابُ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ: Qasr": the words skip ذَٰلِكَ, and al-Jadwal names the figure there taqdīm, not qaṣr. Now `grammar-vs` and `mistake-4`.
6. Exercise 2 (1:1): "the verb is omitted, 'I begin'". Al-Jadwal reads بِسْمِ tied to a left-out khabar, with a left-out mubtadaʾ "my beginning".
7. Exercise 2: the two mercy names as "general and specific". Al-Jadwal's reason is different (the great favours, then the finer ones). Now exercise 3.
8. Exercises 1 and 2: the endings -ān and -īm of 1:1 called sajʿ and "pleasing rhythm". No source for 1:1. Exercise 3 teaches to stop.
9. Exercise 3, pair 1: the Arabic is mis-marked (كَانَْ, عَلَِيمًا) and the question is about "kaana for Allah's eternal attribute" with no verse. Replaced by pairs on real verses.
10. Callout: 17:88 as "Produce a discourse like it". The verse says "the like of this Quran". "Discourse" is the wording of 52:34. Corrected.
11. "Classical Arab poets and orators acknowledged its inimitability" and the five dimensions of iʿjāz (legislative, scientific, historical, prophetic): no source. Dropped.
12. "Why humans cannot imitate it" (multi-layered meaning, timeless relevance, auditory beauty, structural harmony of every surah) and the three dimensions of linguistic iʿjāz: claims with no source. Dropped.
