# Content spec: L1.02 Reading Marks (long vowels, sukun, shadda, tanwin)

Written 2026-10-28. The lesson is `src/content/lessons/level-1/02-reading-marks.mdx`. It is a reading lesson: there is little i'rab. Word-by-word cards that name a grammar role follow al-Jadwal; cards that only describe reading marks say so.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Read a long ā, ī, ū from the stretching letter (alif, yā, wāw) and tell it from a plain wāw or yā that has its own vowel.
2. Read the Uthmani long-vowel signs: small alif ٰ, the wavy madda sign ٓ, small wāw ۥ, small yā ۦ, and a final ى that is ī or ā.
3. Read sukun ْ, the glides ay and aw, and tell sukun from the silent-letter sign ۟.
4. Read shadda (alone, with a vowel, and with a sukun elsewhere in the word).
5. Read tanwin (-un, -an, -in), know that -an is usually written with an alif, that tanwin does not go with ٱل, and say a small mīm ۢ as -m.
6. See that tanwin is also a case ending (preview of L2.04 to L2.06).

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. **أَبِي** with an ordinary ي: the Tanzil text is أَبِى (final ى). The lesson uses Tanzil and explains that a final ى can be ī.
2. **إِنَّآ explained as "hamza plus long alif".** There is no hamza in إِنَّآ. The آ is a long ā with the wavy sign. Fixed in `madda` and exercise 1.
3. **"ٱلْلَهَبِ"** as the definite form of لَهَبٍ: not a real word. Replaced with the real contrast لَهَبٍ against ٱلْحَطَبِ (111:4).
4. **Translation of 111:2** "His wealth will not avail him": the verb is past (Corpus: perfect). Now "did not help him".
5. **"Tanween marks indefinite"** stated as a law. Names such as قُرَيْشٍ (106:1) take tanwin and are definite. Now "a good first rule", with that example.
6. **Made-up examples unlabelled** (بَا, بِي, بُو and the رَجُل forms in the tanween table). The tanwin table now uses Quran words; the بَيْ / بِي question is labelled made-up.
7. **"Long alif in مَٰ"** for ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ: that is a small alif, not a full alif. Fixed.
8. **The small-alif and Uthmani marks were not taught at all**, though every verse in the old lesson uses them. Added.
