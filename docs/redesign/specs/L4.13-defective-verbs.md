# Content spec: L4.13 Defective Verbs (al-Nāqiṣ)

Written 2026-10-28. This is the old L4.13 "Defective Verbs (al-Naqis)", rewritten in the stepped layout. It follows the definition of a complete lesson (end of `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`). The lesson is `src/content/lessons/level-4/13-defective-verbs.mdx`. Part of the weak-verb run L4.11 to L4.15; this lesson stays on verbs whose last root letter is و or ي.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say what a defective verb is (last root letter و or ي) and what the weak letter can do: stay, turn into an alif, or drop.
2. Tell the three past patterns of "he" apart: alif from و (دَعَا), alif written ىٰ from ي (رَمَىٰ), the -iya pattern (نَسِيَ).
3. Build all 13 past forms of a verb of each pattern, and say which of three things happens at the end: the alif stays (he), the alif drops (she, the two women, they (men)), or the weak letter shows (the two men and every consonant ending).
4. Find the hidden root letter (the "I" form, the present) and know when the spelling (alif or ىٰ) misleads.
5. Read the present (ـُو, ـِي, ـَىٰ, with a hidden ḍamma), naṣb (visible fatḥa on و and ي, hidden on an alif), jazm and the command (the weak letter drops), and the "they" forms (يَدْعُونَ, يَرْمُونَ, يَخْشَوْنَ; the women's يَدْعُونَ).
6. Read the past and present passive, the doer word (manqūṣ: دَاعٍ, قَاضٍ), the place noun on مَفْعَل (مَأْوَىٰ), and see the same changes in Forms II to X.
7. Go through four verses word by word and write the iʿrāb line of these verbs.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. **Verbs and case.** The old intro said defective verbs interact with case endings (nominative, accusative, genitive). Verbs have no case (L3.05). Only the doer word (a noun) takes case, and block `doer` says so.
2. **Past table: wrong reasons.** "3FS: final ي becomes ت" and "3MP: ي becomes و before the waw plural". The alif drops; ت is the ending of "she" and و is the doer (`drop`, exercise 3).
3. **Past table contradicts itself.** "Before a vowel suffix (2MS, 1S) the ي stays; before a consonant suffix (3FS, 3FD) the ي drops": تَ and تُ are consonant endings. Example 3 said the opposite ("a consonant suffix brings the letter back; no suffix or a vowel suffix gives the alif"), which is wrong for "the two" (هَدَيَا shows the ي before an alif). Replaced by the three groups: alif stays, alif drops, letter shows (`forms-w`, `drop`, `show`).
4. **Principle 3 (naṣb).** "The final weak letter shortens to fatḥa, shown as alif" and "ي becomes ـِيَ". In naṣb the و and ي stay and show a fatḥa (يَهْدِيَ); only an alif hides it (`nasb`, mistake 1).
5. **Principle 2 and the table: only two presents.** The old lesson said defective-ya verbs have ī and defective-waw verbs ū, yet its own table has بَقِيَ يَبْقَىٰ, لَقِيَ يَلْقَىٰ and نَسِيَ يَنْسَىٰ (ending ā, roots with ي). The third ending ـَىٰ was missing. Fixed with the counted rule (`present-three`).
6. **2:186 دَعَانِ.** The old analysis said "1st person object ن + protective ي": the ن is the protecting nūn and the ي ("me") is the object, left out of the writing (al-Jadwal). The word is also tagged as a command for two people in the Corpus; the lesson follows al-Jadwal and says so (`parse-1`).
7. **2:186 ٱلدَّاعِ.** "The final و drops in the indefinite form": the word is definite. The و turns into ي after the kasra, and the ي is left out of the Quran text (al-Jadwal) (`doer`, `parse-1`).
8. **1:6 ٱهْدِنَا.** "The final ي merges into the vowel structure before نَا": the ي had already dropped in the command; نَا is the object (`parse-2`).
9. **Roots غ ز و and س م و.** The old table listed غَزَا and سَمَا as common Quran verbs. The Corpus has no verb with root غ ز و (0 words) and س م و only in Form II (8 words). Dropped (`common`). مَشَىٰ is also shown in the old table as a past form: in the Quran it is only مَّشَوْا۟ (2:20), with the present and the command.
10. **"Past 3rd person: final weak becomes alif".** Only "he" keeps an alif. She, the two women and they (men) lose it; the others show the letter (`forms-w`).
11. **Alif for و, alif maqṣūra for ي "typically".** True as a tendency; the Quran text has exceptions, counted (`spell`).
12. **"Hundreds of verbs", "extremely common".** Replaced by the count: 3,481 of 19,356 verb words (`count`).
13. **Pronunciation note.** "Long ā in all 3rd person forms" (دَعَوْا, دَعَتْ are not): dropped.
