# Content spec: L4.19 Checkpoint, Surah Ad-Duha

Written 2026-10-28. Lesson file: `src/content/lessons/level-4/19-checkpoint-ad-duha.mdx`. It replaces the old lesson of the same name (file name, `title`, `level` and `order` kept; `titleArabic`, `kicker` and `template: steps` are new). Modelled on L3.22 (Al-ʿAsr) and L2.12 (Al-Kawthar). Sentence places follow al-Jadwal (Maḥmūd Ṣāfī); roots, forms and moods are checked in the Corpus tags (`verse.py -t`); every claim about a Level 4 topic follows the finished Level 4 lessons (L4.01, L4.03, L4.04, L4.06, L4.10, L4.13, L4.14, L4.15) and Level 3 (L3.12, L3.13, L3.22).

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Read all eleven verses of Ad-Duha word by word: role, case, mark, reason, and the lesson that taught the point.
2. Read the oath (verses 1 and 2), its answer (verse 3) and the two sentences joined to it (verses 4 and 5), with the oath lām and سَوْفَ.
3. Tell مَا, لَمْ and the lā of a ban apart by what the verb shows (L4.10).
4. Name the kind, root and form of all 14 verbs: 7 defective, 4 assimilated, 3 sound; Forms I, II and IV.
5. Say why يَجِدْكَ is assimilated and not hollow, and why يَتِيمًا, ضَآلًّا and عَآئِلًا are second objects and not a ḥāl.
6. Read the frame of verses 9 to 11: أَمَّا, a topic, the linking فَ and an answer, and say what gives the jazm on تَقْهَرْ.
7. Say which Level 4 topics the surah does not use (ḥāl, tamyīz, exception, the three other objects, hollow verbs).

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. مَا was said to negate only past verbs and لَمْ only present ones. L4.10: مَا stands before past, present and nouns; لَمْ is a present form with a past meaning. Now in `v3`, `m1`, Ex 4.
2. يَجِدْكَ was called a hollow verb "(L4.12)". Root و ج د has the weak letter first: assimilated (L4.14). Now in `v6`, `m2`, Ex 1.
3. يَتِيمًا, ضَآلًّا and عَآئِلًا were called a ḥāl. Al-Jadwal (93:6): second object; L4.14 reads all three so. The claim that the ḥāl "encodes temporary states" is cut. Now in `two-objects`, `m3`.
4. تَرْضَىٰ was given the root ر ض ي. The Corpus has ر ض و (L4.13: the -iya group).
5. تَرْضَىٰ was said to be subjunctive "after the فَ of result". Al-Jadwal joins the sentence to يُعْطِيكَ (a joining فَ), and the Corpus gives no mood tag.
6. إِذَا was called a temporal conditional particle. Al-Jadwal: a time word stripped of condition, tied to "I swear".
7. The لَ of verses 4 and 5 was "emphatic lām" in free-standing sentences. Al-Jadwal: the oath lām, and the sentences are joined to the answer of the oath; footnote on why the lām is brought.
8. "Every verse ends in the long -a sound" (the old list itself ended with تَقْهَرْ, تَنْهَرْ and فَحَدِّثْ). Verses 1 to 8 end in ىٰ; 9, 10 and 11 do not.
9. ٱلضُّحَىٰ was called a "defective noun" with two possible roots. Now: a genitive with a hidden kasra; Corpus root ض ح و.
10. "Surah Ad-Duha contains EIGHT weak verbs" and "seven defective verbs": now 11 weak verb words (4 assimilated, 7 defective) of 14.
11. أَلَمْ was explained as a "double negative" that gives a positive. Al-Jadwal: a question that makes the listener agree (istifhām taqrīrī).
12. Unsourced readings cut: "Form II adds intensity, to leave completely"; the purposes of the omitted object ("brevity", "saja'", "universalizing"); "symbolizing hope, clarity and divine presence"; the rhetorical arc; "consistent, systematic and complete"; the pairing of blessings and commands; tibaq; the effect of saja'; "grammar serves theological purpose".
13. The old verse text used non-Tanzil tanwīn marks (for example خَيْرٌۭ, يَتِيمًۭا, ضَآلًّۭا, عَآئِلًۭا). Every verse is now typed from the Corpus (`check-verses.py`: match=30, mismatch=0).
14. ٱلْأُولَىٰ was called a "feminine superlative" and خَيْرٌ an "elative adjective". The lesson says what the Corpus and al-Jadwal support: the first is a feminine noun in the genitive with a hidden kasra; the second is the khabar.

## What is not checked

- Al-Jadwal's four pages for 93:1-11 give sentence-level places and a few word-level notes only. Single-word roles follow the standard parse, the Corpus tags and al-Jadwal's parse of the same words elsewhere (for example the hidden vowels follow L4.13 and L4.01).
- The wāw of وَٱلَّيْلِ is read as a joining wāw. Al-Jadwal does not parse it on 93:2; the reading follows its parse of 95:1 (a note in `v2` says so).
- ضَآلًّا and عَآئِلًا as second objects: al-Jadwal names only يَتِيمًا; L4.14 reads all three.
- The mood of تَرْضَىٰ (raised) follows from the joining فَ and the missing Corpus mood tag. Al-Jadwal names no mood.
- أَغْنَىٰ as Form IV: the Corpus gives no bracket (L4.13 notes that some forms are untagged); the lesson reads it from its shape.
- آوَىٰ: that the two hamzas make one long ā follows L3.22 and al-Jadwal's note on ءَامَنَّا (2:8); not read on 93:6 itself.
- That the ٱ of the article is not said after لَ (لَلْ): standard; the Corpus gives only the prefix tags.
- The English meanings of ٱلْءَاخِرَة ("the Hereafter") and ٱلْأُولَىٰ ("the first") are ours. Al-Jadwal gives no gloss; whether ٱلْأُولَىٰ means "this life" is not stated, and the lesson does not say it. The meaning of ضَآلًّا as a state is left to tafsir.
- Glosses of verbs (سَجَىٰ "grows still" from al-Jadwal's "sakana"; قَلَىٰ "hated" from al-Miṣbāḥ's "abghaḍtuhu"; عَآئِلًا "in need" from al-Jadwal's "faqīr"; وَدَّعَ "taken leave of") are ours or from those glosses.
- The reasons of revelation are only al-Jadwal's own three-report summary; not independently checked.
- Al-Jadwal's figure names (the attribution on 93:2, the metaphor on 93:7) are given as al-Jadwal's. L4.17 and L4.18 have no approved source in this project.
- L4.06 calls the لَ on a sentence-opening noun "the beginning lām" (59:13); al-Jadwal calls the لَ of 93:4 the oath lām. A note in `oath-answer` states both.
- The word-count and verb-count figures (40 written words, 14 verb words, 7 + 4 + 3, 9 + 2 + 3 by form, 8 verse endings in ىٰ) are Quran Learn counts from the Corpus, made by script.
- English meanings and Arabic parse lines are unreviewed by an Arabic speaker. The humanizer pass was a read-through for tells.
- No teaching-form tables (no table of invented verb forms). Tables in the lesson that carry Arabic: `toolbox`, `shape`, `weak3`, `map`, and the answers to Ex 1, 4, 5, 6 and 8. Their verse fragments were checked against the Tanzil text by script (every fragment from surah 93 is a substring of it).
