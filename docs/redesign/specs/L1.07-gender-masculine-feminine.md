# Content spec: L1.07 Gender in Arabic (Masculine and Feminine)

Written 2026-10-28. File: `src/content/lessons/level-1/07-gender-masculine-feminine.mdx`. Follows the definition of a complete lesson in `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`. This is a Level 1 lesson: it introduces and previews, and uses the same terms, glosses and transliteration as Level 2.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say that every Arabic noun is masculine (mudhakkar) or feminine (muʾannath), also nouns for things.
2. Name the three signs of a feminine word: tāʾ marbūṭa (ة), alif maqṣūra (long ā), alif mamdūda (ā and hamza). Tell ة from an ordinary ت.
3. Name the common feminine words with no sign (أَرْض, شَمْس, نَار, نَفْس, يَد, عَيْن, أُمّ) and the masculine words that look feminine (خَلِيفَة, مَآء, مَوْلَىٰ).
4. Use the words around a noun (adjective, verb, pronoun) to settle its gender. This is a preview: L2.02, L2.03, L2.09, L3.08 and L3.09 teach the matching.
5. Go through four short verse parts word by word.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. "~90% reliable" for the ة test has no source. Removed; the lesson says "almost always" and shows the exceptions.
2. "Ends with long ى or ا": too wide. A masculine word can end in a long ā (مَوْلَىٰ, هُدًى). The lesson names the sign (alif maqṣūra), says it can be written ىٰ or ا, and says it is a hint, not proof.
3. "Ends with اء": مَآء, "water", ends in -āʾ and is masculine (Corpus M). The lesson shows مَآءً next to ٱلسَّمَآءِ in 25:48.
4. "Hidden feminine" under Al-Fatiha 1:2: ḥamd is masculine and raḥma feminine, which is not a hidden feminine. Dropped.
5. The old lesson says دِين and أَفْوَاج are masculine. The Corpus gives no gender tag for the verse's دِينِ and tags أَفْوَاج only as plural. No such claim is made now.
6. Exercise 4 cites ٱلْكَوْثَرَ as "Al-Kawthar 108:2". The word is in 108:1.
7. "ة in isolation is silent or -h, and 'standing alone (rare)'": reworded. ة is the shape of the letter when it is not joined to the letter after it; the sound depends on whether the word carries an ending.
8. Not an error (corrected in review): exercise 1 says قَلْب is masculine, which is right; the verse itself shows it, بِقَلْبٍ سَلِيمٍ (26:89), with a masculine adjective. The Corpus tags its 19 singular uses FS, which is a tagging quirk. قَلْب was still replaced by بَيْتٌ, so the lesson is not affected. **Corpus gender tags on plain nouns are not reliable:** check gender against an adjective or verb in the verse.
9. Exercise 2 uses سُوْرَة. The Corpus tags it feminine in 9 uses and masculine in 1. Replaced by أُخْتٌ, which also teaches ة against ت.
10. "خَلِيفَة can be masculine when referring to a male, very rare": the Corpus tags every Quran use masculine, and al-Jadwal explains the ة as added for emphasis.

## What is not checked

- English meanings and Arabic `irab` lines are unreviewed by an Arabic speaker.
- The terms أَلِف مَقْصُورَة and أَلِف مَمْدُودَة, the statement that ة is said -h when stopping and -t before an ending, the statement that the ة of a describing word is the usual feminine, and "body parts that come in pairs are often feminine" (a memory hint, not a claim) come from general grammar, not from a source in the allowed list. Check against the Madinah books before relying on the wording.
- Gender of word tiles that are not from a verse (كِتَٰبٌ, بَيْتٌ, أُمٌّ and so on) rests on the Corpus tag only.
- 91:1, 91:2, 93:4, 25:48, 69:22 and 110:1 are read at sentence level in al-Jadwal. 108:1 was not read in al-Jadwal.
