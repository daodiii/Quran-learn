# Content spec: L1.01 Letters and Short Vowels

Written 2026-10-28. A reading lesson: no iʿrāb, so no al-Jadwal parse. Verse text is the Tanzil text from the Corpus file (checked with `check-verses.py`, mismatch=0).

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Read Arabic from right to left.
2. Name the 28 letters and know each one's four forms (two for the six non-joiners).
3. Cut a word into joined runs and name the form of each letter.
4. Tell look-alike letters apart by their dots, and sound-alikes by ear.
5. Read fatḥa, kasra, ḍamma (and see the sukūn) on a letter.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. "Five of the six non-connecting letters have no dots": four do (ا د ر و); ذ and ز have one dot.
2. "That is 6 fewer shapes": six letters lose two shapes each, 12 fewer (112 to 100).
3. ح called "emphatic": it is not one of the four heavy letters (ص ض ط ظ).
4. In ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ the "alif creates a break": that is a small alif mark, so the run is not cut; the break is after the alif waṣla.
5. ٱللَّهِ: "the two lams are in medial forms": the first is a start form, the second a middle form.
6. "The letters are the 28 consonants": alif, wāw, yāʾ also write long vowels; hamza is a sign.
7. "Every consonant carries a vowel mark or sukun": nearly every spoken letter; long-vowel letters and silent letters carry none.
8. "Change the damma to fatha and you get hawa, desire": dropped; the spelling differs (هَوَى).
9. Final yāʾ shown as ـي: the Quran text writes ى at the end of every word.
10. The English-cursive comparison for letter forms was dropped (unclear).
11. Old Fatha example used أَحَدٌ with tanween; replaced by 94:1.
