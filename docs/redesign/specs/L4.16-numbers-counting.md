# Content spec: L4.16 Numbers and counting (al-Aʿdād)

Written 2026-10-28. The old lesson (`git show main:src/content/lessons/level-4/16-numbers-counting.mdx`) was not in `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`, so it is inventoried here. The lesson is `src/content/lessons/level-4/16-numbers-counting.mdx`. It follows the definition of a complete lesson at the end of the inventory.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Read one and two as describing words that follow the noun and agree with it, and say why the Quran adds them (to stress the number).
2. Give the form of a number from 3 to 10 for a masculine or a feminine noun (the number goes against the noun: ة with a masculine noun, no ة with a feminine one), judge a plural noun by its singular, and say what case the number and the counted noun take.
3. Read a number with no counted noun written (12:43, 2:234, 74:30, 8:65) and say what the form of the number tells about the unwritten noun.
4. State the rule as the Quran shows it: 55 of 56 places with a written noun, one exception (6:160), with al-Jadwal's reading of it.
5. Read the teens (two number parts built on fatḥa, twelve declining like a dual, the first part agreeing for 11 and 12 and turning around from 13), the tens (-ūna / -īna, one form for both genders), the hundred and the thousand (singular genitive counted noun).
6. Read the ordinals (pattern فَاعِل, agree with the noun, ordinal + its number in an idafah, ordinal + pronoun).
7. Say what the Quran does and does not use: 11, 12 and 19 only from 11 to 19; 99 once; no 7th, 9th or 10th.
8. Go through four verses word by word (69:7, 2:196, 9:36, 38:23).

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

Not in `CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`. Found by reading the old file against the Corpus text, al-Jadwal and its own examples. 10:

1. **Verse text.** 9 of the 14 `ArabicExample` quotes differ from Tanzil (checked with a script against the Corpus text): 2:29, 2:163 (وَاحِدٌ for وَٰحِدٌ), 67:3, 2:196 (ثَلَاثَةِ for ثَلَٰثَةِ), 12:43, 27:48, 2:96, 9:80 and 18:25 (مِائَةٍۢ for مِا۟ئَةٍ). Most differ in the tanwīn marks (ٍۢ, ۭ) and the alif; 67:3 also begins with a word that is not in the verse, هُوَ (the verse begins ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ). Now every quote is read by `check-verses.py`.
2. "The rule is absolute and consistent throughout the Quran." The Corpus shows 55 of 56 places, and al-Jadwal itself marks 6:160 (عَشْرُ أَمْثَالِهَا) as against the rule. Now "almost always", with the counts.
3. "Why Does This Rule Exist?": a historical claim ("the original forms were feminine ... the masculine forms are shortened") with no source. Dropped.
4. Exercise 2.3 (7:160): "أَسْبَاطًا ... accusative singular form (tamyīz)". It is a plural. Al-Jadwal prefers to read it as a ṣifa of an omitted feminine noun (فِرْقَة, أُمَّة), which also explains the feminine number. Now a "Good to know" note and exercise 4 (c).
5. The tens section used 2:96 (أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ, a thousand) as its example and then said "after hundreds and thousands, the counted noun is genitive singular", under the heading "Tens: counted noun accusative singular". The example did not match the heading. Now 46:15 for the tens and 2:96 in the thousand block.
6. Example 28:43: "ٱلْقُرُونَ is feminine plural (قَرْنٌ treated as collective)". قَرْن is masculine and the Corpus tags ٱلْقُرُونَ as a masculine plural. The feminine singular ٱلْأُولَىٰ agrees with a plural of things. Now a short note labelled as a teaching summary.
7. Exercise 4.2 (5:12): "the twelve tribes of Israel" and "the emphasis underscores the gravity of the covenant". The verse names twelve leaders (نَقِيبًا), not tribes, and the rhetorical claim has no source. Dropped.
8. Example 3 (12:43): أَرَىٰ translated "have seen". It is a present verb: "I see". Fixed.
9. The 13 to 19 table showed 13, 14, 15 and 19 as if the Quran used them, and the ordinals table showed all ten ordinals the same way. The Quran has only 11, 12 and 19, and no 7th, 9th or 10th. Now every table marks which forms come from verses.
10. Made-up phrases in the exercises were not labelled (خَمْسَةِ لَيَالٍ, خَمْسَةُ رِجَالٍ, سَبْعُ لَيَالٍ, أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ). Now "made up" in the question.

## What is not checked

- The English meanings and the Arabic parse lines on the cards are unreviewed by an Arabic speaker.
- **The number rules are a teaching summary.** No approved book in this project states them. Where al-Jadwal states a rule, the lesson says so and names the verse of its note: 3 to 10 against the counted noun (6:160, 7:160), the plural judged by its singular and collective nouns (27:48), the tamyīz cases for 3 to 10, 11 to 99, 100 and 1000 (38:23), the compound numbers (7:160 and 9:36), the pattern فَاعِل of ordinals (18:22), the stress use of one and two (16:51). Not stated in the notes I read: that the tens have one form for both genders (shown by 46:15, a masculine and a feminine noun in one verse), that ordinals agree with their noun (shown by 53:20 and 24:7), that a plural of things takes a feminine singular describing word (28:43, labelled in the lesson), and the forms of 13 to 18, of 19 with a feminine noun, and of the tens and ordinals the Quran does not use (marked "made up").
- The gender I gave each counted noun (by its singular) in the count 28 + 27 + 1 is from my own knowledge of the nouns, not from a dictionary in the project. Doubtful ones: قَرْء (masculine), زَوْج (masculine; al-Jadwal says masculine in 56:7), أَلْف (masculine), رَهْط and طَيْر (collectives, counted apart). The claim "55 of 56" depends on these.
- Where the online al-Jadwal has gaps (see "How each verse is parsed"): the card or hint roles for those words are the standard formulas.
- The counts are Quran Learn counts of Corpus tags. The Corpus gives numbers no tag of their own, so a word of another family with the same lemma could be miscounted; I checked ٱلْوَٰحِدُ (6 words, named in the lesson), `thānī` in 22:9 (not counted) and the lemma `>uwliY` (not a number: "possessors").
- "A tamyīz is not written when the reader can supply it" and "angels" in 74:30 are al-Jadwal's note; the lesson adds that 74:31 names angels (verified in the Corpus text).
- 2:234: "Arabic counts days by their nights" is my plain wording of al-Jadwal's reason ("the nights are the base of counting the days").
- The meaning of ثَلَٰثَةَ قُرُوٓءٍ (2:228) is left as "three qurūʾ, the waiting time after divorce".
