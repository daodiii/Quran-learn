# Content spec: L5.12 Parallelism and repetition

Written 2026-10-28. Lesson file: `src/content/lessons/level-5/12-parallelism-repetition.mdx`. It replaces the old lesson of the same name (file name, `title`, `level` and `order` kept; `kicker`, `titleArabic` and `template: steps` are new). Parses follow al-Jadwal (Maḥmūd Ṣāfī) where its page speaks; roots, forms and moods are checked in the Corpus tags (`verse.py -t`). Figures are named only where al-Jadwal names them (its "البلاغة" paragraph). Everything else about repeating is a count or a look of our own, and the lesson says so each time.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say how many times the question of 55:13 stands in Surah Ar-Rahman (31, from 55:13 to 55:77, counted by script), and how the count was made.
2. Parse 55:13 word by word, including the linking فَ for an unspoken condition, the chain of three idafahs and the dual pronoun and verb.
3. Say what al-Jadwal says about the repeat (takrīr: it fits the many favours and rebukes the denial; its footnote groups the 31 as 8, 7, 8 and 8 and says the repeat came for emphasis and reminding) and what it does not say.
4. Read 55:1 to 55:4 as one mubtadaʾ with three khabars, and see that 55:2 to 55:4 are one build (labelled "our own look", with L4.18's word tawāzī).
5. Read the list of 90:8 to 90:10 (question of rebuke, لَمْ over one verb only, dual, single, dual) and parse 90:8.
6. Parse 77:15 (indefinite mubtadaʾ allowed by a prayer wording; the time word; the phrase tied to the khabar) and 54:17 (oath-answer lām, linking فَ for an unspoken condition, a question with the sense of a command).
7. Read 55:5 and 55:6 correctly (ٱلنَّجْمُ is a plant with no stem in al-Jadwal; istiʿāra and tawhīm named by al-Jadwal).
8. Sort any statement about repeating into: grammar, al-Jadwal's reading, our count or look, or no named source.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. Every verse was typed with non-Tanzil spelling (آلَاءِ, أَيِّ, ٱلْقُرْآنَ, ٱلْجَانَّ, صَلْصَالٍ ...). All are now copied from the Corpus (`check-verses.py`: mismatch=0).
2. The "Grammar of 31 Repetitions" callout put the refrain after "verses 14-16", "17-18", "19-25" and "26-78". 55:14 and 55:15 do not hold the question and the last one is 55:77. Replaced by the count of 31 verses and al-Jadwal's 8, 7, 8, 8.
3. 55:6 ٱلنَّجْمُ was glossed "the stars". Al-Jadwal (ṣarf and balagha): a plant with no stem. The lesson says so; the Corpus root is that of "star" (named in the lesson).
4. The فَ of 55:13 was called a conjunction or resumptive fāʾ in the key, and a "consequential fāʾ" in Ex 3 (three names, none al-Jadwal's). Al-Jadwal: a linking fāʾ for the answer of a condition left out.
5. "أَلَمْ نَجْعَل governs all three verses" (90:8-10). Only نَجْعَل is under لَمْ; هَدَيْنَٰهُ is past and its sentence is joined to the first.
6. Form II تُكَذِّبَانِ was said to be intensive ("persistently deny"). Not sourced; L3.12 Mistake 4 says Form II has no single meaning, and gives كَذَّبَ as "called false".
7. كُمَا was parsed as ك plus مَا, "dual marker". The Corpus tags it one dual pronoun, and L3.02 teaches -kumā.
8. The dual of the verb was said to replace the ḍamma of the plain ending. The plain ending of the five verbs is the nūn that stays; the alif is the doer (L3.07).
9. 54:17: فَهَلْ called "result + interrogative"; مِن "partitive"; مُّدَّكِر "genitive after مِن"; "singular implied" audience; "a genuine-style question". Al-Jadwal: lām of an unspoken oath's answer, linking فَ for an unspoken condition, مُدَّكِر genitive in form and nominative in place, the question has the sense of a command.
10. 77:15: وَيْلٌ was an "exclamatory noun"; يَوْمَئِذٍ plain "accusative"; the khabar "is the prepositional phrase". Al-Jadwal: indefinite allowed because the wording is a prayer; accusative or built with a tanwīn standing for a left-out sentence; the phrase is tied to the khabar.
11. The effect claims: the grammar "creates" emphasis; the list "builds anticipation" and makes the actions "equally important"; the repeat has a different meaning each time and 55:16 is "more fundamental"; the rare plural ءَالَآءِ "adds rhetorical weight"; the nominal sentence of 77:15 "makes the warning timeless"; "grammar form matches rhetorical function". No source; cut. Al-Jadwal's own statements are given instead.
12. "Antithetical parallelism" and a table of "antonyms in the same position" for 92:5-10 (ٱتَّقَىٰ and ٱسْتَغْنَىٰ are not antonyms). Al-Jadwal names no figure there. The lesson describes the build as a look of ours and states only what al-Jadwal's ṣarf note says (ٱلْعُسْرَىٰ is the opposite of ٱلْيُسْرَىٰ).
13. "The tip: grammar serving theology" (يُيَسِّر used for both outcomes suggests wrong choices feel natural). No source; cut.
14. The 55:13 question was labelled "اسْتِفْهَامٌ إِنْكَارِيٌّ" (a rebuke question). Al-Jadwal names no question type at 55:13, only takrīr. The label is cut here and left to L5.13.
