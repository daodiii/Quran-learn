# Content spec: L1.03 Reading the Bismillah

Written 2026-10-28. This says what the new L1.03 teaches, with which verses, how the one grammar parse was checked, and how the old lesson maps onto the new one. The lesson is in `src/content/lessons/level-1/03-reading-bismillah.mdx`.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Read the Bismillah aloud, letter by letter, saying which mark gives each sound.
2. Say what an alif waṣla does (silent when the word follows another word) and why it is *bismi llāhi*, not *bismi allāhi*.
3. Explain the shadda in ٱللَّهِ (two lāms merge) and the lost lām in ٱلرَّ (a sun letter).
4. Tell ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ from ٱلرَّحِيمِ by the mark on the ح and the long vowel (dagger alif ā, yā ī).
5. Join the words into one flow, with the syllables in the right place.
6. Go through 1:1 word by word (preposition, noun, second word of an idafah, two adjectives) as a preview of L2.06 to L2.09.
7. Name the two views of what بِسْمِ is tied to (Basran: a left-out noun; Kufan: a left-out verb) without mixing them.

## How the grammar is parsed

**Source:** al-Jadwal (Maḥmūd Ṣāfī) for the parse of 1:1; al-Durr al-Maṣūn (al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī) for the dispute about what بِسْمِ is tied to. The Corpus file confirms the word tags (1:1: بِ + noun, proper noun, two adjectives, all genitive).

| Ref | Word | Role | Case and mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1:1 | بِ | preposition (ḥarf jarr) | no case |
| | سْمِ | ism majrūr, also the muḍāf of the idafah | genitive, -i (kasra) |
| | بِسْمِ | jār wa-majrūr, tied to a left-out khabar; the mubtadaʾ left out: ابتدائي | the phrase has no case |
| | ٱللَّهِ | muḍāf ilayh | genitive, -i |
| | ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ | naʿt of the name | genitive, -i, follows ٱللَّهِ |
| | ٱلرَّحِيمِ | second naʿt of the name | genitive, -i, follows ٱللَّهِ |

The two views of what بِسْمِ is tied to are those given in L2.07 (note in block `tied-to`, exercise 8). In this lesson they are in block `tied`:
- **Basran, followed by al-Jadwal:** a left-out noun. بِسْمِ is the khabar and the mubtadaʾ “my beginning” (ابتدائي) is left out. A nominal sentence.
- **Kufan, from al-Durr al-Maṣūn:** a left-out verb, “I begin” (or “I read”). A verbal sentence.
- The lesson says to pick one view and keep it, and that adding a verb to the nominal reading changes the sentence type. This avoids the mixture the old L5.01 made (see CONTENT-INVENTORY, L5.01 item 1).
- Al-Durr al-Maṣūn also says that the بِ here means asking for help (*istiʿāna*). That is in the block.

Facts about forms are from al-Jadwal's section on word forms for 1:1: ٱسْم has the root س م و with its last letter dropped and an alif waṣla added; ٱللَّهِ comes from *al-ilāh* with the two lāms merged; ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ is *faʿlān*, ٱلرَّحِيمِ is *faʿīl*, both from ر ح م; the “balāgha” section says ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ is the stronger of the two. Al-Durr al-Maṣūn on 1:1 lists views on whether the name Allah is derived and prefers that it is not (*murtajal*).

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. **The missing alif of ٱسْم was explained by the genitive case.** It is a spelling matter: the Tanzil text leaves the alif out in the Bismillah and writes it in 56:74 (بِٱسْمِ). The sound is the same.
2. **The final kasra of ٱللَّهِ was said to come from the preposition.** بِ acts on ٱسْم. ٱللَّهِ is genitive because it is the second word of an idafah (al-Jadwal: *muḍāf ilayh*).
3. **“Lam with shadda + fatha = llā”** implied that a fatḥa gives a long ā. In this text the long ā of the name is not written; the lesson says so.
4. **The syllables.** The old lesson gave “bis-mil-lā-hir-raḥ-mā-nir-raḥ-īm” and “ar-raḥ-ī-mi”. The long ī belongs with the ح: *ra-ḥī-mi*. The new lesson counts ten beats.
5. **Rahman and Rahim.** The old lesson said one is for all creation and the other for believers. This is not in the course's sources. Replaced with al-Jadwal's pattern and strength notes.
6. **The two views of the Bismillah's tie.** The old lesson did not mention them. They are now given separately and not mixed, to stay consistent with L2.07 and to avoid the L5.01 mixture.
7. **Five examples, all the same verse.** Replaced with three fresh reading verses.

## Not checked

- The English meanings under the verses and the Arabic parse lines (`irab`) have not been read by a person who knows Arabic.
- “Not written at the start of At-Tawbah” was confirmed by al-Jadwal's note on 9:1. The count of 113 surahs is no longer stated.
- The stop at the end of a verse (*waqf*, dropping the last vowel) is a reading rule stated plainly in a note. The course's approved sources do not cover it; it is common to every book on reading the Quran aloud (tajwīd).
- The letter tiles (بِ, سْ, مِ ...) use the `Tiles` component, which colours tiles by word type. A letter-chart component would fit better (no component was built).
