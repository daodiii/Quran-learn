# Content spec: L3.10 Demonstratives (this, that)

Written 2026-10-28. File: `src/content/lessons/level-3/10-demonstrative-pronouns.mdx` (address unchanged). Old lesson: `git show main:src/content/lessons/level-3/10-demonstrative-pronouns.mdx`. Every parse follows al-Jadwal (Maḥmūd Ṣāfī); al-Durr al-Maṣūn for readings that differ.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say what a pointing word (ism ishāra) is: a definite noun, built from an attention word (hā), a pointing part, and for far words the lām of distance and the kāf of address.
2. Read the chart of ten forms (near and far; one male, one female, two, many) and pick the one that matches a noun.
3. Recognise the dual shapes (-āni, -ayni) and say what each shows.
4. Say which job a pointing word has (mubtadaʾ, ism inna, object, second word of an idafah) and which case it stands "in the place of".
5. Read the noun after a pointing word as a badal (or ʿaṭf bayān) or as the khabar, and tell "this book" from "this is a book".
6. Tell the kāf of address (-ka, -kum, -kunna) from the pronoun -ka, and a pointing word from a "who" word.
7. Go through four verses word by word.

Points that needed care:

- **Al-Jadwal's typo in 2:35.** It says ٱلشَّجَرَةَ is a badal of ذِهِ "following it in the genitive" (تبعه في الجر). The word is accusative (an object's badal), so the lesson says accusative, -a. The wording "in the genitive" looks like a slip in the online text.
- **Built on what.** Al-Jadwal names the fixed ending only for ذَا (sukūn, 2:2), ذِهِ (kasra, 2:35), the dual (alif in 20:63 and 28:32, yāʾ in 28:27). For أُولَاءِ and for ذِهِ in 7:73 it says only "in the place of ...". The lesson names no ending where al-Jadwal does not.
- **The dual: built or changing?** The brief said the dual is the exception to "built". Al-Jadwal itself calls the dual pointing words built (on the alif, on the yāʾ). The lesson follows al-Jadwal and says the dual is the only form whose shape changes with its job, and gives the other view (Abū ʿAmr's reading of 20:63 as an accusative ism inna with a yāʾ) in a `Note kind="know"` from al-Durr al-Maṣūn.
- **Why تِلْكَ with ٱلرُّسُلُ** (female singular word, plural noun, people). Al-Jadwal and al-Durr do not say why. The lesson says so and gives no rule.
- **Male dhānika for two female things** (28:32): al-Durr al-Maṣūn gives the reason (the khabar is male) and says a pointing word can be made female to match a female khabar. Al-Jadwal is silent on gender here.
- **No reason is given for far words used for something close** (old lesson's "honorific distance"): neither source discusses it in the i'rab of these verses.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. **Ism inna called nominative.** The old text said هَٰؤُلَآءِ after إِنَّ was "nominative (ism inna)". After إِنَّ the first part is accusative; the pointing word stands in the place of the accusative (L2.10). Now 3:62.
2. **"Indeclinable, so no visible case marker" yet "nominative"** (old exercise 3): a pointing word is not nominative; it stands "in the place of" the nominative.
3. **Demonstratives all "indeclinable".** The old text said the dual declines but also that pointing words do not change. Now: most are built; the dual changes shape, and al-Jadwal still calls it built (on alif or yāʾ).
4. **2:2 analysed two ways at once.** The old lesson said "that book" is "a single unit" and also "That is the Book" with ٱلْكِتَٰبُ as khabar. Al-Jadwal reads ٱلْكِتَٰبُ as badal or ʿaṭf bayān, and allows khabar. Now both readings are named.
5. **"Non-human plurals often take feminine singular demonstratives"** was the reason given for تِلْكَ with ٱلرُّسُلُ, but the messengers are people, and ٱلرُّسُلُ was called a "masculine plural". Both claims removed.
6. **"The noun MUST be definite."** The old text marked هَٰذَا كِتَابٌ as wrong. It is a correct sentence ("This is a book"), as 3:138 shows. Now stated correctly.
7. **"Far means honour and emphasis"**, "Some scholars say ...": unsourced, dropped.
8. **Unlabelled made-up phrases** presented next to verses: now labelled "Made-up example".
9. **A broken link** "L4.07 Pronoun Reference" pointing at L3.01: removed.
10. **Old table said هَٰؤُلَاءِ is for "rational beings"**: the Corpus tags the plural with no gender and no such restriction. Now "many", with no claim about who.
11. **ك called a "far marker" with no analysis.** Al-Jadwal says the kāf is for address and the lām is for distance. Now taught from it.
