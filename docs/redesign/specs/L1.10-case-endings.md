# Content spec: L1.10 Case Endings (a first look)

Written 2026-10-28. The lesson is `src/content/lessons/level-1/10-case-endings.mdx`. Definition of complete: end of `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`. This is a Level 1 lesson: it introduces and previews; L2.04, L2.05 and L2.06 teach each case in full.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say that an Arabic noun changes its last vowel to show its job (iʿrāb), and compare this with English word order.
2. Name the three cases (nominative rafʿ, accusative naṣb, genitive jarr) and their marks: -u or -un, -a or -an, -i or -in.
3. Say the main jobs that give each case: nominative (starting point, news, doer), accusative (object), genitive (after a little word, second word of a pair).
4. Know that some words never change (a pronoun such as huwa), some hide the mark, and some use other marks (dual, sound plural, diptote). Previewed, not taught.
5. Tell a noun case from a verb's fixed ending (khalaqa ends in -a).
6. Go through a short verse word by word: role, case, mark, reason.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

The five in `CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`:
1. Exercise 4 said an accusative ٱلصَّمَدَ "might signal إِنَّ". Inna makes the **first** part accusative; the news stays nominative. Exercise 4 and Mistake 3 now show this with 42:19 and 31:16.
2. "Every Arabic noun takes one of three case endings." Now: the plain pattern is for the common single noun; a pronoun (huwa) has one shape; a word ending in a long ā hides the mark; other kinds use other marks (huwa block, other-marks block).
3. Four of six examples were Al-Ikhlas, two the same verse, and the Bismillah said "from earlier in the lesson" when it was not used earlier. No Al-Ikhlas now; six different verses; no "from earlier".
4. The made-up قَرَأَ ٱلطَّالِبُ ٱلْكِتَابَ was unlabelled. Now labelled "Made-up example".
5. "Level 1 complete" with L1.11 still to come. Now "Next: L1.11 is the Level 1 checkpoint".

Found while rewriting:
6. The memory aid said damma is "up", fatha "middle" and kasra "below". Damma and fatha are both written above the letter. Dropped.
7. The old text wrote `اِسْمِ` (with a kasra under the alif) in the Bismillah analysis; the Tanzil word is ٱسْم (alif waṣl). Fixed.
8. Example 2 was headed "Nominative with Nominal Adjective" but analysed ٱلصَّمَدُ as a predicate. Not carried over.
9. "Case is for NOUNS, not verbs" was too short. Al-Ajurrumiyyah gives verbs their own three (rafʿ, naṣb, jazm). Now said that way, and a past verb's fixed -a is shown as not a case.
10. Exercise 2 said "the subject always takes nominative", which is false after inna. Reworded to "starting point" and "news", with the exception named.
