## What the learner can do at the end

1. Name the three parts of an exception: the group (mustathnā minhu), the little word, the word set apart (mustathnā).
2. Tell a **complete** sentence (the group is named) from an **incomplete** one (it is not named), and a positive sentence from a negative one.
3. Choose the case of the word after إِلَّا in four situations: complete positive (accusative), complete negative (a substitute, the case of the group), cut off (accusative), incomplete (the case of its job).
4. Say that إِلَّا means "only" in an incomplete sentence (ḥaṣr) and that it can be إِنْ + لَا, "if not".
5. Analyse لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ: lā that denies the kind, ism lā built on fatḥa, the missing khabar, ٱللَّهُ as a substitute, and the other readings.
6. Use غَيْر and سِوَىٰ: nouns; the word after them is genitive; غَيْر takes the case the word after إِلَّا would take.
7. Write the iʿrāb line of the word set apart and of إِلَّا.
8. Go through four verses word by word.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

Fifteen errors, then two claims dropped for lack of a source.

1. "Complete" was defined as "has a subject and a verb", and "incomplete" as "missing a subject or object". Complete means the group (mustathnā minhu) is named. This broke Rule 1, Rule 2, the tree and exercise 2 (`complete`, mistake 1).
2. The decision tree sent every complete negative sentence with a connected exception to **accusative** (Rule 1). The rule is: a substitute, with the case of the group; the accusative is also allowed (al-Ajurrumiyyah; 4:66) (`negative`, `choose`).
3. The shahada was called an "incomplete negative sentence". It names the group (إِلَٰهَ), so it is complete and negative (al-Jadwal on 47:19, 2:255, 20:8) (`shahada`).
4. ٱللَّهُ was called "the subject" and "the khabar of lā" in the same breath ("fills the SUBJECT position (khabar of لَا)"). It is a substitute for the hidden pronoun in the omitted khabar (`shahada`).
5. إِلَٰهَ was called accusative "but with no visible ending due to lā's special construction". It is built on a fatḥa, which is visible, and the books say "in the place of the accusative" (`shahada`, parse 3).
6. The old lesson said nominative ٱللَّهُ makes the shahada mean "Allah alone", that accusative ٱللَّهَ would be "WRONG theologically" and "suggest He is merely excluded" (exercise 3 and the theology box), and that 7:59 is "theologically equivalent". These are not grammar rules and no source gives them. Dropped. The case of ٱللَّهُ is explained by the grammar only (`shahada`, exercise 4).
7. مَا مَرَرْتُ بِأَحَدٍ إِلَّا زَيْدٍ was given as an incomplete negative sentence where Zayd replaces a missing word after بِ. It names the group (أَحَدٍ), so it is complete and negative, and Zayd is a substitute (exercise 3 (e) and (f) contrast the incomplete and the complete form).
8. Exercise 2.3, قَرَأَ ٱلطَّالِبُ إِلَّا كِتَابًا, was "Rule 3, or Rule 1 if connected". It is a positive sentence with no group, which gives no rule and no clear meaning. Replaced (exercise 2).
9. Rule 3 example جَاءَ ٱلطُّلَّابُ إِلَّا حِمَارًا was made up, unlabelled, and "a humorous construction". Replaced by 19:62 and 4:157.
10. The Al-Ikhlas section offered three verses with no exception particle as "exception structures", and said 112:4's أَحَدٌ is "conceptually similar to Rule 2". Dropped (items 2 and 22).
11. "Negative particle + indefinite noun creates universal negation ... (لَا, لَمْ, لَنْ)" overstated. Reworded to what the verses show: the extra min makes "any" (`min`, `disconnected`).
12. The forward link "L5.01 Rhetorical Analysis" pointed to `level-4/17-intro-balagha`. Now L4.17.
13. Every made-up example was unlabelled (جَاءَ ٱلطُّلَّابُ إِلَّا أَحْمَدَ, مَا قَرَأْتُ إِلَّا كِتَابًا, مَا مَرَرْتُ بِأَحَدٍ إِلَّا زَيْدٍ, لَا نَعْبُدُ سِوَىٰ ٱللَّهِ and the rest). Now each is labelled "Made-up example" or "made-up".
