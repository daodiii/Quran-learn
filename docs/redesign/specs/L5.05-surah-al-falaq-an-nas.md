## What the learner can do at the end

1. Read all eleven verses of Surah 113 and Surah 114 word by word: role, case, mark, reason, and the lesson that taught the point.
2. Say how al-Jadwal reads the frame: قُلْ an opening sentence, أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ … what is said, and every later phrase hanging on أَعُوذُ.
3. Read مَا (113:2) as a joining noun with the returning pronoun left out, and say that al-Jadwal also names a verbal-noun particle.
4. Read إِذَا (113:3, 113:5) as a time word without a condition, tied to شَرّ, with the clause in the place of the genitive; and say al-Durr ties it to أَعُوذُ.
5. Name غَاسِقٍ and حَاسِدٍ as doer words and ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ as a plural intensive form.
6. Read the chain رَبِّ, مَلِكِ, إِلَٰهِ (114:1 to 114:3) as al-Jadwal does (badal of رَبِّ, then badal of مَلِكِ), and name the other readings.
7. Say why ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ is a four-letter-root noun (not an intensive form), why يُوَسْوِسُ is Form I with four root letters (not Form II), and what the ṣila of ٱلَّذِى is.
8. Say that the last phrase is tied to a ḥāl in al-Jadwal, and that al-Durr numbers eight readings.
9. Say what al-Jadwal says about the sound of Surah 114 and that it names no figure; read counts without reading a reason off them.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. إِلَٰهِ was called a badal of رَبِّ. Al-Jadwal: a badal of مَلِكِ. Now in `n3`, `names`, `m1`, Ex 1.
2. مَلِكِ was "badal OR naʿt". Al-Jadwal also allows ʿaṭf bayān; al-Durr gives the same three. Now in `n2`, `names`.
3. ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ was called an intensive form. Al-Jadwal: a noun for the one who whispers, pattern فَعْلَال; al-Durr: a noun meaning the whispering. Now in `n4`, `m2`, Ex 2.
4. يُوَسْوِسُ was called Form II ("quadriliteral treatment"). L3.04: a Form I verb with four root letters; the Corpus gives no form tag. Now in `n5`, `m3`, Ex 6.
5. The clause مَا خَلَقَ was called a "relative clause modifying شَرّ". Al-Jadwal: خَلَقَ is the ṣila of مَا, and مَا is the muḍāf ilayh of شَرّ. Now in `f2`, `m4`, Ex 3.
6. إِذَا was called a "temporal particle that introduces a temporal clause". Al-Jadwal: a time word stripped of the condition, tied to the verbal noun شَرّ, with the clause in the place of the genitive. Now in `f3`, `idha`, Ex 5.
7. "Active participles emphasize ongoing action" and "intensive patterns emphasize repeated action". L3.19: a doer word has no time of its own. The claims are cut; `words` says so.
8. "Why Lord of daybreak? It invokes light overcoming darkness", "grammar reflects theology", "internal threats are more insidious", "each attribute is higher", the "historical context" of knots, and "Theme: external and internal". All unsourced; al-Jadwal has no balagha paragraph on these surahs. Cut. Counts remain (`counts`) with a note that they say what is there and not why.
9. The pattern of إِلَٰهِ (فِعَال) and of مَلِكِ (فَعِل) were given without a source. Cut.
10. The root of ٱلنَّاسِ was given as أ ن س. The Corpus root is ن و س; al-Jadwal gives none. The lesson uses the Corpus root and says so on the card.
11. ٱلْخَنَّاسِ "retreats when resisted". Al-Durr: he slinks away when Allah is mentioned. Now in `n4`.
12. The three related-lesson links had wrong labels: "L2.05 Idafah Construction" pointed to the idafah lesson (L2.08), and "L4.10 Negation Particles: Understanding refuge formulas" has nothing about refuge.
