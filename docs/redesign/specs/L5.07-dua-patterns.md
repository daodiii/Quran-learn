# Content spec: L5.07 Du'a Patterns in the Quran

Written 2026-10-28. Old lesson: `git show main:src/content/lessons/level-5/07-dua-patterns.mdx`. New lesson: `src/content/lessons/level-5/07-dua-patterns.mdx`. Level 5 rules: `docs/redesign/LEVEL-5-NOTES.md`. The lesson teaches the pattern of the supplication (دُعَاء): a call (رَبِّ, رَبَّنَا), a request (a command of prayer, or a ban with لَا and jazm), a reason with إِنَّ, and the "if" that sometimes sits inside a prayer.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Split a prayer of the Quran into a call, a request and a reason, and name each with al-Jadwal's word (مُنَادَى, جَوَاب النِّدَاء, تَعْلِيلِيَّة).
2. Say what رَبِّ and رَبَّنَا are (a muḍāf that is called, accusative, the word for "O" left out), why the case of رَبِّ cannot be seen, and that the Corpus tags 39 of 67 wrongly.
3. Read a command of prayer with a joined نَا or نِي, with one or two objects, and tell it from the same form said to a person.
4. Read a negative request (لَا and jazm), a request with its answer in jazm (20:28), and a prayer that has a condition (إِنْ, مَنْ) with a left-out answer.
5. Tell a time clause (بَعْدَ إِذْ) from a condition.
6. Say that the lesson's counts (137 calls, 61 followed by a command) show the shape is common and not a rule, and that no source we use ties رَبِّ or رَبَّنَا to one speaker or many.
7. Go through four prayers word by word.

## Corpus counts used in the lesson

Method: Python scripts over `src/data/morphology/quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt` (run 2026-10-28, kept in the writer's scratchpad: `l507-load.py`, `l507-voc.py`, `l507-imp.py`, `l507-calls.py`, `l507-lists.py`; not in the repo). **A call** is a word with the lemma رَبّ that has the pronoun "my" (`PRON:1S`) and ends in a kasra with no written yāʾ (رَبِّ), or has the pronoun "our" (`PRON:1P`) and a fatḥa on the ب (رَبَّنَا). The test found 67 and 72 words; **two** of the 72 are not calls and were left out by hand after reading them: 35:34 (`إِنَّ رَبَّنَا`, the subject of inna) and 25:21 (`نَرَىٰ رَبَّنَا`, the object of "see").

| Claim in the lesson | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 137 calls in 127 verses: 67 رَبِّ, 70 رَبَّنَا (blocks `forms`, `after`) | the test above | 67 + 70 = 137; 127 distinct verses; 40 surahs |
| The call is written with يَا twice (25:30, 43:88) | a `VOC` segment inside the call word or alone before it | 2, both رَبِّ; 0 of the 70 رَبَّنَا. (5:114 has `ٱللَّهُمَّ رَبَّنَآ`: the `VOC` is in the word before and it is a different word, so it is not counted) |
