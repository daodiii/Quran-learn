# Content spec: L5.13 Rhetorical Questions in the Quran

Written 2026-10-28. Lesson file: `src/content/lessons/level-5/13-rhetorical-questions.mdx`. It replaces the old lesson of the same name (file name, `title`, `level` and `order` kept; `kicker`, `titleArabic`, `template: steps` and a new description are added). Models: L4.19 and L4.10 (the lesson that promises this one), L4.18 (the question that does not ask), L5.06 (counts from the Corpus). Parses and names of questions follow al-Jadwal (Maḥmūd Ṣāfī); al-Durr al-Maṣūn (al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī) is named where it adds a reading. Counts are scripts over `src/data/morphology/quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt`.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Name twelve question words the Corpus tags (أَ, هَلْ, مَا, مَاذَا, مَن, كَيْفَ, أَيّ, أَنَّىٰ, كَمْ, أَيْنَ, مَتَىٰ, أَيَّانَ), say which are little words and which are nouns or words for manner, place and time, and give the job al-Jadwal gives each in a verse.
2. Take apart a hamza word (أَلَمْ, أَلَيْسَ, أَفَلَا, أَوَلَمْ, أَفَأَمِنتُمْ) into its pieces, and say what the verb after أَلَمْ shows (jazm).
3. Say which questions al-Jadwal names (confirmation, denial, rebuke, wonder, stirring interest), in which verses, and which questions have no name.
4. Say that a question can hold the sense of "no" or of a command (55:60, 5:91 in al-Jadwal; 2:255 in al-Durr).
5. Read the counts honestly: 946 tagged question words in 830 verses, and name the tag errors.
6. Sort a statement as al-Jadwal's, al-Durr's, or Quran Learn's own look at the text.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. The Arabic of "Al-Isra 17:68" was not that verse. It joined أَفَأَمِنتُم with words from 67:16 (ٱلَّذِي فِي ٱلسَّمَاءِ … فَإِذَا هِيَ تَمُورُ). Now the real 17:68, and 67:16 named in a Note. *Checked against Tanzil.*
2. 55:60 was called a question of confirmation, with a claim about the exclusivity word. Al-Jadwal names no figure; it says هَلْ holds the sense of "no".
3. 101:2 (مَا ٱلْقَارِعَةُ) was called "astonishment" and "how-question". It is "what?", a question noun with no figure in al-Jadwal.
4. 2:255 was called an "inkārī-taqrīrī hybrid". Al-Jadwal names no figure; al-Durr says a question in form with the meaning of "no" (L5.03 already says this).
5. أَلَمْ was called "double negation". It is a question and one negative (لَمْ).
6. "هَلْ precedes a verb": 59 of 93 Corpus tags only.
7. أَيّ called "an interrogative adjective that agrees with the noun". Al-Jadwal: a question noun, the first part of an idafah (6:81).
8. Exercise 4 gave "2:76 (أَوَ لَا)". 2:76 ends with أَفَلَا; the أَوَلَا is in 2:77. *Checked against Tanzil.*
9. Two links went to the wrong lessons: "L5.04-05" pointed to `01-full-irab-analysis`, and "L4.17-18" pointed to `01-hal-clauses`. Now `17-intro-balagha`, `18-figures-of-speech`, `10-negation-particles` and others, each opened and read.
10. The key glossed "rhetorical question" as istifhām (اِسْتِفْهَام). Istifhām is a question. "Rhetorical" is a teaching word here, used only where al-Jadwal names a figure.
11. The parse of نَشْرَحْ gave the plain form as يَشْرَحُ (the third person) for a first-person verb. The plain form is نَشْرَحُ; the root is ش ر ح.
12. Exercise 3 compared with a declarative "نَشْرَحْنَا صَدْرَكَ". That is not a form of the verb (the past is شَرَحْنَا). Cut.
13. "Rhetorical type … Effect: ASSERTS monotheism" (52:35) and "Effect: Allah emphasizes that such security is foolish" (17:68): theological effects no source here states. Cut. Al-Jadwal's names (rebuke) stay.
14. The row for أَنَّىٰ said "adverb of place/manner". Al-Jadwal reads it as meaning كَيْفَ (a ḥāl), with a footnote for "when".
15. 12:51 was glossed with "the king genuinely asks". Kept as an observation about the text (the women answer in the next words), with no type claim.
