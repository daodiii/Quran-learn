# Content spec: L4.15 Hamzated Verbs (al-Mahmuz)

Written 2026-10-28. This is the old L4.15 "Hamzated Verbs (al-Mahmuz)", kept at the same number. It follows the definition of a complete lesson (end of `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`). The lesson is `src/content/lessons/level-4/15-hamzated-verbs.mdx`. It closes the weak-verb run L4.11 to L4.15 and stays on verbs with a hamza as a root letter.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say where the hamza sits in a root (first, middle or last) and tell a hamzated verb from a verb with an added opening أَ (Form IV, the "I" prefix, the question hamza).
2. Write the hamza on the right seat: at the start (always an alif), at the end (the vowel of the letter before decides), in the middle (the strongest of the two vowels: kasra, ḍamma, fatḥa, sukūn).
3. Build the past and the present of a first-letter hamza verb (أَخَذَ, يَأْخُذُ) and read the seats of سَأَلَ and قَرَأَ in many forms.
4. Give the command of a hamzated verb: which ones keep the hamza (وَأْمُرْ, ٱقْرَأْ, ٱئْتِ) and which drop it (خُذْ, كُلُوا۟, سَلْ), with Corpus counts.
5. Read Form IV from أ م ن: two hamzas join into ءَا (ā), إِي (ī) or أُو (ū); the "I" form ءَامُرُ, ءَامَنُ; the present يُؤْمِنُ.
6. Read ٱتَّخَذَ, يُؤَاخِذُ and Form X (ٱسْتَـْٔذَنَ) as forms of hamzated roots.
7. Read the passive (سُئِلَ, أُمِرْتُ, يُؤْخَذُ) and the participles (ءَاخِذ, سَآئِل, مَأْكُول, مَسْـُٔول, مُؤْمِن).
8. Recognise roots with a hamza and a weak letter (جَآءَ, شَآءَ, أَتَىٰ, رَءَا, يَئِسَ) and send them to L4.12 to L4.14.
9. Tell a hamza in a word from a hamza in the root (سَمَآء, دُعَآء, ٱبْتِغَآء).
10. Go through four verses word by word.

1. `past`: `Conjugation` of أَخَذَ, eight forms: أَخَذَ, أَخَذَتْ, أَخَذُوا, أَخَذْنَ, أَخَذْتَ, أَخَذْتُمْ, أَخَذْتُ, أَخَذْنَا. In the Quran as Form I: he (3:81), she (as أَخَذَتْهُ, 29:40), they (women) (4:21), you all (3:81), I (13:32), we (29:40). Not in the Quran as Form I: they (men) أَخَذُوا, you (one man) أَخَذْتَ (only the Form VIII ٱتَّخَذُوا۟ and similar). The lesson says six of eight.
2. `present`: `Conjugation` of يَأْخُذُ: يَأْخُذُ (18:79), تَأْخُذُ (she, 2:255 as تَأْخُذُهُۥ), تَأْخُذُ (you, one man: only the jussive تَأْخُذْ in 20:94), ءَاخُذُ (I: a teaching form for أَأْخُذُ, no word in the Quran; the same rule as ءَامُرُ in 12:32), نَأْخُذُ (only the subjunctive نَّأْخُذَ, 12:79), يَأْخُذُونَ (7:169), يَأْخُذْنَ (teaching form), تَأْخُذُونَ (4:20 as أَتَأْخُذُونَهُۥ). The lesson says the Quran has no "I take".
3. `many-seats`: two tables of real forms of س أ ل and ق ر أ (all words are in the Corpus); the seat column uses ordinary-print rules and says where the Quran draws the hamza differently (stroke, or alone before a wāw).
4. `command`: table of present → command for أَخَذَ, أَكَلَ, سَأَلَ, أَمَرَ, قَرَأَ, with Corpus counts; the starting form اُؤْمُرْ (from al-Jadwal's اومر); ٱئْتِ with a kasra on the helper alif (the middle vowel of تَأْتِي); خُذْ, كُلْ, مُرْ as grammar-book forms (كُلْ and مُرْ are not in the Quran).
5. `form4`: tiles (ءَامَنَ, يُؤْمِنُ, ءَامِنْ, إِيمَٰنُكُمْ, ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ, all in the Corpus) and the table "two hamzas": the "written first" column (أَأْمَنَ, أَأْمِنْ, أَأْمُرُ, أَأْتَى, إِئْمَان, أُؤْتُوا) is made up by the rule; al-Jadwal gives أَأْمَنَ (2:3) and أؤتوا (2:101); the rest follow the same rule. إِئْمَان → إِيمَان is the lesson's own application (no al-Jadwal line).
6. `other-forms`: يُؤَاخِذُ (2:225), ٱتَّخَذَ (25:43), ٱسْتَـْٔذَنَ (24:59), with the ordinary-print form ٱسْتَأْذَنَ.
7. `passive`: table of seven passive forms from the Corpus (أُمِرْتُ, يُؤْخَذُ, تُؤْمَرُونَ, سُئِلَ, أُوتُوا۟), and the made-up أُخِذَ as the plain passive (33:61 has أُخِذُوا۟).
8. `participle`: ءَاخِذ, سَآئِل, مَسْـُٔول, مَأْكُول, مُؤْمِن (all Corpus).
9. `double`: the table of four double roots with Corpus counts; the new line on ي ء س (Corpus: 10 verb words).
10. Practice answers: exercise 2 writes the hamza alone (سُءِلَ ...) and asks for the seat; the answers are ordinary-print words; exercise 3 (c) يَقْرَأُ and exercise 5 (a to e) are teaching forms.

## The lesson, step by step

5 steps, 32 blocks, 20 checks, 11 exercises, 4 word-by-word panels.

**Step 1: Three places** (hero; 4 blocks)
1. `overview`: Start, formula "a root with a hamza + a verb pattern = a hamzated verb".
2. `see`: 7:199, three commands (dropped hamza, kept hamza, added hamza). Check 1.
3. `what`: the definition, hamza as a full consonant, counts 2,951 of 19,356 (15.2%). Check 2.
4. `places`: first, middle, last; table of roots with counts (first 1,711, middle 491, last 749). Check 3.

**Step 2: Seats** (5 blocks)
5. `seat-idea`: the five seats; the idea "the hamza matches a vowel"; the note that the rules are a teaching summary. Check 4.
6. `seat-start`: the start, always an alif; after وَ. Check 5.
7. `seat-end`: the end, the letter before decides (fatḥa alif, kasra yāʾ seat, ḍamma wāw, sukūn or long vowel alone). Check 6.
8. `seat-middle`: the middle, the strongest vowel (kasra, ḍamma, fatḥa, sukūn); the cases with a long ā. Check 7.
9. `seat-quran`: the Quran's own spelling (stroke, alone, ءَا); the madda of L1.02. Check 8.

**Step 3: The verb** (9 blocks)

**Step 4: Put it together** (11 blocks)

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

15 errors fixed:

1. **أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ (108:1) used as the hook of a "hamzated root ع ط ي", with "Wait, where is the hamza?".** The root is ع ط و and the verb is not hamzated. The hamza of إِعْطَاءٌ is a root و turned into a hamza at the end (the same change as in دُعَآء). Dropped as the hook; `mistake-word` shows this change.
2. **Rule 4 "sukūn: on the line or follows the previous vowel".** A hamza with a sukūn follows the vowel of the letter before it (يَأْمُرُ alif, يُؤْمِنُ wāw, بِئْسَ yāʾ seat). The line is for the end of a word (after a sukūn or a long vowel).
3. **"مَسَائِلُ: the letter before (ا) has kasra effect".** The kasra is on the hamza; the alif is a long ā. Fixed in `seat-middle` (سَآئِلٌ: a kasra after a long ā).
4. **"يَقْرَءُ: final hamza with preceding sukūn, various spellings acceptable".** يَقْرَأُ has a fatḥa on the ر, so the hamza is on an alif; the Quran has يَقْرَءُونَ and نَّقْرَؤُهُۥ. Fixed in `many-seats` and exercise 3.
5. **One rule for all places (compare the hamza's vowel with the vowel before it).** That is the rule of the middle. At the start the seat is always an alif; at the end only the letter before counts. There are three rules now.
6. **"Final hamza: seat consistency, most predictable, seat rarely changes".** The seat of the hamza of ق ر أ is an alif, a wāw, a yāʾ seat or alone, depending on the ending (`many-seats`).
7. **The "regular" commands ٱِئْخُذْ, ٱِئْكُلْ, ٱِئْمُرْ (helper alif with a kasra, hamza on a yāʾ).** The vowel of the helper alif follows the middle vowel of the present: a ḍamma, so the starting form is اُؤْمُرْ (al-Jadwal 20:132). The old forms are removed.
8. **"كُلْ وَٱشْرَبْ" quoted from 2:187, and "مُرْ: similar command forms".** 2:187 has وَكُلُوا۟ وَٱشْرَبُوا۟ (plural). The Quran has no كُلْ (one man) and no مُرْ: its 32 commands of أَكَلَ all have an ending and none has a hamza, and its 4 commands of Form I of أَمَرَ are all وَأْمُرْ. `command` and `mistake-quran` say so.
9. **"ٱِسْأَلْ: regular, hamza maintained" as the only command of سَأَلَ.** The Quran has both سَلْ (2 words) and forms with the hamza (14). Fixed in `command`.
10. **The section "Examples from Al-Kawthar".** It holds 96:1, 70:1, 9:103 and 97:1, and none is from Al-Kawthar. Replaced by the verses of the lesson.
11. **Next steps: L4.17 and L4.18, skipping L4.16.** The next lesson is L4.16.
12. **Example 1 headed "Initial hamza root" for ٱقْرَأْ.** The root ق ر أ has its hamza last. The same example says the hamza is "on the line" and also "on alif".
13. **Example 3 headed "أَمَرَ" explains خُذْ from أَخَذَ, and writes "*اُؤْخُذْ" while the table before it had ٱِئْخُذْ.** One account now, from al-Jadwal.
14. **أَامُرُ for "I order".** Two letters for one ā. The Quran has ءَامُرُهُۥ (12:32): the two hamzas join (`form4`, `mistake-two-alifs`).
15. **رَأَسَ, "he presided", under the middle-hamza roots.** There is no verb from ر أ س in the Corpus (only the nouns رَأْس and رُءُوس). Dropped.

## What is not checked

- The English meanings under the verses and the Arabic parse lines are unreviewed by an Arabic speaker.
- **The spelling rules for the seat of the hamza (start, end, middle; kasra > ḍamma > fatḥa > sukūn)** are a teaching summary of the standard spelling rules. No approved source in this project states them; the lesson says so in a note. Each example is a word in the Tanzil text, checked by hand against the rule, and the Quran's differences are shown (`seat-quran`). The wording "the hamza matches a vowel" is the lesson's own.

The helper-alif rule for ٱئْتِ (a kasra because the middle vowel of تَأْتِي is a kasra) is an extension of al-Jadwal's rule for اومر.
