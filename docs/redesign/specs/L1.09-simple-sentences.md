# Content spec: L1.09 Two Kinds of Sentence

Written 2026-10-28. Rewrite of `src/content/lessons/level-1/09-simple-sentences.mdx` in the stepped layout. It follows the definition of a complete lesson (end of `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`). L2.01 and L2.03 point back to this lesson ("You met both in L1.09"; "In L1.09 you learned to sort sentences by their first word"), so this lesson teaches exactly that: sorting a sentence by its first word, and Arabic has no word for "is" in a nominal sentence. Level 2 depth (five kinds of khabar, khabar muqaddam, the three kinds of doer, kāna, inna) is only previewed, each with the lesson that teaches it.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Sort a sentence from the Quran by its first word, skipping a joining or negating little word (وَ، فَ، لَا): noun first is nominal, verb first is verbal.
2. Know that a pronoun counts as a noun for this test.
3. Say that Arabic has no word for "is" in a nominal sentence, and that nothing is hidden.
4. Name the parts of a nominal sentence (mubtadaʾ, khabar) and of a verbal sentence (verb, fāʿil, optional mafʿūl bihi).
5. Say that the verb comes first in a verbal sentence, and that the doer can be hidden inside the verb (قُلْ).
6. Avoid four mistakes: judging by meaning, counting the little word, calling the doer a mubtadaʾ, expecting the doer first.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. "Every Arabic sentence uses one of these two blueprints. No exceptions", and "Arabic is more flexible than English": too strong. The lesson says "this course uses these two kinds" and teaches the first-word test, which is what L2.01 and L2.03 rely on.
2. It said the "is/are" is "IMPLIED" and "not written". That makes learners hunt for a hidden verb. L2.01 says nothing is missing. The new lesson says there is no word for "is" and nothing is hidden, and warns against "implied is" in a Note.
3. The comparison table says a nominal sentence "describes what something IS" and a verbal sentence "what something DOES", and "when to use: static descriptions / actions". This is not the rule, and the Quran breaks it (55:1-2 is nominal and about an action; a verbal sentence can be a khabar). The first word is the rule (Mistake 1). L2.03 says the same.
4. On 109:1 it called "the rest of the verse" the object of قُلْ. Al-Jadwal calls the call "what is said" (*maqūl al-qawl*). The lesson now says "what is said".
5. Exercise 2 said "the predicate can be two words" for غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ. They are two khabars (the second a "second khabar" in al-Jadwal). Fixed in Exercise 2.
6. Exercise 2's Arabic example had no verse reference, and "ٱللَّهُ رَحِيمٌ" was a made-up sentence presented in the same style as verses. Replaced by real, referenced verses.
7. The 109:6 callout called the flipped order "advanced" and "don't worry". Kept as a preview with the lesson that teaches it (L2.01).
8. 109:2's old analysis said the subject "(implied in the verb ending)" without saying the doer is hidden inside the verb. The new lesson names it (hidden doer, `verbal-parts`).
9. Old glossary links pointed to `/resources/glossary/#...` anchors. Replaced by glosses on first use and the `Start` terms list.
