## What the learner can do at the end

1. Spot a mafʿūl muṭlaq and say what it does: it says how the action happened and does not name the one acted on.
2. Name its three jobs: stress (bare verbal noun), kind (with an adjective or an owner), number (one time, a dual, a number).
3. Say that the usual case is the verb's own verbal noun in naṣb (*al-Ajurrumiyyah*'s chapter on the maṣdar), and read the stand-ins: another noun of the same root (نَبَاتًا), كُلّ before a verbal noun (4:129), a number (24:4 ثَمَٰنِينَ), and the book's "meaning only" kind (جَلَسْتُ قُعُودًا).
4. Read a verbal noun that replaces a left-out verb (فَضَرْبَ ٱلرِّقَابِ, مَعَاذَ ٱللَّهِ).
5. Tell a mafʿūl muṭlaq from an object that has the same root (2:79) and from a ḥāl that is a verbal noun (7:55), and know where books read it two ways.
6. Write the endings: -an, -a before a pronoun, -ayni, -īna; and write the iʿrāb line.
7. Go through four verses word by word (4:164, 99:1, 4:119, 24:4).

## The lesson, step by step

5 steps, 26 blocks, 18 `Check`s.

**Step 1: The action again** (hero; 5 blocks)
1. `overview`: Start, formula "a verb plus its own verbal noun equals a stronger or clearer action".
2. `see`: 4:164 (end); al-Durr on the stress. Check 1.
3. `name`: *al-Ajurrumiyyah*'s chapter on the maṣdar; mafʿūl bihi and muṭlaq. Check 2.
4. `same-root`: the four teaching forms and a table with the Corpus attestation. Check 3.
5. `marks`: three signs; the three jobs named. Check 4.

**Step 2: Three jobs** (4 blocks)
6. `emphasis`: 52:9, 52:10; 89:21 named. Check 5.
7. `kind`: 33:11; 33:41 and 3:37 in tiles. Check 6.
8. `idafa`: 99:1; al-Durr on "the shaking it deserves"; made-up أَكْرِمِ ٱلتَّقِيَّ إِكْرَامَهُ. Check 7.
9. `number`: 69:14, 9:101; note on "one time". Check 8.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. **24:4.** The old lesson called جَلْدَةً the mafʿūl muṭlaq and gave "verb + number + masdar" as the pattern. al-Jadwal reads ثَمَٰنِينَ as the stand-in (its number) and جَلْدَةً as the tamyīz (`eighty`, parse 4, exercise 6).
2. **4:129.** The old analysis called ٱلْمَيْلِ the mafʿūl muṭlaq and, in the same place, كُلَّ; its translation read "incline completely toward them with all inclination". al-Jadwal and al-Durr: كُلّ is the stand-in because it is attached to a verbal noun, and ٱلْمَيْلِ is the muḍāf ilayh in the genitive (`kull`).
3. **99:1, the ending.** The old lesson said the accusative ending is "implied before the pronoun". The fatḥa is visible, and the tanwīn is gone because the word is attached to the pronoun (`idafa`, parse 2).
4. **99:1, the meaning.** The old lesson said "its destined, final earthquake" and called ـهَا "possessive". al-Durr says the verbal noun is attached to its doer and means "the shaking it deserves". The lesson gives that reading with its source.
5. **The same-root rule.** The old lesson said "MUST be the masdar of the verb (or from the same root)", which contradicts itself and is too strict. *Al-Ajurrumiyyah* allows a verbal noun with the same meaning but another root, and the Quran has same-root nouns that are not the verb's regular verbal noun (71:17, 4:119). The lesson says "usually" and teaches the stand-ins (`same-root`, `two-types`, `other-form`).
6. **Exercise 4, item 3.** نَصَرَ with عَوْنًا was "NOT a valid mafʿūl muṭlaq... would be a direct object". By the book's second kind (the same meaning, another wording), it fits like جَلَسْتُ قُعُودًا. The item now says so (exercise 4 (c)).
7. **"Direct object has a DIFFERENT root".** 2:79 يَكْتُبُونَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ has the same root and the object is an object (al-Jadwal: mafʿūl bihi). The lesson makes the question the test, and the root only the usual sign (`m-root`, exercise 2).
8. **"Always accusative with fatha or tanwin".** The marks also include the dual -ayni (مَرَّتَيْنِ), the tens -īna and -a before a pronoun; and after كُلّ the verbal noun is genitive and is not the mafʿūl muṭlaq (`m-case`).
9. **"Wrote intensively / did writing" (and "shaken to its very core").** A bare verbal noun stresses the verb and makes it certain; it does not say "intense". Severity comes from an adjective (33:11). The lesson uses al-Durr's reading of 4:164 and 52:9 (`emphasis`, `m-english`).
10. **"Completing... the five major accusative types beyond the direct object".** The classical list has five objects including the direct object. L4.08 and L4.09 add two more beyond this lesson; the time or place word is another. The lesson says "two more objects", and does not count to five.
11. **"Form I quadriliteral... by Allah" (99:1).** Not a grammar error, but an unsupported addition: the old text added "by Allah" to a passive verb whose doer is not named. The lesson says the doer is not named (parse 2) and does not name a form.

Count: 11 items, 10 errors and 1 unsupported addition.
