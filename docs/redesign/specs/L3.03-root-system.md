# Content spec: L3.03 The Root System

Written 2026-10-28. Lesson file: `src/content/lessons/level-3/03-root-system.mdx` (Level 3, order 3; it was L3.01 before the reorder, old file on `main`: `level-3/01-root-system.mdx`). Follows the definition of a complete lesson in `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`. Sources: al-Jadwal (iʿrāb and ṣarf notes), the Quranic Arabic Corpus (roots, verb forms, counts), Tanzil (text).

L1.05 (`05-root-system-preview.mdx`) already gave the first look: root, pattern, the families of ك ت ب, ع ل م and ر ح م, and five limits. This lesson goes deeper and does not reuse L1.05's main verses (6:12, 2:282, 78:29, 7:157, 52:4 to 52:6, 83:9, 2:163, 2:32). Earlier lessons promise it: L1.04, L1.05, L1.11 (patterns, and the shared root و ل د) and L2.12 (the pattern فَوْعَل of ٱلْكَوْثَر) point here for "how to find a root and name a pattern".

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Peel a Quran word (little prefixes, article, pronoun ending, tense prefix, doer ending, plural and case endings) and find its root.
2. Read and write a pattern with the scale ف ع ل: extra letters, a doubled letter, four-letter roots (ف ع ل ل).
3. Test a root by pouring it back into the pattern.
4. Look a root up in a dictionary in the order of the Arabic letters.
5. Name the ten verb forms (I to X), with the scale word and a Quran example of each, and say which lesson teaches each.
6. Name the four noun jobs that have lessons (doer, done-to, action, place) and read the describing patterns al-Jadwal gives.
7. Name the six kinds of root (sound, doubled, assimilated, hollow, defective, hamzated), spot a hidden letter by four clues, and say where each is taught (L4.11 to L4.15). No weak-verb conjugation is taught here.
8. Avoid five mistakes (pattern letter counted as root letter, doubled letter counted twice, long vowel mistakes, a root looked for in every word, lookup under the first letter).

Points that need care, handled in the lesson:

- **ٱللَّهُ** has the root أ ل ه in the Corpus. The lesson gives the tag and says scholars differ on the name's origin.
- **al-Jadwal calls صَوَّرَ "four-letter" (الرباعي)** because the doubled letter counts twice. A Know note says the root has three letters and that a four-letter root is a different thing.
- The weak-root terms (assimilated, hollow, defective, hamzated, doubled) are plain English, matching the L4.11 to L4.15 titles. Al-Jadwal uses مُعْتَلّ ("weak") and مِثَال/نَاقِص in its notes; only مُعْتَلّ appears in the lesson (as a term).

## The lesson, step by step

1. **Root, pattern, scale** (hero, 5 blocks): overview (Start, formula); one root two shapes (96:5, check 1); from sentences to word-building, naḥw and ṣarf and the three skills (check 2); the scale ف ع ل with the ك ت ب table (check 3); extra letters and a doubled letter (62:2, table, check 4).
2. **Peel the word, find the root** (5): what to peel, with the table of pieces and the lessons that teach each; worked example وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ in five steps (check 5); worked example, three names of Allah (59:24); pour the root back (check 6); looking a root up in a dictionary, letter order, the 59.2% figure (check 7).
3. **Patterns, forms and roots that change** (6): ten forms of the verb, table with counts and lessons, two al-Jadwal reading tips (check 8); nouns have patterns, four jobs (40:3); describing patterns (34:26); four-letter roots (114:4, check 9); roots that change, the six kinds (36:52); a hidden letter and its four clues (2:183, check 10).
4. **Five mistakes, four verses word by word** (9): mistakes 1 to 5 (check 11 in mistake 4); Parse 2:268, 110:3, 59:24, 64:11.
5. **Practice** (3): peel, root and pattern (exercises 1 to 5); lookup, forms and hidden letters (6 to 11); summary, related reading, Sources.
