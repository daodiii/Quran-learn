# Content spec: L2.09 Adjectives and the Nouns They Describe

Written 2026-10-27. Lesson file: `src/content/lessons/level-2/09-adjective-agreement.mdx`. Every parse follows al-Jadwal (Maḥmūd Ṣāfī); al-Durr al-Maṣūn read for 7:158 and 2:2.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Name the naʿt (adjective) and the manʿūt (noun described) and say the adjective follows its noun.
2. Show that the adjective matches in case (with the mark, also a hidden -u), definiteness, gender and number.
3. Say what counts as definite (al-Ajurrumiyyah's five kinds), including definite without *al-*.
4. Use the feminine singular (or feminine plural) adjective for a plural of things; dual and sound masculine plural endings.
5. Pick the right noun when two come before the adjective; give one noun several adjectives; know a "which" clause can be a naʿt.
6. Tell a naʿt from a khabar (L2.01), from an idafah (L2.08) and from a badal.
7. Go through four verses word by word.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. Example 2 (1:7) called غَيْرِ both "appositive" and "first part of an idafah" in one entry. Not verified against al-Jadwal; the example was dropped.
2. Example 5 was Al-Fatiha 1:2 "to show feminine agreement", but the verse has no feminine adjective, and the text admitted it. Replaced by 5:21, 4:1, 14:24.
3. "Same Arabic words, completely different grammatical relationships" with the phrase "different cases: nominative + nominative" (a contradiction). Removed.
4. "If BOTH have ال it's an adjective" was given as a rule. A khabar can be definite too (57:3 in L2.01, and 4:13 here). Now a clue plus three questions.
5. The table said a khabar is "separated from the subject" and an adjective "always follows the noun directly" as a test. Neither is reliable. Removed.
6. The non-human plural rule was labelled "Level 3" and the old text called ٱلْكُتُبُ ٱلْجُدُدُ wrong or "classical". The Quran also uses a feminine plural (2:184 مَعْدُودَٰتٍ, أُخَرَ). Now explained here.
7. Bismillah was said to have the adjectives "in a prepositional phrase with iḍāfah", and the old lesson called رَحِيم an active participle. It is not a participle (faʿīl pattern). The participle claim was dropped.
8. Exercise 3 gave "ٱلْبَيْتُ ٱلْكَبِيرُ جَمِيلٌ" with the reason "subject (mubtadaʾ)" without saying the whole phrase is the mubtadaʾ; the made-up sentences were not labelled. They are now.
