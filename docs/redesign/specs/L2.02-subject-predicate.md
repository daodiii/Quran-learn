# Content spec: L2.02 The Two Parts, Matched (Subject and Predicate)

Written 2026-10-27. The lesson is `src/content/lessons/level-2/02-subject-predicate.mdx`. It builds on L2.01 and does not repeat it. Every parse follows al-Jadwal (Maḥmūd Ṣāfī); al-Durr al-Maṣūn is cited for one disputed point (48:29). The English meanings and Arabic parse lines have not been read by a person who knows Arabic.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Make the khabar match the mubtadaʾ in gender (feminine -atun) and number: dual -āni, men -ūna, women -ātun.
2. Choose the khabar for a broken plural of people (a plural) and of things (feminine singular), and know that a plain noun khabar need not match.
3. Name the kinds of definite mubtadaʾ (name, al-, pronoun, pointing word, "who" word, idafah).
4. Say when an indefinite mubtadaʾ is allowed: khabar first, an adjective, a question, a wish or prayer.
5. Tell a definite khabar ("who or what") from an indefinite one ("what kind").
6. Read a khabar that is a phrase (tied to a left-out word) or a sentence, and find its link word (rābiṭ).
7. Go through four verses word by word.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. "The predicate must agree in NUMBER, GENDER, and DEFINITENESS": contradicts its own example (ٱلْعِلْمُ نُورٌ). Definiteness need not match; the lesson teaches what a definite or indefinite khabar means.
2. Exercise 3 makes ٱلَّذِينَ in 2:3 a mubtadaʾ. Al-Jadwal reads it as an adjective of ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ.
3. The same exercise calls the doer of يُؤْمِنُونَ "hidden". It is the visible و.
4. "Example 2" (103:3) is an exception clause, not a nominal sentence, yet is taught as one.
5. "Example 3" is titled "prepositional predicate" but 112:2 has a single-word khabar.
6. "Example 1" says the prepositional khabar of *inna* is "nominative"; a phrase has no case (it stands in the place of the nominative).
7. "Verbs don't take case" (103:3): a present-tense verb is nominative (rafʿ); only the past tense and the imperative do not show case.
8. Agreement rules listed only singular, dual and sound plural; broken plurals and plurals of things were missing.
9. The nominative marks list left out the sound feminine plural.
10. Uses Al-Fatiha 1:2 and Al-Ikhlas 112:2 as main examples; replaced.
11. Typo characters in "ṣilah" and "rābiṭ" in the old file.
