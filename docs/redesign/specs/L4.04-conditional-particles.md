# Content spec: L4.04 Types of Conditional Particles

Written 2026-10-28. This is the old L4.04 "Types of Conditional Particles", kept at the same number. It follows the definition of a complete lesson (end of `docs/redesign/CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`). The lesson is `src/content/lessons/level-4/04-conditional-particles.mdx`. L4.04 is the **words of condition** (which word, what it means, what it does to the verbs). L4.03 is the **sentence pattern** (condition, answer, the فَ), written at the same time by another writer; this lesson links to it and does not teach the فَ.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Name the condition words of the Quran and sort them into two families: those that cut both verbs short (jazm: إِنْ, مَنْ, مَا, مَهْمَا, أَيْنَمَا, حَيْثُمَا, أَيّ) and those that do not (إِذَا, لَوْ, لَوْلَا, لَمَّا).
2. Say what jazm looks like on a present verb (sukūn, lost nūn, lost weak letter) and why a past verb after a jazm word shows nothing ("in the place of jazm").
3. Say that مَنْ and مَا are nouns with a job in the sentence (mubtadaʾ, object), and that إِنْ is a little word.
4. Read إِذَا and إِنْ as a tendency (expected against open), with exceptions, and not as a rule.
5. Use لَوْ for what is not so (not "impossible"), with a past or a present verb; tell لَوْلَا "if it were not for" from the urging لَوْلَا.
6. Tell the two لَمَّا apart ("when" before a past verb, "not yet" before a verb in jazm), the three إِنْ (if, not, and إِنَّ "indeed"), and the condition مَنْ/مَا from the joining noun, question and "not".
7. Go through four verses word by word.

## Corpus counts used in the lesson

Method: a script over `src/data/morphology/quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt`, one row per Corpus segment (STEM). A word counts as a condition word when its segment is tagged `COND` (`إِنْ` lemma `<in`, `law`, `man`, `maA`, `lawolaA^`, and the others), as a time word for إِذَا (`T`, lemma `<i*aA`) and لَمَّا (`T`, lemma `lam~aA`). "The word right after it" is the first segment with `STEM` in the next word of the same verse. A present verb tagged `SUBJ` right after إِنْ is counted as present (these are -ū and -ā forms that lose the nūn, which look the same in both moods); the 30 cases after إِنْ are all 2MP or 3MP. After لَمْ the next verb is looked at.

| Word | Tag counts | Right after it |
|---|---|---|
| إِنْ | COND 578, NEG 114, CERT 5 | past verb 384; present verb 186 (116 tagged jussive, 30 tagged subjunctive, 9 tagged indicative, 31 after لَمْ with 30 jussive and 1 subjunctive); noun or other 8 |
| مَنْ | REL 650, COND 184, INTG 37 | COND: past 86, present 98 (90 plus 8 after لَمْ) |
| مَا | REL 1,476, NEG 705, PREV 162, INTG 95, SUB 83, SUP 21, COND 23 | COND: past 9, present 14 (12 jussive, 2 indicative) |
| إِذَا | T 405, SUR 3, COND 1 | past 321 (79%); present 19 (16 indicative, 2 subjunctive, 1 jussive after لَمْ in 7:203); command 2; noun or other 63 |
| لَوْ | COND 185, SUB 16 | COND: past 121 (65%); present 31; noun, أَنَّ and other 33 |
| لَوْلَا | COND 35, EXH 40 | COND: next word not a verb 34, past verb 1. EXH: verb 34, time word 5, other 1 |

| أَيْنَ, حَيْثُ مَا, مَهْمَا, أَيّ | COND 3, 2, 1, 1 | not counted further |
| مَتَىٰ, أَنَّىٰ, أَيَّانَ | INTG 9; INTG 27 and N 1; INTG 5 and T 1 | none tagged as a condition |

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

The old L4.04 is not in `CONTENT-INVENTORY.md`, so these were found by reading it against the Corpus, al-Jadwal and al-Ajurrumiyyah. 14 errors.

1. "Only three particles trigger jussive mood: إِنْ, مَنْ, مَا" (table, "key observation", mistake box, summary). *al-Ajurrumiyyah* lists twelve condition words of jazm (and إِذَا for poetry); the Quran has seven in use (`name`, `words`).
2. The table's "Verb tense" column: "Present" for إِنْ, مَنْ, مَا; "Past, future meaning" for إِذَا; "past only" for لَوْ. In the Corpus a past verb follows إِنْ 384 times in 578, and مَنْ 86 in 184. Replaced by counts (`in-present`, `man-ma`).
3. "لَوْ always uses past tense", with "❌ Wrong: لَوْ أَكُونُ غَنِيًّا". 31 conditional لَوْ in the Corpus have a present verb after them, and 49:7 is one (`law-present`, `mistake-law`).
4. "لَوْ = impossible". لَوْ says what is not so or did not happen: 6:149 "if He had willed" is not impossible (`law`, `mistake-law`).
5. The certainty line (impossible, uncertain, expected) with three words on one axis. It mixes two kinds of word and states a tendency as the core law. Replaced by two families and a tendency (`name`, `idha-in`).
6. "إِذَا is NOT a conditional particle", "the single most common error", and "❌ Wrong: إِذَا دَرَسْتُ أَنْجَحُ". Al-Jadwal calls إِذَا a time word that carries the sense of a condition. The made-up sentence is good Arabic (`idha`, `mistake-same`).
7. "Why past tense? … so sure to happen, it's spoken of as if it already occurred": a reason with no source. Replaced by the count (321 of 405) (`idha`).
8. مَنْ and مَا called "particles". They are nouns (اسْم شَرْط) with a job in the sentence (`man-ma`).
9. 2:272 يُوَفَّ: "no final diacritic, indicating passive construction". The jazm sign is the lost weak letter (يُوَفَّىٰ). Same for يُجْزَ (4:123), which had no sign named (Exercise 5, `signs`).
10. 12:77: "they don't actually believe Benjamin stole" (no source) and "قَدْ makes it a nominal-like construction requiring فَ". The answer is a past verb with قَدْ; the فَ is taught in L4.03 (Exercise 3).
11. 12:60: "nūn deleted (تَأْتُونَنِى → تَأْتُونِى)". The verb's own nūn is gone; the nūn you see is the protecting nūn before the ي of "me" (Exercise 3).
12. Links and the quotes. The forward links "L5.07" and "L5.09" pointed to the lesson itself; "L3.05 Past & Present Tense" and "L3.07 Subjunctive Mood" had the wrong title. The headings of the Surah Yusuf examples did not match the verses ("Father's Wisdom on Certainty" over 12:65, where no father speaks). 12:69 was quoted with فَلَمَّا; the verse has وَلَمَّا. After NFC normalisation 7 of the 11 quotes differ from Tanzil (6 in a mark: ۭ or ۢ on a tanwin in 17:104, 2:101, 4:123, 2:272, 12:77, and an extra sukūn in 47:7; 1 in a word: 12:69).
13. Exercise 1(a) gave يَأْتِيَ (a subjunctive form) with the options إِنْ, إِذَا, لَوْ; none of them fits that form. The made-up sentences of the old lesson were not labelled.
14. The old lesson did not teach the second لَمَّا ("not yet", a word of jazm in al-Ajurrumiyyah's list), the "not" إِنْ promised by L4.03, or the other condition words (مَهْمَا, أَيْنَمَا, حَيْثُمَا, أَيّ, لَوْلَا). Now taught.

## What is not checked

- The English meanings under the verses and the Arabic parse lines are unreviewed by an Arabic speaker.
- **The إِذَا/إِنْ tendency** ("إِذَا for what you expect, إِنْ for what is open") is a teaching summary. No approved source in this project states it as a rule, and 21:34 breaks it. The lesson says so (`idha-in`, Exercise 4).
- All Corpus counts are Quran Learn counts of tags. The tags have errors (listed above). The percentages (79%, 65%) are of those tags.
- The claim that Levels 1 to 3 teach نَعُدْ and similar forms: only L3.07 shows the weak-middle jazm in a short line, and the lesson sends the rule to L4.12.
- The claim that مَتَىٰ, أَيَّانَ and أَنَّىٰ are question words only is from the Corpus tags, not from a reading of every verse.
- The quoted lines of *al-Ajurrumiyyah* are re-vowelled by Quran Learn from the Wikisource text (the two lines were fetched from `ar.wikisource.org`, "الآجرومية في قواعد علم العربية"). A printed edition has not been compared. The book's word أَيُّ is written with a ḍamma there.
- "Al-Jadwal names the second family in the same way": it says "حرف شرط غير جازم" or "ظرف … متضمن معنى الشرط" word by word; the family name is Quran Learn's.
