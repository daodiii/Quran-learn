# Content spec: L2.03 The Verbal Sentence

Written 2026-10-27. Follows the shape of `L2.01-nominal-sentence.md`. Lesson file: `src/content/lessons/level-2/03-verbal-sentence.mdx`. Source for every parse: *al-Jadwal* (Maḥmūd Ṣāfī); *al-Durr al-Maṣūn* for disputes (none needed here: it has no entry for 2:124, the one verse tried).

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Say whether a sentence is verbal (the first word is a verb) or nominal, also when a verb sits inside a nominal sentence.
2. Find the verb (fiʿl), the doer (fāʿil) and the object (mafʿūl bihi), with their cases: doer nominative, object accusative, verb no case.
3. Tell a written doer from an attached one (-tu, -nā, -ū) and a hidden one ("he"); know a verb has one doer.
4. Explain why a verb before a written doer stays singular and takes -t for a feminine doer.
5. Recognise verbs with no object, one object and two objects, and an object put before the doer.
6. Go through four verses word by word.

Points not stated by al-Jadwal and so worded as "usual" or as our own reading:
- **Verb stays singular before a plural written doer.** 3:52 shows it; al-Jadwal does not state the rule. The lesson says "usual pattern, 3:52 shows it", and names L3.03.
- **Verb matches a feminine doer with -t.** Al-Jadwal names the tāʾ of the feminine in 3:35 and 12:18; the rule itself is not stated. The lesson says "usually".
- **A verb has one doer: no hidden "he" when a doer is written.** Consistent with every entry read (2:124, 3:52, 29:44 entry has no hidden pronoun); not stated as a rule. 2:124's note says a doer put before the verb becomes a mubtadaʾ with a hidden pronoun as doer.
- **The -hu in رَبُّهُ points back to Ibrahim.** Our reading of why the object is first; al-Jadwal only says the object may come first in special cases listed in longer grammar books.
- **Definitions of fāʿil, its two kinds, and mafʿūl bihi** are quoted from *al-Ajurrumiyyah* as printed on Arabic Wikisource (one copy, read 2026-10-27, vowel marks partly ours). To check against a printed edition.
- **"Most stories are told with verbal sentences"** is a softened remark in a Good to know note, marked as a common way to see it. Not sourced.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. Called the doer's pronoun endings (-tu, -nā, -ū) "signals" or "embedded in the conjugation". Al-Jadwal names them as the doer itself (attached pronoun). Separated attached from hidden doers.
2. Exercises 3 and 4 said a hidden pronoun "will be specified by" or "replaced by" the written doer. A verb has one doer: a written doer means no hidden one.
3. Exercise 1 and its answer said the accusative plural ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ takes "kasra + ـِينَ". Its mark is the letter yāʾ (-īna).
4. The table said a nominal sentence's tense is "present (timeless)". A nominal sentence has no time of its own.
5. Exercise 2 claimed the nominal order is for "permanent attributes" and the verbal order for "events". That is not a grammar rule and was not sourced; removed and replaced with a pointer to L4.17.
6. Called أَفْوَاجًا an "adverb of manner". Not used; the term is ḥāl (L4.01).
7. Did not show the object coming before the doer (2:124), although al-Jadwal notes it as a special case.
8. Said the doer "always" comes "immediately after" the verb, while 12:18 and 3:52 put a phrase between them.
9. Unlabelled made-up sentences (خلق الله الإنسان, كتب الكتاب) are now labelled.
10. Lesson links: "L3.03 Past Tense Conjugation" and "L3.04 Present Tense Conjugation" matched; no wrong numbers found in this lesson.
