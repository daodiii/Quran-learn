# Content spec: L2.11 Kāna and Her Sisters

Written 2026-10-27. Follows the shape of `L2.01-nominal-sentence.md`. Every parse follows al-Jadwal (Maḥmūd Ṣāfī); al-Durr al-Maṣūn for points where scholars differ.

## What the learner can do at the end

1. Find kāna or a sister in a verse and name its ism (nominative) and its khabar (accusative).
2. Say why the khabar ends in -a, -an, -īna or -ayni, and that a phrase or sentence khabar stands "in the place of" the accusative.
3. Find the ism when it is a noun, an attached ending (كُنتُمْ, لَسْتَ, دُمْتُ) or hidden inside the verb (كُونُوا, فَكَانَ).
4. Recognise the forms: kāna, kuntum, yakūnu, kun, kūnū, and laysa (few forms).
5. Read a khabar put first, and tell kāna nāqiṣa (with a khabar) from kāna tāmma (a doer, no khabar).
6. Tell kāna from inna, and read a verse with both (4:33).

## The lesson, step by step (5 steps, 32 blocks)

1. **Kāna and the news** (7): overview with Start; look (4:85); what kāna changes (made-up pair); why "incomplete" (nāqiṣa), Ajurrumiyyah in words; the mirror of inna (4:33, table); what kāna adds (time, "has always been", link to L2.01's 4:17/4:26); the marks of the khabar (-an, -īna, -ayni).
2. **Forms and the family** (7): the forms (2:143); command kun, kūnū (7:144, 17:50); the ism inside the verb (3:103, 4:85); the first book's list of 13 (Arabic quote and table); three sisters (3:103, 16:58, 25:64); laysa (13:43, 3:36); the sisters with a negative word and mā dāma (20:91, 19:31, 12:85).
3. **The khabar in different shapes** (6): a phrase (11:43); a sentence (12:85); khabar first (18:82); laysa with khabar first (2:272, 2:177); kāna inside inna (4:33); tāmma (2:280, 19:31).
4. **Five mistakes, four parses** (9): kāna and inna mixed; kāna as plain "was"; missing the hidden ism; trusting order; calling every kāna incomplete. Parses: 4:85, 3:103, 2:272, 11:43.
5. **Practice** (3): 6 exercises, then 5 more; summary and sources.

## Errors fixed from the old lesson

1. 4:33 was quoted as كَانَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ شَهِيدًا. The verse has إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ: ٱللَّهَ is accusative as ism inna, not "ism kāna".
2. 22:55 was quoted as وَمَا زَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟. The verse has وَلَا يَزَالُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ (a present tense, with لَا).
3. The sister table gave مَا دَامَتِ ٱلسَّمَوَاتُ (11:107) as a sister with a khabar. Al-Jadwal reads دَامَتْ there as tāmma (doer ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ, no khabar).
4. Table entries أَمْسَىٰ, صَارَ and ظَلَّ examples (ٱلْجَوُّ بَارِدًا, ٱلْمَاءُ ثَلْجًا, ٱلْمُؤْمِنُ صَابِرًا) were made up and not labelled. The table also gave لَيْسَ ٱللَّهُ بِظَلَّامٍ, which is not the wording of 3:182/8:51 (وَأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَيْسَ بِظَلَّٰمٍ). Those rows now say "not used with a khabar in the Quran" or quote real verses.
5. Exercise 1 quoted "أَصْبَحَ فُؤَادُ أُمِّ مُوسَىٰ" as 28:10; the verse starts with وَأَصْبَحَ. Replaced (al-Jadwal copy has a gap there).
6. 112:4: the old lesson called لَّهُۥ "related to the predicate" under the word-by-word list. Al-Jadwal reads the verse as khabar put first. Not reused (Al-Ikhlas).
7. "Relative pronouns are nominative (fixed form)" for ٱلَّذِينَ: a pronoun that never changes counts as being "in the place of" a case. The new lesson says this wording.
8. "Case: prepositional phrase functioning as accusative predicate": now "stands in the place of the accusative; tied to a left-out khabar" (al-Jadwal's wording).
