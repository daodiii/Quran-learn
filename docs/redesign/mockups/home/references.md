# Home hero: reference notes

Looked at on 2026-10-28 in a 1440 px browser. Notes are about structure and craft, not copying.

## What each site does well, and what we take

| Site | What the first screen does | We take | We leave |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple (home page) | One object on a dark stage, a two-line block of type, two buttons. The object does the talking. | One object, huge, with room around it. Almost no copy. | The product render and the scroll choreography. Our object is the verse. |
| Linear | Two-line headline, one supporting line, then the real product interface cropped under it. | Show the real interface, not an illustration of it. | The dark theme. |
| Stripe | Light page. A paragraph set large in dark ink, a bold field of colour on the right. | Colour used as one big field gives a white page its life. Our colours can be the word-type colours. | The gradient ribbon, the logo row. |
| Brilliant | Light page, big headline, a live card showing the real lesson interaction, two on-ramps ("I'm a learner", "I'm a parent or teacher"). | The hero shows the real learning interaction. Two on-ramps. | The friendly serif and the app-store badges. |
| Quran.com | Search is the hero. A "Start reading" resume card under it. | Search-first is already the Quran norm, so ours must go further: English word to Arabic root. A resume card. | The dark default, the dense lists. |
| Raycast | Two-line headline, one quiet line, one button, a lot of empty space. | Restraint. Nothing competes with the headline. | Dark, and the empty hero with no object. |
| Vercel | Two-line headline left, three short lines right, logos at the foot. | Type scale and a confident left edge. | The dark theme, the logo row. |

## What this means for the three heroes

- Every reference shows **one real thing** big: a product, an interface, a lesson card, a search box. For us the real thing is the dua and its breakdown, so it must be large and working.
- Headlines run to two lines at most. Ours already does: "Understand the Holy Quran for Free."
- Almost every high-tech reference is **dark**. Ours is light, so the tech feel has to come from craft: scale, precision, a working tool. One option I will test: a dark inspector surface inside the light page, like Linear's interface window. It is a surface, not a theme.
- Two on-ramps (Brilliant) fit "for every Muslim": "I'm starting from zero" and "I can already read Arabic".
- Search-first (Quran.com) is the crowded idea. Hero C must lead with something they do not have: type an English word, get the Arabic root and every place it appears.

## Known clashes with the taste-skill rules

The Oasis look is the agreed direction, so these are deliberate exceptions when the inspector runs:

- Gradient colour fields in the Stage panel (rule: no gradients).
- Pill labels such as "The missing word" (rule: no pill badges).
- Word-type tags in small text (rule: no tiny labels). They are part of the colour code and carry text labels.
- No photographs by agreement (the inspector wants four real pictures). Hero-only mockups, so the picture floor does not apply.
