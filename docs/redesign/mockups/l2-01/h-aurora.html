Sentences without a verb

Step 3 of 9

Arabic leaves out “is”

English needs a small word like is, am or are: “The sky is blue.” Arabic does not. It puts two words side by side.

هُوَhe
isEnglish only
ٱلْأَوَّلُthe First

A sentence like this is called a nominal sentence, جُمْلَة اِسْمِيَّة. Ism means noun, so it is a sentence that starts with a noun. No verb is needed.

Remember

You add “is” when you say it in English. There is no Arabic word for it.

Step 4 of 9

Every nominal sentence has two parts

مُبْتَدَأ Mubtadaʾ

The starting point. What the sentence is about.

خَبَر Khabar

The news. What the sentence says about it.

In the second sentence, that is the topic and the Book is the news about it.

Good to know

Many grammar books say subject and predicate. They mean roughly the same as mubtadaʾ and khabar.

Step 5 of 9

The rule: both end in -u

The mubtadaʾ and the khabar both take the nominative case, رَفْع.

بُ -uorبٌ -un

ٱللَّهُ ends in -u. غَفُورٌ and رَّحِيمٌ end in -un. The khabar can be more than one word. Both words say something about Allah.

Good to know

Words like huwa (he) and dhalika (that) never show an ending. They are still the mubtadaʾ.

Watch out

The mubtadaʾ is not the doer of a verb (the fāʿil). Both end in -u, but they belong to different sentence types. You will meet the fāʿil in L2.03.

Step 6 of 9

A longer one

The mubtadaʾ is a single word here, and the khabar is a group of words that belong together.

Look for the -u ending to find where each part starts. ٱللَّهُ is the topic. نُورُ begins the news, and the words after it finish the idea.

Tip

To find the end of the khabar, say the whole phrase out loud. Stop when the idea is finished.

Step 7 of 9

Sometimes the news comes first

The khabar can be a little phrase such as “for you”. Then it often comes before the mubtadaʾ.

Word for word: “For you is your religion.” The mubtadaʾ is still the word that ends in -u.

Tip

A phrase that starts with a little word like li- (for) is not the topic. The topic is the next word that ends in -u.

Step 8 of 9

Try it

Three quick questions. Pick an answer to see if you are right.

1In ٱللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ, which word is the mubtadaʾ (the starting point)?

ٱللَّهُ is what the sentence is about. The other two words say something about Allah, so they are the khabar.

2In هُوَ ٱلْأَوَّلُ, which English word has no Arabic word?

Arabic puts the two words side by side. You add “is” when you say it in English.

3Which ending do the mubtadaʾ and the khabar both take?

Both parts are in the nominative case (rafʿ). That is the -u family.

Step 9 of 9

You have finished the lesson

  • A nominal sentence starts with a noun and needs no verb.
  • It has two parts: the mubtadaʾ (topic) and the khabar (news).
  • Both end in -u or -un. Add “is” in English only.
